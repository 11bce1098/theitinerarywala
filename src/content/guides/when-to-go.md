Look across enough itineraries and a pattern falls out that has very little
to do with when people actually book. The best weeks in most of the northern
hemisphere are **May to early June** and **September to mid-October** — the
weeks either side of the school holidays, when the weather has arrived but
the crowds have not.

That is not a coincidence, and it is not true everywhere. Here is how to
read it.

## The three kinds of season

Almost every destination sorts into one of three patterns, and knowing which
one you are dealing with tells you what "shoulder season" is even worth.

**Temperate — the shoulder-season case is strongest.** Europe, the
northern US, Japan, the Caucasus. Summer is hot, crowded and dear; winter is
cold and quiet; the months between are the answer. The gap is stark: the
same coastal town can be a queue in August and a pleasure in late September,
at two-thirds the price, with the sea still warm.

**Tropical — it is a dry season, not a warm one.** Southeast Asia, the
Indian Ocean, much of Africa and Central America. Temperature barely moves
all year; what changes is rain. The "best time" is the dry window, and the
premium attaches to it because the alternative is genuinely wet, not merely
cooler.

**Inverted — the good months are someone else's winter.** Australia, New
Zealand, southern Africa, Patagonia. A European's instinct is exactly wrong
here, and the peak that matters is the local summer holiday, from Christmas
into January.

## What the library actually says

Across the trips on this site, the recommended windows cluster hard:

| Where | The window that keeps coming up |
|---|---|
| Europe (Austria, Croatia, Czechia, Germany, Greece, Hungary, UK) | May–June, or September to early October |
| Iceland and Norway | May–September, for daylight as much as weather |
| Japan, China, the Caucasus | April–May, or September–October |
| Southeast Asia (Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam) | November–February, the dry months |
| The Indian Ocean (Maldives, Sri Lanka, Malaysia) | December–April |
| The Gulf, Egypt, Oman | October–March — summer is genuinely hostile |
| India, Nepal, Bhutan | October–March, with the Himalaya best in October–November |
| Australia, South Africa | September–November, or March–May |

Two things fall out of that table. **The Gulf, Egypt and the desert Middle
East are the only places where the wrong season is dangerous rather than
merely unpleasant** — 45°C is not a matter of taste. And **the Himalayan
window is narrow**: October and November, between the monsoon clearing and
the cold arriving, which is why those trips book out early.

## When peak is worth paying for

Shoulder season is the default recommendation on this site, but not always.
Peak earns its premium when the thing you came for only happens then:

- **Wildlife on a calendar.** The Serengeti and Masai Mara migration, turtle
  nesting at Ras Al Jinz in June–September, whale seasons. The animals do
  not move for your budget.
- **Snow.** Ski trips and the northern lights need winter, and a mild year
  is a disappointing one.
- **Festivals worth structuring a trip around.** Japan's cherry blossom,
  Bhutan's tshechus, Germany's Christmas markets — where the event *is* the
  trip, going in the quiet month misses the point.
- **Long daylight in the far north.** Iceland and Norway in June give you
  eighteen usable hours; the same trip in October gives you eight.

## When the cheap months are cheap for a reason

The other half of the honesty. Low season is sometimes a bargain and
sometimes a warning:

- **Monsoon closes things.** In parts of Southeast Asia and the Indian
  Ocean, wet season is not just rain — boats stop running, islands become
  unreachable, and some resorts close outright.
- **Hurricane and cyclone windows** run roughly June–November in the
  Caribbean and Atlantic, and the southern Indian Ocean summer.
- **Winter in the far north** closes mountain roads and interior routes
  entirely, which turns a road trip into a much smaller loop.
- **Extreme heat** empties the Gulf and North Africa in July–August for a
  reason.

Cheap is real in all four cases. So is the reason.

## The practical rule

If you have a free choice of dates, **go in the week after the local school
holidays end**. It is the single most reliable saving in travel: the weather
has not changed, the prices have, and the queue you would have stood in has
gone home.
