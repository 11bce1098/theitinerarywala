Fourteen of the countries on this site sit inside the Schengen area, and
one visa covers all of them. That is the good news. The rest of it — which
consulate to apply to, how early, and what actually gets applications
refused — trips people up every season, so here it is in one place.

## First: do you even need one?

Around 60 nationalities enter the Schengen area visa-free for short stays.
If yours is one of them, you need no visa at all, and this page is only
relevant for the two border systems described at the bottom.

Everyone else applies for a **short-stay (Type C) Schengen visa**, which
permits up to **90 days in any 180** across the whole area. The 90 days are
counted rolling backwards from any given date — not per calendar year, and
not per country.

## The main-destination rule

You do not get to pick the easiest embassy. You must apply to the country
where you will **spend the most nights**. If nights are tied, apply to the
country you enter first.

This matters more than it sounds, because consulates differ sharply in how
fast they decide and who runs their application centre:

| Where you apply | Handled by | Typical decision |
|---|---|---|
| France | VFS Global | 5–10 working days |
| Italy | VFS Global | around 15 days |
| Spain | BLS International | around 15 days |
| Czech Republic, Hungary, Netherlands, Norway, Portugal | VFS Global | 10–15 working days |
| Iceland | a VFS Iceland desk, routed to whichever Schengen state represents Iceland where you live | around 15 working days |

A trip built as **Prague → Vienna** with four nights in Prague and three in
Vienna is a Czech application, not an Austrian one. Rearranging the
itinerary to shift the majority of nights is legitimate; misdeclaring where
you will actually be is not, and the itinerary you submit is the one the
border may ask about.

## The cost and the lead time

The consular fee is **€90** for adults, **€45** for children aged 6–11, and
free under 6. On top of that the application centre charges its own service
fee, which varies by country and is not refundable if you are refused.

Lead time is the thing people underestimate. Decisions are often quick —
France routinely decides in a week — but **getting an appointment** is the
bottleneck. In peak season, appointment slots run **4 to 8 weeks out**, and
for summer travel they are gone earlier than that. The practical rule: start
**two to three months before** a summer trip, four to six weeks for shoulder
season.

You can apply at most **six months** before travel.

## What actually gets applications refused

Not cover letters. The documents that decide it:

- **Proof of funds.** The single most common reason for refusal. France
  expects roughly **€65 per person per day** with accommodation prepaid;
  other consulates publish their own figures. Bank statements covering the
  last three to six months carry far more weight than a letter saying you
  can afford the trip.
- **Confirmed bookings.** Flights and hotels must be booked, which is why
  **refundable or free-cancellation bookings** are the standard approach —
  you need the confirmations before you have the visa.
- **Travel insurance** with at least **€30,000** of medical cover, valid
  across the whole area for the whole stay.
- **Ties to where you live** — employment letter, and evidence you have a
  reason to come back.
- **Passport validity**: three months beyond your intended departure, issued
  within the last ten years, with two blank pages.

If you already hold a **valid multiple-entry Schengen visa**, you do not
need a new one — it works for any Schengen country regardless of which one
issued it, as long as you stay inside your 90/180 allowance.

## Two border systems worth knowing about

- **EES (Entry/Exit System)** is live. The Schengen external border now
  registers your face and fingerprints instead of stamping your passport.
  There is nothing to do in advance — just allow extra queue time on your
  first arrival, especially at large airports in summer.
- **ETIAS** is a **€20** online travel authorisation for visa-exempt
  visitors, valid three years, expected from **late 2026**. It is not
  operational at the time of writing, which means **any site selling you an
  ETIAS today is a scam**. Check the official EU page close to your travel
  date. If you need a Schengen visa, ETIAS does not apply to you.
