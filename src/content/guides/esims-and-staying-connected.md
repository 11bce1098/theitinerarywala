Sixty of the itineraries on this site budget for a travel eSIM, usually
somewhere between **US$8 and US$30** for the trip. It is the least
interesting line in any budget and the one that most changes how a trip
feels, because nearly everything else — maps, ride-hailing, tickets,
translation, the restaurant's QR menu — assumes you have data.

## What an eSIM actually is

A SIM card delivered as a QR code instead of a piece of plastic. You buy a
plan before you fly, scan the code, and the moment you land your phone
connects to a local network. No shop, no queue, no swapping out the SIM
that carries your home number — the eSIM runs alongside it, so you keep
receiving calls and messages while using local data.

Providers such as **Airalo** and **Ubigi** sell country and regional plans;
the regional ones are worth a look for any trip crossing borders.

## The one check to do first

**Your phone must be eSIM-capable and carrier-unlocked.** Most phones from
roughly 2019 onward support eSIM, but a handset bought on contract may be
locked even if the hardware supports it.

Check before you buy a plan, and **install the eSIM while you still have
wifi at home** — activation needs a connection, which is awkward to arrange
at an airport in a country whose network you have not joined yet.

## When a local SIM still wins

An eSIM is the right default. Two cases where it is not:

- **Long stays.** Beyond two or three weeks, a local prepaid SIM is usually
  cheaper per gigabyte. In Georgia, for instance, a local Magti SIM takes
  five minutes at any city branch with your passport — the sensible pattern
  is a cheap international eSIM for day one, then a local SIM in town.
- **You need a local number.** Some countries tie deliveries, bank
  verification or certain apps to a local mobile number. An eSIM data plan
  usually does not give you one.

What almost nobody needs any more is a **rental pocket-wifi device**. It is
another object to carry, charge and return, and it costs more than the eSIM
that replaces it.

## How much data to buy

Maps and messaging use very little. What consumes data is video, and
uploading photos.

- **Light use** — maps, messaging, tickets, the occasional search:
  **1–3 GB** for a week.
- **Normal use** — the above plus social media and photo backup:
  **5–10 GB** for a week.
- Top-ups are sold in-app, so **start small**. Buying more is easy; a
  half-used 20 GB plan is money gone.

Hotel and café wifi is near-universal, which takes the pressure off the
photo backup.

## A few things worth doing anyway

- **Download offline maps** for your destination before you go. They cover
  you through the gap between landing and activation, and through the
  valleys, parks and metro tunnels where no plan helps.
- **Screenshot your bookings** — visa approval, hotel confirmations, entry
  registrations. Connectivity fails at exactly the wrong moments.
- Check whether your destination wants an **arrival registration** done
  online before you fly. Several countries on this site do, and they are
  free, quick, and much easier on home wifi than in an arrivals hall.
