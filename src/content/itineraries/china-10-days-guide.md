China is the trip that rearranges your sense of scale. The Great
Wall runs longer than the distance from Dubai to London; the
Forbidden City has 980 buildings; Xi'an buried an army of 8,000
individually-faced soldiers and forgot about it for 2,200 years;
Shanghai built the skyline of the 22nd century while nobody was
looking. And the whole circuit now runs on bullet trains that
make Europe's feel quaint.

Here's the honest headline, though: **China is the most
logistics-sensitive guide on this site.** Not because it's hard —
because it's *different*. Payments are app-based, the internet is
filtered, tickets are passport-locked, and everything rewards the
traveller who set up three things before boarding. Do the prep in
this guide's first section and China is startlingly smooth,
astonishingly safe and cheaper than you think. Skip it and you'll
spend Day 1 hungry outside a cashless noodle shop.

**Go April–May or September–October** — but **never during
Golden Week (Oct 1–7) or Chinese New Year**, when 500 million
domestic travellers have the same idea and every ticket
evaporates. Summer is hot-humid; winter is cold, clear and
crowd-free at the Wall. All costs are in Chinese yuan (CNY).

## Before you go: the three setups that make or break China

### 1. The visa

- **Check whether you need one at all.** China has opened up
  sharply: ordinary passports from much of Europe, plus Japan,
  Korea, Singapore, Malaysia, Australia and New Zealand among
  others, now enter visa-free for short stays, and the
  **240-hour visa-free transit** scheme covers a longer list
  again through the major airports. Neither covers everyone —
  India is not on either list, so an Indian passport needs a
  visa. Check the current list for your passport before you
  assume anything; it has been revised repeatedly.
- **If you do need one**, you apply through a **Chinese Visa
  Application Service Center (CVASC)** rather than the embassy
  directly: book at visaforchina.cn, and expect biometrics.
  Fees vary by nationality and by post — applying from the UAE,
  single-entry runs roughly **AED 270–320** for Indian nationals
  (double ~AED 380–440), paid in cash at the counter.
- Standard processing is about **4 working days**; apply 3–4
  weeks out. Documents: passport (6+ months), proof of residence
  if you are applying somewhere other than your home country, a
  photo to China's strict spec, flight and hotel bookings, and
  the form filled online first.
- The same system runs in every country that has a CVASC, so the
  process is consistent; what varies is demand. Busy posts want
  more lead time.

### 2. Payments — set up Alipay before you fly

China is functionally **cashless**: street food, taxis, temples
and vending machines all live on QR codes, and foreign cards
fail at most shop terminals.

- **Install Alipay at home**, verify your passport, and link an
  international Visa/Mastercard — it works as a tourist wallet
  now, with small fees only above ~¥200 per transaction. Add
  **WeChat Pay** as backup.
- Inside Alipay live the mini-apps you'll actually use: **DiDi**
  (China's Uber), metro QR codes, translation, trains.
- Still carry **¥300–500 in cash** — ruins ticket offices, tiny
  stalls and emergencies. ATMs at Bank of China take foreign
  cards.

### 3. The internet — read this twice

- **Blocked on Chinese networks:** Google (Search, Maps,
  Gmail), WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube.
- **The clean fix:** a **travel eSIM** (Airalo-style) routes
  your data through your home country — everything works, no
  VPN, no drama. Buy and install it *before* departure.
- Hotel Wi-Fi is Chinese network = blocked apps, so anything
  you need on Wi-Fi requires a **VPN installed before arrival**
  (they can't be downloaded inside China).
- **Download offline before flying:** a translation pack
  (Chinese), your hotel names in Chinese characters, and
  **Amap or Apple Maps** — Google Maps is a decorative app in
  China. **Trip.com** is the foreigner's workhorse for trains
  and tickets.

### The other rules of the road

- **Your passport is your ticket** — literally. Trains, the
  Forbidden City and the Terracotta Army are "real-name"
  booked and entered by passport scan. Carry it every day.
- **Hotels must be licensed for foreigners** — not all are.
  Every pick below is; when booking elsewhere, confirm
  "accepts foreign guests" (Trip.com flags it).
- **Safety honesty:** violent and street crime against
  tourists is genuinely rare — China is one of the safest
  places you'll ever wander at midnight. The actual hazards:
  the Nanjing Road "tea ceremony" scam (charming students
  invite you to tea; the bill is ¥2,000 — decline all
  invitations), and silent electric scooters at crossings.
- Tipping: not expected anywhere. Enjoy that.
- **Kids:** China treats children like celebrities; expect
  photo requests. The toboggan, the bullet trains, the pandas
  on every screen — it's a superb family trip.

## Day 1 — Land in Beijing, duck by dinner

Stay near **Wangfujing/Dongcheng** — walking distance to the
Forbidden City, metro everywhere.

- **Afternoon** — Land at PEK or Daxing (direct Gulf flights
  ~8 h). Airport Express + metro in (~¥30–35), or DiDi ~¥100–150.
- **17:30** — Legs-stretch on **Wangfujing** and the snack
  street, drifting toward **Qianmen's** restored Qing-era
  boulevard as the lanterns come on.
- **19:30** — The mandatory first dinner: **Peking duck** —
  Siji Minfu (queue, worth it) or Quanjude (the 1864 original)
  — carved tableside, wrapped in pancakes (~¥150–220 a head).

**Where to stay in Beijing** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Mandarin Oriental Wangfujing** — 5.0/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~2,400 reviews), a small jewel with Forbidden
  City-roof views. ~¥2,800+.
- *Mid-range:* **JEN Beijing by Shangri-La** — 5.0/5 (~4,900
  reviews), modern rooms, superb pool and gym, metro at the
  door. ~¥700–1,000.
- *Value:* **Park Plaza Beijing Wangfujing** — 4.5/5, quiet
  street ten minutes' walk from the palace moat. ~¥500–700.

## Day 2 — Tiananmen and the Forbidden City

**The ticket sprint, explained:** Forbidden City tickets
(**¥60 peak / ¥40 winter**, +¥10 each for the Treasure and
Clock galleries) release **exactly 7 days ahead at 20:00
Beijing time** and peak dates go in minutes; there are **no
gate sales**, it's **closed Mondays**, and you enter by
scanning **the same passport you booked with**. Book on the
official site via a booking service or Trip.com if the Chinese
interface defeats you — and set the alarm.

- **08:00** — **Tiananmen Square** (passport security check to
  enter): the world's largest public square, Mao's portrait on
  the gate, the scale already recalibrating you.
- **09:00** — Through the Meridian Gate into the **Forbidden
  City**: 72 hectares of vermilion walls and golden roofs, the
  ceremonial halls on the axis, then the quieter eastern
  courtyards and the **Treasure Gallery's** nine-dragon wall.
  Four hours; audio guide ¥40.
- **14:00** — Late noodle lunch, then climb **Jingshan Park**
  (¥2) directly behind — the classic overhead shot of the
  entire golden-roofed city you just crossed.
- **16:30** — Rickshaw-free wander of the **hutongs** around
  Nanluoguxiang and the Drum Tower — courtyard life, bicycle
  bells, walnut-shell grandfathers.
- **19:30** — Dinner by **Houhai Lake**, neon reflections
  included (~¥80–150 a head).

## Day 3 — The Great Wall (with a toboggan)

- **06:30** — Early start for **Mutianyu** — the restored,
  forested, less-mobbed section this guide backs over
  Badaling. Options: a small-group tour (**~¥350–500 pp**,
  hotel pickup) or DIY (metro + bus 916 + minibus, ~2.5 h,
  under ¥60 each way).
- **08:30** — Costs at the wall: **entry ¥45**, shuttle ¥20,
  then the choice — **cable car (¥140 return)** or the correct
  answer, **chairlift up + toboggan down (¥140)**: a stainless
  luge chute off the Great Wall of China. Under-1.2 m kids
  free, brakes explained, grins guaranteed.
- **09:00–13:00** — Walk tower 14 toward tower 20: watchtower
  after watchtower riding the ridgeline into haze. Spring
  blossom, autumn flame, winter snow-dust — there is no bad
  version.
- **15:30** — Back in Beijing: foot-massage recovery hour
  (~¥100/hour, everywhere, life-changing).
- **19:00** — Dinner in Sanlitun (modern Beijing) or dumpling
  house near the hotel (~¥60–120 a head).

## Day 4 — Heaven, the Summer Palace, and one last hutong night

- **08:00** — **Temple of Heaven** (through-ticket ¥35): the
  triple-roofed blue Hall of Prayer where emperors negotiated
  with the sky — but come early for the park itself: tai chi,
  sword dancers, choirs, kite men. Beijing's morning show.
- **12:00** — Lunch, then metro west to the **Summer Palace**
  (through-ticket ~¥60): the imperial lake garden — Long
  Corridor's 14,000 paintings, the marble boat, a dragon-boat
  ride across Kunming Lake.
- **17:00** — Optional detour: the **798 Art District's**
  Mao-era factories turned galleries, or the **Bird's Nest**
  stadium glowing at night.
- **20:00** — Farewell-Beijing hotpot — lamb swished in copper
  pots, sesame sauce (~¥100–150 a head). Pack; tomorrow you
  ride the fast ones.

## Day 5 — 350 km/h to Xi'an, dinner in the Muslim Quarter

- **09:00** — Beijing West → **Xi'an North by G-train: ~4.5
  hours, ¥515–555 second class** (airline seat, luggage racks,
  dining car). Book ~2 weeks ahead when 12306's window opens —
  **Trip.com takes the booking earlier and issues on release**;
  your passport is the ticket. Watch the Yellow River basin
  blur past at 350 km/h.
- **14:30** — Metro to the **Bell Tower**; check in.
- **16:00** — **Xi'an City Wall** first look from the South
  Gate, then straight into the **Muslim Quarter**: the Hui
  community has run these lanes for 1,300 years, and for halal
  travellers this is the promised land — *everything* is
  halal. Eat in sequence: **roujiamo** (the original burger),
  **yang rou paomo** (lamb soup over torn bread),
  **biángbiáng noodles** (the character has 58 strokes), cold
  rice-cake dessert, pomegranate juice (~¥60–100 covers a
  feast).
- **21:00** — The **Great Mosque** courtyard if open (a
  1,300-year-old mosque built as a Chinese garden — minaret
  as pagoda), night lights on the Bell and Drum Towers.

**Where to stay in Xi'an** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Ritz-Carlton, Xi'an** — 5.0/5 (~6,600
  reviews), the city's benchmark. ~¥1,200–1,800.
- *Mid-range:* **Novotel Xi'an The Bell Tower** — TripAdvisor's
  #1 value here: modern rooms, free laundry, five minutes from
  the wall and the Muslim Quarter. ~¥400–550.
- *Boutique:* **Eastern House Boutique Hotel** — 4.6/5,
  design rooms by the South Gate. ~¥500–700.
- (Doing the warriors at opening? **Angsana Xi'an Lintong** —
  5.0/5, ~11,200 reviews — sits out by the site itself.)

## Day 6 — The Terracotta Army

- **07:30** — Out to Lintong: the **tourist coach (~¥20, 1 h)**
  from the city, bus 602 via metro Line 9, or a guided tour
  (US$40–90 with pickup). **Tickets are ¥120, real-name,
  released 7 days ahead** — the official site takes passports
  directly; book the 08:30 opening slot.
- **08:30** — **Pit 1 first**, before the tour waves: 6,000
  soldiers in battle formation under an aircraft hangar of a
  roof, every face different. Then **Pit 3** (the command
  post), **Pit 2** (archers mid-excavation, the kneeling
  archer in glass), and the **bronze chariots** — half-scale
  masterpieces that took eight years to reassemble.
- **13:00** — Lunch by the site (biángbiáng again, no
  regrets), optional Huaqing Palace hot springs below Mount
  Li on the way back.
- **17:30** — Back in Xi'an: climb the wall for golden hour,
  or the free **Big Wild Goose Pagoda fountain show** (21:00
  most nights, Asia's largest musical fountain).
- **20:00** — A **Tang Dynasty dinner show** (~¥300–500 with
  dumpling banquet) for the full costume-drama Xi'an, or
  round two in the Muslim Quarter — no jury would convict.

## Day 7 — Bike the wall, bullet on to Shanghai

- **08:30** — The morning this guide fights for: **cycle the
  Xi'an City Wall** — entry **~¥54**, bike hire **~¥45 for
  2–3 hours** — a completely intact 14-km Ming rectangle,
  14 metres wide, ridden above the whole city. The single
  best two hours in Xi'an.
- **12:30** — Noodles, then Xi'an North → **Shanghai
  Hongqiao: ~6 h, ¥620–680** second class (or fly 2h20 if
  train fatigue has set in — similar money once bags are
  counted). China rolls past: loess plateaus, wheat plains,
  canal country.
- **19:30** — Check in, then straight to **the Bund**: the
  colonial stone mile face-to-face with Pudong's neon
  skyline — Oriental Pearl, the corkscrew Shanghai Tower —
  lights on until 22:00. The 19th and 22nd centuries staring
  at each other across a river.

**Where to stay in Shanghai** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Waldorf Astoria Shanghai on the Bund** — 4.9/5
  (~6,900 reviews), inside the old Shanghai Club at Bund
  No. 2. ~¥2,000+.
- *Mid-range:* **Grand Kempinski Hotel Shanghai** — 5.0/5
  (~27,600 reviews), river-view rooms on the Pudong bank.
  ~¥800–1,100.
- *Value:* **Harbour Plaza Metropolitan** — solid rooms,
  breakfast included, metro-close. ~¥450–600.

## Day 8 — Old Shanghai to the sky deck

- **08:30** — **Yu Garden** (¥40) at opening — Ming rockeries
  and zigzag bridges — then the bazaar's dumpling theatre
  around it.
- **11:00** — Soup-dumpling masterclass: **xiaolongbao** at a
  Jia Jia Tang Bao-style counter (~¥30–50) — bite the corner,
  drink the soup, then eat. Sheng jian bao (pan-fried) for
  comparison; science requires both.
- **13:00** — The **former French Concession**: plane-tree
  avenues, Wukang Road's mansions, coffee-shop Shanghai at
  its most livable. Two unhurried hours.
- **16:30** — Choose your altitude: **Shanghai Tower's**
  118th-floor deck (**~¥180** — world's fastest lifts to the
  world's second-tallest building; book a clear day) or the
  Peace Hotel's jazz-bar nostalgia at street level.
- **19:30** — **Huangpu River night cruise** (~¥120, 50 min)
  — the skyline switched fully on, both banks at once. Dinner
  in Xintiandi's restored shikumen lanes (~¥120–250 a head).

## Day 9 — Canals or castles: the choice day

- **Option A — Zhujiajiao water town** (metro line 17, ~1 h,
  free entry): stone bridges, canal boats (~¥150 per boat),
  post-office-from-1903 — the Venice-of-the-East postcard
  without the coach-tour circus of the famous names.
- **Option B — Shanghai Disneyland** (from **~¥475**,
  kid-driven households already know this is not optional):
  the Pirates ride here is the best on earth, book via the
  official app/Trip.com.
- **Option C — museums and markets:** the Shanghai Museum's
  bronzes (free, book ahead), Tianzifang's craft lanes,
  fabric-market tailoring if you left three days for a suit.
- **19:00** — Farewell dinner: hairy-crab season (Oct–Nov) if
  you're lucky, else a last hotpot; nightcap looking at that
  skyline one more time.

## Day 10 — The 430 km/h goodbye

- **Morning** — Packing tip made law: ride the **Maglev**
  (~¥50, 8 minutes, 430 km/h — the fastest commercial train
  on earth) to Pudong Airport even if your flight leaves from
  the other one's alliance; it's the correct final toy. Home
  via the Gulf (~9 h).

## What to pack

- Your passport's daily-carry pouch — it is metro card, museum
  ticket and hotel key deposit in one.
- Power bank (the apps run your whole day) and a Type A/C/I
  adapter — China's sockets take most two-pin plugs.
- Walking shoes with wall-and-watchtower grip; the Great Wall
  is stairs pretending to be a path.
- Layers: Beijing and Xi'an swing hard between noon and night;
  Shanghai adds humidity.
- Tissues and hand sanitiser (many public toilets are BYO),
  plus any medicines by brand — pharmacies stock different
  names.
- A phrase card in Chinese for your hotel addresses, saved
  offline and screenshotted.

## FAQ

**Can Indians use China's visa-free transit?**
No — the 240-hour transit and visa-waiver lists don't include
India. It's the CVASC visa (roughly AED 270–320 single-entry
from the UAE, ~4 working days) — annoying to assemble,
routinely granted for tourism with clean paperwork.

**Will my WhatsApp / Google Maps / Instagram work?**
On a foreign roaming eSIM: yes, all of it — that's the whole
trick, and why the eSIM is bought *before* you fly. On hotel
Wi-Fi: only through a pre-installed VPN. And regardless of
connection, Google Maps' China data is poor — navigate with
Amap or Apple Maps.

**Is the language barrier manageable?**
Yes, with tools: hotels and big sights run fine in English;
taxis, stalls and everything wonderful do not. Translation
apps (offline pack downloaded), the hotel's Chinese-character
card, and pointing at pictures cover 95% of situations. The
remaining 5% becomes your best story.

**Train or plane, Xi'an to Shanghai?**
Train if you can spare the six hours: city-centre to
city-centre, no baggage fees, and rural China scrolling past
beats any in-flight map. Fly if you're squeezing a day —
2h20 plus the airport overhead makes it roughly even
door-to-door.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
China's secret: **Xi'an's Muslim Quarter is one of the great
halal food destinations on earth**, and Hui-run *Lanzhou
lamian* (hand-pulled beef noodle) shops — halal by
definition — sit on practically every block in every city.
Beijing adds the Niujie mosque quarter. Vegetarians manage
with Buddhist restaurants, mapo tofu (ask for no mince),
egg-tomato everything and dumpling houses — but broths and
cooking fat lean meaty, so learn "wo chi su" (I eat veg) and
deploy the translation app.

**Is China safe? Can we walk at night?**
Street-safe to a degree that surprises everyone — midnight
noodles are a cultural institution, and children commute
alone. The realistic risks are scams (the tea-ceremony
classic, "art student" galleries), jaywalking into silent
scooters, and queues at Golden Week. Standard document care
and you're fine.

**What's the next China trip?**
Two pull hardest: **Zhangjiajie's Avatar pillars + Guilin's
karst rivers** (the nature epic) and **Chengdu's pandas +
Chongqing's cyberpunk stack** (the food-and-wonder city
pair). One of them is our second China guide.
