**Aitutaki's lagoon** is the reason to come. It is a triangle
roughly 15 km across, enclosed by reef, with an island at one
corner and a scattering of uninhabited motu around the rim — and
the water inside is shallow, still and a colour that photographs
accurately and still looks edited. People who have seen a lot of
the Pacific put it at the top.

**Rarotonga**, the main island, is the other half: a volcanic
cone 650 m high ringed by a single 32 km road, with a reef lagoon
all the way round, a cross-island track over the middle, and a
bus service that runs clockwise and anticlockwise and is called
exactly that.

The Cooks are **self-governing in free association with New
Zealand**. English is official, the currency is the New Zealand
dollar, and there are no visas for most people — which makes this
the most frictionless island trip in the Pacific.

## Before you go: the flight, the lagoon day and Sundays

### Entry and money

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for 31 days, extendable.
  You need an onward ticket and accommodation booked.
- **New Zealand dollar.** Cards work in Rarotonga's main
  businesses; **Aitutaki and the smaller places are cash**, and
  ATMs are limited. Draw in Rarotonga.
- There are **Cook Islands coins** in circulation, including a
  triangular two-dollar — keep one, it is the best souvenir here
  and worthless anywhere else.

### Getting there, and getting between

- Most arrivals are from **Auckland** (about four hours), with
  some from Sydney, Los Angeles and Tahiti.
- **Flights cross the dateline**, so you frequently land the day
  before you left. Check your dates twice when booking.
- **Rarotonga to Aitutaki is a 45-minute flight** on Air
  Rarotonga, several times daily. **Book it early** — the aircraft
  are small and the day trips fill them.
- **Baggage on the inter-island flight is limited**; leave a bag
  at your Rarotonga accommodation, which everyone does and nobody
  minds.

### Getting around Rarotonga

- **The bus is the whole system**, and it is excellent: one road
  round the island, a **clockwise** bus and an **anticlockwise**
  bus, roughly hourly, and you flag it down anywhere.
- **Scooters are the local way.** A Cook Islands licence is
  required and is issued at the police station in Avarua —
  historically after a practical test, which generations of
  visitors have treated as a rite of passage. **Check the current
  rule when you arrive**; it has changed over the years.
- Hire cars exist and are rarely necessary.

### Sunday, and church

- **Sunday is observed.** Most shops shut, many restaurants close,
  and lagoon tours do not run.
- **Go to a church service.** The **CICC** churches hold them
  mid-morning, the harmony singing is genuinely extraordinary, and
  visitors are welcomed and often fed afterwards. Dress modestly
  and sit at the back until shown otherwise.
- Plan a beach day or the cross-island walk for Sunday.

### When to go

- **April to November is the dry season**, with **May to October**
  the pick — warm, lower humidity, calm lagoon.
- **December to March is the wet and cyclone season**: hot, humid,
  heavy showers, and cheaper.
- Water is swimmable year-round at 25–29°C.

## Day 1 — Land in Rarotonga

- **Arrival** — Flights from Auckland typically land in the very
  early hours, and **the airport has a ukulele player greeting
  every one of them**, at four in the morning, which sets the tone.
- **10:00** — Having slept, walk **Avarua**, the capital: a
  harbour, a handful of streets, the **Punanga Nui Market** (best
  on a Saturday morning), and the Cook Islands Christian Church
  with its graveyard of elaborate tombs.
- **12:30** — Lunch — *ika mata*, raw fish in coconut cream and
  lime, which you will eat repeatedly.
- **14:00** — Pick a side of the island. **Muri** on the east is
  the lagoon beach everyone heads for; **Aroa** and the west have
  the sunsets.
- **15:30** — First swim. **Muri lagoon** is waist-deep a long way
  out with four motu across it, and the snorkelling off the beach
  is immediately good.
- **18:00** — Sunset, which on the west coast is the event of the
  day.
- **19:30** — Dinner. **Book the Aitutaki flight and a lagoon
  cruise tonight** if you have not already.

## Day 2 — Around the island

- **09:00** — Take the **clockwise bus** and get off wherever
  looks interesting — this is the correct way to spend a day here.
- **09:30** — **Black Rock (Tuoro)** on the north-west, where
  Polynesian belief holds that spirits of the dead departed for
  Avaiki. Good snorkelling and a rock you can jump off.
- **11:00** — **Te Vara Nui** or the **Highland Paradise** marae
  site above Arorangi — a restored hilltop village with the
  terraces and sacred ground of the pre-Christian settlement, and
  a guide from the family that owns it.
- **13:00** — Lunch at a roadside stall. **Cook Islands food is
  genuinely good** — *rukau* (taro leaves), *poke* (banana and
  pawpaw pudding), and whatever came off a boat.
- **15:00** — **Wigmore's Waterfall** and the **Papua stream**,
  inland on the south side.
- **16:30** — **Titikaveka** beach on the south coast, which has
  the clearest water on Rarotonga and far fewer people than Muri.
- **19:00** — Dinner, and if it is a Thursday or Saturday the
  **island night** shows — drumming and dance, which in the Cooks
  is fast, percussive and quite unlike Hawaiian hula.

## Day 3 — The cross-island track

- **08:00** — The **cross-island walk**, and start early before
  the heat. It is about **three to four hours**, roughly 6 km, from
  the north side to the south.
- **09:00** — The track climbs steeply through forest to **Te Rua
  Manga — the Needle**, a 413 m volcanic plug that stands clear of
  the ridge. The scramble to its base is steep, rooty and often
  muddy; there are ropes on the worst of it.
- **11:00** — The view from the Needle takes in both coasts and
  the reef all the way round.
- **12:00** — Down the south side to **Wigmore's Waterfall**, with
  a pool at the bottom that you will want.
- **13:30** — Bus back round.
- **15:00** — Rest, and swim.
- **18:30** — Dinner. **Pack a small bag for Aitutaki** and leave
  the rest.

## Day 4 — Fly to Aitutaki

- **08:30** — **Fly to Aitutaki**, 45 minutes, in a small
  turboprop. **Sit on the left** on the way out — the approach
  over the lagoon is the first time you see the colour and it is
  worth the seat.
- **09:30** — Land. Aitutaki has about 1,800 people, one village
  of any size, and a lagoon three times the area of the land
  inside it.
- **10:30** — Check in, then hire a scooter or a bike — the island
  is small and flat on the inhabited side.
- **12:00** — Lunch at **Arutanga**, the main village.
- **14:00** — **Maunga Pu**, the highest point at 124 m, which is
  a ten-minute walk and gives you the entire lagoon laid out —
  the triangle, the reef line, the motu scattered along it.
- **16:00** — Swim off **O'otu beach** at the eastern end.
- **18:30** — Dinner, and the island is dark and quiet after it.

## Day 5 — The lagoon cruise

- **09:00** — **The lagoon cruise, and this is the day.** Boats
  run from the main wharf and cross the triangle stopping at the
  motu.
- **10:00** — **Snorkelling at the coral gardens**, with giant
  trevally that come to the boat, and **giant clams** on the
  bottom.
- **11:30** — **One Foot Island (Tapuaetai)**, which has a
  **post office that stamps your passport** — a genuine novelty
  stamp, and the only reason anyone brings a passport on a boat.
  The sandbar off its tip runs out into water a metre deep for
  several hundred metres.
- **13:00** — Barbecue lunch on the motu, cooked on the beach.
- **14:30** — **Honeymoon Island** and the sandbars — you stand in
  the middle of the lagoon with nothing above the water in any
  direction but your own ankles.
- **16:30** — Back to the wharf, thoroughly sunburnt if you were
  careless.
- **19:00** — Dinner.

## Day 6 — Aitutaki, slow

- **08:30** — Bike the inhabited side: **Arutanga's 1828 church**,
  the oldest in the Cooks, and the marae at **Te Poaki o Rae**.
- **10:30** — **Kayak or paddleboard** across to the nearer motu
  under your own power, which is entirely doable in that water.
- **13:00** — Lunch.
- **14:30** — **Bonefishing** on the flats if that appeals —
  Aitutaki is a serious destination for it and the guides pole you
  across water a foot deep.
- **16:30** — Or simply the beach. Aitutaki does not require a
  programme.
- **18:00** — Sunset from the western shore.
- **19:30** — Last island dinner.

## Day 7 — Back to Rarotonga

- **10:00** — Fly back, 45 minutes, and take a window again.
- **11:30** — Collect the bag you left, and move to whichever side
  of Rarotonga you did not stay on.
- **13:00** — Lunch.
- **14:30** — **Snorkel Titikaveka** or take the **glass-bottom
  boat at Muri**, which does a motu stop and a fish barbecue and
  is better than it sounds.
- **17:00** — **Punanga Nui Market** if it is Saturday — produce,
  pareu, carving, and a food court that is the best cheap meal on
  the island.
- **19:30** — Last dinner. **Te Vara Nui** or an island night if
  you missed one.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- **Flights to Auckland typically leave in the small hours**, so
  the last day is usually entirely yours and the night is not.
  Many accommodations will hold a room or a shower for a late
  departure — ask.
- **Last stop** — black pearls from the northern islands, which
  are the one thing worth carrying home, and the triangular
  two-dollar coin.
- **Note** — You cross the dateline again and arrive in Auckland
  the following day.

## What to pack

- **Reef shoes.** The lagoon floor is coral rubble in places and
  the reef edge is sharp.
- **Reef-safe sunscreen**, and a rash vest — you will be in the
  water for hours and the reflection off that lagoon is brutal.
- **Your own mask and snorkel** if you have them.
- **Cash**, drawn in Rarotonga, for Aitutaki.
- **A modest outfit** for church, if you go, and for the villages.
- **Australian/NZ-style Type I plugs**, 240V.
- **Insect repellent** — mosquitoes at dusk, and dengue occurs.

## FAQ

**Is Aitutaki really worth the extra flight?**
Yes, and it is the single clearest recommendation in this guide.
Rarotonga is a lovely island with a good lagoon; **Aitutaki's
lagoon is in a different category** and people who have travelled
the Pacific widely still rate it first. Three nights is right —
day trips exist and waste most of what makes it good.

**Cook Islands or French Polynesia?**
**The Cooks are far cheaper, English-speaking and use the NZ
dollar**; the distances are small and the admin is nil. **French
Polynesia** is more dramatic — Bora Bora's peak, Moorea's spires
— and roughly twice the price. For a lagoon, Aitutaki holds its
own against anything in Tahiti.

**How strictly is Sunday kept?**
Shops and most restaurants close, and tours do not run. Go to a
church service — the singing is remarkable and visitors are
genuinely welcome — and keep the rest of the day for a beach.

**Do I need a scooter licence?**
The Cook Islands licence requirement has changed over the years
and historically involved a practical test at the Avarua police
station. **Check on arrival rather than relying on anything
written in advance.** The bus covers Rarotonga perfectly well.

**When should I go?**
**May to October.** December to March is cyclone season — wetter,
hotter and cheaper.
