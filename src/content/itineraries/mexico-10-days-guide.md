Mexico is two brilliant countries wearing one name. The capital
is the Spanish-speaking world's greatest city — Aztec temple
foundations under a colonial square, Diego Rivera's murals,
neighbourhoods that out-eat Paris at a tenth of the price. A
two-hour flight east, the Yucatán is something else entirely:
flat jungle pierced by turquoise sinkholes, thousand-year-old
Maya pyramids, and the Caribbean doing its best screensaver
impression.

Ten days covers both properly — four in the capital, five on
the coast — and the logistics are gentler than Mexico's
headlines suggest: one internal flight, comfortable ADO coaches,
Uber in the city, and tour pickups for the ruins.

**Go November–April** — dry, warm, mosquito-light (December–
January is peak price). May–October is hot, humid and
hurricane-season on the coast (September–October is the real
risk window), with sargassum seaweed a variable beach guest
April–August. Mexico City is spring-like all year — and
2,240 m high, so expect one breathless first day. All costs are
in Mexican pesos (MXN).

## Before you go: the visa rule worth checking first

- **Hold a valid US, UK, Canadian, Japanese or Schengen visa?
  You're in free.** Whatever passport you carry, Mexico waives
  its own visa for holders of a valid, multiple-entry visa from
  any of those countries — or permanent residence in them. Show
  it at check-in and at immigration, fill the entry form, done.
  Two catches: electronic authorisations like ESTA or the UK ETA
  do **not** count, it has to be a visa in the passport; and an
  ordinary residence permit is not permanent residence, so a
  Gulf residence visa won't do it on its own. If you have been
  through our [USA guide](/itineraries/usa-east-coast-guide/),
  that $435 B1/B2 keeps paying dividends.
- **Check whether you need anything at all.** Mexico admits a
  long list of nationalities visa-free for tourism — the EU and
  UK, the US and Canada, Japan, Australia, New Zealand and most
  of South America among them. Look your own passport up before
  assuming you need paperwork.
- **Neither of those?** You need a **consular Mexican visa**:
  ~US$56, biometrics in person, typically 1–2 weeks' processing,
  and often issued **up to 10 years multiple-entry** — apply 4–6
  weeks ahead at the Mexican embassy serving where you live, and
  confirm which one that is before you book. Mexico's SAE online
  authorisation covers only Russian, Turkish and Ukrainian
  passports.
- **Routing warning:** many cheap fares into Cancún connect
  through the USA, which needs a US transit visa even if you
  never leave the airport. Without one, route via Europe,
  Istanbul or Panama instead.

## Money, safety and the small print

- **Cash still matters:** cards work in cities and resorts, but
  ruins ticket booths, cenotes, colectivos and taco stands are
  **pesos-only** — carry 1,500–2,000 MXN in small notes; ATMs
  at real banks beat airport exchange desks.
- **The safety honesty:** Mexico's violent headlines are real
  and overwhelmingly elsewhere — the CDMX tourist core, the
  Riviera Maya corridor and the ruins circuit see millions of
  visitors with ordinary big-city precautions. The rules that
  matter: **Uber/DiDi, never street taxis, in Mexico City**;
  authorised prepaid taxis only at airports; nothing flashy on
  the metro; beach-town common sense after midnight.
- **Water:** bottled or filtered only — including teeth-
  brushing for sensitive stomachs. Street food is glorious and
  safest at busy stalls with high turnover (the queue *is* the
  hygiene certificate). Ease in; carry rehydration salts.
- Tipping: 10–15% at restaurants (often not included), 20–50
  MXN for porters and tour guides' hats.
- eSIM ~US$10–15; Telcel coverage is excellent on this route.
- **Kids:** Mexicans adore them — under-11s pay 100 MXN at the
  big ruins, under-13s enter Teotihuacán free, cenotes are
  nature's swimming pools (life jackets provided), and the
  whole Yucatán runs on family logistics.

## Day 1 — Land in Mexico City, the Zócalo at night

Stay in the **Centro Histórico** (walk to the big sights —
our verified picks below) or leafy **Roma Norte/Condesa** (the
café-and-taquería quarter, 15 min by Uber).

- **Afternoon** — Land at MEX. Uber or authorised airport taxi
  in (~250–350 MXN, 30–60 min by traffic).
- **18:00** — Walk into the **Zócalo**: one of the world's
  largest squares, the Metropolitan Cathedral sinking gently
  into the old lake bed, the flag the size of a tennis court.
- **19:30** — First tacos: **al pastor** carved off the
  trompo, five for ~80 MXN, salsa courage optional. Follow
  with churros dipped at **El Moro** (open till late).

**Where to stay in Mexico City** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Gran Hotel Ciudad de México** — 4.9/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~8,300 reviews), the Art Nouveau lobby with the
  Tiffany stained-glass ceiling, rooftop breakfast over the
  Zócalo. ~3,500–5,000 MXN.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Histórico Central** — 4.8/5 (~3,100
  reviews), free breakfast and all-day snacks a block from the
  cathedral. ~2,000–2,800 MXN.
- *Budget:* **Hotel Catedral** — 4.6/5 (~2,800 reviews), the
  centro's long-standing value pick. ~1,200–1,700 MXN.

## Day 2 — Aztec centro and the murals

- **09:00** — **Templo Mayor** (~100 MXN): the Aztec great
  temple excavated beside the cathedral — Tenochtitlan
  surfacing through the Spanish city built on top of it.
- **11:00** — **Palacio Nacional** (free, passport ID):
  Diego Rivera's staircase mural — the entire history of
  Mexico in one wall. Then the gold-domed **Palacio de Bellas
  Artes** (~90 MXN) for Rivera's *Man at the Crossroads* —
  the one Rockefeller destroyed, repainted here in defiance.
- **13:30** — Lunch at a centro fonda: comida corrida (soup,
  guisado, agua fresca) for ~90–130 MXN.
- **15:30** — Coffee at the **Sears café terrace** for *the*
  Bellas Artes dome photo, then ride the **Torre
  Latinoamericana** (~200 MXN) for smog-and-volcano sunset
  views over the endless city.
- **20:00** — Dinner in **Roma Norte** — this is one of the
  world's great food neighbourhoods; queue where the locals
  queue (~150–300 MXN a head).

## Day 3 — Teotihuacán, then the Anthropology Museum

- **07:00** — Beat the sun to **Teotihuacán** (entry **210
  MXN**, under-13s free): the 80 MXN public bus from Terminal
  del Norte takes ~1 h, tours run 900–1,500 MXN with guide and
  pickup, and the **sunrise hot-air balloon** over the
  Pyramid of the Sun (~3,300–5,000 MXN) is the splurge people
  frame. Walk the Avenue of the Dead between the two great
  pyramids (climbing is no longer allowed — the views from
  the plazas still stun). Three hours before the heat.
- **14:00** — Back in the city: late lunch, then the **Museo
  Nacional de Antropología** (~100 MXN, closed Mondays) in
  Chapultepec Park — the Aztec Sun Stone, Pakal's jade mask,
  and the finest museum in the Americas. Two hours minimum;
  it earns them.
- **19:30** — Stroll Reforma at dusk to the Angel of
  Independence; dinner in Polanco (upscale) or back in
  Condesa (better tacos, half the bill).

## Day 4 — Frida, floating gardens, flying wrestlers

- **09:00** — **Coyoacán**: cobbled plazas, the mercado's
  tostadas, and the **Frida Kahlo Museum (Casa Azul)** —
  **320 MXN foreigners, timed entry, and it sells out weeks
  ahead** on the official boletos.museofridakahlo.org.mx —
  this is the trip's second-hardest ticket after nothing.
  Book it the day your flights fix.
- **13:30** — **Xochimilco**: hire a painted *trajinera* boat
  on the Aztec canals (~600–800 MXN per boat per hour — the
  whole boat, not per person; take two hours, buy elotes and
  micheladas from passing canoes, pay the floating mariachis
  for one song).
- **19:00** — If it's Tuesday or Friday: **Lucha Libre at
  Arena México** (~300–700 MXN) — masked wrestlers, flying
  grandmothers' insults, pure joy. Otherwise: farewell-to-
  CDMX dinner and an early pack.

## Day 5 — Fly to the Caribbean

- **09:30** — MEX → Cancún (~2h15; Aeroméxico, Viva, Volaris
  from ~1,200–2,200 MXN — **budget carriers charge for every
  bag**, price the total). 
- **13:30** — Land CUN, skip the taxi hustle: the **ADO
  coach** to Playa del Carmen (~275 MXN, 1h15, hourly) is
  comfortable, cold and scam-proof.
- **16:00** — Check in, then **Quinta Avenida** (5th Avenue)
  — the walking street's 20 blocks of everything — and toes
  in the Caribbean before dinner.
- **20:00** — Dinner off the Quinta (two blocks inland,
  prices drop 40%): tacos de camarón, aguachile
  (~150–250 MXN a head).

**Where to stay on the Riviera Maya** (five nights, Playa del
Carmen base — central for every day trip below)

- *Luxury all-inclusive:* **Grand Velas Riviera Maya** —
  4.8/5 (~7,000 reviews), the coast's benchmark resort.
  ~12,000+ MXN all-in.
- *Beachfront charm:* **Mahekal Beach Resort** — 4.6/5,
  palapa-roof bungalows on the sand at the quiet north end,
  no wristbands. ~4,500–6,500 MXN.
- *Family all-inclusive value:* **Paradisus Playa del
  Carmen** — 4.5/5 (~18,500 reviews), kids' club and swim-up
  everything. ~6,000–8,500 MXN all-in.
- Town-centre budget beds (Calle 38 area) run 1,100–1,800
  MXN — plentiful; check current reviews before booking.

## Day 6 — Chichén Itzá, done right

- **06:30** — Leave early — this is the day that punishes
  lie-ins. Options: a tour with Valladolid + cenote + lunch
  (~1,400–1,800 MXN pp, pickup included) or DIY by ADO coach.
  Entry is **697 MXN** (100 MXN under-12s) — **cash pesos;
  the state and federal desks are separate, keep both
  tickets.**
- **10:00** — **El Castillo**: the calendar-pyramid whose
  staircases count the year, where the serpent-shadow crawls
  down at equinox. The ball court's acoustics, the Temple of
  Warriors, the cenote of sacrifices. Two to three hours;
  the vendor gauntlet inside is part of the experience —
  a polite "no, gracias" on repeat works.
- **13:00** — **Valladolid**: pastel colonial streets, lunch
  of cochinita pibil, and a swim at **Cenote Zací or
  Suytun** (the Instagram light-beam one, ~150–250 MXN).
- **19:00** — Back in Playa by dark; easy dinner.

## Day 7 — Tulum: ruins on a cliff, cenotes in the jungle

- **07:30** — Colectivo van down the highway (~50 MXN, every
  10 min) to **Tulum's ruins at 08:00 opening** (**265 MXN**
  including the national-park and Jaguar Park fees; +20 MXN
  shuttle): the only Maya city built on a cliff — iguanas,
  palm shade, and that view of green-blue water that sells a
  million posters. Ninety minutes before the heat and
  cruise crowds.
- **10:30** — Swim below the ruins at **Playa Paraíso**, or
  head straight for cenote hours: **Gran Cenote** (~500 MXN
  — snorkel with turtles under stalactites) and **Cenote
  Calavera or Azul** (~150–250 MXN) — two is the right
  number; bring biodegradable-only sunscreen, it's checked.
- **15:00** — Lunch in Tulum pueblo (~150–250 MXN), a look
  at the beach-road boutiques if you must, colectivo home.
- **20:00** — Playa dinner and, if energy allows, the
  Quinta's people-parade with marquesitas (Yucatán's crêpe-
  cousin, ~50 MXN) for dessert.

## Day 8 — Island day: Cozumel

- **09:00** — The ferry from Playa's pier (**Ultramar/
  Winjet, ~270 MXN each way, 45 min**) to **Cozumel** —
  Mexico's best reef water.
- **10:30** — Snorkel trip to the **Palancar and Colombia
  reefs** (~800–1,100 MXN, 2.5–3 h, gear included) — visibility
  runs 30 m+; expect rays, turtles, walls of fish. Non-
  swimmers: rent a jeep/scooter and loop the wild east coast
  beaches instead.
- **15:00** — Lunch and a wander in San Miguel, late ferry
  home, sunset from the Playa pier.
- **The alternative day:** families with a parks budget swap
  Cozumel for **Xcaret** (~2,600–3,300 MXN pp — underground
  rivers, wildlife, the night show; expensive and, honestly,
  excellent) — or simply take the beach day a day early.

## Day 9 — The slow one

- **Morning** — Beach proper: Playa's **Fundadores and
  Mamitas** stretches, or a lazy Mahekal-style beach-bed day.
  You've earned a day of nothing; the Caribbean is best
  consumed horizontally.
- **16:00** — Last-souvenir sweep (vanilla, habanero salsas,
  a lucha mask for the desk at work), fish-tacos-and-
  marquesitas farewell crawl.
- **19:30** — Book-the-window-table dinner: whole grilled
  fish, coconut shrimp, one last agua de horchata
  (~250–400 MXN a head).

## Day 10 — Home from Cancún

- **Morning** — ADO back to CUN (1h15 — leave 4+ hours before
  the flight; the highway has moods), fly home via Europe or
  Istanbul (or the US, for those whose visa made this trip
  free in the first place).

## What to pack

- Reef-safe (biodegradable) sunscreen — cenotes and parks
  check bottles; regular sunscreen gets confiscated.
- Water shoes for cenote ladders and rocky entries; a dry
  bag for boat days; your own snorkel mask if you're
  particular.
- A light layer for Mexico City evenings (2,240 m = 12–15°C
  nights) and arctic ADO air-conditioning.
- Mosquito repellent (jungle cenotes at dusk), rehydration
  salts, and the stomach kit you hope not to open.
- Small-note pesos habit: 20s, 50s and 100s make colectivos,
  tips and taco stands frictionless.
- Modest cover for churches; a lucha mask goes home in the
  suitcase whether you plan it or not.

## FAQ

**Is Mexico safe for tourists right now?**
On this route, with city sense — yes. The cartel violence
behind the headlines is concentrated in regions this trip
never touches; CDMX's tourist core and the Riviera corridor
are heavily policed and heavily visited. The rules that carry
the day: Uber not street taxis in the capital, authorised
airport transport, valuables boring, nights on main streets.
Millions do exactly this trip uneventfully every year.

**We only have a US visa — is entry really that simple?**
Yes: a valid multi-entry US (or UK/Canada/Japan/Schengen)
visa in the passport waives Mexico's visa at check-in and
immigration. It must be a visa sticker/foil, not an ESTA or
ETA, and ideally valid beyond your stay. Without one, the
Mexican consular visa (~US$56, up to 10-year multi) is a
straightforward 1–2 week process — just start 4–6 weeks out.

**Chichén Itzá or Tulum — if we must choose?**
Chichén for the wonder (it's the one your grandmother has
heard of, and El Castillo lands hard in person); Tulum for
the setting and the swim after. This itinerary refuses the
choice — but if a day dies, protect Chichén and do Tulum's
ruins as a 90-minute add-on to a cenote morning.

**Cancún vs Playa del Carmen vs Tulum as the base?**
Playa wins for this trip: mid-point of the coast (Chichén,
Tulum and Cozumel all within reach), walkable centre, real
town prices two blocks off the Quinta. Cancún's hotel zone
is a resort corridor (great all-inclusives, zero Mexico);
Tulum is beautiful, boutique and 30–40% pricier with worse
transport. Self-drivers touring ruins should add a
Valladolid night — the colonial charmer of the peninsula.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Vegetarians do better than Mexico's meaty reputation
suggests — quesadillas de flor de calabaza, chilaquiles,
bean tacos, guacamole by the litre — but ask "¿tiene
manteca?" (lard hides in beans and tamales). Halal-certified
kitchens are rare outside a handful of CDMX spots, so the
working strategy is seafood (superb and everywhere on the
coast) plus vegetarian street food; strict travellers should
plan around fish and pack patience.

**Do we need Spanish?**
Tourist-route English is fine (hotels, tours, Playa), thinner
at taco stands and colectivos — where twenty words of
Spanish, numbers and a smile upgrade every interaction.
Download Google Translate offline; menus photograph well.

**What about Oaxaca / the south?**
The food-and-mezcal deep cut — with Monte Albán, the
pueblos' craft villages and Day of the Dead's true home. It
deserves its own trip and is first in line for our second
Mexico guide.
