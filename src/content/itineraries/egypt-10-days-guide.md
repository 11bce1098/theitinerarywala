Egypt is where "old" recalibrates. The pyramid you'll stand
under on Day 2 was already ancient when Rome was founded;
Tutankhamun's mask — now finally reunited with his full
treasury in the **Grand Egyptian Museum** — is a teenager's
funeral gear from 3,300 years ago; and the Nile cruise between
Luxor and Aswan floats you past temples the way other rivers
pass warehouses.

It's also a destination that rewards a briefing: Egypt runs on
**baksheesh** (small tips, constantly), the famous sites have
gone **card-only**, and the hustle around Giza is a
performance with a script you can learn in two minutes. This
guide hands you the script. Well-run, Egypt is not just
magnificent — it's *easy*, and for Gulf residents it's a
four-hour flight.

**Go October–April** (Luxor is 22–28°C and perfect;
December–January is peak and books out). May–September cooks
the south at 40–45°C — doable with dawn discipline, cheaper,
sweatier. **Prices are in US dollars** (Egyptian-pound
volatility makes EGP menus a moving target; sites price in
EGP but the card-only rule spares you the exchange maths —
current EGP figures below at ~50/USD). 

## Before you go: visa, cards and the baksheesh decoder

- **E-visa (most Indians):** **US$25 single / US$60
  multiple**, on the official **visa2egypt.gov.eg** only —
  5–7 working days, apply ~2 weeks out. Passport 6+ months.
- **Visa on arrival, a shortcut worth checking:** holders of
  a **valid US, UK or Schengen visa, or a GCC residence
  permit** can buy the **$25 sticker at Cairo airport**
  whatever passport they carry — bring exact **USD cash** for
  the bank counter before immigration. Without one of those,
  the e-visa above is the route.
- **The card-only update:** Giza, GEM and most major sites
  now **refuse cash at the ticket window** — carry a
  Visa/Mastercard that works internationally *plus a
  backup card*. (Ironic twin: everything *outside* the
  ticket window runs on cash.)
- **The baksheesh decoder** — tipping is the operating
  system, not corruption: **EGP 10–20** for toilets and
  small favours, **EGP 50–100** for porters and unexpected
  kindnesses, **US$5–7 pp/day** for the cruise crew
  (envelope at the end), similar for your Egyptologist
  guide. Land in Egypt with a brick of small EGP notes
  (airport ATM, withdraw an odd amount like 950 to force
  small bills) and the whole country smiles.
- **The hustle script, pre-empted:** "the pyramid entrance
  is closed today, my friend" (it isn't — walk on), the
  free camel photo that costs $10 to *end*, the papyrus
  "museum" your taxi insists on. One phrase — **"la
  shukran"** (no thanks), smiling, while walking — solves
  90%. It's theatre, not threat; Egypt's tourist police
  presence is heavy and genuine.
- **Uber/Careem work in Cairo** and beat every meter
  negotiation. Dress: shoulders/knees covered for mosques
  (scarves lent); women will find modest dress reduces
  attention everywhere.

## Day 1 — Land in Cairo, first Nile night

- **Afternoon** — Land CAI (~4 h from the Gulf). Uber to
  the hotel; absorb the traffic (it's a spectator sport).
- **17:30** — Ease in gently: a **felucca sail** from the
  Corniche (~$15–20/boat/hour) as the sun drops behind
  the skyline — or straight into **Khan el-Khalili's**
  lantern-lit lanes and a mint tea at **El Fishawy**,
  pouring since 1773.
- **20:00** — First **koshary** (the national
  carb-on-carb masterpiece, ~$2–3 at Abou Tarek) or
  grilled kofta downtown (~$8–15).

**Where to stay in Cairo** (3 nights now, 2 at the end)

- *The icon:* **Marriott Mena House** — the 1869 hunting
  lodge with the Great Pyramid filling your balcony;
  pyramid-view rooms are the single most worthwhile
  splurge in Egypt. ~$350–500.
- *Luxury on the Nile:* **Kempinski Nile Hotel** — 4.8/5
  on TripAdvisor (~7,100 reviews), rooftop pool over the
  river, Garden City calm. ~$180–260.
- *Value:* **Paradise Boutique Hotel** — 4.7/5, downtown
  walkability at honest prices. ~$60–90.

## Day 2 — The Pyramids, then Tutankhamun's new palace

- **06:45** — At the **Giza plateau** gate for 07:00
  opening (**EGP 700**, card-only): an hour of
  near-private pyramid time before the coaches. Walk the
  Khufu perimeter — the scale only works up close.
- **08:30** — **Go inside Khafre's pyramid (EGP ~280)** —
  the smart-money interior: same crouched-passage
  experience as Khufu's **EGP 1,500** ticket at a fifth
  of the price (claustrophobes: skip both, zero shame).
- **09:30** — The **panoramic viewpoint** for the
  nine-pyramids photo. Camel protocol: agree the **total**
  price *before* touching the camel — EGP 400–500 for a
  short ride with photos is fair; "free" costs more.
- **10:30** — Down to the **Sphinx** (included) — smaller
  and stranger than expected, in the best way.
- **12:00** — Lunch with a pyramid view (9 Pyramids
  Lounge, on the plateau, ~$10–18).
- **14:00** — The **Grand Egyptian Museum** (**EGP
  1,590**, timed slots, book online): the grand staircase
  of colossi, Khufu's 4,500-year-old **solar boat**, and
  — the reason it took two decades — **Tutankhamun's
  complete treasury, all 5,000+ objects together for the
  first time since the tomb**. Three hours minimum;
  the mask alone repays the airfare.
- **19:00** — Optional **Sound & Light show** back at the
  Sphinx (~$20; gloriously retro) or an early night —
  tomorrow digs deeper.

## Day 3 — Saqqara, Dahshur and Islamic Cairo

- **08:00** — Private driver-guide day (~$60–90/car —
  hotels arrange; worth it here): **Saqqara** (**EGP
  600**): the **Step Pyramid** — the first large stone
  building on earth — freshly-opened noble tombs with
  paint still bright, and the eerie **Serapeum** bull
  vaults.
- **11:00** — **Dahshur**: the **Bent Pyramid** (the
  prototype that changed its mind mid-build) and the Red
  Pyramid — climb inside this one; you'll likely have it
  to yourselves.
- **13:00** — Village lunch under palms (~$8–12), photo
  stop at Memphis's fallen colossus of Ramses II.
- **15:30** — **Islamic Cairo**: the **Citadel and
  Muhammad Ali Mosque** (alabaster domes, city
  panorama), then golden hour down **Muizz Street** — a
  thousand years of minarets — dissolving into **Khan
  el-Khalili** for souvenir combat (start at a third of
  the ask, smile throughout).
- **20:00** — Feteer (Egyptian layered pastry, sweet or
  cheese) for dinner (~$5–10). Pack for the south.

## Day 4 — Fly to Luxor, board the boat, Karnak by afternoon

- **07:30** — CAI → **Luxor** (~1 h; **~$80–110** with
  bags). Transfer to your ship, settle in, lunch aboard.
- **The cruise maths, honestly:** the classic **4-night
  Luxor→Aswan** on a solid 5★ boat (Mövenpick, Sonesta,
  Jaz class) runs **~US$500–750 pp** including full
  board, the Egyptologist guide and the excursion
  programme — confirm entrance fees are bundled (most
  packages include the standard temples; the **extra
  tombs below are always separate**). Boutique sails
  (Oberoi Zahra-class, dahabiyas) run $1,200+ and earn
  it; sub-$400 boats explain themselves at dinner.
- **15:00** — **Karnak**: the largest religious complex
  ever built — the hypostyle hall's 134 columns are the
  single most overwhelming room in Egypt. Two hours with
  the guide.
- **18:00** — **Luxor Temple** at dusk, lit gold as the
  lights come on — walk the sphinx avenue's first
  stretch.
- **20:30** — Dinner aboard; sleep on the Nile.

## Day 5 — The Valley of the Kings (and the balloon question)

- **05:15** — **The hot-air balloon option** (~$80–130,
  weather-permitting): dawn over the Theban hills,
  temples and green-desert seam below — one of the
  world's great flights. *The honesty: Luxor ballooning
  has had accidents over the years; regulation has
  tightened, flights cancel readily in wind — book
  reputable operators through your cruise and treat a
  cancellation as the system working.*
- **07:30** — **Valley of the Kings** (**EGP 750**,
  3 tombs included; photo pass extra): painted corridors
  as fresh as last week. Add-ons that matter:
  **Tutankhamun's tomb (EGP 700)** — small, and he's
  *in it*; **Ramses V/VI (EGP 220)** — the ceiling
  masterpiece and best value; **Seti I (EGP 2,000)** —
  the Sistine Chapel of Egypt, for the once-in-a-lifers
  (buffer line).
- **10:30** — **Hatshepsut's temple** (EGP 440) rising
  out of the cliff amphitheatre, then the **Colossi of
  Memnon** photo stop.
- **13:00** — Sail. This is what you came for:
  sundeck, tea, the Nile's green ribbon sliding past
  buffalo, minarets and 5,000 years of riverbank.
- **19:30** — Captain's dinner; the ship locks through
  Esna to applause.

## Day 6 — Edfu and Kom Ombo: the sailing day

- **08:00** — **Edfu** (EGP 550): the best-preserved
  temple in Egypt — complete roof, nesting falcons,
  Horus myths in wall-comic form. The horse-carriage
  ride from the dock is traditional and hustly: **agree
  the round-trip price before boarding** (~EGP 200–300
  per carriage) and hold firm at the temple end.
- **10:30** — Sail all afternoon — the day the cruise
  justifies itself. Read on deck; wave at fishermen;
  accept the fourth hibiscus tea.
- **17:30** — **Kom Ombo** (EGP 450) at golden hour: the
  double temple to Sobek the crocodile god — with the
  **mummified-crocodile museum** as the kids'
  highlight of the week.
- **20:30** — Galabeya party aboard (yes, you'll wear
  one; no, resistance doesn't work; the photos are
  forever).

## Day 7 — Aswan: Philae and the felucca hour

- **08:30** — Dock in Aswan, the Nile at its most
  beautiful. Motorboat out to **Philae** (EGP 550) —
  the island temple of Isis, moved stone-by-stone above
  the dam waters; arrive by water as pilgrims did.
- **11:00** — The **High Dam** overlook (quick,
  Cold-War fascinating) and the Unfinished Obelisk if
  granite excites you.
- **16:00** — **Felucca hour** around Elephantine
  Island (~$15–25/boat) — lateen sails, black granite
  boulders, birdsong: the single most peaceful hour in
  Egypt. Optional **Nubian village** visit (painted
  houses, henna, crocodile photo-ops of dubious
  consent — go with the village-run boats).
- **19:00** — Sunset tea on the **Old Cataract**
  terrace (where Agatha Christie wrote *Death on the
  Nile*; the minimum spend is the ticket) — then last
  night aboard.

## Day 8 — Abu Simbel, then back to Cairo

- **04:00** — Disembark into the pre-dawn convoy south
  (3 h each way by road, **~$50–70 pp** shared
  van-with-guide; flights exist at ~$250+): **Abu
  Simbel** (**EGP 820**) — Ramses II's four 20-metre
  colossi carved into the mountain, relocated block by
  block above Lake Nasser in the 1960s' great UNESCO
  rescue. The interior sun-alignment halls justify
  every yawn.
- **13:00** — Back in Aswan: lunch, a last souq sweep
  (hibiscus, spices, Nubian baskets).
- **17:30** — Fly ASW → CAI (~$90–120); check in for
  the Cairo finale nights.

## Day 9 — Cairo, round two

- **09:00** — **Coptic Cairo**: the Hanging Church,
  Ben Ezra synagogue and Roman towers — the city's
  oldest quarter, quiet and moving.
- **11:30** — Your pick: the **Egyptian Museum, Tahrir**
  (EGP 550 — the atmospheric old halls; royal-mummy and
  star pieces have moved to GEM/NMEC, but the crowded
  cabinets have their own magic) or the **National
  Museum of Egyptian Civilization** (the royal mummies'
  new home, in a hall of appropriate hush).
- **14:00** — Lunch, then last-day shopping with
  calibrated haggling: spices, brass, a cartouche if
  the kids won.
- **17:30** — **Al-Azhar Park** at sunset — Cairo's
  green lung with Citadel views — then the farewell
  feast: stuffed pigeon (hamam mahshi) for the brave,
  grills and molokhia for everyone (~$10–20 pp).

## Day 10 — Home from Cairo

- **Morning** — Uber to CAI, four hours home. Your
  camera roll now spans 4,600 years, and airport
  security will ask about the papyrus tube. Worth it.

## What to pack

- **Two working cards** (ticket windows are card-only)
  + a cash wallet organised by note size (the
  baksheesh armory).
- Sun kit at desert strength: hat, SPF50+, sunglasses,
  refillable bottle (buy big bottles, decant).
- Modest layers: linen trousers/long skirts,
  shoulder-cover for mosques; a scarf does triple
  duty. Warm layer for winter desert dawns (Abu
  Simbel at 5am is cold).
- Comfortable closed shoes — sand, tomb ramps and
  temple flagstones eat sandals by Day 3.
- A small torch/phone-light for tomb corridors; power
  bank for long guide days.
- Stomach kit + hand sanitiser; bottled water only,
  salads with judgement.

## FAQ

**E-visa or visa on arrival?**
If anyone in the family holds a **valid US/UK/Schengen
visa or a GCC residence permit**, the **$25 airport
sticker** (exact USD cash) is the fastest option in
this book. Everyone else: the e-visa on
visa2egypt.gov.eg (official site only — copycats
charge triple), two weeks out.

**Is Egypt safe? And the hassle — how bad, really?**
The tourist corridor (Cairo–Giza–Luxor–Aswan) is
heavily policed and statistically kind; this route
never approaches the areas advisories flag. The hassle
is real but scripted — vendors, not threats — and
collapses before a cheerful "la shukran". Women
travelling without men report the guide-and-driver
structure of this exact itinerary as the comfortable
format.

**Which cruise boat class?**
The solid 5★ tier ($500–750 pp) is the sweet spot:
proper cabins, pool deck, good Egyptologist guides.
What $1,200+ buys is fewer cabins and finer food, not
better temples. Two checks before paying: **entrance
fees included in writing**, and a **top-deck cabin**
away from the engine. Dahabiyas (sail-powered, 8–12
guests) are the romantic's answer at luxury prices.

**Is the balloon worth the risk?**
It's the best aerial view in Egypt and the industry's
record, while improved, isn't spotless — our line:
book only through your cruise/reputable operators,
fly only in calm weather, and treat cancellation as
good news. Nervous flyers lose little: the Valley
delivers from the ground.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Halal is the default — this is the easiest halal
destination on the site after Malaysia — and
vegetarians eat like locals do half the week anyway:
**koshary** (the national dish is vegan), taameya
(fava falafel), molokhia, stuffed vine leaves, foul
medames. The only navigation is hygiene, not
ingredients: busy places, cooked food, bottled water.

**Add the Red Sea or Jordan?**
Both natural extensions: **Hurghada/El Gouna** (4 h
by road from Luxor) bolts 3–4 reef days onto this
route and headlines our Egypt #2 shortlist;
**Jordan (Petra + Wadi Rum)** is a 1.5-h flight and
its own future guide — the ancient-world double bill
of a lifetime.
