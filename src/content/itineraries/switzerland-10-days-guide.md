Switzerland is the country other mountains audition for. Trains
that leave at :04 because :05 would be late; lakes coloured by
glacier flour; villages where the loudest sound is a cowbell —
and above it all, the three-name wall of **Eiger, Mönch and
Jungfrau**, and a mountain so perfectly shaped they put it on
the chocolate.

It's also Europe's most expensive country, and this guide is
built around the three tools that fix that: the **Swiss Travel
Pass** (one price, every train-bus-boat, kids free), the
**Coop/Migros picnic strategy**, and the **weather rule** that
stops you spending CHF 200 to see the inside of a cloud.

And a note our readers will feel: **Bollywood has been filming
here since the 1970s** — you will recognise meadows you've
never visited. Switzerland knows it, too: Interlaken may have
the highest Indian-restaurant density in the Alps.

**Go June or September** — the sweet spots (everything open,
crowds and prices below the July–August peak). May and October
are beautiful with a catch: **many cable cars close for
maintenance in late April–May and November** — check before
building days around a peak. Winter is a different, white,
wonderful trip. All costs are in Swiss francs (CHF).

## Before you go: the pass maths and the price survival kit

### Visa

Switzerland is Schengen — the full UAE-application playbook is
in our [France guide](/itineraries/france-9-days-guide/): VFS Global,
~CHF 90/€90, EES biometrics live, 4–8 weeks of appointment
lead in season, bank statements and refundable bookings.

### The Swiss Travel Pass, decoded (this trip's spine)

- **What it is:** unlimited **trains, buses and boats**
  nationwide, 500+ museums free, three mountains free
  (**Rigi, Stanserhorn, Stoos**) — and the big peaks
  discounted: **50% off most cable cars (Titlis, Gornergrat,
  First, Harder Kulm...), 25% off Jungfraujoch**.
- **2025-tier prices (2026 tracks closely):** 3 days CHF 244,
  4 days 295, 6 days 379, **8 days 419**, 15 days 459 —
  second class, which is genuinely fine here.
- **This itinerary's play:** the **8-day pass** bridging
  Days 2–9, with cheap point-to-point tickets on the two
  edge days. Two people ≈ CHF 840, and it swallows every
  transfer, lake cruise and half of every summit below.
- **The family bombshell: children 6–15 travel FREE** with a
  paying parent (free Swiss Family Card; under-6s free
  always) — Switzerland quietly becomes one of Europe's
  best-value family trips.
- Solo alternative for slower trips: the **Half Fare Card**
  (~CHF 120–190 by month/offer) halves everything for a
  month.

### The price survival kit (read once, save hundreds)

- **The picnic strategy:** every station has a **Coop or
  Migros** — bread, alp cheese, fruit, a CHF 12 lunch with
  the best view in Europe. Restaurant mains run CHF 25–45;
  do one proper dinner a day, picnic the rest.
- **Tap water is glacier-grade and free** — refill at the
  village fountains (if it doesn't say *kein Trinkwasser*,
  drink it).
- Hotel breakfasts are included and enormous — engineer
  accordingly.
- Tipping: included, round up at most. Sundays: shops shut
  (station Coops the exception).
- **The weather rule (this trip's one commandment):** never
  pre-book a summit more than a day out. Check the **webcams
  at 7 am** (jungfrau.ch, gornergrat.ch) and swap days
  freely — this guide keeps every peak day swappable on
  purpose. Blue sky = go now; cloud = valleys, lakes, Bern.

## Day 1 — Land in Zurich, straight to Lucerne

- **Afternoon** — Land ZRH, which takes non-stops from
  practically anywhere.
  Trains leave from *inside* the airport: **Lucerne in ~1 h**
  (point-to-point ~CHF 26 — the pass starts tomorrow).
- **16:30** — The old town on foot: the 14th-century
  **Chapel Bridge** and its water tower (the postcard), the
  painted facades of the Weinmarkt, the city walls if legs
  insist.
- **18:00** — The **Lion Monument** — the dying lion carved
  into the cliff ("the most mournful and moving piece of
  stone in the world," said Mark Twain) — ten quiet minutes.
- **19:30** — Dinner in the old town (rösti, älplermagronen
  — Alpine mac-and-cheese with apple sauce, CHF 24–35) or
  lakeside picnic round one.

**Where to stay in Lucerne** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Hotel Schweizerhof** — 4.8/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~1,300 reviews), lake-front grande dame. ~CHF 350–450.
- *Old-town riverside:* **Hotel des Balances** — 4.6/5
  (~2,900 reviews), balcony rooms over the Reuss.
  ~CHF 250–350.
- *Value:* **Cascada Boutique Hotel** — 4.5/5 (~2,000
  reviews), two minutes from the station. ~CHF 180–240.

## Day 2 — A revolving cable car (or the world's steepest railway)

Two Lucerne mountain days — pick by webcam:

- **Titlis (3,020 m):** train to Engelberg (45 min, pass),
  then the **Rotair — the world's first revolving cable
  car** — to year-round snow, the **Cliff Walk** suspension
  bridge and the glacier cave. **~CHF 96 full / ~48 with
  the pass's 50%.** The reliable choice with kids (snow,
  always).
- **Pilatus (2,132 m):** the **Golden Round Trip** — boat
  down the lake (free with pass), the **world's steepest
  cogwheel railway (48%)** up the dragon mountain, gondolas
  down. Pass discounts apply; views over seven lakes.
- **17:00** — Back by boat either way: a **Lake Lucerne
  cruise is free with the pass** — ride the paddle steamer
  at golden hour just because you can.
- **19:30** — Fondue night #1 (CHF 28–35 pp; yes it's a
  tourist ritual; yes it's still great).

## Day 3 — Into the Jungfrau: Grindelwald

- **09:30** — Pass-powered ride to **Grindelwald** (~2 h
  via Interlaken; sit right, the lakes of Thun and Brienz
  perform in turquoise).
- **12:00** — Check in under the **Eiger North Face** —
  the village's permanent movie backdrop.
- **14:00** — **Grindelwald First** (gondola, 50% with
  pass ≈ CHF 32): the **First Cliff Walk** (free — the
  metal walkway bolted around the summit cliff), then the
  gorgeous flat-ish hour to **Bachalpsee** — the lake that
  mirrors Schreckhorn on calm afternoons. (The Flyer
  zipline and mountain carts are the kids'/adrenaline
  add-ons, CHF 29+ each.)
- **19:00** — Dinner in the village; tomorrow's webcam
  check sets the alarm.

**Where to stay in Grindelwald** (four nights)

- *The pick:* **Hotel Belvedere** — 4.8/5 (~1,800
  reviews), Eiger-view rooms, indoor-outdoor hot tubs
  aimed at the North Face. ~CHF 280–380.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Spinne** — 4.4/5, central with an
  infinity pool at the Eiger. ~CHF 200–280.
- *Value:* Grindelwald's chalet B&Bs and the famed
  Mountain Hostel (Eiger view from the terrace) run
  CHF 90–160 — book early; pick by recent reviews.

## Day 4 — Jungfraujoch: the Top of Europe

**The blue-sky day — deploy the weather rule.**

- **07:30** — The **Eiger Express** tri-cable from
  Grindelwald Terminal (15 min to Eigergletscher —
  shaving an hour off the old route), then the tunnel
  railway *through the Eiger* to **Jungfraujoch, 3,454 m**.
  **Tickets: CHF 100.60 round trip from Grindelwald
  (~CHF 75 with the pass's 25%; kids 6–15 a flat CHF 20;
  + CHF 10 seat reservation May–Oct).** Note the trap:
  the same trip from Interlaken costs CHF 234 full — 
  **basing in Grindelwald is the discount.**
- **09:30** — On top of Europe: the **Sphinx terrace**
  over the **Aletsch Glacier** (23 km of UNESCO ice),
  the Ice Palace's frozen corridors, snow underfoot in
  August. **Altitude honesty:** 3,454 m makes some heads
  swim — walk slowly, drink water, and it passes; the
  45-minute walk to **Mönchsjochhütte** is the earn-your-
  hot-chocolate option.
- **13:30** — Descend the classic way: train to **Kleine
  Scheidegg** (lunch with the North Face), then either
  rail on down or the lovely 1.5-h **panel-flat walk to
  Wengernalp/Wengen** through cowbell country.
- **18:30** — Back in Grindelwald, summit-drunk. Early
  night or hot-tub debrief.

## Day 5 — Lauterbrunnen: the valley of 72 waterfalls

- **09:00** — Pass-hop to **Lauterbrunnen** — the cliff-
  walled valley that inspired Tolkien's Rivendell,
  **Staubbach Falls** free-falling 297 m beside the
  church.
- **10:30** — **Trümmelbach Falls** (CHF 14 — not
  pass-covered, worth every rappen): ten glacier
  waterfalls thundering *inside* the mountain, reached by
  tunnel lift. The valley's loudest secret.
- **13:00** — Cable car + ridge rail to **Mürren**
  (car-free, 1,638 m) — picnic on the terrace lane with
  the Eiger-Mönch-Jungfrau wall filling the sky. Optional
  add: Allmendhubel funicular flower park (kids' Flower
  Playground).
- **16:30** — Down via **Gimmelwald** (the village that
  time and Rick Steves preserved) and home.
- **19:00** — Optional evening flourish: **Harder Kulm**
  above Interlaken (funicular till late, 50% with pass ≈
  CHF 20) — the two-lakes-and-Jungfrau platform at
  sunset.

## Day 6 — The flex day (weather's choice)

- **Sunny:** the **Männlichen "Royal Walk"** — gondola up
  (50% pass), the crown-viewpoint stroll, then the
  celebrated 1.5-h high path to Kleine Scheidegg with the
  trio in your face the whole way. Or **paragliding over
  Interlaken** (~CHF 180–230 — the Alps' most-flown
  tandem site, and it earns its fame).
- **Cloudy:** the pass earns its keep — **Bern** (50 min):
  UNESCO arcades, the Zytglogge clock, Einstein's flat,
  bear park; or Thun's castle and lake lido. Museum
  entries: free with pass.
- **19:30** — Farewell-Jungfrau dinner — the Belvedere's
  hot tub facing a moonlit Eiger is a strong closing
  argument.

## Day 7 — To Zermatt, the Matterhorn appears

- **09:30** — Rail south (~2h15 via Spiez and Visp,
  pass-covered; the Lötschberg line's Rhône-valley
  reveal is the warm-up act) to **Zermatt** — car-free,
  electro-taxi quiet, larch-chalet perfect.
- **12:30** — Check in, then the moment: walk to
  **Kirchbrücke bridge** — and there it is, the
  **Matterhorn**, exactly as advertised, absurdly
  self-confident.
- **15:00** — Old-village lanes (the 500-year-old
  granaries on stone mushrooms), Bahnhofstrasse
  window-pain, the mountaineers' cemetery — sobering,
  moving, part of the story.
- **19:00** — Fondue night #2, properly Alpine this time
  (~CHF 30 pp), Matterhorn silhouette included.

**Where to stay in Zermatt** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Omnia** — 5.0/5 (~1,600 reviews), the
  design lodge on the rock with its tunnel-and-lift
  entrance. ~CHF 500+.
- *Family/mid:* **Hotel Firefly** — 4.9/5 (~1,200
  reviews), suites with kitchens, superb spa.
  ~CHF 300–400.
- *Value:* **Hotel Alphubel** — clean, quiet,
  two minutes from the station. ~CHF 160–220. (Ask
  every hotel for a **Matterhorn-view room** — the
  upcharge is the best CHF 30–50 of the trip.)

## Day 8 — Gornergrat at sunrise, lakes by afternoon

- **06:30** — First cogwheel up the **Gornergrat**
  (since 1898; **~CHF 132 full round trip, ~66 with the
  pass's 50%**): 33 minutes to a 3,089 m platform facing
  the Matterhorn, **29 four-thousanders and the Gorner
  glacier** — sunrise turns the pyramid bronze and the
  observatory hotel serves coffee.
- **09:30** — The move connoisseurs make: train one stop
  down to Rotenboden, walk 10 minutes to **Riffelsee** —
  the lake that mirrors the Matterhorn on still
  mornings — then hike the flower path down to
  Riffelberg (or ride).
- **13:00** — Options by energy: the **Five Lakes Walk**
  from Sunnegga (funicular; Stellisee's reflection is
  the famous one; 2.5 h, gentle), or the budget-buffer
  splurge — **Matterhorn Glacier Paradise** (3,883 m,
  Europe's highest cable station, ~CHF 120 full/60
  pass): year-round snow tubing and the glacier palace.
- **18:00** — Golden hour at Kirchbrücke again (the
  photographers' row assembles nightly), farewell
  raclette (~CHF 25–35).

## Day 9 — Across the country to Zurich

- **09:30** — Zermatt → Zurich (~3h15, pass): the Rhône
  valley, the Lötschberg, lake after lake — Switzerland
  runs its highlight reel one last time.
- **13:00** — Zurich afternoon: **Lindenhof** hill over
  the Limmat, Grossmünster towers, **Bahnhofstrasse**
  for the chocolate raid (Läderach/Sprüngli — the
  hot chocolate at Sprüngli is the correct final
  ceremony), lake promenade to the arthouse west if
  time allows.
- **19:00** — Last supper: Zeughauskeller's history-hall
  classics or Hiltl — **the world's oldest vegetarian
  restaurant (1898)** and a fitting flag for how easy
  veg-Switzerland is.

## Day 10 — Home from ZRH

- **Morning** — Ten minutes to the airport by train
  (edge-day ticket), chocolate somehow already
  rationed. Fly home from ZRH; phone wallpaper
  already changed.

## What to pack

- Layers over logic: valley 25°C, Jungfraujoch 0°C —
  every big day spans both. A real warm layer + light
  gloves even in August.
- Sunglasses and SPF50 (glacier glare is fierce),
  refillable bottle (fountain country).
- Broken-in walking shoes with grip; the trails here are
  groomed but the gravel is real.
- Picnic kit: a folding knife (checked bag!), tote,
  and respect for cheese counters.
- Motion-sickness tabs for the cable cars if prone;
  altitude-sensitive travellers hydrate before summit
  days.
- Swimwear — hotel spas, lake lidos and hot tubs facing
  the Eiger.

## FAQ

**Swiss Travel Pass, Half Fare, or point-to-point?**
For this itinerary: **8-day STP, no contest** — the
transfers alone approach its price before a single
discount fires, and the kids-free rule ends the debate
for families. Slower or one-region trips (a week just
in Grindelwald) pencil out toward the **Jungfrau
regional pass or Half Fare Card** — do the maths on
your two biggest days and it's obvious.

**Is Jungfraujoch worth CHF 75–100 — really?**
On a clear day, yes — the Aletsch from the Sphinx is a
sight nothing else on the route matches. In cloud,
it's an expensive tunnel. Hence the commandment: book
nothing until the 7 am webcam, and know the fallback —
**Mürren/Männlichen deliver 80% of the drama at 20% of
the price**, and Gornergrat awaits in Zermatt anyway.

**When exactly — and the shoulder-season trap?**
**Mid-June to mid-July and September** are the golden
windows: trails open, crowds sane. The trap: **late
April–May and November** look cheap because half the
cable cars are closed for maintenance — check the
specific lifts before booking those months. Winter
(Dec–Mar) is glorious and a different guide.

**Bollywood filming spots — seriously?**
Seriously: Yash Chopra shot here for decades (there's
a statue of him in Interlaken and a suite named for
him), DDLJ's church scenes used **Saanen near Gstaad**,
and the meadows of the Jungfrau region *are* the
default song-sequence backdrop. Interlaken's
Indian-restaurant row exists because of it — your
thali with an Eiger view is a genuine Swiss tradition
now.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Vegetarian Switzerland is thriving — rösti, fondue,
älplermagronen are meat-free by default, Hiltl in
Zurich is the world's oldest veg restaurant, and
Interlaken/Grindelwald/Zermatt all keep Indian
kitchens (Bollywood legacy) with veg and often halal
menus. Dedicated halal butchery is city-bound
(Zurich/Geneva/Bern kebab and Turkish spots
everywhere); in the villages, the Indian restaurants
and fish/veg dishes are the reliable lane.

**What's Switzerland guide #2?**
The **scenic-trains special** — Glacier Express +
Bernina Express + GoldenPass in one loop with St
Moritz and a dip into Italy — or the **winter
edition** (Zermatt/Grindelwald under snow, sledging
runs, Christmas-market tie-in with our
[German markets guide](/itineraries/germany-christmas-markets-guide/)).
The trains are currently winning.
