The Maldives has a price myth and a geography fact. The myth:
it costs $5,000 a person. The fact: 1,192 islands include ~200
inhabited local islands where Maldivians actually live — and
since guesthouses were legalised, you can sleep 40 metres from
that same turquoise for **$70–100 a night**, eat grilled reef
fish for $8, and day-trip to sandbanks and whale sharks for
less than a resort breakfast.

This guide runs the smart split: **three local-island days**
(Maafushi — the budget-Maldives capital) for the ocean
adventures, then **three resort days** for the thing you came
to photograph — the water villa, the glass floor, the
nothing-but-blue. You get both Maldives, and the bill stays
earthbound.

**Go December–April** for the dry-season postcard (premium
prices, booked-out February). **May–October is the southwest
monsoon**: 30–40% cheaper, whale sharks still on duty, greener
skies with dramatic squalls between sun — the honest-value
window this budget assumes shoulder edges of. All costs are in
US dollars — the Maldives' second currency in practice.

## Before you go: the free visa and the two decoders

- **Every passport gets a free 30-day visa on arrival** —
  India, UAE, everyone — with a confirmed accommodation
  booking and onward ticket. The one task: the **IMUGA
  Traveller Declaration**, filed free at
  **imuga.immigration.gov.mv within 96 hours before
  arrival** (airlines check it; any site charging for it is
  a scam). File the departure one on the way out too.
- **Decoder #1 — the resort "++" bill:** quoted room rates
  sprout **10% service charge + 17% TGST** (that's the
  "++"), plus the **Green Tax — now ~$12 per person per
  night at resorts, ~$6 at guesthouses** — a family's
  "cheap" villa quote can grow ~30% at checkout. This
  guide's numbers include the reality; when comparing
  resorts, compare **total-with-taxes**, always.
- **Decoder #2 — the transfer trap:** resorts control their
  own transfers and bill them separately: **shared
  speedboats ~$100–250+ round trip per person** (South/North
  Malé atolls), **seaplanes ~$400–800** (farther atolls) —
  and **seaplanes stop flying ~4 pm**, so an international
  arrival after ~3 pm means a forced airport-hotel night
  ($80–150). This itinerary's resort choice (speedboat
  range) exists to dodge the whole trap. Maafushi's shared
  speedboat, by contrast: **~$25–30**.
- **The rules that surprise people, told now:** local
  islands are ordinary Muslim communities — **bikinis only
  on the designated "bikini beach"**, shoulders/knees
  covered on village streets, and **zero alcohol anywhere
  on local islands** (resorts are licensed; Maafushi's
  workaround is a floating bar anchored offshore — that's
  the whole loophole). Friday is the quiet holy day;
  excursion schedules bend around prayers.

## Day 1 — Land, speedboat, sunset jetty

- **Afternoon** — Land MLE (a mercy of a flight: ~4 h from
  the Gulf, under 2 h from South India). IMUGA already
  filed; visa stamp in minutes.
- **15:30** — **Shared speedboat to Maafushi** (~$25–30,
  45 min, several daily — your guesthouse books it; the
  $2 public ferry exists for the patient). Arrive on an
  island you can walk end-to-end in 20 minutes.
- **17:00** — Orientation stroll: the **bikini beach** (the
  roped, tourist-designated stretch — swimwear lives here
  and only here), the harbour, tomorrow's excursion desks.
- **18:15** — Sunset from the jetty — the first of seven
  progressively more absurd ones.
- **19:30** — Grilled reef fish, coconut rice and a fresh
  juice at a beachside grill (~$10–15 pp). Book Day 2's
  whale-shark trip before bed.

**Where to stay on Maafushi** (three nights)

- *Top of the island:* **Kaani Palm Beach** — 4-star
  guesthouse-hotel with pool and bikini-beach frontage.
  ~$120–170.
- *The sweet spot:* **Kaani Grand Seaview / Triton Beach
  Hotel tier** — sea-view rooms, rooftop breakfasts,
  excursion desks in-house. ~$80–120.
- *Value:* Maafushi's family guesthouses (Crystal Sands
  class) — clean AC rooms two lanes from the beach.
  ~$55–80. (Pick by recent reviews; the island's standard
  is high and honest.)

## Day 2 — Whale sharks: the big blue day

- **07:30** — Full-day **South Ari whale-shark trip**
  (~$60–75 pp, shared boat, lunch + gear included): the
  Dhigurah–Maamigili channel hosts **year-round whale
  sharks** — the only reliable resident aggregation on
  earth.
- **10:30** — The drill: spotter sees the shadow, boat
  positions ahead, you slip in and swim beside a 6–8 metre
  filter-feeding bus. **The ethics that matter: no
  touching, no flash, 3–4 m distance, never block its
  path** — choose operators who brief this and cap
  swimmers; crowding on busy days is the honest downside.
- **13:00** — Reef stops on the return: turtles at the
  coral gardens, a manta if the plankton gods say so.
- **17:00** — Back; shower off the salt; the floating bar
  dinghy shuttle for those so inclined.
- **19:30** — Maldivian night: **mas huni** (tuna-coconut-
  chilli, the national breakfast worth eating at every
  meal) and garudhiya fish broth (~$8–14 pp).

## Day 3 — Sandbank morning, dolphin evening

- **09:00** — **Sandbank + snorkel half-day** (~$40–50
  pp): a white comma of sand in open turquoise — the
  Maldives logo made real — with fruit, photos and
  nothing else; then two reef stops (nurse sharks and
  stingrays at the famous points, turtles at the grass
  beds).
- **13:00** — Lazy lunch; nap in the AC like a local.
- **16:30** — **Sunset dolphin cruise** (~$25–35 pp):
  spinner-dolphin superpods — fifty, sometimes hundreds —
  doing corkscrews off the bow as the sky goes tangerine.
  Kids' single best hour in the country.
- **19:30** — Last local-island dinner; settle the
  guesthouse bill in the morning (cards fine, small USD
  cash smooths everything).

## Day 4 — The upgrade: water-villa check-in

- **10:30** — Transfer to your resort (Maafushi sits in
  South Malé Atoll — several resorts run **direct
  speedboat pickups**; otherwise back via the airport
  jetty; ~$100–150 pp round trip at this range).
- **12:30** — The arrival ritual: cold towel, coconut,
  golf-buggy past the spa, and then — **the water villa**:
  steps into your own lagoon, the glass floor panel, the
  deck net if you chose well.
- **The board decoder, once:** **all-inclusive wins** for
  most couples the moment you'd order two drinks
  (mocktails count — AI covers them) and lunch daily —
  resort à-la-carte prices are engineered against you
  ($25 pizzas, $9 juices). Half-board suits villa-hermits;
  **confirm what AI includes in writing** (house wine vs
  labels, minibar, snorkel gear, one excursion).
- **15:00** — **House-reef orientation snorkel** — the
  drop-off 30 m from your steps is the resort's real
  crown jewel.
- **18:15** — Sunset on your own deck. Nobody speaks.
- **20:00** — First AI dinner; walk the lit jetty home
  over black-tip reef sharks patrolling the shallows.

**Choosing the resort** (three nights — book by category,
not brochure): at this budget's tier (~$550–700/night
all-in for two, speedboat range) live the honest
water-villa classics — *Adaaran Club Rannalhi,
Fihalhohi Island, Centara Ras Fushi (adults-only) class*.
One tier up (~$900+) buys bigger villas and finer dining
(Sun Siyam, OZEN's all-inclusive-everything). Check three
things always: **house reef quality (reviews say),
transfer type/price, and adults-only vs family policy** —
some villas ban under-12s for safety.

## Day 5 — The full resort day

- **07:00** — House reef at first light — best visibility,
  turtles commuting, the reef to yourself.
- **09:30** — The long breakfast; then included toys:
  kayak or SUP across the lagoon glass.
- **14:00** — Spa hour (~$90–150 — the overwater
  treatment rooms with glass floors justify one splurge)
  or the dive centre's intro dive (~$90–120, no licence
  needed, house-reef gentle).
- **17:30** — Golden-hour photo lap of the villa jetty —
  the pictures that launched this whole trip.
- **21:30** — Walk the beach with the lights off:
  **bioluminescent plankton** sparks underfoot on lucky
  nights (no schedule; monsoon months oblige more often).

## Day 6 — The clichés, performed sincerely

- **08:00** — **The floating breakfast** (~$50–90/villa):
  yes it's for the photo; yes the croissants get damp;
  yes, do it once — the tray, the lagoon, the grin.
- **10:30** — Resort snorkel excursion or the sandbank-
  for-two drop-off if offered; alternatively: aggressive
  hammock research.
- **16:00** — Last swim inventory: glass floor ✓, net ✓,
  ladder into lagoon ✓, one more turtle ✓.
- **19:30** — Farewell dinner — if the resort (or a
  neighbouring one) runs an **underwater restaurant**,
  that's the anniversary-grade splurge ($150–250 pp);
  the beach-BBQ under torches is the classic otherwise.

## Day 7 — Home, salt-cured

- **Morning** — Speedboat to MLE timed by the resort to
  your flight (file the IMUGA departure declaration).
  With hours to spare, Malé city offers the Grand Friday
  Mosque and fish market — pleasant, skippable honesty.
  Four hours later you're home, phone full of blue.

## What to pack

- Reef-safe sunscreen (double supply — resort shops
  charge $30), SPF lip balm, after-sun.
- Rash vest + leggings for the whale-shark day (sun beats
  sunscreen at hour three), your own snorkel mask if
  you're particular.
- **Modest layer for local-island lanes** (sarong/kaftan,
  shoulders-knees) — swimwear stays at bikini beach.
- Dry bag, waterproof phone pouch, GoPro-or-equivalent —
  this is the trip it was invented for.
- A power bank and book for boat days; motion tablets
  for the speedboat chop.
- Cash: small USD notes tip cleanly everywhere; cards
  cover the rest.

## FAQ

**Is the Maldives really doable on this budget?**
Yes — that's the split-stay's whole point: the ocean
(whale sharks, sandbanks, dolphins) lives on the cheap
half, the villa fantasy on the splurge half. Skip the
resort and it's a $700–900 pp week; skip the local
island and you're back at the myth. Together they're the
best-of-both at {{USD 1750}}.

**Local island or resort — which "is" the real
Maldives?**
Both, differently: Maafushi is the country (school runs,
mosques, fishermen, the actual culture) with world-class
water; resorts are the invention (one island, one hotel,
licensed bars, silence money). Honeymooners who skip the
local leg miss the whale sharks *and* the context;
budgeteers who skip the villa miss the image they came
for. Hence: split.

**Dry season or monsoon?**
December–April: postcard skies, glassy lagoons, peak
prices (February books out months ahead). May–October:
squalls that pass in an hour, 30–40% off, whale sharks
unchanged, better bioluminescence and surf. Verdict:
honeymoon-perfectionists go dry; value-hunters go
May–June or September–October and rarely regret it.

**Alcohol and dress — what exactly are the rules?**
Resorts: licensed and liberal — bars, wine lists,
bikinis anywhere. Local islands: none, anywhere, ever
(Maafushi's offshore floating bar is the tolerated
loophole), and swimwear only inside the roped bikini
beach; on village lanes cover shoulders and knees. It's
a courtesy the islands are open about, and visitors who
honour it are warmly hosted.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
The easiest halal answer on the site: a 100% Muslim
nation — local islands are halal by default (resorts
import pork/alcohol for tourists but label clearly and
always offer halal mains). Vegetarians do fine —
dhal, curries, rotis and the coconut-everything
kitchen — though menus lean tuna; resorts' AI buffets
solve it entirely.

**With kids?**
Brilliant — with two checks: some adults-only resorts
(and many water villas' unfenced decks) exclude or
age-limit children — **beach villas or netted decks for
under-8s** — and the dolphin cruise + sandbank beat the
whale-shark day (long, deep water) for little ones.
Maafushi itself is stroller-simple.

**What's the Maldives guide #2?**
The **local-islands-only ferry route** (Maafushi →
Fulidhoo → Dhigurah — whale sharks and mantas on
backpacker money) or the **liveaboard diving special**.
Divers are currently outvoting backpackers.
