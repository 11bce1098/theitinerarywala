Morocco is the closest a ten-day trip gets to time travel with
Wi-Fi. Marrakech's main square still runs on storytellers,
snake-flutes and orange juice; Fes hides the world's largest
car-free medieval city behind one blue gate; and between them
the Atlas mountains stand guard over the real prize — a night
in the **Sahara**, where the dunes go silent and the sky
switches on.

It's also a country that negotiates — prices, directions,
attention — and travellers who land with the script enjoy every
minute of it. This guide hands you the script, the e-visa
shortcut Indians now qualify for directly, and a route that
ends each argument with mint tea.

**Go March–May or September–November.** June–August cooks
Marrakech past 40°C and makes the desert a dare;
December–February is bright-days-cold-nights (the camp drops
near 0°C — romantic with blankets, pack accordingly). Ramadan
shifts rhythms gently — cafés quieter by day, streets festive
by night. All costs are in Moroccan dirhams (MAD; ~10.3/US$).

## Before you go: the e-visa and the rulebook

- **Indians get Morocco's e-visa directly** — no supporting
  US/Schengen visa needed anymore: **MAD 770 (~₹6,400),
  72-hour processing** (express 24 h at MAD 1,100), single
  entry, 30-day stay, on the official **acces-maroc.ma**
  only (copycat sites charge double). UAE residence changes
  nothing; UAE nationals enter visa-free.
- **The dirham is a closed currency** — you can't legally
  import/export meaningful amounts: exchange or withdraw on
  arrival, keep exchange receipts, and **spend or re-exchange
  before departure**.
- Cash rules the medinas (cards at riads and restaurants) —
  carry MAD 300–500 daily in small notes; ATMs everywhere.
- **The haggling rulebook** (souks only — food, taxis-by-
  meter and marked shops are exempt): opening prices run
  3–4× — counter at a third, climb slowly, keep it smiling,
  and know that **walking away is the strongest bid** (half
  of all deals close on the walk). Accepting mint tea
  obliges nothing but pleasantness.
- **The hustle script, answered in advance:** "the square is
  closed / wrong way, I'll show you" (it isn't; a polite
  *la, shukran* while moving works); henna ladies who grab a
  hand (firm no before contact); tannery "guides" (see Day
  7); photo-first-pay-later performers (agree MAD 10–20
  first or don't shoot); and the snake/monkey acts — skip
  them entirely, the animal welfare is grim.
- **Women travellers' honest note:** comments happen,
  especially in Fes's lanes — persistent, not dangerous;
  sunglasses, purpose-walking and zero engagement is the
  universal local technique. Modest dress (shoulders/knees)
  lowers the volume everywhere and is simple respect in
  Fes and Chefchaouen; Marrakech's tourist zones are
  lenient.

## Day 1 — Land in Marrakech, the square at full volume

Stay in a **medina riad** — the courtyard-house hotels are
half the reason to come.

- **Afternoon** — Land RAK (one stop from the Gulf; the
  return flies direct from Casablanca). Taxi to the medina
  edge (~MAD 100–150, agree first), porter to the riad
  door.
- **17:30** — **Jemaa el-Fnaa** as it ignites: the UNESCO-
  listed nightly circus of storytellers, gnawa drummers,
  dentists' teeth on tables and a hundred grills firing.
  Fresh orange juice (MAD 5–10) from a numbered stall,
  chaos absorbed from a rooftop café (Le Grand Balcon
  tier — buy a tea, keep the view).
- **20:00** — Dinner at the **food-stall rows** (follow
  the fullest benches: harira, grilled skewers, MAD 50–80
  pp) or the riad's home cooking.

**Where to stay in Marrakech** (three nights)

- *The legend:* **La Mamounia** — Churchill's palace-
  garden grande dame; even non-guests book tea. MAD
  6,000+.
- *Riad-luxury:* **La Maison Arabe** — 4.9/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~3,700 reviews), jazz bar, pool and the
  city's famous cooking school. ~MAD 1,800–2,800.
- *Value riad:* **Riad Dar Anika** — 4.9/5 (~2,400
  reviews), courtyard breakfasts, medina-central.
  ~MAD 700–1,100.

## Day 2 — Palaces, tombs and the souk deep-end

- **09:00** — **Bahia Palace** (MAD 70) at opening —
  zellige and cedar ceilings before the coaches — then
  the **Saadian Tombs** (MAD 70, rediscovered 1917) and
  El Badi's stork-topped ruins (MAD 60) if appetite
  allows.
- **11:30** — **Ben Youssef Madrasa** (~MAD 70–100): the
  most photogenic courtyard in Morocco — 14th-century
  student cells around a carved-stucco masterpiece.
- **13:00** — Lunch at **Amal Centre** (the women's
  training-kitchen nonprofit — the best-value tagine in
  town and the best cause, ~MAD 60–90).
- **14:30** — **The souks, properly**: dyers' wool
  rainbows, slipper mountains, lantern constellations —
  deploy the rulebook, buy the thing you'll regret
  missing (argan and spices near-last, they weigh
  nothing).
- **18:00** — First **hammam**: tourist-spa versions
  (~MAD 250–400 with gommage scrub) ease you in; the
  MAD 15 neighbourhood original is the graduate course.
- **20:00** — Rooftop dinner above the square's roar
  (~MAD 120–200 pp).

## Day 3 — The blue garden, then breathe

- **08:30** — **Jardin Majorelle** at the first slot
  (book online — the queue is real): Yves Saint
  Laurent's cobalt-blue cactus garden, with the **YSL
  Museum** next door (combo ~MAD 300–330; garden-only
  ~MAD 150–170). An hour of the best colour in Africa.
- **11:00** — Gueliz (the French new town) for coffee
  and a breather from the medina's volume.
- **13:00** — Choice afternoon: the **La Maison Arabe
  cooking class** (~MAD 600–800 — tagine secrets you'll
  actually reuse), the Secret Garden's calm (MAD 100),
  or pool time — tomorrow starts early.
- **19:00** — Last Jemaa lap; pack a small overnight
  bag for the desert (big luggage waits at the riad or
  rides along — tours allow both). Cash for the road.

## Day 4 — Over the Atlas to the kasbahs (Desert day 1)

- **07:00** — Pickup for the **3-day Sahara route to
  Fes** — the itinerary's spine. **The tour maths:**
  shared minibus with standard camp ~MAD 1,700–2,200
  pp; **shared with a luxury camp (ensuite tents — this
  guide's pick) ~MAD 2,300–2,800 pp**; private 4x4 from
  ~MAD 4,500 pp. Includes transport, driver,
  dinner-and-breakfast at both nights. Book a
  well-reviewed operator online — never the tout at the
  square.
- **09:30** — The **Tizi n'Tichka pass** (2,260 m) —
  hairpins over the High Atlas (motion tablets for the
  prone; views as payment).
- **12:30** — **Aït Benhaddou** (small entry ~MAD 30 at
  the granary): the mud-brick ksar of *Gladiator* and
  *Game of Thrones* — cross the river, climb to the
  agadir roof, squint and see caravans.
- **15:00** — Ouarzazate ("the door of the desert";
  Atlas Studios optional), then the Valley of Roses
  into **Dades Gorge** — overnight at a gorge hotel,
  dinner under the "monkey fingers" cliffs.

## Day 5 — Todra's walls, then the dunes (Desert day 2)

- **08:30** — **Todra Gorge**: a 300-metre-high crack
  you walk into, river at your feet, climbers on the
  walls.
- **12:30** — Palm-oasis lunch stop; fossil country at
  Erfoud (the marble full of ammonites is real —
  bargain rulebook applies).
- **17:00** — **Merzouga**: swap wheels for **camels**
  (about an hour into the dunes — hypnotic, mildly
  bottom-bruising; walkers may walk alongside, and
  it's the more comfortable choice) as **Erg Chebbi**
  turns copper at sunset.
- **19:00** — **The luxury camp**: proper beds and
  ensuite showers under canvas, tagine dinner, gnawa
  drums at the fire — then the main event: **dune
  silence and the Milky Way** with zero light
  pollution. Sandboards usually free; midnight dune
  sits mandatory by instinct.

## Day 6 — Sunrise sand, the long road to Fes (Desert day 3)

- **06:15** — Climb the big dune for sunrise — the
  Sahara doing colour theory. Breakfast, 4x4 back to
  the road.
- **09:00–17:30** — The honest long haul to **Fes**
  (~7 h with stops): the Ziz Valley's million-palm
  canyon, lunch in Midelt (apple capital), the cedar
  forests of Azrou with their **Barbary macaques**
  (photo yes, feeding no), and Ifrane — the alpine
  town nicknamed "Morocco's Switzerland", storks on
  Swiss roofs.
- **18:00** — Fes riad, hammam-grade shower, rooftop
  dinner (~MAD 120–200) with the world's oldest
  medina humming below.

**Where to stay in Fes** (two nights)

- *The pick:* **Riad Laaroussa** — 4.9/5 (~1,900
  reviews), 17th-century palace courtyard with a
  proper spa-hammam. ~MAD 1,200–1,800.
- *Boutique:* **Riad Le Calife** — 5.0/5 (~1,200
  reviews), antique-filled calm above the maze.
  ~MAD 900–1,400.
- *Modern alt:* **Hotel Sahrai** — 4.4/5, hilltop
  infinity pool overlooking the medina (for
  maze-weary evenings). ~MAD 1,500–2,200.

## Day 7 — Fes: the medieval deep-end

**Hire the licensed guide** (~MAD 350–500 half-day via
your riad — the one city where it transforms the day):
Fes el-Bali's 9,000 lanes are the world's largest
car-free urban maze, and the donkeys have right of way.

- **09:00** — In through **Bab Boujloud** (blue
  outside, green inside), down **Talaa Kebira** past
  fondouks and dye vats.
- **10:00** — **Bou Inania Madrasa** (MAD 20 — the
  finest Marinid building open to non-Muslims) and the
  Nejjarine woodwork museum's carved fondouk.
- **12:00** — **The Chouara Tanneries**, decoded: entry
  is via leather shops whose terraces overlook the
  honeycomb of dye pits — "free" means a mint sprig
  against the smell, a pitch afterwards, and **MAD
  20–50 tip expected**; browse without buying is fine,
  firmness required. The 11th-century spectacle below
  is worth all of it.
- **13:30** — Medina lunch (~MAD 60–100): bissara,
  kefta, or the Fassi masterpiece **pastilla** (sweet-
  savory pigeon/chicken pie).
- **15:00** — **Al-Qarawiyyin** — founded 859 AD by
  Fatima al-Fihri, the world's oldest continuously
  operating university (non-Muslims view the exquisite
  courtyard from the doorways) — then Seffarine
  Square's coppersmith percussion.
- **18:30** — Sunset over the whole medina from the
  Marinid Tombs viewpoint; dinner in a palace
  restaurant (~MAD 150–250) or the riad.

## Day 8 — Into the blue: Chefchaouen

- **09:00** — CTM bus north (~4 h, ~MAD 110–130 —
  book seats a day ahead) into the Rif mountains, the
  land turning green.
- **13:30** — **Chefchaouen**: fifty shades of blue
  paint on every wall, stair and flowerpot. Check in,
  then wander the compact medina — the famous lanes
  (Callejon El Asri, the blue stairways) hit their
  colour best in soft light. **Etiquette:** the
  prettiest doorways are people's homes — shoot the
  lane, not through the door, and a smiled request
  works wonders.
- **17:45** — The 25-minute walk up to the **Spanish
  Mosque** for sunset over the entire blue bowl —
  the town's one mandatory appointment.
- **20:00** — Rooftop tagine (~MAD 70–120) —
  goat-cheese salads are the Rif's specialty. One
  overnight is the trick: dawn belongs to you.

## Day 9 — Blue at dawn, then the coast

- **07:30** — Empty-lane photos (the town sleeps in;
  8–9 am is yours alone), msemen-and-honey breakfast.
- **10:30** — CTM toward **Casablanca** (~6 h; the
  rail-lovers' alternative: taxi to Tangier and the
  smart new train south).
- **17:30** — Casablanca check-in; **the honesty:**
  Casa is a working metropolis, not a postcard — it's
  here for tomorrow's mosque and the airport. Corniche
  stroll, seafood dinner, or the wink of a nightcap at
  **Rick's Café** (built 2004 to match the movie;
  knowingly, enjoyably fake).

## Day 10 — The mosque on the ocean, home

- **09:00** — **Hassan II Mosque guided tour (MAD 140;
  non-Muslims welcome on tours** — one of very few in
  Morocco): the world's tallest minaret (210 m), a
  glass-section floor over the Atlantic, doors of
  titanium and cedar, room for 105,000. The single
  most jaw-dropping modern building in North Africa —
  and the perfect final image.
- **12:00** — Spend the last dirhams (closed currency,
  remember), airport train/taxi to CMN — direct Gulf
  flights home.

## What to pack

- Layers with range: Marrakech afternoons 30°C+, the
  desert camp near 0°C in winter — a real warm layer
  and a scarf/cheche (buy one blue, MAD 30–50, it's
  sun-shield, dust-mask and photo-prop).
- Modest-cover basics (shoulders/knees) — comfort in
  Fes and Chefchaouen, required at Hassan II.
- Closed shoes for medina cobbles and dune-cool
  mornings; sandals for the camp.
- Motion tablets (Atlas hairpins), torch/headlamp for
  the camp, power bank (desert days are long).
- Wet wipes + hand sanitiser (medina life), basic
  stomach kit; bottled water throughout.
- Small-notes wallet: MAD 10/20 coins are the tipping,
  toilet and orange-juice currency.

## FAQ

**The e-visa is really direct for Indians now?**
Yes — India sits in the direct-eligibility category:
MAD 770, 72 hours, no supporting US/Schengen visa
required (that old workaround is obsolete). Use
acces-maroc.ma only; agents and copycat sites add
50–100% for form-filling. Longer or multi-entry needs
go via the embassy/BLS sticker route.

**Is the hassle as bad as people say?**
It's persistent, not dangerous — and it's a
transaction, not a threat. The whole defence is three
tools: *la, shukran* (no thanks) while moving, prices
agreed before services (taxis, photos, guides), and
the walk-away in shops. Travellers who arrive with the
script report the souks as the trip's joy, not its
tax. Violent crime against tourists is genuinely rare.

**Which desert tour tier — and why end in Fes?**
The **shared-transport + luxury-camp** middle path is
the sweet spot: same route and sunsets as the
backpacker bus, but ensuite tents and real mattresses
at the night that matters. Private 4x4 buys schedule
freedom, not better dunes. And **ending in Fes**
(rather than looping back) converts a wasted
return-drive day into your Fes arrival — the
route-design trick this whole itinerary hangs on.

**Chefchaouen and Casablanca — worth it?**
Chefchaouen: yes, precisely once and overnight — it's
a two-hour town that becomes magical when the
day-trippers leave. Casablanca: honestly, it's one
transcendent mosque attached to an airport — which is
exactly how this route uses it. Spare days go to
Essaouira or the Fes medina, not to more Casa.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Halal is the national default — with Malaysia and the
Maldives, the easiest tier on this site (resort bars
aside, alcohol is hotel-limited anyway). Vegetarians
do well with intent: vegetable tagine and couscous
(Friday's dish), bissara fava soup, zaalouk and
taktouka salads, msemen breakfasts — but broths and
couscous steam often meet meat, so "bla lham"
(without meat) is the phrase, riads happily oblige,
and the produce souks are a feast in themselves.

**What's Morocco guide #2?**
**Essaouira + the coast** (windsurf ramparts, gnawa
music, seafood grills) bolted to southern kasbahs —
or the **north loop** (Tangier's new-train corridor,
Tetouan, deeper Rif). The coast is currently winning.
