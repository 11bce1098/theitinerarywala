Most safaris are conducted from a vehicle. **South Luangwa is
where that stopped being the only option** — Norman Carr ran the
first guided walking safaris here in the 1950s, trained the
guides, and the park has done it longer and better than anywhere
since.

Walking changes the proportions of everything. You notice tracks,
dung, bark stripped at shoulder height, the direction of the wind.
A giraffe at eighty metres on foot is a different animal from a
giraffe at eighty metres through a windscreen.

Zambia also holds the **other side of Victoria Falls** — and in
low water, a rock pool at the very lip of it that you swim to —
and the **Lower Zambezi**, where you drift past elephant in a
canoe at water level.

This is not a cheap country. It is the one where the guiding is
the product.

## Before you go: walking, seasons and the cost of flying

### Entry

- **Most nationalities get a visa on arrival or an e-visa.** The
  **KAZA UniVisa** covers **Zambia and Zimbabwe together for 30
  days** and allows day trips to Botswana through Kazungula — if
  you plan to cross to the Zimbabwean side of the Falls, this is
  the one to ask for.
- **Yellow fever certificate** is required if you are arriving
  from a country where it is endemic.

### Why Zambia is walking country

- **Walking safaris run roughly June to October**, when the grass
  is short enough to see through and the ground is dry.
- You walk in single file with an **armed scout in front and a
  guide behind**, for three to four hours in the early morning.
  It is not a hike — you cover perhaps five kilometres and stop
  constantly.
- **Zambian guides are the most rigorously examined in Africa**,
  and in the walking parks the qualification takes years. This is
  what you are paying for.
- **Night drives are permitted** in Zambian national parks, which
  is unusual — and South Luangwa's leopard density is among the
  highest anywhere, so the spotlight hour matters.

### The season decides everything

- **June to October — dry season.** The bush thins, animals
  concentrate on the river, every camp is open, and walking is at
  its best. **September and October are the peak** for game and
  also the hottest — 40°C is normal in October.
- **November to April — the emerald season.** Spectacular green,
  dramatic skies, migrant birds, newborn animals, and **half the
  price**. But many camps close, roads flood, and walking largely
  stops. Some camps run boat safaris over ground that was a road
  in September.
- **May** is the shoulder — green still, roads drying, few people.

### Flights, and why they cost what they do

- **The parks are reached by light aircraft**, not road. Proflight
  runs Lusaka–Mfuwe for South Luangwa and Lusaka–Jeki or Royal for
  the Lower Zambezi.
- **Budget USD 600–800 per person** for the internal legs. The
  road alternative to South Luangwa is 10–12 hours from Lusaka on
  a road that is good until it suddenly is not.
- **20 kg in a soft bag** on the light aircraft, and they weigh
  passengers too.

### Money and health

- The **kwacha (ZMW)** is the currency, but **camps, parks and
  activities are quoted and paid in US dollars**, which is why
  this guide prices in them. Carry clean USD notes; worn or
  pre-2009 ones are refused.
- **Malaria is present year-round.** Take prophylaxis and use the
  repellent; the Luangwa valley is a serious transmission area.
- Tipping is cash, in USD, in the envelopes camps provide — guide
  and general staff separately.

## Day 1 — Land at Livingstone

- **13:00** — Land at **Harry Mwanga Nkumbula (LVI)**, 20 minutes
  from town and ten from the Falls.
- **14:30** — Drop bags. Stay near the river rather than in
  Livingstone town, which is a functional place with a good museum
  and not much else.
- **16:00** — You can hear the Falls from several kilometres away.
  The Lozi name — ***Mosi-oa-Tunya***, the smoke that thunders —
  is the accurate one, and the spray column is visible from the
  air on the way in.
- **16:30** — A **sunset cruise on the Zambezi** above the Falls:
  two hours, hippo pods, elephant coming down to drink, crocodile
  on the sandbars, and the river running wide and flat right up to
  the point where it disappears.
- **19:30** — Dinner. Bream from the river, and Mosi lager, which
  is named after the Falls.

## Day 2 — The Falls, Zambian side

- **07:00** — Into the park at opening, before the coaches and in
  the best light.
- **07:15** — The **Knife-Edge Bridge**, and this is what Zambia
  has that Zimbabwe does not: you walk out onto a spur **directly
  opposite the Eastern Cataract**, with the gorge below you on
  both sides. In high water you are walking through solid spray
  and you will be soaked in ninety seconds.
- **09:00** — The **Boiling Pot** trail, down 200 m of steps to
  the whirlpool where the river turns the corner under the bridge.
  The climb back out is the morning's exercise.
- **11:00** — **Know the trade-off before you come:** Zimbabwe has
  the panoramic face of the Falls; **Zambia puts you on top of
  them**. In **low water (roughly October to December) the Zambian
  side can run nearly dry**, which is when Zimbabwe is clearly the
  better view — and also exactly when Devil's Pool opens.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then the **Victoria Falls Bridge** — the 1905
  steel arch, with the bungee off the middle and a walkway to the
  halfway point.
- **16:00** — The **craft market**, and the **Livingstone Museum**
  if the heat has beaten you.
- **19:00** — Dinner.

## Day 3 — Devil's Pool, or the gorge

- **Option A — Livingstone Island, 07:00.** In **low water,
  roughly mid-August to mid-January**, boats run to the island at
  the lip of the Falls, and from it you swim a few metres to
  **Devil's Pool** — a rock basin at the very edge, with a natural
  lip holding you, where you lie with your head over a 100 m drop
  while a guide holds your ankles. It is as alarming as that
  sounds, it is properly run, and it is unrepeatable.
- **Option B — rafting, 08:00.** The **Zambezi below the Falls**
  is among the best commercial whitewater anywhere — grade V, big
  warm water, 25 rapids. **Low water only**, and the 200 m climb
  out of the gorge at the end is harder than the rapids.
- **Option C — Mosi-oa-Tunya National Park, 06:30.** Small, on the
  riverbank, and the only place in Zambia with **white rhino** —
  tracked on foot with rangers who guard them around the clock.
  Two hours, and a gentle introduction to walking before Luangwa.
- **15:00** — The **microlight or helicopter** over the Falls if
  you want the shape of the seven gorges, which only makes sense
  from the air.
- **19:00** — Repack into a soft bag. Tomorrow you fly light.

## Day 4 — Fly to South Luangwa

- **09:00** — Fly **Livingstone → Lusaka → Mfuwe**, usually with a
  connection and most of the morning gone.
- **13:00** — Land at **Mfuwe**. The transfer to camp is a game
  drive in itself — the road crosses the Luangwa and the park
  boundary is unfenced, so the animals are on it.
- **15:00** — Arrive, settle, and take the briefing seriously:
  **you are escorted between tent and dining area after dark**,
  every time, and hippo walk through camp at night to graze.
- **16:00** — First **afternoon drive**, running into dusk.
  **South Luangwa holds Thornicroft's giraffe and Crawshay's
  zebra, found nowhere else**, along with enormous herds of
  buffalo and some 400 bird species.
- **19:00** — The drive finishes with a **spotlight hour** —
  leopard, genet, civet, porcupine, and the honey badger that
  nobody ever sees in daylight.
- **20:30** — Dinner, and the Luangwa making noise all night.

## Day 5 — The walking safari

- **05:30** — Coffee at the fire, then out on foot. This is the
  day the trip is built around.
- **06:00** — Single file: **scout in front with the rifle, guide
  behind him, you behind that**, and nobody talks above a murmur.
  You walk into the wind and the guide reads the ground
  continuously.
- **07:30** — What you actually do is **stop, constantly** —
  tracks in dust, a termite mound's architecture, which tree the
  elephant stripped and why, the difference between hyena and wild
  dog scat, a leopard print over last night's vehicle tyre.
- **09:00** — Big animals at a distance, approached carefully and
  downwind. Standing on the ground two hundred metres from
  buffalo, with nothing between you, is an entirely different
  physiological experience from seeing them from a vehicle. **That
  is the point, and no photograph conveys it.**
- **10:00** — Breakfast cooked in the bush, under a tree, at the
  end of the walk.
- **12:00** — Back to camp. **Rest** — it will be in the thirties.
- **16:00** — Afternoon drive and a second spotlight hour.
- **20:00** — Dinner.

## Day 6 — Luangwa, and the lagoons

- **05:45** — Morning drive, or a second walk if the first one
  took.
- **07:30** — The **oxbow lagoons** are where the density is: the
  Luangwa changes course constantly and leaves horseshoe pools
  behind, which hold hippo in absurd concentrations and draw
  everything else to drink. **Luangwa has one of the highest hippo
  densities of any river in the world.**
- **10:00** — Back, brunch, and the long middle of the day.
- **15:30** — Tea, then out.
- **16:30** — **Leopard.** South Luangwa's population density is
  among the highest in Africa and the guides know individuals by
  name and territory. The late afternoon and the spotlight hour
  are when you find them, often in a sausage tree with a kill.
- **19:30** — Dinner.
- **21:00** — If your camp offers a **night drive as a separate
  activity**, take it. Zambia permits them and most countries do
  not.

## Day 7 — Fly to the Lower Zambezi

- **09:00** — Fly **Mfuwe → Lusaka → Jeki** or **Royal**, light
  aircraft both legs, landing on a strip in the valley.
- **13:00** — Arrive at camp on the **Zambezi**, with the
  escarpment behind you and **Mana Pools in Zimbabwe directly
  across the water** — the same ecosystem, two countries.
- **14:00** — Lunch, and the heat.
- **16:00** — A **boat safari**, which is this park's signature.
  Elephant swim between the islands, and you watch from the water
  with the engine off — plus hippo, crocodile, skimmers and
  fish eagles.
- **18:30** — Sundowners on the river with the Zimbabwean
  escarpment going pink opposite.
- **20:00** — Dinner.

## Day 8 — Canoeing the Zambezi

- **06:00** — **Canoeing**, and it is the reason to come here.
  Two-person Canadian canoes, a guide leading, drifting down the
  channels between islands with the current doing most of the
  work.
- **07:30** — You pass **elephant feeding on the banks at eye
  level**, buffalo in the shallows, and you give hippo pods a very
  wide and carefully briefed berth. The guide reads the water
  constantly; this is run seriously and safely, and it is still
  the most exposed you will feel all trip.
- **10:30** — Out, and breakfast.
- **12:00** — Rest.
- **15:30** — A **game drive** on the floodplain, or **tiger
  fishing** if that appeals — the Zambezi is one of the great
  fisheries and it is catch-and-release.
- **18:00** — Last sundowner.
- **19:30** — Dinner. **Settle tips in cash tonight** — guide and
  camp staff separately.

## Day 9 — Fly home

- **09:00** — Light aircraft back to **Lusaka**, then the
  international connection.
- **Leave a long gap in Lusaka.** Light aircraft schedules move
  with weather and passenger loads, and the connection is the only
  part of this trip with no slack.
- **Note** — Spend your kwacha before you go. The camps took
  dollars; the airport shops will take kwacha and nowhere outside
  Zambia will.

## What to pack

- **Neutral colours for walking** — khaki, olive, grey. **No blue
  or black**, which attract tsetse flies, and nothing white.
- **Closed walking shoes** that you have already worn in, plus
  long trousers for thorns and for the sun.
- **Binoculars**, and better ones than you think. On foot they are
  the difference between seeing and looking.
- **Malaria prophylaxis** and strong repellent.
- **A 20 kg soft bag** — light aircraft refuse hard cases.
- **Clean USD notes** in small denominations for tips and park
  fees; anything torn or pre-2009 is rejected.
- **A head torch** for camp after dark.

## FAQ

**Is walking actually safe?**
It is run with an armed scout in front and a guide who has
qualified over years, you walk into the wind, and you are briefed
before you leave. The animals are given a wide margin and the
guides turn groups around routinely. It demands that you do
exactly what you are told, immediately, which is the whole of the
safety procedure.

**Zambia or Zimbabwe for Victoria Falls?**
Zimbabwe has the full panoramic face and the better view in low
water. **Zambia puts you on the lip** — the Knife-Edge Bridge and,
in season, Devil's Pool. The KAZA visa lets you do both, and the
bridge between them is a twenty-minute walk.

**Is South Luangwa better than the Serengeti or Kruger?**
Different. It has no great migration and fewer rhino. What it has
is the walking, far fewer vehicles at a sighting, exceptional
leopard, and guiding of a standard that other countries send their
guides here to learn.

**When exactly should I go?**
**June to October** for walking and concentrated game, with
September and October the peak and the hottest. **Avoid February
and March** unless you specifically want the emerald season — much
of the park is inaccessible and many camps are shut.

**Can I do this without the internal flights?**
Lusaka to Mfuwe by road is 10 to 12 hours and the Lower Zambezi is
a long drive plus a boat. It saves real money and costs you two
days of the nine. On a trip this short, fly.
