Cyprus is sold as a beach island and is one, for about five months
a year. The rest of it is more interesting.

**Paphos** has Roman villas whose **floor mosaics are still in
place** in the rooms they were made for, under cover but otherwise
as laid. The **Troodos** mountains hide ten small painted churches
on the UNESCO list, barn-roofed and unremarkable outside, covered
inside with Byzantine frescoes.

And **Nicosia is the last divided capital in Europe**. The
**Green Line** has run through the middle of it since 1974, and
you cross it on foot at a checkpoint in the main shopping street,
show a passport, and walk into the **north** — which uses a
different currency, a different language and a different time of
day for lunch.

## Before you go: the crossing, the car and the heat

### Entry, and the two sides

- **The Republic of Cyprus is in the EU**; most nationalities
  enter easily and EU citizens freely. **It is not in Schengen.**
- **The north — the "Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus" — is
  recognised only by Turkey.** You can cross as a visitor at
  several points; the **Ledra Street** crossing in Nicosia is on
  foot and takes minutes.
- **Carry your passport for any crossing.** You are stamped on a
  separate slip, not in the passport.
- **Enter the island through the south** (Larnaca or Paphos). The
  Republic regards entry through the north's airports as illegal
  entry, and a hire car from the south usually cannot be taken
  north without buying separate insurance at the checkpoint —
  which is sold there, for a few euro, in cash.
- **This guide crosses on foot in Nicosia and by car to
  Famagusta**, which is the normal and uncomplicated way to do it.

### Getting around

- **Hire a car.** There is no useful rail network, buses between
  towns are slow, and the Troodos is unreachable without one.
- **Driving is on the left**, a British inheritance, and the
  motorways are excellent.
- **Distances are small.** Paphos to Larnaca is 90 minutes end to
  end; nothing in this itinerary is more than two hours.
- **Two currencies:** euro in the south, **Turkish lira in the
  north**. Carry some euro cash north — it is widely accepted,
  usually at a poor rate.

### When to go

- **April to early June and September to November** are the
  answer: hot without being punishing, sea warm from June, and
  the Troodos green rather than parched.
- **July and August** reach the high thirties and the archaeology
  becomes genuinely unpleasant by eleven.
- **December to March** is mild on the coast and **there is skiing
  on Mount Olympus** — Cyprus is one of very few places where you
  can ski in the morning and swim in the afternoon, which people
  do mostly to say they have.
- **Note:** many archaeological sites close at 16:00 or 17:00 in
  winter, and museums shut on Mondays.

## Day 1 — Land at Paphos

- **14:00** — Land at **Paphos (PFO)**, 15 minutes from town.
  Collect the car here.
- **15:30** — Drop bags in **Kato Paphos**, near the harbour and
  the archaeological park.
- **17:00** — The **harbour** and the **medieval castle** on the
  mole — a small Lusignan fort the Venetians dismantled and the
  Ottomans rebuilt, which is Cypriot history in one building.
- **18:30** — Walk the sea path toward the **Tombs of the Kings**
  for the light.
- **20:00** — Dinner — a ***meze***, which is twenty-odd small
  dishes and arrives whether or not you were ready. Order it for
  two and do not order anything else. *Halloumi*, grilled, which
  is Cypriot and which the island has a protected designation for.

## Day 2 — Paphos, and the mosaics

- **08:30** — The **Paphos Archaeological Park** at opening,
  before the heat. The reason to be here is the **House of
  Dionysos** — a 3rd-century Roman villa whose **floor mosaics
  remain in situ** across some 2,000 m², under a modern roof: the
  triumph of Dionysos, hunting scenes, Narcissus at the pool.
- **10:30** — The **Houses of Theseus and Aion** next to it, the
  **odeon**, and the **Saranta Kolones** castle ruins.
- **12:30** — The **Tombs of the Kings**, a kilometre north: no
  kings in them, but rock-cut Hellenistic tombs built like houses
  with peristyle courtyards, cut down into the ground beside the
  sea.
- **14:00** — Lunch, then out of the sun.
- **16:30** — **Agios Neophytos monastery**, 10 km inland, where a
  12th-century hermit carved his own cell and chapel out of the
  cliff and then commissioned frescoes inside them — including one
  of himself between two archangels.
- **19:30** — Dinner in **Kato Paphos** or up in the old town,
  which is where Cypriots eat.

## Day 3 — The west coast and into the hills

- **08:30** — North up the coast to **Petra tou Romiou** —
  **Aphrodite's Rock**, where the goddess is said to have come
  ashore out of the foam. It is a sea stack with a pebble beach
  and the swimming is better than the legend.
- **10:00** — The **Akamas peninsula**: the island's wildest
  corner, with the **Baths of Aphrodite** at the start of it and a
  rough track beyond that needs a 4x4 — or walk the **Aphrodite
  trail**, 7.5 km, for the coast without the vehicle.
- **13:00** — Lunch at **Latchi** on the harbour — fish, landed
  that morning.
- **15:00** — **Polis**, quiet and un-resorted, or a boat to the
  **Blue Lagoon** off the peninsula tip.
- **17:00** — Turn inland and climb toward the **Troodos**,
  roughly 90 minutes, through vineyards.
- **19:00** — Check in at a mountain village — **Omodos**,
  **Platres** or **Kakopetria**.
- **20:00** — Dinner. It is cooler up here, and the menu turns to
  mountain trout and *afelia*.

## Day 4 — The painted churches

- **09:00** — This is the day Cyprus surprises people. **Ten small
  churches in the Troodos are on the UNESCO list**, and from
  outside they look like barns — steep pitched roofs down almost
  to the ground, built that way to shed snow.
- **09:30** — **Asinou (Panagia Phorbiotissa)** first, which is
  the best of them: a single-aisle 12th-century chapel whose
  **interior is covered floor to vault in frescoes**, repainted
  over four centuries so you can read the styles changing. The
  custodian keeps the key and will open it.
- **11:30** — **Agios Nikolaos tis Stegis** near Kakopetria, and
  **Panagia tou Araka** at Lagoudera.
- **13:00** — Lunch in **Kakopetria**, a stone village on a river
  with its old quarter intact.
- **15:00** — **Kykkos Monastery**, the richest and most important
  on the island — gold mosaic everywhere, and founded around an
  icon attributed to St Luke which nobody is permitted to look at.
- **17:00** — **Mount Olympus** (1,952 m) for the view, or the
  **Caledonia Falls** trail, 3 km down a river valley through
  pine.
- **19:30** — Back to the village for dinner.

## Day 5 — Down to Nicosia, and across the line

- **09:00** — Descend to **Nicosia (Lefkosia)**, about an hour,
  and leave the car at a hotel or a car park outside the walls.
- **10:30** — The **Venetian walls**, a perfect circle with eleven
  heart-shaped bastions, built in 1567 and finished just in time
  to lose the city to the Ottomans in 1570.
- **11:00** — The **Cyprus Museum**, which is the best
  archaeological collection on the island and makes sense of
  everything you saw at Paphos.
- **13:00** — Lunch in **Laiki Geitonia**, the restored old
  quarter.
- **15:00** — **Cross at Ledra Street.** This is the point of the
  day: a pedestrian checkpoint in the middle of the main shopping
  street, a passport, a slip of paper, fifty metres of no-man's
  land, and then the **north**.
- **15:30** — The **Büyük Han**, a 1572 Ottoman caravanserai
  around a courtyard, now cafés and craft shops. Then the
  **Selimiye Mosque** — which is a **French Gothic cathedral**,
  built by the Lusignans in the 13th century, with minarets added
  after 1570. Standing inside a Gothic nave with a mihrab in it is
  the clearest possible statement of this island's history.
- **18:00** — Tea in the Büyük Han, and cross back before the
  checkpoint's late closing.
- **20:00** — Dinner in the south.

## Day 6 — Famagusta and Salamis

- **08:30** — Drive east and cross by car at **Agios Dometios**,
  buying the separate insurance at the booth. Famagusta is about
  90 minutes.
- **10:30** — **Salamis**, a Greek and then Roman city on the
  shore: a gymnasium with its colonnade re-erected and headless
  statues along it, a theatre for 15,000, baths, and a basilica —
  spread over a huge site with pine and sand drifting through it.
- **13:00** — Lunch in **Famagusta (Gazimağusa)**.
- **14:30** — The **walled city**: Venetian walls that held out
  against the Ottomans for ten months in 1570–71, **Othello's
  Tower**, and the **Lala Mustafa Pasha Mosque** — another Gothic
  cathedral, St Nicholas, converted after the siege, with a
  minaret on one tower and the other left as it fell.
- **16:30** — **Varosha**. Once the island's most fashionable
  beach resort, abandoned in 1974 when its population fled, fenced
  off and left to decay for decades. Parts have been reopened to
  visitors since 2020 and you can now walk a marked route past
  collapsing hotels with trees growing through them. **It is
  politically contested and uncomfortable**, and it is the single
  most striking thing on the island.
- **18:30** — Drive back south, about two hours to **Larnaca**.

## Day 7 — Larnaca, the salt lake and Lefkara

- **09:00** — **Hala Sultan Tekke** on the **salt lake** — the
  tomb of Umm Haram, a relative of the Prophet, and one of the
  most important Islamic sites on the island, in a palm grove
  beside the water.
- **10:00** — **The salt lake itself.** Between roughly November
  and March it fills and **thousands of flamingos arrive**; in
  summer it dries to a white crust.
- **11:30** — **Lefkara**, 30 minutes inland: a stone village
  famous for **lefkaritika** lace, which Leonardo da Vinci is
  locally said to have bought here for Milan Cathedral, and for
  silverwork. Go into a workshop rather than a shop.
- **13:30** — Lunch in Lefkara.
- **15:30** — Back to **Larnaca**: the **Church of Saint Lazarus**,
  built over the tomb of the man raised from the dead, who
  according to tradition came here afterwards and was bishop for
  thirty years.
- **17:30** — The **Finikoudes** palm promenade, and a swim.
- **20:00** — Last dinner on the seafront.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- **Morning** — **Larnaca (LCA)** is ten minutes from the
  seafront, which is unusual and welcome.
- **If you have hours** — **Kiti** village's Panagia Angeloktisti
  has a **6th-century mosaic of the Virgin** in the apse, one of
  very few to survive the iconoclasm, and it is fifteen minutes
  from the airport.
- **Return the car** with time for the inspection and note the
  fuel policy.

## What to pack

- **Serious sun protection.** The archaeological sites have no
  shade at all, and that is the whole problem with July.
- **Proper shoes** for Salamis and the Paphos park, which are
  loose stone and sand.
- **Shoulders and knees covered** for the monasteries and mosques,
  and a scarf for women at Kykkos and Hala Sultan Tekke.
- **A warm layer for the Troodos**, which is a thousand metres up
  and cold after dark in any season.
- **Your passport**, carried, for the crossings.
- **UK-style Type G plugs**, 240V.
- **Some euro cash for the north**, and the insurance booth, which
  does not take cards.

## FAQ

**Is crossing to the north complicated?**
No. At Ledra Street you walk through a checkpoint in a shopping
street, show a passport, get a slip of paper, and continue. By car
you buy separate insurance at the booth for a few euro in cash.
**Enter the island through the south**, though — arriving through
the north's airports is treated as illegal entry by the Republic.

**Is it safe?**
Yes, on both sides, and the crossings are routine — thousands of
people use them daily. The division is political and visible
rather than dangerous.

**Cyprus or Malta?**
Different trips. **Malta** is smaller, denser and its headline is
prehistoric — temples older than the pyramids. **Cyprus** is six
times the size with mountains, forests and Roman mosaics, and it
has the divided capital, which has no equivalent anywhere.

**When is the sea warm?**
**June to October**, and warmest in August and September. April
and May are glorious on land and cold in the water.

**Do I need a car?**
For this itinerary, yes. The Troodos churches in particular are
scattered down side roads with no bus service, and they are the
best thing on the island.
