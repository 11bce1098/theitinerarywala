Victoria Falls is not the highest waterfall or the widest, but it is
the largest sheet of falling water anywhere: 1,700 m across, 100 m
down, and in full flood pushing enough spray into the air to be seen
from 50 km away. The local name, **Mosi-oa-Tunya** — the smoke that
thunders — is the accurate one.

Most people see it in a day and leave. That is a waste, because
Zimbabwe behind it is one of Africa's better safari countries and
one of its cheapest. **Hwange** is the size of Belgium and holds one
of the continent's largest elephant populations. **Matobo Hills** is
a landscape of balancing granite boulders with 13,000-year-old rock
paintings and the best rhino tracking in the country.

Eight days does all three properly.

## Before you go: the season, the money and the two sides

### Entry

- **Visas are available on arrival** for most nationalities at the
  airports and land borders, payable in **US dollars cash**, or
  in advance as an eVisa.
- **The KAZA UniVisa** covers **Zimbabwe and Zambia together** plus
  day trips into Botswana, and is the right choice if you plan to
  see both sides of the Falls. Availability has fluctuated — check
  before relying on it, and carry dollars either way.
- Passport valid six months with blank pages.

### The season decides what you see

This matters more at Victoria Falls than at almost any sight.

- **High water (roughly February to May, peaking around April)** —
  the Falls at maximum: overwhelming, thunderous, and so much spray
  that you may not see the far wall of the gorge at all. You will
  get drenched.
- **Low water (roughly September to December)** — far less water,
  clear views of the rock face and the whole structure, and the
  **Devil's Pool** on the Zambian side opens, which is a natural
  rock pool at the lip of the drop.
- **The compromise is June to August**: still substantial flow,
  good visibility, and the best time for the safari half of the
  trip.
- **For game, August to October is peak** — dry, and animals
  concentrated at Hwange's pumped waterholes.

### Which side of the Falls

- **Zimbabwe has about 70% of the frontage** and the longer
  rainforest walk with more viewpoints. This guide is based there.
- **Zambia has the Knife-Edge Bridge and Devil's Pool**, and gets
  closer to the water at low flow.
- **You can do both in a day.** The border at the Victoria Falls
  Bridge is a short walk; take your passport and check the visa
  position first.

### The money situation, plainly

- **Zimbabwe's own currency has been through repeated redenominations
  and remains unstable.** Tourism runs almost entirely on **US
  dollars**, and this guide budgets in them for that reason.
- **Bring US dollars in cash, in clean, undamaged notes.** Older
  series notes are sometimes refused. Small denominations are
  genuinely useful — change is often scarce.
- **Cards work at hotels and larger operators** and frequently
  nowhere else. Do not rely on ATMs.
- **South African rand and Botswana pula** are accepted in border
  areas.

## Day 1 — Land at Victoria Falls

- **Afternoon** — The airport is 20 minutes from town. Drop bags.
- **Evening** — **Sunset cruise on the Zambezi** above the Falls,
  with hippo, elephant on the banks and the spray column downstream.

## Day 2 — The Falls, properly

- **Morning** — The **rainforest walk**: sixteen viewpoints along
  the Zimbabwean rim, through forest kept permanently wet by spray.
  Allow two to three hours. In high water you will be soaked
  regardless of what you wear.
- **Afternoon** — The **bridge** between the two countries, where
  the bungee jump is, and **Lookout Café** over the gorge.
- **Evening** — Town. Zimbabwe's crafts are good and the market
  pressure is real but good-humoured.

## Day 3 — Activities, or the Zambian side

- **Option A** — **Cross to Zambia** for the Knife-Edge Bridge and,
  in low water, **Devil's Pool**.
- **Option B** — Whitewater rafting the Zambezi below the Falls —
  among the best commercial rafting anywhere, and only in low water.
- **Option C** — Helicopter over the Falls, which is the only way
  to understand the shape of the gorge.

## Day 4 — South to Hwange

- **Morning** — Road transfer to **Hwange National Park**, about
  two and a half hours.
- **Afternoon** — First game drive. Hwange has **no natural
  permanent water** — the waterholes are pumped, which is why the
  game concentrates so predictably in the dry season.
- **Evening** — A waterhole hide after dark, which some camps have
  and which is worth choosing a camp for.

## Day 5 — Hwange

- **Dawn** — The long morning drive, which is when the predators
  are still moving.
- **Midday** — Rest.
- **Afternoon** — A second drive, ending at a waterhole for the
  elephant parade at dusk. Herds here run to hundreds.

## Day 6 — To Matobo

- **Morning** — Drive south-east to the **Matobo Hills**, around
  four hours, via **Bulawayo** if you want the railway museum or
  lunch.
- **Afternoon** — Arrive among balancing granite kopjes unlike
  anything else in the region.
- **Evening** — Sunset from **World's View**, where Cecil Rhodes is
  buried and the argument about that continues.

## Day 7 — Rhino and rock art

- **Morning** — **Rhino tracking on foot** with a guide and a
  ranger. Matobo has one of Africa's densest white rhino
  populations and the walking approach is extraordinary.
- **Afternoon** — **Rock art**: the caves here hold paintings up to
  13,000 years old, and the guides explain them properly.
- **Evening** — Last night.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- **Bulawayo** has domestic connections to Harare and Johannesburg;
  many visitors drive back to Victoria Falls and fly from there.

## What to pack

- **A waterproof and a dry bag for the Falls.** In high water the
  rainforest walk is a soaking, not a drizzle. Ponchos are rented at
  the gate and are not enough.
- **Neutral safari colours** for Hwange and Matobo.
- **Warm layers for dawn drives**, June to August.
- **US dollars in cash**, clean and in small denominations.
- **Binoculars.**
- **Type D/G plugs.**
- **Malaria prophylaxis** — advice needed for Hwange and the
  Zambezi valley.

## FAQ

**When should I see the Falls?**
**April** for maximum power and minimum visibility; **September to
December** for clear views and Devil's Pool; **June to August** for
the best balance, and the best safari conditions.

**Zimbabwe or Zambia side?**
Zimbabwe has most of the frontage and the better walk. Zambia gets
closer at low water and has Devil's Pool. Both in a day is normal.

**Is it safe?**
For visitors on this route, yes. Zimbabwe's difficulties are
economic rather than a matter of visitor safety, and tourism areas
are well run. Carry dollars, since the economy runs on them.

**How does it compare with Botswana?**
Zimbabwe is considerably cheaper for similar game, and it has the
Falls. Botswana has the Okavango, which has no equivalent. See our
[Botswana guide](/itineraries/botswana-9-days-guide/) — the two
combine easily, since Kasane is 80 km from Victoria Falls.
