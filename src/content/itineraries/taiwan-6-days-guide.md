Taipei is what happens when Japanese order, Chinese heritage
and a fierce island identity share one food-obsessed city:
temples wedged between noodle shops, a bamboo-shaped
skyscraper rising over green hills, hot-spring valleys at the
end of the metro, and night markets so good UNESCO should
intervene. It's safe, cheap, endlessly polite — and almost
nobody from our part of the world goes, because they assume
the visa is hard.

It isn't — that's this guide's headline. Indians holding a
**valid (or expired within 10 years) visa or residence permit
from the US, UK, Canada, Schengen, Japan, Korea, Australia or
New Zealand** qualify for Taiwan's **Travel Authorisation
Certificate (TAC): free, online, approved in minutes — 90
days' validity, up to 6 entries a year, 14 days each.** If
you've done our [USA](/itineraries/usa-east-coast-guide/),
[UK](/itineraries/uk-10-days-guide/) or any Schengen guide's trip, Taiwan
is already unlocked.

The shape: **one Taipei base, five nights** — the city's
icons, a hot-spring afternoon, the tea-mountain gondola, and
the **Jiufen + Shifen** day among lantern-filled hills.
Figure **{{TWD 16000}} per person**
excluding flights — night-market economics make this one of
Asia's cheapest great weeks. **Go October–April**;
July–September is typhoon-roulette season (fine, but built-in
flexibility helps).

## Before you go: the admin

### The TAC, decoded

- **The TAC, decoded:** apply at Taiwan's **National
  Immigration Agency portal** — passport details plus
  the qualifying visa's details, and the certificate
  PDF lands **in minutes, free**. Print it or keep the
  QR on your phone; airlines check it at check-in.
  Conditions that matter: the qualifying visa must
  **physically exist** (in a current or old passport —
  past travel alone doesn't count), and TAC entries are
  **14 days, no extensions**.
- **No qualifying visa?** The regular **visitor visa**
  runs through Taipei's representative offices (TECO —
  Offices sit in major cities worldwide; in India that is
  Delhi, Mumbai or Chennai, in person, ~₹4,200 single
  entry, 2–5 working days). Doable — but the TAC route
  is why this trip pairs so well with the site's
  US/UK/Schengen alumni.

### Flights

- **Flights:** **Emirates flies Dubai–Taipei
  non-stop (~8½ h)**, commonly US$630–980 return.

### Money and transit

- **Money & transit:** buy an **EasyCard** at any
  metro station (NT$100 deposit, top up as you go) —
  it runs the superb MRT, buses, and even
  convenience-store payments. Taipei's metro is
  Singapore-clean and Tokyo-punctual; you will not
  need a taxi all week. Cards are common but **night
  markets are cash — keep NT$1,000–2,000 in notes**.

### Manners in sixty seconds

- **Manners in sixty seconds:** stand right on
  escalators, no eating on the MRT (fines are real),
  queue like it's a sport, and take the free umbrella
  bags at every doorway. Convenience stores (7-Eleven
  every 200 m) are legitimate civic infrastructure —
  meals, tickets, coffee, everything.

## Day 1 — Land, meet the city from 89 floors up

- **Afternoon** — Emirates lands mid-afternoon;
  airport MRT into town (36 min, NT$150), check in
  around Ximending.
- **17:00** — **Taipei 101** (**NT$600 adult**, book
  a sunset slot): the once-tallest building on Earth,
  shaped like a bamboo stalk, with the world's
  fastest lift era behind it and the giant golden
  **wind damper** — a 660-tonne pendulum you can
  actually see — steadying the tower above you. City
  lights on one side, green mountains on the other.
- **19:30** — First night market, gently: **Ningxia**
  (compact, food-only, locals' favourite) — oyster
  omelette, taro balls, the gua bao pork belly bun
  (or its tofu cousin), NT$400 feeds two well.

**Where to stay in Taipei** (five nights)

- *Luxury:* **Regent Taipei** — 4.6/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~3,600 reviews), rooftop pool + galleria polish in
  Zhongshan. ~NT$8,500+. (Palais de Chine, 4.6/3,400,
  is the design rival by Main Station.)
- *Mid-range:* **amba Taipei Ximending** — 4.3/5
  (~1,100 reviews), pop-modern rooms above the
  neon-quarter action. ~NT$3,000–4,200.
- *Value:* **CityInn Hotel Plus, Ximending** — 4.8/5
  (~2,300 reviews) — spotless small rooms at
  ~NT$1,900–2,600. (Cosmos, 4.5/4,500, by Main
  Station, for breakfast-included ease.)

## Day 2 — Old Taipei: temples, memorials, the food streets

- **09:00** — **Longshan Temple** (free): 1738,
  incense spirals, fortune blocks clattering on
  stone — Taipei's spiritual living room. The
  surrounding Bopiliao heritage lanes fill the
  photo quota.
- **11:00** — **Chiang Kai-shek Memorial** (free):
  the blue-roofed colossus, the top-of-the-hour
  guard change, and the gardens either side.
- **13:00** — Lunch pilgrimage: the original **Din
  Tai Fung** experience (xiao long bao soup
  dumplings; ~NT$600–800 pp; the queue moves) — or
  its street rival: a NT$180 beef noodle soup that
  Taipei considers a civic achievement.
- **15:30** — **Dihua Street**: the 1850s
  merchants' row — dried goods, tea houses,
  fabric — Taipei's best souvenir strip (pineapple
  cakes bought here, not the airport).
- **18:30** — The big one: **Shilin Night Market** —
  Taiwan's largest — or the connoisseurs' pick
  **Raohe** (one straight lane, temple at the gate,
  the famous pepper buns from the coal oven at the
  entrance, NT$60). Graze; that's dinner.

## Day 3 — Jiufen and the sky lanterns

- **08:30** — Train from Taipei Main to **Ruifang**
  (~NT$60, 40 min), connect to the hills.
- **09:30** — **Jiufen**: the gold-rush village
  stacked on a mountainside — lantern-strung
  **Shuqi Road** steps, teahouses gazing over the
  Pacific, taro-ball dessert shops. Its
  Spirited-Away-esque mood made it world famous;
  go early, because by noon the lanes are a
  polite crush.
- **12:30** — Bus/taxi over to the **Pingxi line**
  and **Shifen**: release a **sky lantern** on the
  railway tracks (NT$200–350 by colours — write
  the wishes, step back for the whoosh) and walk
  to **Shifen Waterfall** ("little Niagara",
  free) — the day's gentle second act.
- *(Swap option: **Yehliu Geopark**, NT$120 — the
  Queen's Head rock and a shoreline of alien
  hoodoos — pairs with Jiufen on coastal bus
  routes.)*
- **18:00** — Back to Taipei (~1 h); Ximending
  neon evening — the walking district that's
  Tokyo-Harajuku's Taiwanese cousin.

## Day 4 — Hot springs and tea mountains

- **09:30** — MRT north to **Beitou** (the
  hot-spring suburb AT the end of a metro spur —
  the journey is part of the charm): the steaming
  jade **Thermal Valley** (free), the elegant
  wooden **Hot Spring Museum** (free), then your
  soak — the historic **public baths (NT$60!)**
  or a private-room onsen hotel hour
  (NT$800–1,500 for two). Sulphur steam +
  NT$60 = the best-value spa on this site.
- **13:00** — Noodles in Beitou, MRT south.
- **15:00** — **Maokong Gondola** (NT$120 each
  way, EasyCard works; the glass-floor "Eyes"
  cabins queue separately): up over tea terraces
  to **Maokong village** — pot-of-tea terraces
  with the city spread below. Sunset pot of
  Tieguanyin (~NT$300–500, refills for hours) IS
  the activity.
- **19:30** — Ride down; dinner near Taipei Zoo
  station or back in town; optional add: **Tonghua
  (Linjiang) night market** — the local one
  tourists miss.

## Day 5 — The Palace Museum and Elephant Mountain

- **09:00** — **National Palace Museum**
  (**NT$350**): the greatest Chinese art
  collection anywhere — 700,000 imperial pieces
  that crossed the strait in 1949, headlined by
  the jadeite cabbage and the meat-shaped stone
  (a jasper braised pork belly; the queue for a
  rock shaped like lunch says everything about
  Taiwan). Three hours flies.
- **13:30** — Lunch back in town; siesta —
  tonight has stairs.
- **16:30** — **Elephant Mountain (Xiangshan)**:
  20 sweaty minutes of steps from the MRT to THE
  Taipei view — 101 rising out of green with the
  basin behind. Summit for golden hour, stay for
  the lights-on moment (headlamp/phone torch for
  the way down).
- **19:30** — Farewell feast: Yongkang Street's
  food quarter (mango shaved ice at its
  birthplace, NT$180–250) or the Din Tai Fung
  encore nobody regrets.

## Day 6 — Fly home

- Morning: last pineapple-cake run (Chia Te or
  SunnyHills boxes survive luggage beautifully),
  airport MRT out, and the ~9½-hour ride home.
  Six entries a year on that TAC, remember —
  Taipei makes repeat customers.

## What to pack

- Walking shoes with grip (Jiufen's steps,
  Elephant Mountain's 600 stairs — both shine
  when damp, and damp is likely).
- Compact umbrella OR embrace the doorway
  umbrella-bag culture; a light rain shell
  Oct–Mar, real breathables in summer humidity.
- Modest-casual for temples (shoulders fine
  covered; nothing strict), swimwear + cap only
  if using pool-style springs (Beitou public
  bath requires swimwear; private rooms don't).
- **Cash envelope for markets** (NT$1,000–2,000);
  coins accumulate — feed them to bubble tea.
- Type A/B (US-style) plugs — adapters needed
  from UAE kit.
- Empty stomach margin of roughly 20%: Taiwan
  will fill it nightly.

## FAQ

**Is the TAC really that easy — any catches at
immigration?**
Genuinely that easy — it's the same system
Taiwan runs for several Southeast Asian
countries, and Indians with the qualifying
visas are squarely eligible. The catches are
mechanical: the qualifying visa/permit must be
shown if asked (carry the old passport if the
US/UK visa lives there), the 14 days are
non-extendable, and airline check-in staff
sometimes need the TAC PDF pointed out to
them — print it. Onward/return ticket within
14 days is expected.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Better than almost anywhere in East Asia.
**Vegetarian:** Taiwan's Buddhist tradition
means dedicated veg (素食, "su shi")
restaurants and buffet canteens in every
district — thousands island-wide — plus veg
night-market classics; it's arguably East
Asia's best vegetarian country. **Halal:**
Taipei's Grand Mosque anchors a certified
scene (the tourism board actively certifies
restaurants — look for the Muslim-friendly
label), with halal beef-noodle houses near
the mosque and main station. Night markets
need the usual pork-awareness; stall-holders
patiently confirm.

**Is the China–Taiwan situation a reason to
hesitate?**
Life on the ground is famously, almost
surreally normal — Taipei consistently ranks
among the world's safest cities, and tourism
runs uninterrupted. Do what we advise for
every destination: glance at headlines when
booking, buy flexible tickets if you're the
worrying kind, and then enjoy one of Asia's
calmest capitals.

**Jiufen or Yehliu — and is Shifen worth the
lantern cliché?**
Jiufen + Shifen is the classic pairing this
guide runs; add Yehliu only on a longer day
tour (all three exist as ~NT$1,200 pp
tours if you'd rather not navigate).
And yes — the lantern is touristy, and
watching your own wishes lift off between
mountain walls beats the cynicism every
single time.

**Do we need Mandarin?**
No — the MRT, menus-with-pictures, and
translation apps carry you, and Taiwanese
strangers are famous for walking you to the
thing you asked about. Learn "xièxie"
(thanks) and the 7-Eleven bow-nod;
that's fluency here.

**What's Taiwan guide #2?**
**Sun Moon Lake + Taichung** by high-speed
rail — the lake-and-mountains leg — or the
**round-island rail loop** (Tainan's temples,
Kaohsiung's harbour, the wild east coast)
for the full 10-day epic. Votes welcome;
both on the list.
