Russia in one trip means three different countries: Moscow's scale
and golden domes, St Petersburg's canals and palaces, and — the leg
almost no first-timer guide covers — Murmansk, 200 km above the
Arctic Circle, where you stand under the northern lights two hours'
flight from the capital. This route does all three in 10 days, with
the two big cities linked by Russia's best train.

One thing upfront: Russia is the most admin-heavy trip on this
site. None of it is hard, but all of it must be done **before you
fly** — the payment, SIM and visa systems do not forgive
improvisation. The first section below is longer than usual for
exactly that reason.

All costs are in Russian rubles (RUB), with conversions calculated
at today's exchange rate. Opening hours are approximate — treat
anything marked ~ as a guide, not a contract.

## Before you go: the admin that actually matters

### Know before you book — honestly

- Several Western governments currently advise their citizens
  against travel to Russia. Check **your own government's advice**
  before booking, and check whether your travel insurance stays
  valid under it — some policies are void in advise-against
  destinations.
- Your **Visa and Mastercard will not work** — not at ATMs, not in
  shops, not online. This is solvable (below), but only before you
  fly.
- **WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook are blocked.** Telegram works.
  Set up alternatives with family before you leave.
- None of this makes the trip unpleasant on the ground — cities
  feel orderly and tourists are welcomed — but go with accurate
  expectations, not 2019 assumptions.

### Visas

- **E-visa (most travellers):** citizens of 64 countries —
  including India, China, and most of the EU — apply at
  [evisa.kdmid.ru](https://evisa.kdmid.ru), ~USD 52, processed in
  ~4 days, **30-day stay**, valid 120 days from issue. You need a
  passport (6+ months), a digital photo, and medical insurance —
  no invitation letter, no hotel confirmations.
- **Visa-free:** UAE, Qatar and several other Gulf and Asian
  passports have bilateral visa-free agreements — check the
  official list for yours.
- **Not eligible for the e-visa:** US, UK, Canadian, Australian
  and NZ passports — these need a full consular visa with an
  invitation, a longer and pricier process.
- **Medical insurance is mandatory** with the e-visa and must be
  arranged before arrival — a standard travel policy naming Russia
  and your dates does it. Carry the PDF offline.
- Unlike Georgia, a residence permit does **not** substitute for a
  visa here — eligibility follows your passport.

### Money — read this twice

- Foreign Visa, Mastercard and Amex are dead in Russia: no ATMs,
  no card machines, no Russian websites.
- **Bring cash**: USD, EUR or AED in **crisp, unmarked notes** —
  Russian banks reject creased or stamped bills. Exchange at bank
  branches in the city, never at the airport counters.
- **Set up a prepaid virtual MIR card before you fly** — several
  services issue one remotely in minutes; it lets you pay by QR
  and buy train/museum tickets online. This single step removes
  most of the pain.
- UnionPay from a foreign bank is unreliable — don't build your
  plan on it.
- Budget in cash terms: Russia is cheap for what you get — a good
  restaurant meal runs 800–1,500 RUB per person, a metro ride ~60
  RUB.

### SIM and data

- Buying a **physical Russian SIM is no longer practical for
  tourists** — since 2025 it requires biometric registration and a
  Russian government services account.
- The workaround: an **international travel eSIM** (Yesim, Ubigi,
  Nomad and similar, ~USD 15–40) that roams onto Russian networks.
- Expect a **~24-hour block** on data the first time your foreign
  SIM/eSIM touches a Russian network — a 2025 security rule.
  Activate the eSIM the day before you land if the provider allows
  it, and download offline maps regardless.
- **Yandex Maps** is the map that works properly here (Google Maps
  works but with degraded data). Install **Yandex Go** (taxis) and
  **Yandex Translate** before you fly, plus a reputable VPN if you
  need your blocked apps.

### Getting around

- **Moscow:** the metro is the fastest way everywhere — and a
  genuine attraction; stations look like ballrooms. Buy a
  **Troika card** at any station (~60 RUB per ride). Signage has
  English.
- **Taxis:** **Yandex Go**, with payment set to **cash** — the app
  works fine on a foreign number, and cash settles the
  foreign-card problem.
- **Moscow → St Petersburg:** the **Sapsan** high-speed train,
  ~4 hours city centre to city centre, roughly 2,000–8,000 RUB
  depending on class and demand. Book ahead — via the RZD site
  with your MIR card, or an international reseller before you
  leave. Departs Leningradsky Station, arrives Moskovsky.
- **St Petersburg → Murmansk / Murmansk → Moscow:** ~2–2.5 hour
  domestic flights (Aeroflot, Smartavia and others), typically
  4,000–8,000 RUB one way, booked with the MIR card or through an
  agent.

### Travelling with kids?

- This route was first run with a toddler — it works, with
  adjustments: the metro has long stair runs and few lifts, so a
  carrier beats a stroller everywhere except the big parks.
- Moscow's **VDNKh** is the kids' day: the Moskvarium aquarium
  (~2,300 RUB family ticket on weekdays) and the Cosmonautics
  Museum's rockets.
- Aurora hunts are car-based — children can nap in the van between
  stops — but they run late; plan the next morning slow.
- Pack a thermos for warm drinks, hand warmers for Arctic nights,
  and a stroller rain/snow cover in autumn and winter.

## Day 1 — Land in Moscow, first look at Red Square

Assumes a daytime arrival. Stay inside or near the Garden Ring —
anywhere within a few metro stops of Red Square.

- **14:00** — Check in, drop bags. Buy a **Troika card** at the
  nearest metro station and top it up.
- **15:30** — **Zaryadye Park** (open all day, free) — the modern
  park beside the Kremlin; walk the **Floating Bridge** for the
  postcard river-and-Kremlin panorama.
- **17:00** — First walk across **Red Square** (open access, free
  unless an event closes it) — St Basil's domes, GUM's lights,
  Kremlin walls. Arriving at dusk is the right introduction.
- **19:00** — Dinner: **Stolovaya 57** inside GUM — a
  Soviet-canteen revival that is cheap, fast and genuinely good
  (~400–700 RUB/person); the queue moves quickly. Walk GUM's
  arcades after.

**Where to stay in Moscow**

- *Splurge:* **Hotel Metropol** — the 1905 Art Nouveau landmark
  two minutes from Red Square; still operating under its own name,
  history in every corridor.
- *Mid-range:* **Cosmos Selection Tverskaya** — the former
  Marriott on Tverskaya under local management: central, modern,
  reasonable.
- *Budget:* **Godzillas Hostel** — the long-running backpacker
  favourite near Tverskaya, walking distance to the centre.

## Day 2 — The Kremlin day

Book Kremlin tickets online ahead ([tickets.kreml.ru](https://tickets.kreml.ru),
MIR card needed) — and note the whole complex is **closed
Thursdays**.

- **9:00** — Red Square in morning light — the photos without the
  crowds.
- **10:00** — **Kremlin Cathedral Square** (winter ~10:00–17:00,
  summer 9:30–18:00, closed Thu; ~1,100 RUB) — the cathedrals
  where tsars were crowned, plus the giant broken Tsar Bell.
- **12:00** — **Armoury Chamber** (timed sessions 10:00 / 12:00 /
  14:30 / 16:30; ~1,400 RUB) — Fabergé eggs, coronation gowns,
  carriages. No photos inside; worth it anyway.
- **14:00** — Late lunch in GUM or nearby — **Teremok** (blini
  chain, ~400 RUB) is the fast local option.
- **15:30** — **St Basil's Cathedral** (daily, winter ~11:00–17:00;
  ~1,000 RUB) — smaller and stranger inside than you expect.
- **17:30** — Walk Nikolskaya Street's lights → Bolshoi Theatre
  facade → up **Tverskaya**.
- **20:00** — Dinner around Tverskaya or Kamergersky Lane —
  Georgian restaurants are Moscow's favourite night out, a nice
  full-circle if you've read our Georgia guide.

## Day 3 — Space, parks and the Arbat

- **9:30** — Metro to **VDNKh** — the vast Soviet exhibition
  grounds: golden fountains, grand pavilions, pure
  Stalin-Empire-style theatre. Free to wander.
- **10:30** — **Cosmonautics Museum** (closed Mon; ~10:00–19:00,
  ~300–500 RUB) — Gagarin, real capsules, the space-race story
  told from the other side. With kids, swap in the **Moskvarium**
  aquarium next door.
- **13:00** — Lunch at VDNKh's food courts.
- **14:30** — Metro south to **Gorky Park + Muzeon Park** — the
  riverside park pair: playgrounds, cafés, and Muzeon's open-air
  graveyard of decommissioned Soviet statues.
- **17:30** — **Old Arbat Street** — buskers, souvenir shops,
  caricaturists; touristy and fun to walk once.
- **19:30** — Dinner on or near the Arbat, then pack — early train
  tomorrow.

## Day 4 — Sapsan to St Petersburg

- **8:00** — Metro to **Leningradsky Station** (allow buffer;
  passport checked at boarding).
- **9:00–13:00** — **Sapsan** to St Petersburg — flat green Russia
  at 200 km/h; the bistro car does decent coffee.
- **13:30** — Arrive Moskovsky Station — you step out directly
  onto **Nevsky Prospekt**. Check in nearby.
- **15:00** — First walk down Nevsky: Kazan Cathedral's colonnade,
  the Singer Building bookshop, canal bridges.
- **17:00** — **Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood** from the
  Griboyedov Canal embankment — the most photographed onion domes
  in Russia; go inside tomorrow or now (~9:00–18:00ish, closed
  Wed; ~500 RUB).
- **19:00** — Dinner near Nevsky. For the classic cheap St
  Petersburg experience: **Pyshechnaya 1958** on Bolshaya
  Konyushennaya — sugar-dusted pyshki doughnuts and sweet coffee,
  unchanged since 1958, a few hundred rubles.

**Where to stay in St Petersburg**

- *Splurge:* **Grand Hotel Europe** or **Hotel Astoria** — the two
  imperial-era grand dames, both still running: ballrooms,
  history, St Isaac's views.
- *Mid-range:* **Helvetia Hotel** — a quiet courtyard hotel just
  off Nevsky with a strong service reputation.
- *Budget:* **Soul Kitchen Hostel** — repeatedly rated among the
  best hostels in Europe, on the Moika embankment.

## Day 5 — Hermitage and the imperial centre

The Hermitage is **closed Mondays** — plan around it. Pre-book at
hermitagemuseum.org (foreign cards fail; use the MIR card or buy
with cash at the machines).

- **10:45** — Be at the **Hermitage / Winter Palace** for the 11:00
  opening (Tue/Fri/Sat until 20:00, other days to 18:00; ~1,200
  RUB, under-14s free). Head first to the Jordan Staircase, the
  state rooms and the Rembrandts; 4 hours covers the highlights of
  a 350-room palace.
- **14:30** — Late lunch off **Palace Square** — the **Marketplace**
  chain does fresh canteen-style Russian food (~500 RUB).
- **15:30** — **St Isaac's Cathedral colonnade** (~10:30–18:00,
  colonnade ~400 RUB) — 262 steps to the best panorama in the
  city.
- **17:30** — Cross to **Peter & Paul Fortress** (grounds open
  late, free; cathedral with the Romanov tombs ~800 RUB) — the
  city's birthplace on its own island.
- **19:30** — Evening option A: **Neva river cruise** (May–Oct,
  heated cabins in autumn, ~1,000–1,500 RUB). Option B: ballet or
  opera at the **Mariinsky** — book online weeks ahead; even the
  cheap seats are an event.

## Day 6 — A palace out of town

Pick one palace — doing both in a day is a forced march. Book
either online in advance.

- **Option A — Peterhof** (grounds ~9:00–20:00; Grand Palace
  closed Mon): the "Russian Versailles" on the Gulf of Finland.
  The 140-fountain cascade runs **May to mid-October** — outside
  those months go for the palace and sea-edge park. Hydrofoil from
  the Hermitage embankment (~45 min, seasonal) or minibus.
- **Option B — Catherine Palace, Pushkin** (closed Tue; ~12:00
  entry slots sell out — book early): the blue-and-gold baroque
  palace with the reconstructed **Amber Room**. Better in late
  autumn/winter when Peterhof's fountains are off.
- **14:30** — Back in the city; late lunch.
- **16:00** — **General Staff Building** (Hermitage's modern wing:
  Matisse, Picasso, ~700 RUB) if you have art left in you — or
  browse Nevsky's bookshops and food halls.
- **20:00** — Dinner reservation somewhere memorable — St
  Petersburg's new-Russian restaurants around Rubinstein Street
  are the city's food heart; book ahead on weekends.

## Day 7 — Fly north: Murmansk and the first aurora hunt

Aurora season runs **late August to mid-April** — solar maximum
makes 2025–2027 statistically the best years this decade. Book
tours 1–2 weeks ahead in season.

- **11:00** — Fly St Petersburg → **Murmansk** (~2h15). Landing,
  you're above the Arctic Circle — the world's largest city north
  of it.
- **14:30** — Check in, rest, early dinner (the Azimut's
  restaurant does Arctic fish well).
- **20:00–00:00** — **Northern lights hunt** (~3,000–7,500
  RUB/person small-group, ~15,000–25,000 RUB private) — guides
  chase gaps in the cloud by forecast, minivan-based with short
  walks. Dress in every layer you brought; the van stays warm.
  No lights tonight? You have two more nights — that's the whole
  reason this leg is three nights long.

**Where to stay in Murmansk**

- *Best in town:* **Azimut City Hotel Murmansk** — the 18-storey
  landmark, well-rated, central, and the easiest tour pickup point
  in the city.
- *Budget:* **Rooms & Breakfast Mini Hotel** — small, central and
  warmly reviewed.

## Day 8 — Teriberka: the edge of the world

- **8:30** — Full-day trip to **Teriberka** (~4,000–5,000
  RUB/person incl. transport) — 2.5 hours across open tundra to
  the Barents Sea.
- **11:00** — The famous **ship graveyard** — rusting wooden hulls
  in the shallows — then the whale-skeleton arch and the beach of
  round "dragon egg" boulders.
- **13:00** — Lunch in a local café: Barents cod, and **king crab**
  if it's on — this is the cheapest place on earth to eat it.
- **14:30** — Walk to the **Arctic Ocean waterfall** that drops
  straight into the sea; tundra views the whole way.
- **16:00** — Drive back; sunset over the tundra.
- **21:00** — Optional second aurora hunt, or watch the forecast
  from the hotel — some nights the lights come to you.

## Day 9 — Reindeer, huskies and Soviet Arctic history

- **9:00** — **Sami village + husky park** (~40 min out;
  ~3,500–4,500 RUB/person with transport) — feed reindeer, short
  sled rides, husky enclosures, Sami tents and fish soup. The most
  child-delighting morning of the trip.
- **14:00** — Back in Murmansk: **Lenin nuclear icebreaker**
  (the world's first nuclear-powered ship, now a museum at the
  port; tour days vary — check locally, ~few hundred RUB).
- **16:00** — **Alyosha Monument** — the colossal WWII memorial
  above the bay; go for the panorama at dusk.
- **19:00** — Farewell Arctic dinner — try the local trio:
  venison, cod, cloudberries.

## Day 10 — Fly home via Moscow

- **Morning** — Fly Murmansk → Moscow (~2.5 h), connecting onward
  from there.
- **Long layover?** The **Izmailovsky Market** (weekends are
  fullest) is 40 minutes from the airports by taxi+metro — the
  best souvenir hunting in Russia: matryoshkas, Soviet watches,
  fur hats, amber.
- Evening departures get Gulf and Asian travellers home overnight.

## What to pack

- Serious layers for Murmansk in aurora season: thermal base,
  fleece, windproof shell, insulated boots, warm hat and gloves —
  you'll stand still outdoors at −5 to −15°C for hours.
- Hand warmers and a thermos — aurora-hunt essentials, and phone
  batteries die fast in the cold; bring a power bank too.
- Normal city clothes for Moscow and St Petersburg — locals dress
  up a little for restaurants and theatres.
- Crisp USD/EUR/AED banknotes — worn bills get refused at
  exchange.
- VPN installed and tested **before** arrival; offline Yandex
  Maps downloaded.
- Printed copies of your e-visa and insurance, plus offline PDFs.
- Universal adapter (Russia uses European two-pin, type C/F).

## FAQ

**Should I go to Russia right now?**
That's a personal call. Several Western governments advise against
travel; many Gulf, Asian and Latin American travellers visit
routinely. Day to day, Moscow and St Petersburg feel safe and
orderly, and tourists are treated well. Read your own government's
guidance, confirm your insurance is valid under it, and decide
with current information — this guide tells you how, not whether.

**When is the best time for this exact route?**
September–March, because Murmansk's aurora needs dark skies (the
season runs late August to mid-April; deep winter adds polar-night
atmosphere but −20°C cold). For the cities alone, June's White
Nights in St Petersburg are magical — but you'll see no aurora, so
that's a different trip.

**Will my cards really not work?**
Really. No foreign Visa/Mastercard/Amex, in machines or online.
Cash plus a prepaid virtual MIR card set up before you fly covers
everything; UnionPay is a lottery not a plan.

**Is language a problem?**
Less than you'd think: both metros have English signage, museums
sell English audio guides, and Yandex Translate's camera mode
handles menus. Learn Cyrillic's 33 letters on the flight — an hour
of effort that makes street signs readable all trip.

**What about vegetarians?**
Harder than most destinations but workable: blini, borscht without
meat (ask), mushroom everything, Georgian restaurants (vegetarian
gold) in every city. Strict vegans should self-cater partly.

**How far ahead should I book?**
E-visa: 2–3 weeks. Aurora tours and the Murmansk hotel: 1–2 weeks
(more around New Year and February). Hermitage, Catherine Palace
and Mariinsky tickets: as soon as dates are fixed. Sapsan: 2+
weeks for the cheap fares.
