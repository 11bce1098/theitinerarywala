There are two Turkeys. The one on postcards is dry, golden and
ancient. The other one is this: the Black Sea coast, where
tea terraces climb into permanent mist, wooden villages sit above
the clouds, and a Byzantine monastery hangs off a cliff face 300
metres over a forest. Gulf travellers discovered it years ago —
**direct flights link Dubai and Abu Dhabi to Trabzon in ~3.5
hours** — while most English-language guides still haven't. This
is the second Turkey trip, and for mountain people it's the better
one.

**Go May–September.** The high plateaus (Ayder and above) properly
open in June; October is beautiful and moody; winter closes the
mountain roads and turns Uzungol into a snow globe reachable but
limited. And be told honestly: this coast gets rain 200+ days a
year — the mist is not bad luck, it's the scenery working.

As with our first Turkey guide, prices are in EUR — lira figures
inflate too fast to print — with lira noted where tickets are still
set in it.

## Before you go: the short version of the admin

Visas, money and the scam list are identical to our 11-day Turkey
guide, where they're covered in depth (short form: many passports
visa-free; **Indian passports need the conditional e-visa — a valid
US/UK/Schengen/Ireland visa required, UAE residence alone does not
qualify — or the sticker visa, 3+ weeks early**). Route-specific
notes:

- **Fly direct:** flydubai (Dubai) and Air Arabia (Abu Dhabi/
  Sharjah) serve Trabzon nonstop seasonally-to-year-round; from
  everywhere else it's a cheap 2-hour hop from Istanbul (Turkish
  Airlines, AJet, Pegasus — many daily).
- **Rent a car.** This is the one route where we say it plainly:
  the sights are spread along valleys, dolmuş minibuses are slow
  and tour buses rigid. Roads to every stop in this guide are
  paved; you need nothing special until the optional jeep
  plateaus. ~€40–70/day plus fuel. Alternative: a private driver
  (~€80–120/day) or the cheap group day tours from Trabzon's
  Meydan if you'd rather not drive mountain switchbacks.
- **Cash note:** more cash-reliant than western Turkey — village
  restaurants, trout farms and plateau bungalows often prefer
  lira notes.
- **Halal travellers relax entirely:** this is conservative,
  pork-free, alcohol-light Turkey; everything is halal by default,
  and the region is extremely used to Gulf families.

### Travelling with kids?

- One of Turkey's easiest family routes: short drives, lakes,
  waterfalls, cable cars, hot springs — and locals who spoil
  children on sight.
- Uzungol's lake loop is flat and stroller-perfect; Sumela's final
  path is stepped and railed — manageable, but a carrier beats a
  stroller.
- The one judgement call: the optional jeep tracks to the high
  plateaus are bumpy — skip with infants, brilliant with
  school-age kids.

## Day 1 — Land in Trabzon, city and sunset

- **13:00** — Land at Trabzon (TZX), collect the car (the airport
  is 10 minutes from the centre), check in.
- **15:00** — **Trabzon Hagia Sophia** (free outside prayer times)
  — a 13th-century Byzantine church by the sea, frescoes and lawn.
- **16:30** — **Meydan Square** — the city's tea-drinking heart;
  buy roasted hazelnuts (this coast grows most of the world's) and
  watch the backgammon.
- **18:00** — **Boztepe cable car or drive** (~10:00–23:00; cable
  car ~150 TL return) — the whole city and the Black Sea from the
  hilltop tea gardens at sunset.
- **20:00** — Dinner: **Akçaabat köfte** — the region's famous
  grilled meatballs; the originals are in Akçaabat town 15 minutes
  west, worth the drive (~€8–12/person).

**Where to stay in Trabzon** (nights 1, 2 and 6)

- *Upscale:* **Radisson Blu Trabzon** — sea views, spa, the
  business-hotel comfort pick.
- *Mid-range:* **Zorlu Grand** — the old-school grande dame right
  on Meydan; location unbeatable.
- *Budget:* **Nur Hotel** — clean, central, cheap; Trabzon's
  hotels are functional rather than romantic, which is fine — the
  romance is up the valleys.

## Day 2 — Sumela: the monastery on the cliff

- **8:15** — Drive to **Sumela Monastery** (~45 km, 50 min into
  the Altındere valley).
- **9:00** — Park at the lower lot, shuttle up (small fee, cash),
  then the forest path to the entrance (**€20**; ~8:00–17:00,
  ticket office closes 16:30). Be early: the frescoed chapel and
  cliff-hung courtyards are a different place before the tour
  groups land at ~10:30. The setting — a white facade grown out of
  a sheer rock face over pine forest — is worth the whole flight.
  2–3 hours.
- **12:30** — Lunch in the valley — riverside trout farms grill
  what they raise (~€6–9).
- **14:00** — **Hamsiköy** village (20 min) — famous across Turkey
  for one thing: **sütlaç**, caramelised rice pudding. Order it
  with hazelnuts, argue later about whether pudding justifies a
  village's fame (it does).
- **15:30** — Optional: continue up to the **Zigana pass**
  viewpoint, or head back for the **Atatürk Pavilion** (small fee)
  — a white chalet in gardens above Trabzon.
- **19:30** — Dinner in the city; try Black Sea pide — the
  butter-and-cheese boat this coast does better than anywhere.

## Day 3 — Up the valley to Uzungol

- **9:30** — Drive east along the coast, then up the Solaklı
  valley (~95 km, ~2 h) — the road narrows, tea terraces stack the
  slopes, minarets appear out of the mist.
- **11:30** — **Uzungöl** — the lake with the mosque on its shore
  and green walls of mountain on every side: the Black Sea's
  postcard. Check in, then the flat **lake loop walk** (~40 min,
  free, stroller-friendly).
- **13:30** — Trout lunch at a lakeside farm restaurant.
- **15:00** — Drive or taxi to the upper viewpoints above the
  village for the full lake-and-mosque frame, or rent bikes; kids
  fish, adults drink tea.
- **Evening** — This is why you overnight instead of day-tripping:
  the coaches leave at five, the mist rolls down, the mosque lights
  come on and the lake goes mirror-still. Dinner slow, sleep early.

**Where to stay in Uzungol** (one night)

- Lakefront hotels vs hillside bungalows is the choice; the
  long-established lakeside houses (Inan Kardesler and its
  neighbours) and the newer bungalow terraces above the village
  both book out on summer weekends — reserve ahead and pick by
  current reviews. Expect €50–90 for a double with breakfast.

## Day 4 — Mist photos, then over to Ayder

- **7:00** — The famous shot: **morning mist lifting off the
  lake** — photographers' Uzungöl exists between 6:30 and 8:30.
  Then breakfast.
- **10:00** — Drive to **Ayder plateau** (~2 h): back down the
  valley, east along the coast into **Rize province**, then up the
  **Fırtına valley** — stop at the humpbacked **Ottoman stone
  bridges** over the rushing green river, and Çamlıhemşin's
  riverside cafés.
- **13:00** — Lunch in Çamlıhemşin or on arrival in Ayder.
- **14:30** — **Ayder** (1,350 m): a plateau of wooden chalets and
  waterfalls under the Kaçkar peaks. Walk the meadows to the
  **Gelintülü waterfall**, ride the zipline if it's running.
- **17:00** — The **Ayder hot springs** (kaplıca; modest fee,
  separate men's/women's sections) — thermal baths the region has
  used for centuries; the correct end to a driving day.
- **19:30** — Dinner at your lodge: **muhlama** — the Black Sea's
  molten cheese-and-cornmeal fondue — with honey from the meadows
  you walked.

**Where to stay in Ayder** (two nights)

- Wooden bungalow lodges and small chalet hotels are the entire
  market (€60–100 doubles with breakfast in season). Book early
  for July–August — Gulf-holiday demand is real — and choose a
  balcony facing the falls; the view is the product.

## Day 5 — Above the clouds: the high plateaus

The day this trip was built for. From Ayder, local jeeps and your
own courage go higher.

- **8:30** — Jeep or guided 4x4 up to the **Pokut and Sal
  plateaus** (~€25–40/person with local drivers; rough track,
  ridiculous reward): hamlets of weathered wooden houses on a
  ridge **above the cloud line** — the sea of clouds below your
  feet on the right mornings is the single best photograph on
  Turkey's Black Sea.
- **12:30** — Plateau lunch in a village house — eggs, honey,
  cheese, bread, all from within sight.
- **14:30** — Back down; afternoon at leisure — the **Huser
  plateau** viewpoint as an alternative or add-on, a Fırtına
  valley walk, or nothing at all in a hammock, which is also
  correct.
- **17:00** — Second visit to the hot springs (you will want it),
  or tea on the balcony as the evening mist performs.
- **19:30** — Last highland dinner. If the clouds never lifted
  today — that happens here — you got the mist version, which the
  locals will tell you is the true one.

## Day 6 — Down through the tea country, back to Trabzon

- **9:30** — Descend to the coast and stop in **Rize**, Turkey's
  tea capital: the **Ziraat botanical tea garden** above town
  (free; tea for pennies with the whole coast below), and a
  factory-shop stop to carry home the çay you've been drinking all
  week.
- **12:30** — Coast road west; lunch of **hamsi** (Black Sea
  anchovies, the regional obsession — best November–February, good
  always) or Akçaabat köfte round two.
- **15:00** — Back in Trabzon; the bazaar streets below Meydan for
  souvenirs — Trabzon's braided **hasır gold bracelets**, tea,
  hazelnut everything.
- **19:00** — Farewell dinner; Boztepe again if the sunset invites.

## Day 7 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Vakfıkebir bread and honey breakfast, drop the
  car, fly — Gulf travellers are home by evening on the direct;
  everyone else connects through Istanbul.

## What to pack

- A rain shell each and one umbrella — 200+ rain days a year is a
  promise, not a risk; the drizzle is soft and the gear makes it
  irrelevant.
- Layers: coast at 26°C, plateaus at 14°C the same afternoon.
- Shoes with grip for wet stone paths (Sumela, waterfalls,
  bridges).
- Swim/modesty wear per your preference for the segregated hot
  springs.
- Motion-sickness tablets for valley switchbacks if you're prone.
- An empty corner for tea, hazelnuts and honey — heavier than you
  plan, always.

## FAQ

**How does this compare with Cappadocia and the classic route?**
Different planet: that Turkey is dry stone and ancient empires;
this one is green, wet, alive and Ottoman-village flavoured. Do
the classic route first (our 11-day guide); come here when you
want mountains and mist instead of minarets and marble.

**Is the mist/rain going to ruin the photos?**
It *is* the photos. Uzungol at dawn and Pokut above the clouds
exist because of the humidity. Build flexibility (two Ayder
nights, Uzungol overnight rather than day trip — exactly as this
route does) and you'll catch the clear windows.

**Do I really need the car?**
Need, no — Trabzon's Meydan runs cheap daily group tours to every
stop in this guide. But the route's magic is stopping at the
unnamed viewpoint, the trout farm, the stone bridge — and tours
don't stop. If you drive anywhere in Turkey, drive here: traffic
is light and roads are good.

**Is it suitable for a honeymoon / older parents / kids?**
All three, unusually: it's Turkey's favourite honeymoon region
(bungalow balconies, waterfalls), drives are short and paved for
older travellers, and kids get lakes, cable cars and zip lines.
The only fitness test is optional (plateau walks).

**When exactly is peak season?**
July–August, driven by Gulf and domestic holidays — book those
months well ahead and expect Uzungol lively. June and September
give the same green with half the people; May and October are
moody, cheaper and still worth it.
