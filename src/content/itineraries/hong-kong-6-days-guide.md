Hong Kong is what you get when seven million people, a
British century, a Cantonese soul and some of the steepest
real estate on Earth share 1,100 square kilometres with
subtropical mountains: double-decker trams under neon,
bamboo scaffolding on glass towers, incense spirals over
temple courtyards, and a harbour crossing that has cost
pocket change since 1888.

The entry rules are refreshingly simple too. Most Western passports
get **90 days visa-free**; **Indian passport holders need no visa
either — just a free online Pre-arrival Registration (PAR)**,
fifteen minutes, covering **six months of visits, 14 days at a
time**, and **Macau adds itself visa-free for 30 days**. Compare
that with the mainland-China paperwork in our
[Beijing–Xi'an–Shanghai guide](/itineraries/china-10-days-guide/)
and this is the easy door into the Chinese world.

One base does the whole trip: **five nights in Tsim Sha
Tsui**, Kowloon's harbour-front, facing the skyline you came
for. Costs are in Hong Kong dollars — figure **{{HKD 7800}}
per person** excluding flights
(Emirates and Cathay fly Dubai–Hong Kong non-stop, ~7½ h,
commonly US$520–870 return). **Go October–December** —
the clear, dry, 22–26°C window — and treat June–September as
typhoon roulette.

## Before you go: the admin

- **PAR, the whole visa story:** register free at the
  Immigration Department's GovHK site — passport details
  must match **exactly**, passport needs 6+ months
  validity. Approval prints as a **notification slip:
  carry it with the passport** at check-in and
  immigration (no slip, no boarding). It's **valid 6
  months, multiple visits, 14 days each** — and dies if
  you renew the passport, so re-register after any
  renewal. Beware agent sites charging for what is a
  free two-page form.
- **Macau needs nothing**: Indian passports are **visa-free 30
  days** (passport + hotel/itinerary if asked). Your HK
  PAR covers the return since it's multiple-entry.
- **Land smart:** the **Airport Express** does
  airport→Kowloon in 22 minutes (~HKD 105; kids half).
  Buy an **Octopus card** on arrival (or add it to
  Apple/Google Wallet) — it pays trains, trams, ferries,
  buses, 7-Elevens and half the city's cafés. Budget
  HKD 300/person of top-ups for the week.
- **Money:** cards + Octopus everywhere; keep ~HKD 500
  cash for markets and dai pai dongs. Tipping: round up,
  10% service is usually already on the bill.
- **Weather honesty:** typhoon signals T8+ (Jun–Sep)
  shut the city — attractions, ferries, sometimes
  flights — for a day at a time. October onward, skies
  clear and the humidity finally surrenders.

## Day 1 — Land, cross the harbour, watch it light up

- **Morning** — The overnight flight lands early.
  Airport Express + MTR to **Tsim Sha Tsui**; most
  hotels hold bags before check-in.
- **12:30** — First lunch sets the tone: **dim sum**
  (har gow, siu mai, char siu bao — HKD 100–160 pp at
  an honest local house).
- **15:00** — The **Star Ferry** to Central (**under
  HKD 7** — the planet's best-value harbour cruise,
  running since 1888) and back; then the **Avenue of
  Stars** promenade as the towers switch on.
- **20:00** — **A Symphony of Lights** (free, nightly):
  the skyline performs; the Kowloon waterfront is the
  auditorium. Dinner after in TST's lanes (HKD
  150–300 pp).

**Where to stay (Tsim Sha Tsui, five nights)**

- *Luxury:* **Regent Hong Kong** — 4.9/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~850 reviews), floor-to-ceiling harbour glass on the
  TST waterfront; the skyline is your wallpaper.
  ~HKD 3,500+. (Island-side icon: Mandarin Oriental,
  4.9/~7,800.)
- *Mid-range:* **The Royal Garden** — 4.6/5 (~6,000
  reviews), atrium calm, rooftop pool, ten minutes'
  walk from the ferry. ~HKD 1,400–1,900.
- *Value:* **Holiday Inn Golden Mile** — 4.1/5 (~4,400
  reviews), Nathan Road MTR doorstep at Kowloon's
  centre of gravity. ~HKD 950–1,300.

## Day 2 — The Peak and old Central

- **07:45** — Beat the queue culture: the **Peak Tram**
  from Garden Road at opening (**Sky Pass — tram +
  Sky Terrace 428 — HKD 149–158 return; tram-only
  HKD 88 weekday/108 weekend**; queues hit 90 minutes
  by late afternoon, 5 at 8am). The 27° tilt makes
  skyscrapers lean past the window.
- **09:00** — **Sky Terrace 428** for the full
  amphitheatre — harbour, towers, green peaks — then
  the flat, gorgeous **Lugard Road loop** (45 min,
  free) for the same view without glass.
- **12:00** — Down to Central: ride the **Mid-Levels
  escalators** (world's longest outdoor set), incense
  clouds at **Man Mo Temple** (free), the revived
  police-station arts hub **Tai Kwun**, and PMQ's
  design floors.
- **16:00** — A **'ding ding' tram** end-to-half ride
  (HKD 3.30 flat — the moving museum) through Wan
  Chai's markets.
- **19:00** — Cross back for the **Temple Street Night
  Market**: fortune tellers, opera buskers, clay-pot
  rice and haggled souvenirs.

## Day 3 — Lantau: the Buddha above the clouds

- **09:00** — MTR to Tung Chung, then the **Ngong Ping
  360 cable car** (**HKD 295 return standard; HKD 365
  Crystal glass-floor** — do Crystal one way, HKD 330
  mixed): 25 minutes over bay, jungle ridges and the
  airport's runways.
- **10:30** — The **Tian Tan "Big" Buddha** — 34 m of
  bronze serenity up 268 steps (free; small fee only
  with the museum) — and **Po Lin Monastery's** famous
  vegetarian kitchen for lunch (HKD 100–160 set).
- **14:00** — Bus 21 to **Tai O**: the stilt-house
  fishing village that time forgot — dried seafood
  lanes, a HKD 40 boat spin past the stilts (white
  dolphins if the sea is kind).
- **18:30** — Back via Citygate Outlets (Tung Chung)
  if shopping calls; harbour-side dinner otherwise.

## Day 4 — Disneyland

- **09:30** — **Hong Kong Disneyland** (1-day tickets
  are **date-tiered: HKD 639–789 adult / 479–599
  child** — buy online for your exact date; weekdays
  sit at the bottom of the band). It's the world's
  most manageable Disney: compact, 45-minute maximum
  queues on a normal weekday, and home to the
  planet's first **World of Frozen** — plus Mystic
  Manor, which adults rate the best ride Disney has
  built anywhere.
- **Strategy:** arrive at rope drop, Frozen first,
  Grizzly Gulch after lunch, parade at 15:30,
  "Momentous" fireworks over the castle to close.
- **21:00** — MTR home, feet filing complaints.

## Day 5 — Macau: over the bridge

- **08:30** — The **HZMB shuttle bus** from Kowloon's
  port terminal: 40 minutes across the **55 km
  Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge**, the world's longest
  sea crossing (**~HKD 65 each way**, passports +
  PAR slip in hand; Macau stamps Indian passports in
  visa-free).
- **10:30** — Old Macau on foot: the **Ruins of St
  Paul's** facade, **Senado Square's** wave-tiled
  Portuguese plaza, Rua do Cunha's sample-happy
  bakeries — and the compulsory **pastel de nata**
  (Lord Stow's, ~MOP 12; HKD spends 1:1 everywhere).
- **14:00** — A-Ma Temple (Macau's namesake) and the
  lighthouse-topped Guia Fortress — or straight to
  **Cotai**: the Venetian's absurd indoor canals
  (free to wander; gondolas extra), the wave-roofed
  Morpheus lobby, fountains at Wynn.
- **20:00** — Bus back over the bridge, lights of
  three cities out the window.

## Day 6 — Temples, markets, takeoff

- **09:00** — **Wong Tai Sin Temple** (free): the
  wish-granting shrine where fortune sticks clatter —
  then the Tang-dynasty calm of **Chi Lin Nunnery &
  Nan Lian Garden** (free), a wooden monastery built
  without a single nail.
- **12:00** — Last blitz by appetite: **Mong Kok's**
  Ladies Market and Sneaker Street for the hagglers,
  or a final dim sum blowout for the eaters.
- **15:00** — Airport Express out (in-town check-in
  at Kowloon station lets you drop bags early and
  roam luggage-free). Egg tart at the gate:
  mandatory.

## What to pack

- Walking shoes with grip — this city is stairs,
  escalators and 20,000-step days.
- One warm layer even in autumn: the MTR and malls
  run arctic air-con.
- Compact umbrella (subtropics keep their own
  schedule), sunscreen for Lantau and ferry decks.
- Power: HK uses **UK-style Type G** — UK and Gulf plugs
  work as-is.
- Loose smart-casual for nicer dinners; shoulders
  covered for temple halls.
- An empty bag corner: Mong Kok will fill it.

## FAQ

**Is the PAR really all we need? Any catch?**
For a tourist trip, yes — free, online, six months
of 14-day visits. The catches are procedural: the
printed slip must accompany the exact passport you
registered (renew the passport, redo the PAR), and
airlines check it at check-in. Families
register each member separately, kids included.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Better than its pork-forward reputation. Halal:
the **Kowloon Mosque** anchors Nathan Road, and the
**Islamic Centre Canteen in Wan Chai serves famous
halal dim sum**; Chungking Mansions is a
subcontinental food court in a tower block; Macau
day included, you'll never be far from options.
Vegetarian: monastery kitchens are a genuine
cuisine here — **Po Lin on Lantau and the Chi Lin
vegetarian house** — plus veg dim sum lists at most
big tea houses. State "no oyster sauce" clearly.

**Is Hong Kong safe? Anything to know?**
One of the safest big cities anywhere — midnight
MTR rides, solo travellers, kids on trams: all
normal. Real-world cautions: pickpocket-level
awareness in Mong Kok crowds, respect typhoon
signals when they fly, and cross roads looking
RIGHT first (they drive on the left).

**Disneyland or Ocean Park?**
Disney for under-10s and Frozen pilgrims; **Ocean
Park** (HKD ~498) for teens — proper coasters, a
giant aquarium and a cable car over cliffs. Both
in one trip is a lot of park; this itinerary's
Day 4 swaps cleanly either way.

**Is Macau worth a whole day?**
Yes — it's two cities in one hour's loop: a
UNESCO Portuguese old town you walk in a morning
and a Vegas-outgrowing strip you gawp at after
lunch, joined by free casino shuttles. The bridge
crossing itself is half the fun. Gamblers: set a
number before entering; the Venetian has no
clocks for a reason.

**What's Hong Kong guide #2?**
**The green Hong Kong**: Dragon's Back ridge hike,
Sai Kung's beaches, Cheung Chau and Lamma island
ferries — the 70% of the territory that's country
park, for the return trip nobody expects to want
this much. On the list.
