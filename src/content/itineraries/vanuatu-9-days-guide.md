**Mount Yasur** on Tanna has been erupting continuously for
several hundred years, and a road goes most of the way up it. You
walk the last fifteen minutes to a rim with no railing, in the
dark, and watch the vents below throw molten rock three hundred
metres into the air every few minutes. Captain Cook navigated to
Tanna by its glow in 1774.

Nothing else in this guide is quite that, but Vanuatu keeps
delivering: **Espiritu Santo** has freshwater **blue holes** so
clear the trees are reflected from underwater, white-sand beaches
nobody is on, and the **SS President Coolidge** — a 200-metre
luxury liner turned troopship that struck a mine in 1942 and sank
in the harbour, now one of the most accessible large wreck dives
in the world.

It is an archipelago of 83 islands, about 300,000 people, and
**more than 100 living languages** — the highest linguistic
density per head anywhere on earth.

## Before you go: flights, volcano logistics and cyclones

### Entry and money

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for 30 days or more.
- **Vanuatu vatu (VUV).** Cash dominates everywhere outside Port
  Vila; **on Tanna and in rural Santo there are effectively no
  card machines and no ATMs.** Draw everything you need in Port
  Vila before you fly out.
- Australian dollars are sometimes accepted in Vila at a poor
  rate.

### Domestic flights are the whole plan

- **Air Vanuatu** flies Port Vila to **Tanna** (about 50 minutes)
  and to **Santo** (about an hour). There is no practical
  alternative — the inter-island boats are slow cargo runs.
- **Schedules change, and flights are cancelled or retimed with
  little notice.** This is normal and everybody plans for it.
  **Never connect a domestic flight to an international one on the
  same day.**
- **Book the domestic legs early** and keep the itinerary loose at
  the edges.
- Baggage allowances on the small aircraft are low.

### Mount Yasur, practically

- You go **in the late afternoon**, arriving for dusk, because the
  lava only reads as lava once the light has gone.
- Access is through the **Yakel-area custom owners** and the fee
  is paid to them. Guides are required and the access road is 4x4
  only.
- **The volcano has an alert level from 0 to 4.** At level 2 you
  can usually reach the rim; **at 3 and above it closes**, and
  that happens with no notice. There is no refund for weather or
  geology — build a spare day on Tanna.
- It is genuinely loud, the ground moves, and ash and grit fall on
  you. Standing there is not a mild experience.

### Cyclones and seasons

- **May to October is the dry season** — cooler, lower humidity,
  best diving visibility, and the time to come.
- **November to April is cyclone season**, with the peak January
  to March. Vanuatu is hit regularly and hard; **Cyclone Pam in
  2015 damaged most of the country**. Flights and resorts shut.
- **Take travel insurance that covers cyclone disruption**, and
  mean it.

### Kastom, and how to behave

- Much of rural Vanuatu runs on ***kastom*** — customary law,
  land ownership and practice. **Almost every beach, blue hole and
  track is owned by a family or village** and carries a small fee,
  which is correct and should be paid cheerfully.
- **Ask before photographing anybody**, always.
- **Nakamal** — the kava bar — is a men's space traditionally,
  though visitors of both sexes are generally welcome in Vila.
  Vanuatu kava is considerably stronger than Fijian.

## Day 1 — Land in Port Vila

- **13:00** — Land at **Bauerfield (VLI)**, ten minutes from town.
- **14:30** — Drop bags. Port Vila wraps around a harbour with
  Iririki island in the middle of it.
- **16:00** — **Draw all the cash you will need for the week.**
  This is the practical priority and there is no second chance
  once you fly out.
- **17:00** — The **seafront market** and the harbour walk.
- **18:30** — A ***nakamal*** for **kava** if you want it. The
  shell is drunk in one, you spit, and then you sit in the dark
  without talking much, which is the custom. **Vanuatu kava is
  strong** — one shell is a reasonable introduction and three is
  a decision.
- **20:00** — Dinner. Port Vila eats unexpectedly well, a
  leftover from the Anglo-French condominium that governed the
  islands jointly until 1980 — the one place in the Pacific with
  genuinely good French food.

## Day 2 — Efate, around the island

- **08:30** — Hire a car or take a tour round **Efate**, which is
  a 130 km loop on a sealed road and takes a comfortable day.
- **09:30** — **Mele Cascades**: a series of limestone pools
  climbing through forest to a 35 m fall, which you walk up
  through the water itself holding a rope.
- **11:30** — **Blue Lagoon** on the east coast — a freshwater
  swimming hole with rope swings, and the first of the island's
  improbable blues.
- **13:00** — Lunch at a roadside stall. **Laplap** is the
  national dish: grated root vegetable baked in banana leaf in an
  earth oven, with coconut cream.
- **15:00** — **Eton Beach** or **Nasama** on the east coast, both
  community-run with a small fee.
- **17:00** — Back round the north, past the cattle country —
  Vanuatu beef is excellent and exported to Japan.
- **19:30** — Dinner, and repack light for Tanna.

## Day 3 — Fly to Tanna, and the volcano

- **09:00** — Fly to **Tanna**, about 50 minutes, over the reef
  and into a grass-and-coral airstrip.
- **10:30** — Transfer to your bungalows, 4x4 and slow — the
  island's roads are rough volcanic track and the 40 km across can
  take two hours.
- **12:30** — Lunch, then rest. The afternoon is the volcano.
- **15:30** — Drive to the **Yasur** access point across the
  **ash plain** — a grey moonscape of consolidated ash where
  nothing grows, which is itself worth the trip.
- **16:30** — The 4x4 climbs to the car park, then **a fifteen-
  minute walk to the rim.**
- **17:30** — **And then you are standing on the edge.** The
  crater is a few hundred metres across, there is no railing, and
  the vents below erupt every few minutes — a crack like artillery,
  then glowing rock thrown clear of the rim and arcing back down.
  **As the light goes it becomes extraordinary**, and nobody
  talks much.
- **19:30** — Down in the dark, covered in grit.
- **20:30** — Dinner at the bungalows, by generator light.

## Day 4 — Tanna, the island and the villages

- **08:30** — A ***kastom* village** visit, arranged through your
  accommodation — and this is where to be careful about why you
  are going. These are communities that chose to keep traditional
  dress and practice, not a display, and the visits that work are
  the ones arranged respectfully and paid for properly.
- **11:00** — The **Yakel** or **Louniel** area, with a guide who
  will explain the kastom system of land and exchange, and the
  banyan trees that serve as village meeting grounds.
- **13:00** — Lunch.
- **14:30** — The **hot springs** at Port Resolution, where the
  volcanic heat comes up through the beach and villagers cook in
  the sand, and **Captain Cook's landing point** — he named the
  bay after his ship in 1774.
- **16:30** — **Blue Cave** if the sea allows: you swim in through
  a low opening and surface inside a chamber lit blue from below.
- **18:30** — Back.
- **Optional** — **A second night at Yasur** if the alert level
  was high and you did not get the rim, or simply because it was
  worth it twice.

## Day 5 — Back to Vila, on to Santo

- **09:00** — Fly Tanna to **Port Vila**, then connect to
  **Espiritu Santo** — usually the same day, sometimes not. This
  is the day the schedule can slip.
- **14:00** — Land at **Luganville**, Santo's main town — a wide
  single street that was an American base of **100,000 troops**
  during the Pacific war and has felt oversized ever since.
- **16:00** — **Million Dollar Point**: at the end of the war the
  Americans offered the equipment to the condominium authorities,
  who declined in the expectation of getting it free, so the US
  **bulldozed bulldozers, jeeps and cranes straight into the sea.**
  You snorkel over the lot of it, rusting in shallow water.
- **18:30** — Dinner in Luganville.

## Day 6 — The blue holes, and the beaches

- **08:30** — **The blue holes**, which are Santo's own thing:
  freshwater springs welling up through limestone into pools of a
  blue that photographs look edited. **Nanda (Jackie's)**,
  **Riri** — reached by canoe up a river through forest — and
  **Matevulu**, with a rope swing off a banyan branch.
- **11:30** — The water is cold, startlingly clear, and you can
  see the tree canopy reflected from several metres down.
- **13:00** — Lunch.
- **14:30** — **Champagne Beach**, which earns its reputation:
  white sand, a perfect curve, and volcanic gas bubbling up
  through it in places at low tide. There is a village fee.
- **16:30** — **Port Olry** further north, quieter still, with
  beach restaurants serving coconut crab.
- **19:00** — Back to Luganville.

## Day 7 — The SS President Coolidge

- **08:00** — **The Coolidge**, and it is one of the great dives
  anywhere. A **200 m luxury liner** converted to a troopship,
  which struck friendly mines entering the harbour in October 1942.
  The captain beached her and **all but two of 5,340 men got off**.
- **08:30** — She lies on her port side from **20 m at the bow to
  70 m at the stern**, and you enter from the shore — **you walk
  into this dive**, which is why it is so accessible. Guns,
  jeeps, a tiled swimming pool, chandeliers, and *The Lady*, a
  porcelain relief of a woman and a unicorn, deep inside.
- **11:30** — Surface interval, then a second dive.
- **Not a diver?** **Million Dollar Point** and the blue holes
  cover the day instead, or take a **discover-scuba** session —
  the shallow end of the Coolidge is within reach of a first-timer
  with an instructor.
- **15:00** — **Matevulu** or a beach afternoon.
- **19:00** — Dinner.

## Day 8 — Santo, slow

- **09:00** — **Riri Blue Hole by canoe** if you missed it, or the
  **Millennium Cave** — a full day of jungle walking, ladders,
  wading a river gorge and floating out through a cave, run by
  three villages together. It is the hardest and best day on Santo
  and it is genuinely strenuous.
- **13:00** — Lunch.
- **15:00** — **Luganville market** for baskets and the fresh
  produce, then the last beach.
- **18:00** — Sunset.
- **19:30** — Last dinner, and spend the remaining vatu.

## Day 9 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Fly **Santo → Port Vila**, and connect
  internationally.
- **Leave a full day between the domestic and international
  legs** if you possibly can, or at the very least several hours.
  Domestic cancellations here are routine and the international
  flights are few.
- **Note** — Vatu cannot be changed outside Vanuatu. Spend it.

## What to pack

- **Closed shoes with grip for Yasur** — the rim is loose ash and
  grit, and it will ruin whatever you wear.
- **A fleece or windproof layer** for the rim after dark, which is
  cold and exposed.
- **A torch**, and a spare. Tanna has no street lighting and
  generators go off.
- **All the cash you need**, drawn in Port Vila.
- **Reef shoes** — much of the swimming is over coral and rock.
- **Strong repellent.** Malaria is present in Vanuatu; check
  current advice on prophylaxis, and dengue occurs.
- **Australian-style Type I plugs**, 220V.
- **A dry bag** for every boat and canoe.

## FAQ

**How close do you actually get to the lava?**
You stand on the crater rim, a few hundred metres above and
across from the active vents, with no barrier. Eruptions throw
glowing rock clear of the rim on a regular cycle. It is the most
accessible active volcano on earth and the access is managed by
the custom owners with guides — but it is not a viewing platform
behind glass, and it should not be treated as one.

**What if the volcano is closed?**
The alert level moves and at 3 or above the rim is shut, with no
warning and no refund. **Build a spare day into Tanna.** People do
fly home having not got up it.

**Do I need to dive to make Santo worth it?**
No. The blue holes, Champagne Beach, Million Dollar Point and the
Millennium Cave fill the days without a tank. But the Coolidge is
one of the best wrecks in the world and you walk into it from the
beach, which is rare — if you have ever thought about learning,
this is the place.

**Is Vanuatu expensive?**
Yes, by Pacific standards, and the domestic flights are why.
Almost everything else — village fees, market food, local
transport — is cheap. The resorts are where the money goes if you
let it.

**When should I go?**
**May to October.** Outside that you are in cyclone season, and
Vanuatu gets hit properly rather than theoretically.
