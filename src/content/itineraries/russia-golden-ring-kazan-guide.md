Moscow and St Petersburg are Russia's front door. This trip is what's
behind it: Sergiev Posad's golden monastery domes, Suzdal — a
medieval town preserved whole, Nizhny Novgorod's clifftop kremlin
over the Volga, and Kazan, the 1,000-year-old Tatar capital where
minarets and onion domes share one fortress wall.

The geography does you a favour: Moscow → Vladimir/Suzdal → Nizhny
Novgorod → Kazan is **one eastward railway line**. Every leg moves
you forward, nothing repeats, and Kazan has its own international
airport — including direct flights to Dubai — so you fly home from
the far end instead of riding back.

This pairs with our 10-day Moscow–St Petersburg–Murmansk itinerary:
that one first if Russia is new to you, this one when the capitals
are done.

All costs are in Russian rubles (RUB), with conversions calculated
at today's exchange rate. Opening hours are approximate — treat
anything marked ~ as a guide, not a contract.

## Before you go: the short version of the admin

The payment, SIM and visa rules are identical to our first Russia
guide, where they're covered in depth. The non-negotiables:

- **Visas:** e-visa for 64 nationalities including India and most
  of the EU — [evisa.kdmid.ru](https://evisa.kdmid.ru), ~USD 52,
  ~4 days processing, 30-day stay. Medical insurance is mandatory
  and arranged before arrival. UAE and several Gulf passports are
  visa-free. US/UK/Canada/Australia/NZ passports need a full
  consular visa instead.
- **Money:** foreign Visa/Mastercard **do not work anywhere**.
  Bring crisp USD/EUR/AED cash to exchange at bank branches, and
  set up a **prepaid virtual MIR card** before you fly — you'll
  need it for train and museum tickets online.
- **SIM:** physical Russian SIMs are impractical for tourists now
  (biometric registration); use an **international travel eSIM**,
  and expect a ~24-hour data block the first time it touches a
  Russian network — activate early, download offline **Yandex
  Maps**.

Install it before you fly; a foreign eSIM can take ~24 hours to clear
onto a Russian network.
- **Apps:** WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook are blocked; Telegram
  works. Install **Yandex Go** (taxis — set payment to cash) and
  **Yandex Translate** before you fly. English is thinner out here
  than in Moscow — the translate app earns its place daily.
- **Advisories:** several Western governments advise against
  travel to Russia — check yours, and whether your insurance stays
  valid under it, before booking anything.

### Booking trains — the skill this trip runs on

- All four rail legs sell on the RZD site/app (MIR card needed) or
  through international resellers before you leave. Passport
  details are required per ticket; carry the passport at boarding.
- **Moscow → Vladimir:** Lastochka or Strizh fast trains, ~1h45.
- **Vladimir → Nizhny Novgorod:** the same eastbound trains, ~2h.
- **Nizhny Novgorod → Kazan:** the overnight sleeper (~7–9 h) — a
  classic Russian experience in its own right. Book a 4-berth
  **kupe** compartment (or buy all four berths as a family/couple
  for privacy), pack slippers and tea money; the carriage samovar
  runs all night.
- Book the sleeper and the fast trains 2+ weeks ahead for choice
  of berths and the cheap fares.

### Travelling with kids?

- This route was first run with a toddler: the towns are calmer
  and greener than Moscow, and every leg is short except the
  sleeper — which children usually love (moving bunk beds).
- Strollers work in Suzdal's lanes, the NN embankments and Kazan's
  Bauman Street; pack the carrier for monastery grounds and
  staircases.
- Suzdal's honey-mead tastings have zero kid appeal — trade it for
  the riverside meadows and wooden-architecture museum, which have
  plenty.

## Day 1 — Land in Moscow, one evening in the centre

Stay near a Metro station inside the Garden Ring; this trip only
sleeps in Moscow twice, so location beats luxury.

- **15:00** — Check in, buy a **Troika card**, rest.
- **17:30** — The classic evening circuit: **Zaryadye Park**
  floating bridge → **Red Square** at dusk → GUM's lights.
  (First time in Moscow? Steal Day 2 from our other Russia guide
  and add a day here.)
- **19:30** — Dinner: **Stolovaya 57** in GUM (~400–700
  RUB/person) or a Georgian restaurant — Moscow does them better
  than anywhere outside Georgia.

**Where to stay in Moscow**

- *Splurge:* **Hotel Metropol** — the 1905 Art Nouveau landmark by
  Red Square.
- *Mid-range:* **Cosmos Selection Tverskaya** — the ex-Marriott on
  Tverskaya: central, modern, sensible.
- *Budget:* **Godzillas Hostel** — the long-running central
  backpacker standard.

## Day 2 — Sergiev Posad: the Russian Vatican

The most important monastery in Russia, 70 km out — an easy
self-guided day trip by suburban train.

- **8:00** — Suburban express from **Yaroslavsky Station**
  (~1h15, a few hundred rubles; trains all morning).
- **9:30** — **Trinity Lavra of St Sergius** (grounds ~daylight
  hours; free entry, small fees for some churches/museums) — the
  UNESCO fortress-monastery: Trinity Cathedral with the relics of
  St Sergius, the Kremlin-model Assumption Cathedral, the 88 m
  baroque bell tower. Working monastery: modest dress, headscarves
  for women inside churches. Allow 2–3 hours.
- **12:30** — Lunch: **Russkiy Dvorik** opposite the Lavra
  (~600–800 RUB/person) or the monastery refectory canteen —
  cheaper and more atmospheric.
- **14:00** — The **Toy Museum** (~150–200 RUB) — Sergiev Posad is
  the birthplace of the matryoshka; buy them here, not in Moscow.
- **16:00** — Train back; evening free in Moscow. Pack for an
  early start.

## Day 3 — East to Vladimir, sleep in Suzdal

- **8:00** — Fast train from **Kursky Station** to **Vladimir**
  (~1h45). Leave luggage at the station lockers.
- **10:30** — **Assumption Cathedral** (~200 RUB) — the
  12th-century church the Kremlin's cathedrals were modelled on,
  with Andrei Rublev frescoes inside.
- **11:30** — **St Demetrius Cathedral** (~200 RUB) — one room,
  and every inch of the facade carved with lions, saints and
  griffins.
- **12:30** — **Golden Gate** (~150 RUB) — the last surviving
  medieval city gate in Russia; small military museum inside.
- **13:30** — Lunch in Vladimir's centre, collect bags.
- **15:00** — Taxi to **Suzdal** (~30–40 min, ~800–1,200 RUB by
  Yandex Go) — the road ends in the 11th century.
- **16:30** — Check in, then first wander: the **Kamenka River**
  meadows at golden hour, domes in every direction. No big sights
  tonight — Suzdal's whole point is the pace.
- **19:00** — Dinner: **Gostiny Dvor** on the trading square —
  hearty traditional Russian (~600–900 RUB/person) — with a glass
  of **medovukha**, the honey mead Suzdal is famous for.

**Where to stay in Suzdal** (two nights)

- *Splurge:* **Pushkarskaya Sloboda** — the riverside resort
  village of wooden houses: pools, banya, big breakfasts; the
  full Suzdal-postcard stay.
- *Mid-range:* **Kremlyovskiy Hotel** — well-rated, right by the
  Kremlin ramparts.
- *Budget:* **Petrovih Guest House** — small, warm and superbly
  reviewed; Suzdal at family scale.

## Day 4 — Suzdal: Russia's open-air museum

A town of 10,000 people and some fifty churches — the best-kept
piece of old Russia anywhere.

- **9:00** — **Suzdal Kremlin** (grounds ~9:00–19:00; ~400 RUB) —
  earth ramparts, the star-spangled blue domes of the Nativity
  Cathedral, river views.
- **11:00** — **Museum of Wooden Architecture** (~9:00–18:00,
  ~400 RUB) — churches, windmills and peasant houses built without
  a single nail, moved here from vanished villages.
- **13:00** — Lunch: **Russkaya Restoratsiya** — rustic and
  family-friendly (~600–900 RUB/person).
- **14:30** — **Spaso-Evfimiev Monastery** (~10:00–18:00, closed
  Mon in some seasons; ~500 RUB) — the fortress-monastery upriver;
  time your visit to the **bell-ringing concerts** (short, several
  times daily — ask at the gate).
- **16:30** — Browse the trading rows: medovukha by the bottle,
  pickles, birch-bark crafts.
- **18:00** — The meadow across from the Kremlin for sunset — the
  photo that sells Suzdal.
- **19:30** — Slow dinner; you've earned the town's rhythm by now.

## Day 5 — Down the line to Nizhny Novgorod

- **9:30** — Taxi back to Vladimir station.
- **11:00** — Eastbound fast train to **Nizhny Novgorod** (~2h).
- **13:30** — Check in near Bolshaya Pokrovskaya; lunch on the
  street itself.
- **15:00** — **Nizhny Novgorod Kremlin** (grounds free, wall walk
  ticketed seasonally) — 16th-century red walls stepping down a
  200-metre cliff above the meeting of the Volga and Oka.
- **17:00** — **Fedorovsky Embankment** viewpoints, then descend
  the great **Chkalov Staircase** (open always, free) — 560 steps
  with the Volga filling the horizon; sunset here is the day's
  event.
- **19:30** — Dinner around Bolshaya Pokrovskaya — Nizhny's
  pedestrian spine; the city's café scene has quietly become one
  of Russia's best.

**Where to stay in Nizhny Novgorod** (one night)

- *Comfort:* **Kulibin Parkhotel & SPA** — well-rated, spa on
  site, walking distance to Pokrovskaya.
- *Budget:* **Smile Hostel** — five minutes from the Kremlin on
  the main street.

## Day 6 — The Volga by cable car, then the night train

- **9:30** — **Cable car across the Volga** to Bor (~10:00–21:00;
  ~150 RUB each way) — 3.6 km over the river; ride out and back
  for the views alone.
- **11:30** — **Rukavishnikov Mansion** (closed Mon; guided visits;
  ~300–500 RUB) — a merchant palace that shows what Volga trade
  money built.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then the lower city: the Volga embankment and
  the old fair district.
- **16:00** — Collect bags; supermarket run for train supplies —
  tea, snacks, instant noodles (doing it like a local).
- **Evening** — Board the **overnight sleeper to Kazan** (~7–9 h).
  Kupe berths come with linen; the samovar at the carriage end has
  boiling water all night. Falling asleep to the rails and waking
  in Tatarstan is the leg of this trip people remember.

## Day 7 — Kazan: where Europe meets the Tatar world

- **7:30** — Arrive Kazan, taxi to the hotel; most will hold
  luggage before check-in. Breakfast and a slow start.
- **10:30** — **Kazan Kremlin** (grounds open all day, free;
  museums 200–450 RUB) — the UNESCO fortress where the
  **Kul Sharif Mosque** (~9:00–19:30, closed to visitors Fri
  ~11:30–13:15; free) rises beside Orthodox cathedrals and the
  leaning Söyembikä Tower. Women cover hair in the mosque; shoes
  off; it's a working mosque and a stunning one.
- **13:30** — Lunch: **Tatar by Tubetey** — modern Tatar fast-casual:
  echpochmak (meat triangles), kystybyi flatbreads (~400–700
  RUB/person).
- **15:00** — **Bauman Street** — Kazan's 1.9 km pedestrian artery:
  street performers, the Epiphany bell tower, chak-chak shops.
- **17:30** — **Family Center viewpoint** (~10:00–22:00, ~200 RUB)
  — the giant cauldron-shaped building's 360° platform: Kremlin,
  Volga and the new city in one sweep at dusk.
- **20:00** — Dinner: **Tugan Avilym** — a folk-village restaurant
  complex, touristy and delicious (~1,200 RUB/person); order the
  horse-meat platter if you're brave and the chak-chak regardless.

**Where to stay in Kazan** (two nights)

- *Splurge:* **Shalyapin Palace Hotel** — the grand old name right
  on Bauman Street.
- *Mid-range:* **Grand Hotel Kazan** — modern tower a block from
  Bauman; rooftop views.
- *Budget:* **Hotel Tatarstan** — Soviet-retro high-rise, central
  and cheap.

## Day 8 — Sviyazhsk island and the last evening

- **9:00** — Day trip to **Sviyazhsk** (~60 km; organised tour
  ~2,000–2,500 RUB/person, or taxi + entry) — the island fortress
  Ivan the Terrible built in four weeks in 1551 to besiege Kazan:
  UNESCO monasteries, wooden lanes, Volga views on all sides.
  Summer option: get there by boat along the river.
- **12:00** — On the way back, the **Temple of All Religions**
  (~9:00–18:00, ~200 RUB) — one architect's lifelong folly gluing
  a mosque, church, synagogue and pagoda into one building.
  Photogenic exactly once; 45 minutes is plenty.
- **14:30** — Late lunch back in Kazan.
- **16:00** — The **Old Tatar Sloboda** quarter — painted wooden
  merchant houses and the Märcani Mosque along Kaban lake.
- **19:00** — Farewell dinner + the lakefront **Kazan riviera**
  lights. Buy boxed **chak-chak** tonight — airport prices double.

## Day 9 — Fly home from Kazan

- **Morning** — Kazan airport is 30–40 minutes from the centre.
  **Direct flydubai flights connect Kazan and Dubai** (from
  from roughly {{RUB 31000}} one way), and Gulf/Istanbul
  connections cover everyone else — no need to backtrack to
  Moscow, though Moscow connections are plentiful if the fares
  beat the direct.

## What to pack

- Comfortable walking shoes — this route is cobblestones, ramparts
  and one 560-step staircase.
- Layers and a rain shell; Volga wind is real even in summer.
- A headscarf — needed in both monastery churches and the mosque.
- Sleeper-train kit: slippers or flip-flops, an eye mask, a small
  towel, and snacks to share — compartment tea with strangers is
  half the experience.
- Crisp USD/EUR/AED notes, your MIR card set up, VPN installed,
  offline Yandex Maps for every city on the route.
- Printed e-visa and insurance, plus offline PDFs.

## FAQ

**Is this doable as a first Russia trip?**
Yes, but do the capitals first if you can — this route assumes
you've had your Red Square moment and want depth over icons. If
you only have one trip, our 10-day Moscow–St Petersburg–Murmansk
itinerary is the starter.

**When is the best time to go?**
May–September is prime: green meadows in Suzdal, boat options to
Sviyazhsk, long evenings on the Volga. Early autumn (September)
is the sweet spot — golden foliage and no crowds. Winter is
beautiful but hard: short days and rural distances.

**Is English a problem out here?**
More than in Moscow — menus and museum captions are often
Russian-only beyond Kazan's centre. Yandex Translate's camera
mode plus learning Cyrillic letters covers 90% of it, and Kazan
itself is well set up for visitors.

**Is Kazan worth two nights?**
Yes — it's the surprise of the trip for most people. A
millennium-old Muslim-majority city inside Russia, Gulf-friendly
food (halal options everywhere), and a Kremlin unlike anything in
Moscow. It regularly outranks both capitals in Russian domestic
tourism polls.

**Can I do this route in reverse?**
Yes — fly into Kazan and ride west, ending with Moscow. It works;
we prefer eastbound because arrival admin (SIM block, cash,
Troika) is easier to sort in Moscow, and the trip then ends on
its most distinctive note.

**What about vegetarians?**
The provinces are meat-and-dough country, but every town on this
route has options: mushroom and potato dishes in Suzdal, Nizhny's
modern cafés, and Kazan's kystybyi and pumpkin dishes. Georgian
restaurants — reliably vegetarian-friendly — exist in every city
here too.
