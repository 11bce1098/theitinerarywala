Spain rewards the triangle. Barcelona is architecture on
hallucinogens and the Mediterranean at the end of every street;
Madrid is the grand capital — three of Europe's greatest museums
within one walkable mile — with medieval Toledo half an hour away;
Seville is the south: orange trees, Moorish palaces, and flamenco
done where it was born. High-speed rail sews them together in
2.5-hour hops, and Spain's train-price war (three competing
operators) means the tickets can cost less than lunch.

And 2026 is the year to go: on 20 February, the **Sagrada Família's
Tower of Jesus Christ was completed** — 172.5 metres, now the
tallest church on earth, 144 years after the first stone. You'll
see it in the year it finally happened.

**Go April–June or September–October.** July–August grills Madrid
and Seville (40°C+ in the south — this route survives it only with
siesta discipline); winter is mild in Seville, crisp in Madrid, and
quiet everywhere.

All costs are in euros (EUR).

## Before you go: the admin

### Visas and borders — the short version

The Schengen rules are covered in depth in our [Schengen visa
guide](/guides/schengen-visa-how-it-works/). The essentials: ~60
nationalities enter visa-free (with the **EES biometric border** now
live — allow extra time at first entry — and the **€20 ETIAS**
authorisation expected from late 2026); Schengen-visa passports
(including India) apply with the usual file — note **Spain's
applications run through BLS International** (not VFS), fee €90 +
service charges, and appointments in peak season need 4–6 weeks of lead.
Proof of funds and confirmed bookings remain the make-or- break
documents.

### Money and the small print

- Cards everywhere; carry €50 in coins-and-small-notes for
  markets and tapas counters.
- Tipping: rounding up is plenty — service is included.
- **Tourist taxes are real in Barcelona** (~€5–8 per person per
  night after 2026's Catalonia increase, collected by the hotel)
  and smaller elsewhere — budget them, don't be surprised by them.
- **The pickpocket honesty** (Barcelona edition): Las Ramblas, the
  metro, and the Boqueria are Europe's premier league. Phones off
  tables, zips forward, nothing in back pockets — that's the whole
  defence, and it works.

### The Spanish clock — read this or eat alone

Spain runs two hours later than you do: lunch 14:00–16:00, dinner
from 21:00 (Seville later still). Restaurants at 19:00 are either
closed or tourist traps — the **menú del día** (a full lunch for
€13–18) is the country's great bargain and the schedule's reward.
Siesta closures (roughly 14:00–17:00 for small shops) are real
outside the big-city centres.

### Trains — the price war is your friend

- **Three operators** compete on the main lines: **Renfe AVE**
  (most frequent), **iryo** (newest trains, good food) and
  **Ouigo** (from €9, bags charged). Compare all three on
  Trainline/Omio; book when sales open (~4 months) for the €9–35
  fares.
- Barcelona Sants → Madrid Atocha: **~2h30–3h**. Madrid → Seville:
  **~2h30**. Security X-ray before boarding — arrive 20–30 min
  early.
- **Fly open-jaw**: into Barcelona, home from Seville (or via a
  cheap Seville routing through Madrid) — no backtracking.

### SIM, apps, kids

- EU eSIMs ~€10–20 for the trip; metro apps: TMB (Barcelona),
  Metro de Madrid; taxis: FreeNow/Cabify beat street-hailing.
- **Kids:** Spain adores them and keeps their hours — children in
  restaurants at 22:00 is culture, not crisis. Park Güell,
  Barceloneta sand, Retiro's rowboats and the Alcázar's peacocks
  are the child-anchors; the trains are nap machines.

## Day 1 — Land in Barcelona, Gothic first

Stay in the **Eixample** (Gaudí's grid, broad and calm) or the
**Gothic Quarter/El Born** (medieval, lively, noisier).

- **Afternoon** — Land at BCN (direct Gulf links daily); Aerobús
  or metro in. Check in, vermút o'clock.
- **17:30** — First walk: the **Gothic Quarter** — the cathedral
  and its cloister of thirteen geese, Plaça Sant Felip Neri's
  scarred quiet, the Roman walls — drifting into **El Born**.
- **20:30** — First tapas crawl, El Born style: standing room,
  anchovies, pan con tomate, one bar per course (~€40–60 for two).

**Where to stay in Barcelona** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Majestic Hotel & Spa** — Passeig de Gràcia grande
  dame with a Gaudí-view rooftop.
- *Mid-range:* **H10 Madison** — superbly rated by the cathedral;
  rooftop pool.
- *Budget:* **Hotel Acta Antibes** — clean, metro-close, honest.

## Day 2 — Gaudí day: the finished masterpiece

- **9:00** — **Sagrada Família** at opening (book on the official
  site 2+ weeks ahead in season — peak dates sell out 7–14 days
  out; official entry with audio guide from ~€26, tower add-on
  ~€36 total; shoulders/knees covered). Stand under the forest
  nave, then outside for the new skyline fact: **the Tower of
  Jesus Christ, completed February 2026** — you're looking at the
  world's tallest church in its first year of wholeness. Two
  hours minimum.
- **12:00** — Walk Gaudí's **Passeig de Gràcia**: Casa Batlló and
  La Pedrera facades (interiors optional and pricey — €35+;
  the street view is free and half the joy). Menú del día lunch
  nearby.
- **15:30** — **Park Güell** (timed entry, ~€13 official — book) —
  the mosaic terrace, the dragon stair, the porticoed paths;
  late-afternoon light flatters it and the crowds thin.
- **18:30** — Golden hour option: continue uphill to the
  **Bunkers del Carmel** viewpoint (free) — all Barcelona to the
  sea.
- **21:00** — Dinner in Gràcia's plazas — the neighbourhood above
  the park stays local.

## Day 3 — Markets, sea and Montjuïc

- **9:00** — **La Boqueria market** off Las Ramblas early, before
  the crowds (juice, jamón, breakfast counters) — then walk the
  **Ramblas** once for the theatre of it, valuables forward.
- **11:00** — **Picasso Museum** (El Born; book) or the Palau de
  la Música's facade-and-foyer; snack through El Born's counters.
- **13:30** — **Barceloneta**: paella or fideuà by the sand (pick
  rice places one street back from the beachfront for honesty),
  toes in the Mediterranean after.
- **16:30** — **Montjuïc**: cable car or funicular up for castle
  ramparts and harbour views; MNAC's steps at sunset are the
  city's grand balcony. (The Magic Fountain's evening show runs a
  reduced schedule these years — check current status.)
- **21:00** — Dinner in Poble-sec — the Carrer de Blai
  pintxos street runs €2-a-bite democracy.

## Day 4 — Montserrat, or your Barcelona

- **The recommended out-of-town:** **Montserrat** — the serrated
  holy mountain an hour out (R5 train + rack railway or cable
  car; combo tickets ~€25pp): the Black Madonna basilica,
  boys'-choir midday singing (schedule varies), and ridge walks
  above the monastery. Half-day to mid-afternoon.
- **The stay-in-town alternatives:** Casa Batlló's interior done
  properly, the Gothic Quarter's museums, or Camp Nou's
  post-renovation tour for the football pilgrims (check tour
  status).
- **18:00** — Back for a slow evening: vermút in Sant Antoni,
  pack for the morning train.

## Day 5 — The AVE to Madrid, capital evening

- **9:30** — **Barcelona Sants → Madrid Atocha** (~2h45; from
  €9–35 booked early across Renfe/iryo/Ouigo). Zaragoza and
  Aragón's badlands out the window.
- **13:00** — Check in near Sol or the museum mile; menú del día.
- **15:30** — **Retiro Park**: rowboats on the lake, the Crystal
  Palace, buskers and book stalls.
- **18:00** — **Puerta del Sol → Plaza Mayor** walk, then the
  **Mercado de San Miguel** for a standing dinner of everything
  (~€35–50 for two, touristy and worth it once).
- **21:00** — Sunset from the **Círculo de Bellas Artes rooftop**
  (~€6) or the **Templo de Debod** lawns (free) — Madrid's two
  best evening views.

**Where to stay in Madrid** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Palace (Luxury Collection)** — the belle-époque
  dome across from the Prado.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Liabeny** — superbly located between Sol
  and Gran Vía.
- *Budget:* **SLEEP'N Atocha** — 4.8-rated design-budget by the
  station and museums.

## Day 6 — Royal Madrid and the Prado

- **9:30** — **Royal Palace** (book; ~€15) — Europe's largest
  royal palace by floor area: the armoury and the staircase are
  the showstoppers. Almudena Cathedral next door, free.
- **12:30** — **La Latina** lunch: the Cava Baja street of
  tabernas — Madrid's huevos rotos and cocido belong here.
- **15:00** — **The Prado** (€15, timed; free-entry evenings exist
  Mon–Sat 18:00–20:00 but run 60–90-minute queues — pay for the
  afternoon instead): Velázquez's Las Meninas, Goya light and
  dark, Bosch's Garden. Three hours vanish.
- **19:00** — Decompress on Calle de las Huertas' literary
  pavements.
- **21:30** — Dinner in Barrio de las Letras; nightcap churros at
  **San Ginés** (since 1894, open till late) because Madrid.

## Day 7 — Toledo day, Guernica evening

- **8:50** — Avant train from Atocha to **Toledo** (33 min;
  ~€22–28 return — book the slots, they're specific). The whole
  walled city on its rock is the sight.
- **9:45** — Old Toledo on foot: the **Cathedral** (~€12 —
  Spain's Gothic high-water mark), the El Greco trail (Santo Tomé's
  Burial of the Count of Orgaz, €4), the synagogues and mosque of
  the "city of three cultures", marzipan from cloistered nuns.
- **14:00** — Lunch with a view or carcamusas in a taberna.
- **16:30** — Taxi or city bus to the **Mirador del Valle** — the
  postcard of Toledo across the gorge — then train back.
- **19:00** — **Reina Sofía** on the free evening window (typically
  19:00–21:00; closed Tuesdays; otherwise €12): one artwork makes
  the visit — **Picasso's Guernica**, which needs the standing
  time. The perfect capital finale.
- **21:30** — Last Madrid dinner; pack.

## Day 8 — South on the AVE: first Seville evening

- **10:00** — **Madrid → Seville** (~2h30; from €20–40 early) —
  Castile's plains give way to olive geometry.
- **13:00** — Check in around Santa Cruz; the south's schedule
  begins: long lunch, shaded siesta.
- **17:00** — **Plaza de España** (free) — the 1929 half-moon of
  bridges, boats and tiled provinces — then María Luisa Park's
  fountains.
- **19:30** — **Las Setas (Metropol Parasol)** rooftop (~€16,
  includes the light-show hour at dusk) — the wooden waffle over
  the old city.
- **21:30** — Tapas the Seville way: standing, sherry-adjacent,
  espinacas con garbanzos and solomillo al whisky (~€35–50 for
  two across two or three bars).

**Where to stay in Seville** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Hotel Alfonso XIII** — the 1929 Andalusian palace
  hotel; even non-guests come for the courtyard bar.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Fernando III** — Santa Cruz location,
  rooftop pool, breakfast included.
- *Budget:* **Exe Sevilla Macarena** — plain, well-connected,
  rooftop pool for the summer survival.

## Day 9 — The Alcázar, the Giralda and flamenco

- **9:30** — **Real Alcázar** at opening (€15.50 — **book days-to-
  weeks ahead**; free Monday-evening slots exist but vanish
  instantly): the Mudéjar courtyards, the gardens and their
  peacocks, a thousand years of layered palace. Two-plus hours.
- **12:30** — **Cathedral + Giralda** (€13 online; Mon–Sat from
  10:45, Sundays afternoon-only) — the world's largest Gothic
  cathedral, Columbus's tomb, and the minaret-turned-belltower
  climbed by ramps, not stairs (horses once rode up).
- **14:30** — Long Santa Cruz lunch in a shaded courtyard.
- **17:30** — Cross the river to **Triana**: the ceramics
  workshops, the market on the bridge's far side, the tilework
  streets where flamenco families still live.
- **20:30** — **Flamenco, properly**: a small tablao or cultural
  centre show (€25–45; the Flamenco Dance Museum's nightly show is
  the reliable central pick, the Triana peñas the deeper cut).
  An hour of it rearranges you.
- **22:00** — Last dinner, latest of the trip — this is Seville.

## Day 10 — Fly home from Seville

- **Morning** — Final orange-scented walk, breakfast churros
  con chocolate, then **SVQ** (20 min by taxi/EA bus) and home via
  Madrid or a European hub. Triangle closed, no side repeated.

## What to pack

- Walking shoes for cobbles and Gaudí queues; 15,000-step days
  are standard.
- Modest-cover layer for the Sagrada Família, Montserrat and
  Toledo's cathedral (shoulders/knees).
- A summer siesta plan: hat, refillable bottle, and acceptance
  that 14:00–17:00 in Seville is for shade.
- Swimwear (Barceloneta, rooftop pools), light scarf, sunglasses
  non-negotiable.
- Crossbody bag that zips — see the pickpocket paragraph.
- Appetite scheduling: arrive hungry at 14:00 and 21:00, never at
  19:00.

## FAQ

**Is 10 days enough for three cities?**
Yes — the AVE makes the transfers 2.5-hour naps, not travel days.
The route deliberately weights Barcelona (4) over Seville (2);
reverse that weighting in winter, when the south is the star.

**Which is the sellout risk — really?**
Two tickets rule the trip: **Sagrada Família** (7–14 days ahead in
season, longer for towers) and **Seville's Alcázar** (same). Book
those the day your dates fix; everything else forgives
procrastination.

**Barcelona pickpockets — how bad?**
The theft capital of Europe by volume, and almost entirely
preventable: it's distraction lifting, not violence. Zipped
crossbody, phone in hand or pocket-front, extra care on metro
line 3 and the Ramblas. Travellers who follow that report nothing
worse than paella prices.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Trickier than France: Spanish classics lean pork hard. But every
city has a halal scene (Madrid's Lavapiés, Barcelona's Raval,
Seville near the mosque), seafood is everywhere and unambiguous,
and vegetarians live well on tortilla, gazpacho/salmorejo, pisto,
espinacas con garbanzos and market produce — say "sin jamón" early
and often.

**Add Granada and the Alhambra?**
The famous extension: Seville → Granada is 2.5 h (train/bus), the
**Alhambra needs booking ~4–8 weeks ahead**, and it deserves a
full day plus a night in the Albaicín. As a +2-day add-on to this
route it's superb — as a squeezed day-trip, a regret. It's first
in line for our second Spain guide.

**Train or fly between the cities?**
Train, no contest: city-centre stations, 20-minute check-ins,
€9–35 fares, luggage free (Ouigo excepted). Flying this triangle
in 2026 is a hobby, not a strategy.
