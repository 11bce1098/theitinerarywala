New Zealand is the trip your camera roll has been training
for: five million people, thirty million sheep, and scenery
that changes genre every two hours' drive — subtropical
harbours, steaming volcanic valleys, movie-set hills, glacial
lakes the colour of blue milk, and a fiord so dramatic it
makes grown adults go quiet.

It's also the **longest haul on this site** — there is nowhere
on earth it is a short flight from — which is exactly why
this guide does both islands in one go: if you're crossing
that much planet, you collect the whole set. **Auckland →
Hobbiton → Rotorua**, then fly south for **Queenstown → Milford
Sound → Lake Tekapo/Mt Cook → Christchurch**.

Costs are in New Zealand dollars — figure **{{NZD 4250}} per
person (≈ AED 9,300 / ₹2.25 lakh)** excluding international
flights. Remember the hemisphere flip: **NZ summer is
December–February** (peak prices, school-holiday crowds);
**September–November spring** is the value sweet spot —
waterfalls at full blast, lambs everywhere, snow still on the
peaks behind Queenstown.

## Before you go: the admin

- **Visa first, everything else second — but check which one
  you need.** Around 60 nationalities travel visa-free on an
  **NZeTA** (NZD 17–23 online, approved in minutes to days).
  Everyone else, India included, needs a **Visitor Visa —
  NZD 441 all-in**, including the NZD 100 tourism levy,
  applied **online at immigration.govt.nz**. Official
  processing is **80% inside 2 weeks**, but allow 4–6 weeks
  in peak season and apply the moment flights are pencilled.
  Proof of funds and ties comes from wherever you live — bank
  statements and an employment letter. Multiple-entry visitor
  visas allow generous stays (6–9 months), so one visa can
  cover a future trip too.
- **Onward ticket + funds** (~NZD 1,000/month guideline) —
  standard file: bank statements, employment letter,
  hotel bookings.
- **Your own licence is legal here for up to 12 months**,
  provided it is in English — if it isn't, you need an
  accredited translation or an International Driving Permit.
  Carry an IDP regardless; rental desks like seeing it and it
  ends every argument.
- **Biosecurity is not a joke here**: declare ALL food,
  honey, seeds, wooden items — even the snacks in your bag. Fines start at NZD 400, and "I forgot" is not
  a defence. When in doubt, tick yes.
- **Nearly every route in connects** — through Australia,
  Southeast Asia or the US west coast, depending where you
  start. Whatever the routing, book it as **"multi-city: into
  AKL, out of CHC"**: it costs little more than a return and
  saves a day of backtracking at the end.

## The driving truth (read before you rent)

New Zealand's roads are beautiful, safe — and **slow**.
Google's "3 hours" means 3 hours of winding two-lane road
at 80 km/h behind a campervan, so **plan 60–70 km/h
averages**, drive left, and learn the two local
specialties: **one-lane bridges** (the arrow pointing your
way has right of way) and the merciless UV (sunscreen ON
for driving days — you burn through glass here). Fuel runs
~NZD 2.70–3/litre. Campervans look romantic and cost
NZD 250–450/day plus holiday-park fees — for this two-week
shape, **car + motels is cheaper and warmer**, which is
how we've budgeted it.

## Day 1 — Land in Auckland

- **Morning** — Clear the biosecurity beagles, pick up
  the eSIM (or airport SIM, ~NZD 40), SkyDrive/taxi into
  the city. No car yet — Auckland central is walkable
  and parking is robbery.
- **13:00** — Britomart and the **Viaduct Harbour**:
  lunch by the superyachts (~NZD 25–35), then the
  ferry-building waterfront.
- **16:00** — **Sky Tower** (~NZD 45): 328 m of
  orientation — both harbours, volcano cones on the
  horizon, glass floors underfoot.
- **19:00** — Dinner on **Federal Street** under the
  tower, or — trust us — **Sandringham**, Auckland's
  little India, where the butter chicken debate is
  settled nightly (halal signs everywhere, mains
  NZD 18–26).

**Where to stay in Auckland** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Park Hyatt Auckland** — 4.7/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~1,300 reviews), on the Viaduct water.
  ~NZD 700+.
- *Mid-range:* **Cordis Auckland** — 4.5/5 (~3,800
  reviews, the city's #1 "Best Value"), big rooms and a
  famous club lounge. ~NZD 260–340.
- *Value:* **M Social, Viaduct Harbour** — 4.7/5
  (~1,400 reviews), harbour-front rooms at motel money.
  ~NZD 180–240.

## Day 2 — Auckland: volcano, ferry, beaches

- **08:30** — Walk up **Mt Eden** (Maungawhakahika)
  — a grassy volcanic crater with the city poured
  around it; free, 30 minutes, best light of the day.
- **11:00** — Ferry to **Devonport** (~NZD 15
  return): gelato, Victorian villas, North Head's
  tunnels and harbour views.
- **15:00** — Pick between the **Auckland Museum**
  (Māori taonga + the daily cultural performance,
  ~NZD 32) or a west-coast run to **Piha's** black-sand
  surf if you've rented a day car.
- **19:30** — Wynyard Quarter dinner; early night —
  road trip starts tomorrow.

## Day 3 — Hobbiton, then the sulphur city

- **08:00** — Collect the rental, roll south through
  Waikato dairy country (2 h) to **Matamata**.
- **11:00** — **Hobbiton™ Movie Set — the Signature
  Tour: NZD 130 adult, youth (11–17) NZD 65, under-11s
  free**, 2.5 hours through all 44 hobbit holes, Bag
  End up the hill, and a **free drink at the Green
  Dragon Inn** at the end. It is exactly as delightful
  as you hope, even for non-fans. **Book weeks-to-months
  ahead** — slots open 10 months out and prime times
  vanish.
- **14:30** — On to **Rotorua** (1 h) — you'll smell
  it before you see it (geothermal sulphur; you stop
  noticing by dinner).
- **16:00** — Walk **Kuirau Park's** free bubbling mud
  pools and steaming fences — this city leaks.
- **19:00** — Eat Streat's covered strip for dinner
  (~NZD 30–40 pp), feet warmed by geothermal pavement
  heating.

**Where to stay in Rotorua** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Pullman Rotorua** — 4.2/5 (~450 reviews),
  the polished big-hotel option, breakfast included.
  ~NZD 220–280.
- *Mid-range:* **Regent of Rotorua** — 4.6/5 (~1,900
  reviews), boutique charm plus mineral pool.
  ~NZD 180–240.
- *Value:* **Aura Accommodation** — 4.8/5 (~930
  reviews) and the genuinely Rotorua trick: **your own
  geothermal hot pools** at motel prices. ~NZD 140–190.

## Day 4 — Geysers, Māori culture, a floating forest

- **09:00** — **Te Puia** (~NZD 95 adult): the
  **Pōhutu geyser** firing 30 m on its own schedule,
  boiling mud, the national **Māori carving and weaving
  schools**, and a kiwi conservation house (your one
  near-guaranteed kiwi sighting). Guided entry makes
  it — take the tour, not just the gate ticket.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then the **Redwoods Treewalk**
  (~NZD 40) — suspension bridges through a Californian
  redwood canopy — or **Wai-O-Tapu's** Champagne Pool
  (30 min south, ~NZD 45) if colours beat trees.
- **17:00** — **Polynesian Spa** (~NZD 60–90):
  lakeside alkaline hot pools that have been easing
  road-trip shoulders since 1878.
- **19:00** — If it's in budget, swap tonight's dinner
  for a **hāngī + cultural evening** (Te Pā Tū or Te
  Puia's Te Pō, ~NZD 150–200) — earth-oven feast,
  haka, and the reason Rotorua is the Māori culture
  capital.

## Day 5 — Fly south into the mountains

- **09:00** — Drop the car at Rotorua Airport and fly
  to **Queenstown** (one quick stop, ~NZD 250–350 —
  book with the internationals; or drive back to
  Auckland for the direct). **Window seat, left side
  landing**: the approach over the Remarkables is
  routinely ranked the world's most scenic.
- **14:00** — Check in central; walk the lakefront —
  Lake Wakatipu is absurdly clear and the town is
  15 minutes end to end.
- **16:30** — **Skyline Gondola + 3 luge rides**
  (~NZD 85): up to Bob's Peak for THE Queenstown
  postcard, then gravity karting above it. Kids
  (and you) will demand extra rides.
- **19:30** — The **Fergburger pilgrimage** (~NZD 17,
  queue 20–40 min, worth exactly one visit) or
  lakefront dinner without the queue.

**Where to stay in Queenstown** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Rees Hotel & Lakeside Apartments** —
  4.5/5 (~2,800 reviews), full-kitchen suites over the
  lake — built for families. ~NZD 450+.
- *Mid-range:* **Novotel Queenstown Lakeside** — 4.2/5
  (~4,100 reviews, the town's review heavyweight),
  unbeatable steps-from-everything corner. ~NZD 260–340.
- *Value:* **Melbourne Lodge B&B** — 4.3/5 (~490
  reviews), five minutes uphill, half the price.
  ~NZD 150–200.

## Day 6 — Milford Sound: the big day

- **06:45** — The **Milford Sound coach + cruise**
  day (RealNZ from **NZD 292**; smaller operators
  ~NZD 200–260): 5 hours each way on one of Earth's
  great roads — mirror lakes, the Homer Tunnel hewn
  through a mountain, kea parrots dismantling
  someone's aerial — then **2 hours cruising under
  Mitre Peak**, waterfalls falling 150 m onto the
  boat's bow.
- **The rain truth:** Milford gets ~200 rain days a
  year — and is arguably **better in rain**, when a
  thousand temporary waterfalls switch on. Don't
  cancel for weather; cancel only for a closed road
  (winter chains days).
- **Shortcut for the coach-averse:** fly-cruise-fly
  from Queenstown (~NZD 500–600, 4 h door to door) —
  scenic beyond reason, weather-dependent.
- **20:00** — Back by dark; takeaway and collapse.
  (Sandflies at Milford are a biting welcome
  committee: repellent ON before you step off the
  bus.)

## Day 7 — Adrenaline (or Glenorchy serenity)

- **Option A — the full Queenstown:** watch the
  **Kawarau Bridge bungy** (world's first commercial
  jump site — jumping from ~NZD 320, watching free
  and nearly as entertaining), then **Shotover Jet**
  canyon spins (~NZD 189) or the **TSS Earnslaw**
  steamship + Walter Peak farm show for the
  gentler crew.
- **Option B — the road to Glenorchy** (45 min of
  lake-edge tarmac that outranks most destinations):
  wharf photo, Paradise valley (actual name; LOTR
  location), easy lagoon boardwalk, pies at Mrs
  Woolly's. Rent a day car (~NZD 90) or join a
  half-day tour.
- **18:00** — **Onsen Hot Pools** if pre-booked weeks
  ago (private cliff-edge tubs over the Shotover,
  ~NZD 175/2 people) — Queenstown's best "we earned
  this".

## Day 8 — Arrowtown and a breather

- **09:30** — **Arrowtown** (20 min): gold-rush
  streets, the restored **Chinese miners' settlement**
  (free, quietly moving), pastry-fuelled riverside
  stroll where they panned gold — hire a pan
  (~NZD 15) and let the kids find flecks.
- **13:00** — Winery lunch in **Gibbston Valley**
  (mains ~NZD 35, tastings ~NZD 15 — famous pinot
  noir; zero judgement for juice) or lakefront picnic
  from the supermarket.
- **15:30** — Free afternoon: Queenstown Gardens'
  frisbee golf, lake swim for the brave, gear
  re-pack. Collect the South Island car this evening
  or at 8 tomorrow.
- **19:30** — Farewell-to-Queenstown dinner on the
  pier.

## Day 9 — Over the Lindis to Aoraki / Mt Cook

- **08:30** — Drive north (3 h 15 via the **Lindis
  Pass's** tussock moonscape, coffee in Cromwell's
  old town or under Omarama's merino statues).
- **12:30** — Lunch at **Lake Pukaki's** salmon shop
  or your boot lid — the lake is unphotoshoppable
  glacial blue with **Aoraki/Mt Cook** (3,724 m,
  NZ's highest) floating at the far end.
- **14:30** — **Hooker Valley Track**: the country's
  best easy walk — 3 hours return, dead flat, three
  swing bridges, ending at an iceberg lake under the
  south face. **Free**, like all NZ national parks.
- **19:00** — Dinner and dark-sky stargazing — the
  entire Mackenzie Basin is an International Dark Sky
  Reserve; step outside and look up.

**Where to stay, Mt Cook/Tekapo nights:** **The
Hermitage** (Mt Cook village's landmark, mountain-view
wings), **Peppers Bluewater Resort** or **Lake Tekapo
Village Motel** in Tekapo, or the holiday parks' motel
units — tiny-inventory towns: **book these two nights
first after flights**, and check current reviews.

## Day 10 — Lake Tekapo: church, hot pools, stars

- **09:30** — Short hop to **Lake Tekapo** (1 h 15);
  coffee stop at Pukaki's viewpoint because you
  physically can't not.
- **11:00** — **Church of the Good Shepherd**: the
  tiny 1935 stone chapel whose altar window frames
  the lake — NZ's most photographed building
  (exterior always open; be gentle at service
  times). Lupins riot purple-pink in
  November–December.
- **14:00** — **Mt John summit walk** (2–3 h loop
  through the observatory domes; café with the
  360° at the top) — or drive up (~NZD 8).
- **17:00** — **Tekapo Springs** hot pools
  (~NZD 42) as the alpenglow show starts.
- **21:30** — **Dark Sky Project** observatory tour
  (~NZD 120–190, book ahead) — or the free version:
  lakeshore, blanket, the Milky Way core with the
  naked eye. Southern-hemisphere sky, so even the
  stars are new.

## Day 11 — To Christchurch

- **09:00** — Cross the Canterbury Plains (3 h) to
  **Christchurch**; drop the one-way rental
  (~NZD 100 drop fee, budgeted).
- **13:00** — The rebuilt city walk: the **Cardboard
  Cathedral**, the 185 white chairs earthquake
  memorial (quiet; take the moment), then **punting
  on the Avon** (~NZD 40) or the free **Botanic
  Gardens**.
- **16:00** — Ride the historic tram loop or the
  **Christchurch Gondola** (~NZD 45) for the
  Port Hills goodbye view.
- **19:00** — Last supper at **Riverside Market** —
  every NZ food group under one roof, souvenir mānuka
  honey from the supermarket next door (declare it
  nowhere — it's OUTBOUND, and half the price of the
  airport).

## Day 12 — Fly home

- International departures from Christchurch connect
  through Australia or Southeast Asia for most of the
  world. Be there 3 h early, board with a camera roll
  that needs its own visa.

## What to pack

- **Layers over jackets**: four seasons a day is
  the national weather forecast. Thermal base +
  fleece + rain shell beats one big coat.
- Sunscreen SPF 50 and sunglasses — NZ's UV is among
  the world's harshest, and the hole in the ozone layer
  is not folklore. You will burn faster than you expect.
- **Insect repellent for Milford/Fiordland**
  (sandflies) — non-negotiable.
- Walking shoes with tread (Hooker Valley, Mt John),
  swimwear for hot pools, warm hat for stargazing
  even in summer.
- Type I plugs (same as Australia — angled pins).
- **Zero food in the luggage inbound.** Seriously.
  The beagle always wins.

## FAQ

**Is New Zealand doable without driving at all?**
Mostly, at a cost: InterCity buses link the route
and Queenstown works car-free (tours pick up
centrally), but the Mackenzie leg (Pukaki, Hooker
Valley, Tekapo) is built for a car and thin on
tours. If driving's out, swap those two nights for
more Queenstown + a Tekapo day tour from
Christchurch. But NZ roads are the easiest
right-hand-drive introduction there is — quiet,
polite, brilliantly signed.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Comfortably easy. Auckland's Sandringham and
Dominion Road are halal-restaurant districts;
Rotorua and Queenstown both keep multiple Indian
kitchens (Queenstown's curry scene is a post-bungy
institution); supermarkets label clearly and
motels' kitchenettes cover the rest. Vegetarians
are mainstream — every café has real options, not
one sad risotto.

**Hobbiton with someone who's never seen the
films?**
Still yes. It's a beautifully absurd garden village
with a free drink at the end — the craft
(real vegetable plots, tiny washing lines,
forced-perspective doors) lands without a single
frame of Frodo. Film fans should also grab
Glenorchy's Paradise valley on Day 7.

**Milford or the fly-over? What if it rains?**
Do the cruise regardless of forecast — rain
multiplies the waterfalls and the mist is pure
cinema. The coach day is long (12 h); under-6s and
motion-sensitive travellers should weigh the
fly-cruise-fly. Only winter road closures are a
real cancel.

**Queenstown adrenaline with kids or
grandparents?**
The town scales beautifully: luge, gondola,
Earnslaw steamship, Arrowtown gold panning and
Glenorchy work for ages 4–84; bungy and Shotover
Jet have minimums (10+ and height rules) and
watching is genuinely great sport. Nothing on this
itinerary requires courage — only the option of
it.

**What's New Zealand guide #2?**
**The South Island deep-cut**: the West Coast
glaciers, Wanaka's tree, Kaikōura's whales and the
TranzAlpine train — the 10-day loop for people who
finished this trip already planning the return. On
the list.
