Prague is what Europe looked like before the twentieth
century happened to it. The wars that flattened Warsaw and
burned Dresden skipped this city almost entirely, so a
thousand years of architecture stand shoulder to shoulder —
Gothic spires, baroque domes, art-nouveau facades — above a
river with swans and a castle that glows like a theatre set
every night of the year.

It's also the sweet spot of European travel maths: **Emirates
flies Dubai–Prague non-stop (~6½ h)**, the country uses its
own **koruna (CZK)** instead of the euro — meaning dinner
costs two-thirds of Paris — and one **Czech Schengen visa**
opens the whole zone.

The shape: **Prague (5 nights)** with a bone-church day trip to
**Kutná Hora**, then **one night in Český Krumlov**, the tiny
river-loop town that becomes pure fairy tale after the day-trippers
leave at five. Figure **{{CZK 23500}} per person** including the
visa, excluding flights. **Go April–June or September–October**;
December is Christmas-market magic (that's a whole other guide),
July– August is lovely and mobbed.

## Before you go: the admin

- - **Schengen visa, Czech flavour:** same VFS drill as our
  [France](/itineraries/france-9-days-guide/),
  [Italy](/itineraries/italy-10-days-guide/) and
  [Germany](/itineraries/germany-10-days-guide/) guides — apply
  through **your nearest VFS Global centre for the Czech Republic**
  as your main-stay country: **€90** plus a service fee, biometrics
  in person, residence valid 3+ months beyond return, bank
  statements, employment letter, full hotel + flight itinerary and
  **€30,000 travel insurance**. Standard turnaround runs to **15
  calendar days** — apply 4–6 weeks out, longer before summer.
- **Currency truth:** the Czech Republic is **not a euro
  country**. Some shops "accept" euros at robbery rates —
  pay in koruna, by card (tap works everywhere), and when
  a card machine offers to charge you in AED/EUR, **always
  choose CZK** (that "convenience" costs 5–10%).
- **The exchange-office warning** (Prague's one classic
  hustle): street bureaux advertising "0% commission" bury
  a terrible rate. Rule: **withdraw from bank ATMs
  (decline conversion) or pay by card; never change cash
  at tourist-strip counters.**
- **Taxis:** hail nothing. **Uber/Bolt** are cheap and
  honest; the Airport Express bus (~CZK 100) and metro
  do the rest.

## Day 1 — Land, meet the Old Town

- **Afternoon** — Emirates' daytime rotation lands
  mid-afternoon. Airport bus/Uber in (30–40 min),
  check in, out before golden hour.
- **17:00** — **Old Town Square**: the Týn Church's
  witch-hat towers, pastel palaces, and the
  **Astronomical Clock's** hourly show — honest
  review: 45 seconds of medieval puppet theatre; watch
  once from the back of the crowd (wallets front —
  this is pickpocket centre court), then climb the
  **Old Town Hall tower** (~CZK 325) for the red-roof
  panorama instead. That's the real show.
- **19:30** — First Czech tavern dinner: svíčková
  (beef in cream sauce with dumplings) or goulash,
  ~CZK 250–380, and the national institution — Pilsner
  tanková, **CZK 60–75 a half-litre, cheaper than the
  water** and better poured than anywhere on Earth.

**Where to stay in Prague** (five nights)

- *Luxury:* **Aria Hotel** — 4.9/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~2,200 reviews), music-themed rooms in Malá Strana
  with a rooftop facing the castle. ~CZK 9,000+.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Hastal Prague Old Town** —
  4.6/5 (~2,000 reviews), family-run quiet corner
  five minutes from the square. ~CZK 3,200–4,300.
  (Design darling: Mosaic House, 4.9/~2,600.)
- *Value:* **ibis Praha Old Town** — 4.2/5 (~4,100
  reviews), plain, spotless, unbeatable placement.
  ~CZK 2,200–2,800.

## Day 2 — Castle day, done the smart way

- **08:30** — Tram 22 up the hill (the classic
  move — ride it like a tour bus; it IS the
  pickpockets' favourite line, so bags zipped).
- **09:00** — **Prague Castle at opening** (Circuit
  A/basic ticket **CZK 250**: St Vitus Cathedral, Old
  Royal Palace, St George's Basilica, Golden Lane;
  grounds and courtyards free 6:00–22:00). At 9 am
  **St Vitus is nearly empty** and the stained glass
  (including Mucha's window) burns in morning light;
  by 10:30 the groups arrive and the magic halves.
- **11:30** — **Golden Lane's** doll-sized houses
  (Kafka wrote at No. 22), then down the castle
  steps into **Malá Strana**: baroque lanes, the
  **Wallenstein Garden's** free peacocks-and-grotto
  weirdness, lunch in a cellar tavern (~CZK 250).
- **15:00** — **Kampa Island** and the Lennon Wall,
  coffee by the millwheel.
- **18:00** — **Petřín**: funicular up (city transit
  ticket), the mini-Eiffel **lookout tower**
  (~CZK 200; 299 steps) for sunset over a city of a
  hundred spires. Mirror maze for anyone under 12
  or over 60.
- **20:00** — Dinner in Malá Strana; the castle
  floodlights switch on and the whole hill goes
  golden.

## Day 3 — Dawn on the bridge, the Jewish Quarter

- **06:30** — The alarm that pays for the trip:
  **Charles Bridge at dawn** — 30 statues, mist on
  the Vltava, swans, and nearly nobody. By 09:00
  it's a procession; at 06:30 it's yours. (Rub the
  plaque at St John of Nepomuk; wish accordingly.)
- **09:30** — Breakfast, then **Josefov, the Jewish
  Quarter** (combined ticket ~CZK 550): the
  **Old Jewish Cemetery's** 12,000 tilted stones
  stacked twelve deep, the Spanish Synagogue's
  Moorish gold, and the **Pinkas Synagogue** —
  walls hand-written with 78,000 names. Quietly
  devastating; give it the morning it deserves.
- **13:00** — Lunch at a New Town lunch-menu spot
  (the "polední menu" — ~CZK 180–260 for two
  courses — is the local economy's gift to
  travellers).
- **15:00** — Free-range hours: Náplavka riverbank
  (Saturday farmers' market), the upside-down horse
  in Lucerna Passage, or shopping for Bohemian
  crystal (Moser for the real thing; anything
  street-side is Chinese glass — honesty as usual).
- **19:00** — **Vltava evening cruise** (~CZK
  400–500/hour): bridges and the lit castle from
  water level. Cheesy, lovely, worth it once.

## Day 4 — Kutná Hora: the bone church

- **08:37** — Direct train from Praha hl.n.
  (~CZK 180–240, ~1 h — go **weekday morning**; the
  bus groups land mid-morning and leave by 14:00).
  Local shuttle-train or 20-min walk to Sedlec.
- **10:00** — The **Sedlec Ossuary**: the remains of
  some 40,000–60,000 people arranged — chandelier,
  coat of arms, garlands — by a 19th-century
  woodcarver into the most sincerely strange
  church in Europe (**CZK 120, or the CZK 250
  combo** with the cathedral). It manages to be
  contemplative rather than ghoulish; kids
  generally find it "the coolest thing all week".
- **12:00** — Walk or hop into town for lunch on
  the medieval square (~CZK 220).
- **14:00** — **St Barbara's Cathedral** (combo
  ticket): the miners' Gothic masterpiece — silver
  from these hills funded half of Bohemia — with
  frescoes of coin-minting where saints usually
  go. The terrace view over vineyards is the
  bonus.
- **17:00** — Train back; evening at a Letná or
  Riegrovy Sady **beer garden** — sunset over all
  of Prague with the locals, ~CZK 65 a glass.

## Day 5 — Vyšehrad, New Town, and Prague at your pace

- **09:30** — **Vyšehrad** fortress (free): the
  quiet other castle — ramparts above the river,
  the Slavín cemetery where Dvořák and Mucha rest,
  and Prague's best picnic lawns.
- **12:30** — Lunch, then the **Dancing House**
  photo and a walk up **Wenceslas Square** — 1918,
  1968 and 1989 all happened on this boulevard;
  the museum-café balconies overlook it all.
- **15:00** — Pick your Prague: the Mucha Museum
  (~CZK 380), the Klementinum library tour, or
  simply the lanes of Nové Město with a chimney
  cake — and here's the site's beloved honesty:
  **trdelník is an Instagram-era import, not an
  old Czech tradition. Eat it anyway; it's
  delicious. Just don't call it heritage.**
- **19:00** — Farewell-to-Prague dinner: a classic
  cellar (U Modré Kachničky duck) or modern Czech
  (Eska) — ~CZK 500–900 pp at the top end.
- **21:00** — Jazz at Reduta or AghaRTA (genuinely
  good; the ubiquitous "black-light theatre" is
  tourist kitsch — skipping it is a service this
  guide happily performs).

## Day 6 — Český Krumlov: stay past five

- **09:00** — **RegioJet/FlixBus to Český Krumlov**
  (~3 h, **CZK 200–350** — book seats a few days
  ahead). Drop bags at your pension inside the
  river loop.
- **13:00** — The town is a UNESCO miniature: a
  Vltava horseshoe wrapped around medieval lanes
  under a castle on a crag. Climb the
  **castle tower** (~CZK 200) for THE view, walk
  the painted **Cloak Bridge**, and in summer
  watch rafters splash down the weir with beer in
  hand (join them — rentals ~CZK 400).
- **17:00** — **The reason you're overnighting:**
  the day-tripper tide (Prague and Vienna buses)
  drains away, and the town exhales. Golden-hour
  lanes, swifts over the rooftops, riverside
  tables with castle views — this is the Krumlov
  postcards pretend is always there.
- **19:30** — Dinner in a riverside cellar
  (trout, duck, dark lager from the local Eggenberg
  brewery — ~CZK 300–450 pp). Night walk: the
  floodlit tower with nobody under it.

**Where to stay in Krumlov (one night):** the
in-loop pensions and small hotels — **Hotel Růže**
(the grand Jesuit-college option), riverside
**Pension Athanor / Krumlov House-style guesthouses**
(~CZK 2,200–3,200) — tiny inventory; book early and
by current reviews.

## Day 7 — Slow morning, fly home

- **08:00** — Krumlov before breakfast — mist on
  the river, empty lanes — then the Egon Schiele
  Art Centrum (the painter's complicated hometown
  museum) or a last castle-garden stroll.
- **11:00** — Bus back to Prague (~3 h), landing
  you at the airport corridor by mid-afternoon —
  **book an evening departure**; if your flight
  leaves earlier, flip Krumlov to a long day trip
  and keep night 6 in Prague instead.

## What to pack

- Cobblestone-proof shoes — Prague is a city of
  beautiful, ankle-testing stones; heels are a
  mistake locals enjoy watching.
- Layers + a rain shell year-round; a proper coat,
  hat and gloves November–March.
- A scarf for synagogue and church interiors;
  smart-casual passes everywhere.
- **European Type C/F adapter** (UK and Gulf plugs
  don't fit).
- Day-bag that zips (tram 22, Charles Bridge,
  clock-show crowds — that's the whole crime map).
- An empty half-kilo of luggage for crystal and
  gingerbread.

## FAQ

**Is Prague safe? What's the actual scam list?**
Very safe — violent crime is rare and families
wander at midnight. The complete honest list:
pickpockets at the clock show, on Charles Bridge
and tram 22 (zip and front-carry); "0% commission"
exchange offices (never); card machines offering
AED conversion (decline); un-hailed taxis
(Uber/Bolt); and the strip-club promoters on
Wenceslas Square at night (ignore). That's it —
handle those and Prague is easier than most capitals
Mall on a Saturday.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Prague has grown into both. Halal: a solid bench
of Turkish, Lebanese, Pakistani and Bengali
kitchens across New Town and Žižkov (the word
"halal" is displayed where it applies — the
classic Czech tavern, being pork-and-beer-built,
mostly isn't). Vegetarians do better than the
cuisine's reputation: smažený sýr (fried cheese —
the national guilty pleasure), bramboráky potato
pancakes, Lehká Hlava/Maitrea's famous veg menus,
and dumplings in mushroom sauce. Krumlov is
thinner on both — the trout is the local
non-meat classic.

**Do we need the Prague tourist card / guided
tours?**
Mostly no. The city IS the sight — transit costs
pennies, the castle ticket is CZK 250, and this
guide's timings (castle at 9, bridge at dawn,
Kutná Hora on a weekday) do what skip-the-line
products charge for. One guide worth paying for:
a Josefov walking tour, where the history needs a
human voice.

**Kutná Hora or a castle day trip (Karlštejn)?**
Kutná Hora, clearly: the ossuary is unlike
anything else in Europe and St Barbara's outranks
Karlštejn's interior (which is view-first,
tour-only). Karlštejn wins only for
castle-collecting kids.

**Is one night in Český Krumlov enough?**
Yes — that's the trick. Day-trippers see a
crowded stage set, 10:00–17:00; overnighters get
the dawn and dusk town, which is the entire
point. Two nights only if you're adding summer
rafting or the Lipno lake.

**What's Czech guide #2?** **Prague Christmas markets** — Old Town
Square's tree, mulled wine economics, and Krumlov under snow — the
December cousin of our [Germany Christmas-markets
guide](/itineraries/germany-christmas-markets-guide/). On the list.
