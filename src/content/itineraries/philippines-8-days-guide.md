Palawan is what happens when Ha Long Bay, the Maldives and a
pirate map have a beautiful child: black-streaked limestone
towers erupting from water so clear the boats look parked on
glass, lagoons hidden behind cliff walls, and beaches that
close the day with mango shakes and reggae covers.

And the door keeps opening. Most Western passports get **30
days visa-free** on arrival, and since **June 2025 Indian
passport holders join them** — 14 days on nothing but a
passport, hotel booking and return ticket, or **30 days with
a valid US, UK, Schengen, Japan, Singapore, Canada or
Australia visa**. No embassy, no fee, no file: one of Asia's
best archipelagos is now a no-paperwork trip for most people
reading this.

The route is Palawan's greatest-hits pairing: **El Nido (4 nights)**
for the Bacuit Bay lagoons, a **3½-hour fast ferry** across to
**Coron (2 nights)** for Kayangan Lake and the wreck reefs, with a
Manila transit night up front. Costs are in Philippine pesos —
figure **{{PHP 46000}} per person** excluding international flights.
**Go December–May**; June–November is typhoon season, when boats
stay in port and lagoons go grey.

## Before you go: the admin

- **Visa-free, officially:** most Western passports get
  **30 days** on arrival. Indian nationals get **14 days**
  (passport 6+ months, confirmed hotel, return ticket,
  proof of funds) or **30 days with a valid AJACSSUK visa**
  (US, Japan, Australia, Canada, Schengen, Singapore, UK) —
  both **non-extendible**. Longer trips: the e-visa at
  **evisa.gov.ph**. For this 8-day route, the free 14
  days covers it with room to spare.
- **eTravel registration** (etravel.gov.ph — free) within
  72 hours before arrival; screenshot the QR. Beware
  lookalike sites that charge — the real one costs
  nothing.
- **Flights:** Emirates and Etihad fly Dubai/Abu Dhabi →
  Manila non-stop (~8½ h), commonly US$490–820
  return. Book internal legs the same day you book these
  — Palawan planes are small and peak dates vanish.
- **The El Nido access decision:** fly **Manila → El Nido
  direct** (AirSwift/Cebu Pacific, 1h20, ~PHP 6,000–9,000
  one-way, strict 10–20 kg baggage) — or save thousands
  flying **Manila → Puerto Princesa** (~PHP 2,000–3,500)
  + a 5–6 h shuttle van (PHP 700–900). We budget the
  direct flight out, and **Coron → Manila** home
  (Busuanga airport, ~PHP 3,500–6,000).
- **Cash rules the islands:** El Nido and Coron ATMs are
  few, moody and fee-happy — **carry your pesos from
  Manila** (cards work at hotels; tours and takeaways are
  cash/GCash country).

## Day 1 — Land in Manila, sleep by the runway

- **Evening arrival** — Clear immigration (show the
  hotel + return-ticket PDFs if asked), grab pesos at
  the airport, and sleep smart: **Newport City**, the
  walkway-connected strip across from Terminal 3
  (Belmont Hotel or Savoy Manila — book by current
  reviews), so tomorrow's domestic hop is a stroll, not
  a Manila-traffic odyssey.
- If you land early enough: Newport's mall for a first
  halo-halo and a SIM/eSIM top-up (Globe/Smart,
  ~PHP 500–800 for the week).

## Day 2 — Fly into the postcard

- **09:00** — Manila → **El Nido** (1 h 20). The
  approach — karst towers rising from turquoise — is
  the trailer for the whole week. Lio airport is 20
  minutes from town (shared vans PHP 200–350).
- **13:00** — Check in, lunch on the beach at
  **Corong-Corong** (grilled catch + rice, PHP 250–400),
  and pay the town's **Eco-Tourism Development Fee —
  PHP 400, valid 10 days** — your pass to every boat
  day (kids 6–13 PHP 250, under-6 free).
- **15:30** — Ease in: swim off **Las Cabañas Beach**
  (sunset swing, beanbags, the works) — tricycle from
  town PHP 150.
- **19:00** — Dinner on the El Nido beachfront — the
  town is five sandy streets of grills, shake stands
  and dive shops. Book tomorrow's Tour A in person
  (or ahead online): shared boats run **PHP
  1,200–1,500 pp including lunch**.

**Where to stay in El Nido** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Miniloc Island Resort** (El Nido
  Resorts) — 4.7/5 on TripAdvisor (~1,600 reviews),
  the original water cottages under the cliffs,
  boat-transfer arrival, house reef turtles. All-in
  from ~PHP 30,000/night (Pangulasian, 4.8, is the
  flagship sister).
- *Mid-range:* **Seda Lio** — 4.4/5 (~1,100 reviews),
  beachfront near the airport on Lio's quiet sand,
  pools + breakfast. ~PHP 8,000–11,000.
- *Value:* **H Hotel El Nido** — 4.9/5 (~430 reviews),
  central, rooftop views, breakfast and beach shuttle
  included. ~PHP 3,500–5,000.

## Day 3 — Tour A: the lagoons

- **09:00** — The classic **Tour A** (shared boat,
  PHP 1,200–1,500 with buffet-on-the-beach lunch):
  **Big Lagoon** — kayaking between 200-metre walls
  into a silence with fish in it — plus **Secret
  Lagoon**, Shimizu Island's snorkel wall and
  **7 Commandos Beach**. Lagoon slots are rationed
  and rotated by the municipality, so operators
  assign what their permit holds that day — if Big
  Lagoon matters most, say so when booking and book
  early.
- **16:30** — Back by late afternoon, salt-crusted
  and grinning. Shake o'clock (PHP 120).
- **19:00** — Dinner + book Tour C for the day after
  tomorrow. Tonight, nothing — island time is the
  attraction.

## Day 4 — Nacpan Beach day

- **09:30** — Tricycle or rented scooter north to
  **Nacpan** (45 min, PHP 800–1,200 return by trike):
  four kilometres of gold sand with a twin-lagoon
  viewpoint at the north end and beach clubs doing
  fresh coconut + kinilaw (Filipino ceviche —
  seafood, lime, coconut milk).
- **15:00** — Back for pool/beach time; sunset at Las
  Cabañas round two or the Corong-Corong strip.
- **19:30** — Splash-out dinner night (Ungasan-style
  cliff spots and Spanish-Filipino kitchens run
  PHP 600–900 pp) — still less than a city brunch back home.

## Day 5 — Tour C, the wilder one

- **09:00** — **Tour C** (same PHP 1,200–1,500
  format): **Hidden Beach** through a slot in the
  rock, the **Matinloc Shrine** viewpoint, **Secret
  Beach** — the swim-through-a-hole cove that
  allegedly inspired *The Beach* — and Helicopter
  Island's snorkel reef. Rougher, prettier, our
  pick of the two.
- **17:00** — Pack for the crossing; settle bills in
  cash.
- **19:00** — Last El Nido dinner; early night — the
  ferry check-in is punctual even when the ferry
  isn't.

## Day 6 — Across the bay to Coron

- **11:00** — **Fast ferry El Nido → Coron**
  (~3½ h, **PHP 2,800–3,300**, daily midday
  departures — Montenegro/Atienza/Jomalia and
  friends; **book online days ahead in season**,
  arrive an hour early, take the tablet if seas are
  up — this is open water).
- **15:30** — Land in Coron town (on Busuanga
  island), check in, climb **Mt Tapyas** for sunset:
  721 steps to a cross with the whole karst bay
  going orange (free, bring water).
- **19:30** — Dinner in town (Coron is a working
  fishing town with a tourist fringe — dinners
  PHP 200–450), then book tomorrow's boat.

**Where to stay in Coron** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Two Seasons Coron Island Resort** —
  4.4/5 (~520 reviews), private-island bungalows a
  boat ride from town. ~PHP 12,000+.
- *Mid-range:* **The Funny Lion** — 4.9/5 (~3,200
  reviews — the area's darling), rooftop sunset
  bar and jacuzzis at the town's edge.
  ~PHP 6,500–9,000.
- *Value:* **Bacau Bay Resort** — 4.5/5 (~430
  reviews), pool + private dock + breakfast.
  ~PHP 3,000–4,500.

## Day 7 — Kayangan Lake and the wrecks

- **08:30** — The **Coron Island Super Ultimate
  tour** (shared **PHP 1,500–2,000 incl. lunch**;
  some site fees like Kayangan's PHP 300 sometimes
  billed separately — ask): **Kayangan Lake**,
  the cliff-ringed freshwater bowl consistently
  called Asia's cleanest (the viewpoint on the way
  up is THE Philippines photo); **Twin Lagoon**,
  where warm sea and cool spring water shimmer
  into each other through a swim-through; **Barracuda
  Lake's** thermocline weirdness; the **Skeleton
  Wreck** — a WWII Japanese hull in snorkel-depth
  water — and CYC Beach.
- **17:00** — Massage (PHP 500/hour, earned) and
  the last mango shake audit.
- **19:30** — Farewell dinner at the town grills —
  Coron's crayfish moment.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Busuanga airport (45 min from town,
  shared vans PHP 200–300) → Manila (1 h 10) →
  connect to the Gulf. Book the Manila leg with
  4+ hours' buffer — Palawan flights catch weather
  delays, and NAIA terminal transfers eat time.

## What to pack

- Reef shoes (lagoon entries are sharp), snorkel
  mask from home if you're picky — rentals run
  PHP 150/day.
- Dry bag (every day is a boat day), reef-safe
  SPF 50, rash vest — the equatorial burn is
  quick.
- Cash wallet strategy: pesos drawn in Manila,
  split between bags.
- Motion tablets for the ferry, basic first-aid
  (pharmacies thin out past El Nido), mosquito
  repellent for dusk.
- 10 kg-friendly soft duffel if flying AirSwift —
  hard shells eat the allowance.
- Power bank; island brownouts are brief but real.

## FAQ

**Is it really visa-free now — and what do they
check?**
Yes — official since 8 June 2025. Carry printouts:
return ticket, hotel bookings, and a bank statement
you never end up showing. The 14 days are
**non-extendible**, so date your flights inside the
window; frequent flyers with a US/UK/Schengen visa
in the passport get 30 days instead. Plus the free
eTravel QR, done on your phone in ten minutes.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
The honest one: Filipino cuisine loves pork, and
**halal certification is rare** outside Manila's
big malls and Muslim quarter — but island Palawan
runs on **grilled fish, squid, prawns and rice**,
which keeps seafood-halal travellers comfortable
without paperwork. Vegetarians work harder than in
Thailand: fruit shakes, chopsuey, egg silog
breakfasts and pinakbet exist everywhere, but ask
for no fish sauce/bagoong. Self-catering isn't
really a thing; adaptability is.

**Is Palawan safe? What about the travel
advisories for the Philippines?**
Advisories concern the far southwest (Sulu,
western Mindanao) — **800+ km from this route**.
Palawan itself is one of Asia's most easygoing
tourist regions: solo travellers, families,
honeymooners. Real risks here are sunburn, boat
schedules and PHP 500 massages becoming daily.
Manila: normal big-city rules (Grab taxis, watch
phones in crowds).

**When exactly should we go — and what about
typhoons?**
**December–March is prime** (dry, 28–32°C,
crowds + peak prices around Christmas);
April–May hotter but glorious. **June–November is
the honest no**: typhoons don't hit Palawan
head-on often, but wet-season swells cancel tours
and the ferry for days at a time. If you must go
then, build slack days and refundable rooms.

**Tour A or Tour C if we only have one day?**
Tour C — Hidden and Secret Beach out-magic even
the Big Lagoon, and the snorkelling is better.
But the correct answer is both; they're PHP 1,500
each and there is nothing on Earth quite like
this bay.

**What's Philippines guide #2?**
**Cebu + Bohol**: Kawasan Falls canyoneering, the
Moalboal sardine tornado, Chocolate Hills and
bug-eyed tarsiers — plus a frank section on the
Oslob whale-shark question. On the list.
