Uganda has the gorillas, the chimpanzees and the savanna, and it is
the country people choose when they have looked at Rwanda's permit
price and looked again.

**That is the real comparison**, so here it is up front: a gorilla
permit in Uganda costs **USD 800**, against Rwanda's **USD 1,500**.
The animals are the same species in the same mountain range. What
you pay for in Rwanda is the short, excellent road from Kigali —
under three hours. In Uganda, **Bwindi is eight to ten hours by road
from Entebbe**, and that is the trade.

What you get in return is a much fuller trip. **Kibale** has the
best chimpanzee tracking in Africa. **Queen Elizabeth** has the
Kazinga channel and tree-climbing lions in the Ishasha sector.
**Lake Bunyonyi** is deep, cold, bilharzia-free and the right place
to end.

## Before you go: permits, driving and the two primates

### Entry

- **Uganda requires a visa for most nationalities**, applied for
  online in advance through the official eVisa portal. Print the
  approval; it is checked.
- **The East Africa Tourist Visa** covers Uganda, Kenya and Rwanda
  together and is the right choice if you are combining them.
- **Yellow fever vaccination is mandatory** and the certificate is
  checked on arrival.

### Permits, and how they differ

- **Gorilla permits: USD 800** for foreign non-residents, issued by
  the Uganda Wildlife Authority. **Rwanda's are USD 1,500.**
- **Book months ahead**, through UWA or an operator. Peak months —
  June to September, December to February — sell out.
- **Gorilla habituation experience**: a separate, more expensive
  permit that gives **four hours** with a semi-habituated group
  instead of one. Limited numbers daily.
- **Chimpanzee permits at Kibale** are far cheaper and near-certain
  for sightings.
- **Minimum age 15** for gorillas, as in Rwanda.
- **You will not be allowed to trek with a cold.** Gorillas catch
  human respiratory infections and it kills them. No refunds, and
  the rule is right.

### The driving is the trade-off

- **Entebbe to Bwindi is 8–10 hours**, on a mix of good tarmac and
  rough murram track. Most itineraries break it with Kibale and
  Queen Elizabeth on the way, which this one does.
- **A 4x4 with a driver-guide** is the standard arrangement and is
  how almost everyone does it. Self-drive is possible and slow.
- **Domestic flights** from Entebbe to Kihihi or Kisoro cut the
  Bwindi leg to about ninety minutes and cost considerably more.
  Worth it if your time is short.
- **Do not drive at night.** Unlit vehicles, pedestrians and
  potholes.

### On the trek itself

- **Treks run from one to six hours each way**, on steep, muddy,
  nettle-covered ground. Bwindi is called the Impenetrable Forest
  for a reason.
- **Hire a porter.** It is cheap, genuinely helpful, and direct
  local income — often to former poachers now employed by the park.
- **Gaiters and gardening gloves** are not excessive here.

### Money and the practicalities

- **The shilling (UGX)**, with large numbers. Cards work in Kampala
  and Entebbe and at some lodges; **carry cash** elsewhere.
- **US dollars are used for permits** — clean, post-2009 notes.
- **Malaria is present countrywide.** Prophylaxis and repellent.
- English is an official language and widely spoken.

## Day 1 — Land at Entebbe

- **Afternoon** — Land on the shore of Lake Victoria. Stay in
  **Entebbe** rather than Kampala; it is calmer and closer.
- **Evening** — The botanical gardens, or the chimp sanctuary on
  Ngamba Island if you have a full day.

## Day 2 — West to Kibale

- **All day** — Drive west, five to six hours, through tea estates
  and the Fort Portal crater lakes.
- **Evening** — **Kibale**, and a very loud forest after dark.

## Day 3 — Chimpanzees

- **Morning** — **Chimpanzee tracking** in Kibale, which has the
  highest primate density in Africa. Sightings are close to
  guaranteed and considerably noisier than gorillas.
- **Afternoon** — **Bigodi wetland** walk for monkeys and birds,
  run by the community.
- **Evening** — Kibale.

## Day 4 — South to Queen Elizabeth

- **Morning** — Drive south, about three hours, past the crater
  lakes and across the equator.
- **Afternoon** — **Kazinga Channel** boat: hippo in the hundreds,
  buffalo, elephant and extraordinary birdlife, all from the water.
- **Evening** — Lodge on the escarpment.

## Day 5 — Game drives, and the Ishasha lions

- **Dawn** — Game drive on the **Kasenyi** plains for lion,
  elephant and Uganda kob.
- **Afternoon** — South to the **Ishasha** sector, where lions climb
  fig trees and lie along the branches. Nobody is quite sure why.
- **Evening** — Ishasha.

## Day 6 — Into Bwindi

- **Morning** — The drive into **Bwindi Impenetrable Forest**, three
  to four hours on rough road and increasingly steep.
- **Afternoon** — Settle and attend the briefing.
- **Evening** — Early night.

## Day 7 — Gorillas

- **06:00** — Briefing, family allocation, drive to the trailhead.
- **Morning** — The trek: one to six hours through dense forest,
  then **one hour** with the family. Masks required, distance
  enforced, flash banned.
- **Afternoon** — Back. Most people need the rest of the day.
- **Evening** — A **Batwa community** visit, if arranged
  respectfully through the lodge.

## Day 8 — To Lake Bunyonyi

- **Morning** — Drive to **Lake Bunyonyi**, about three hours, into
  terraced hills.
- **Afternoon** — The lake: 29 islands, very deep, cold, and **free
  of bilharzia and crocodiles**, which makes it one of the few
  swimmable lakes in the region.
- **Evening** — A canoe at sunset.

## Day 9 — The long road back

- **All day** — Drive to Entebbe or Kampala, eight to nine hours,
  with a stop at the **equator** marker.
- **Evening** — Entebbe.

## Day 10 — Fly home

- Entebbe airport is fifteen minutes from the town's hotels.

## What to pack

- **Gaiters and thick gloves** for Bwindi's nettles and thorns.
- **Waterproofs.** It is a rainforest, in every month.
- **Boots with grip** for steep mud.
- **Long sleeves and trousers** for forest and mosquitoes.
- **US dollars in clean notes** for permits and tips.
- **Binoculars** for Queen Elizabeth.
- **Type G plugs**, UK-style, 240V.

## FAQ

**Uganda or Rwanda for gorillas?**
Uganda if the permit price matters and you want a fuller trip —
USD 800 against USD 1,500, plus chimps and savanna. Rwanda if you
have limited days and want the short drive from Kigali. Our
[Rwanda guide](/itineraries/rwanda-8-days-guide/) covers that side.

**How hard is the trek?**
Anywhere from one to six hours each way on steep, muddy ground at
altitude. Tell the briefing honestly and you will be matched to a
suitable family. A porter makes a genuine difference.

**Is the habituation experience worth the extra?**
Four hours instead of one, with a group still being habituated, so
behaviour is more natural and less settled. It costs substantially
more and numbers are very limited. For most people the standard
hour is enough.

**When should I go?**
**June to September** and **December to February** are the drier
seasons and the easier treks. The wet months are green, quieter and
considerably muddier.
