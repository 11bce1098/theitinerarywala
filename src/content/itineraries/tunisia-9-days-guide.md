Tunisia is two hours from southern Europe, uses a currency you have
never heard of, and holds Roman remains that would be the main
attraction of any European country. **El Jem**'s amphitheatre seats
35,000 and stands almost complete in the middle of an ordinary town.
The **Bardo Museum** in Tunis holds the best collection of Roman
mosaics anywhere. **Dougga** is a whole hillside Roman city with
almost nobody in it.

South of that the country changes entirely: salt flats you drive
across, mountain oases, and the underground Berber dwellings at
**Matmata** that George Lucas used for Luke Skywalker's home.

It is also **the cheapest country on the Mediterranean**, by a
considerable margin.

## Before you go: driving, the south and the season

### Entry

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for 90 days — the EU, UK,
  US, Canada, Japan and many others. A few require a visa in
  advance; check your own case.
- **The dinar (TND) is a closed currency.** You cannot legally
  obtain it outside Tunisia or take meaningful amounts out. Change
  on arrival, keep the receipts, and **spend or re-convert before
  you leave** — you need the receipts to convert back.
- Cards work in hotels and larger restaurants in the north; the
  south is cash.

### Driving, and the alternative

- **Hire a car** for this route. The Roman sites, the oases and
  Matmata are not usefully connected by public transport.
- **Roads between cities are good.** Desert roads are fine in a
  normal car on this itinerary; the deep Sahara needs a 4x4 and a
  guide, which is a different trip.
- **Louages** — shared long-distance taxis — are the local network
  and are cheap, fast and how Tunisians travel between towns.
- **Trains** link Tunis, Sousse, El Jem and Sfax along the coast and
  are comfortable.
- **Do not drive at night** in the south; unlit vehicles and
  livestock.

### Security, stated plainly

- **Tunisia has been calm for years**, and tourism has recovered.
  The route in this guide is entirely standard.
- **Border regions with Libya and parts of the western mountains
  carry official travel advisories.** Check your own government's
  current advice before booking, and stay off those areas.
- **You will see police and military checkpoints**, particularly in
  the south. They are routine, polite, and want to see a passport.

### The season matters in both directions

- **March to May and September to November** are the answer: warm,
  not punishing, and everything open.
- **June to August** is very hot inland and in the south — the
  desert reaches 45°C and sightseeing becomes impractical by
  mid-morning. The coast is fine.
- **December to February** is mild on the coast, cold at night in
  the desert, and quiet.
- **Ramadan** shifts each year and changes opening hours and
  restaurant availability considerably. Check the dates.

## Day 1 — Land in Tunis

- **Afternoon** — Land, drop bags in the **medina** or **Sidi Bou
  Said**, the blue-and-white village on the cliff.
- **Evening** — Mint tea with pine nuts above the bay at Sidi Bou
  Said.

## Day 2 — Carthage and the Bardo

- **Morning** — **Carthage**: the Antonine baths, the ports, and the
  Byrsa hill. What Rome left of it is fragmentary and the setting is
  extraordinary.
- **Afternoon** — The **Bardo Museum**, whose Roman mosaic
  collection is the finest in the world and is displayed in a
  former palace.
- **Evening** — The **Tunis medina**, a UNESCO site and a working
  souk rather than a preserved one.

## Day 3 — Dougga, and south

- **Morning** — **Dougga**, two hours inland: a complete Roman
  hillside town with a capitol, theatre and baths, usually almost
  empty.
- **Afternoon** — South toward **Kairouan**, Islam's fourth holiest
  city, and the **Great Mosque** with its courtyard of recycled
  Roman columns.
- **Evening** — Kairouan.

## Day 4 — El Jem, and to the desert edge

- **Morning** — **El Jem**: an amphitheatre for 35,000 standing
  almost intact in a small town, which you can walk into and under.
- **Afternoon** — The long drive south-west to **Tozeur**, on the
  edge of the Chott.
- **Evening** — The **Ouled el-Hadef** quarter, built in patterned
  yellow brick.

## Day 5 — The Chott and the mountain oases

- **Morning** — **Chott el Djerid**, a salt lake you cross on a
  causeway, with mirages that are genuinely disorienting.
- **Afternoon** — **Chebika**, **Tamerza** and **Mides** — mountain
  oases with palm gorges and waterfalls against bare rock.
- **Evening** — Back to Tozeur.

## Day 6 — Star Wars, and Matmata

- **Morning** — **Ong Jemel** and the **Mos Espa** set, still
  standing in the sand, if that appeals — or the Tozeur palmeraie if
  it does not.
- **Afternoon** — East to **Matmata**, where Berber families built
  homes as pits dug into the ground with rooms off the sides. One
  is the hotel used as the Lars homestead.
- **Evening** — Sleep underground, which is cooler than it sounds
  and warmer than the desert night.

## Day 7 — To Djerba

- **Morning** — Drive to **Djerba**, crossing on the Roman causeway
  or the ferry.
- **Afternoon** — **Houmt Souk**, the island's whitewashed main
  town, and the **El Ghriba synagogue**, among the oldest in Africa
  and still in use.
- **Evening** — The beach.

## Day 8 — Djerba, slow

- **Morning** — The island's villages, the pottery at Guellala, and
  the flamingo lagoons in season.
- **Afternoon** — Beach, or the street-art village of **Erriadh**.
- **Evening** — Last night.

## Day 9 — Fly home

- **Djerba has its own international airport**, which saves the
  drive back to Tunis. If your return is from Tunis, allow a full
  day for it.

## What to pack

- **Modest clothing for mosques and the south** — shoulders and
  knees, and a scarf for women at religious sites.
- **Serious sun protection.** The Chott has no shade at all.
- **A warm layer for desert nights**, which are cold even after a
  40°C day.
- **European Type C/E plugs**, 230V.
- **Cash in dinars, changed on arrival**, and keep the receipts to
  convert back.
- **Closed shoes** for ruins with loose stone.

## FAQ

**Is Tunisia safe?**
For this route, yes, and it has been calm for years. Border regions
with Libya and parts of the western mountains carry travel
advisories — check your government's current advice and stay away
from those. Checkpoints in the south are routine.

**Is it worth it just for the Roman sites?**
El Jem and Dougga alone would justify a trip, and the Bardo's
mosaics are the best collection anywhere. That they cost a few
dinars each and are nearly empty is the remarkable part.

**When should I go?**
**March to May or September to November.** Summer in the south is
genuinely too hot for sightseeing, though the coast is fine.

**What about the currency rule?**
The dinar cannot legally be taken out of the country in any
quantity. Change what you need on arrival, keep the exchange
receipts, and convert the remainder back at the airport before you
fly.
