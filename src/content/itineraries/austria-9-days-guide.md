Austria runs on three registers. **Vienna** is the imperial one:
a capital built to rule half of Europe, still wearing the
Habsburgs' palaces, gilded cafés and an opera house where the
best deal in classical music costs less than a kebab.
**Hallstatt** is the postcard one: a 16th-century village
pressed between mountain and lake, so photogenic China built a
copy. **Salzburg** is the musical one: Mozart's birthplace,
a fortress on a crag, and the meadows where Julie Andrews
spun.

A tidy rail line strings them together — including the trip's
best five minutes, the **ferry across the Hallstätter See**
from station to village — and this guide adds the one move
most itineraries miss: **sleeping in Hallstatt**, so you meet
it empty at dawn instead of shoulder-to-shoulder at noon.

**Go April–June or September–October** (December is its own
magic — Vienna and Salzburg run some of Europe's loveliest
Christmas markets; see our
[German markets guide](/itineraries/germany-christmas-markets-guide/),
whose Salzburg day already belongs to this country).
July–August is busy but never Rome-hot. All costs are in
euros (EUR).

## Before you go: the admin

- **Visa:** Austria is Schengen — the full UAE playbook
  (VFS Global, €90, EES biometrics, 4–8 weeks of lead,
  bank statements + refundable bookings) is in our
  [France guide](/itineraries/france-9-days-guide/). Austria's UAE
  applications also run through VFS.
- **Money:** cards near-universal; carry €40–60 for
  sausage stands, church towers and tips. Tipping: round
  up ~5–10% ("machen Sie 20"). Tap water is free,
  Alpine-fresh and served without eye-rolling if you ask.
- **Sundays:** shops close (museums, cafés and bakeries
  don't) — plan shopping for Saturday.
- **Transport honour system:** Vienna's U-Bahn has no
  gates — buy and **validate** (stamp) your ticket; plain-
  clothes inspectors fine €105 with genuine indifference
  to your tourist status. A 72-hour Vienna pass
  (~€17.10) covers everything.
- **The café rulebook** (this matters in Vienna): a
  coffee-house table is rented, not borrowed — one
  melange buys you hours, the newspaper is communal, the
  tuxedoed waiter's aloofness is the service. UNESCO
  lists Viennese café culture as heritage; treat it as
  the attraction it is.

## Day 1 — Land in Vienna, cathedral first

- **Afternoon** — Land VIE, which takes non-stops from across
  Europe, the Gulf and Asia.
  CAT train or S7 into town (~€12–15/€4.40), check in.
- **17:00** — First walk: **Stephansplatz** — the
  chevron-tiled cathedral filling the square — then the
  Graben's plague column and Kohlmarkt's golden shopfronts
  toward the Hofburg gates.
- **19:30** — The schnitzel initiation: **Figlmüller**
  (the plate-sized original; queue or book) or any honest
  beisl (~€18–26 with a radler). Note for halal readers:
  classic schnitzel is veal/pork — the Turkish grills'
  chicken versions solve it deliciously (Vienna's kebab
  scene is vast).

**Where to stay in Vienna** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Hotel Sans Souci** — 4.9/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~3,100 reviews), MuseumsQuartier polish with a proper
  pool. ~€300–420.
- *Location king:* **Boutique Hotel Am Stephansplatz** —
  4.8/5 (~4,300 reviews), literally on the cathedral
  square; breakfast with gargoyle views. ~€220–300.
- *Value-boutique:* **The Harmonie Vienna** — 4.8/5,
  quiet street, afternoon tea included. ~€150–200.

## Day 2 — Schönbrunn, Klimt, and €15 at the opera

- **08:30** — **Schönbrunn Palace** at opening (timed
  slots — book ahead): the **Grand Tour (€42 adult / €30
  child)** walks the full imperial floor; the **gardens
  are free** — climb to the **Gloriette** for the
  city-over-palace panorama, lose the kids in the maze
  (Classic Pass €49 bundles it all).
- **13:00** — **Naschmarkt** lunch: Vienna's mile-long
  market — falafel, cheese stalls, Austro-Levantine
  everything (~€10–18).
- **15:00** — **Upper Belvedere** (~€18, timed): Klimt's
  **The Kiss** in its golden room — plus Schiele and the
  garden view back over Vienna.
- **18:00** — **The opera hack, executed:** the
  **Staatsoper sells standing tickets (~€13–18)** from
  80 minutes before curtain (app or box office) — full
  Puccini or Mozart from the rail, smart-casual fine,
  leave at the interval if the kids wilt and it still
  beat any cinema. (Sold out? The Musikverein's cheap
  seats or a Mozarthaus concert stand in.)
- **21:30** — Late würstelstand käsekrainer by the opera
  (~€6) — the traditional post-performance meal of
  duchesses and students alike.

## Day 3 — Empire in the morning, coffee-house afternoon

- **09:00** — **The Hofburg**: the **Sisi Museum +
  Imperial Apartments** (~€19) — the empire told through
  its most reluctant, fascinating empress; add the
  Imperial Treasury (the actual crown) if regalia calls.
- **12:30** — Lunch, then the day's real appointment: a
  **grand café** — Café Central's palm-court vaults
  (queue moves fast) or Sacher's red velvet for the
  **Sachertorte ceremony** (melange + torte ~€15). Sit.
  Stay. That's the exercise.
- **15:30** — Climb **St Stephen's south tower** (343
  steps, €7) for the tiled-roof dragon up close, or the
  catacombs tour below.
- **17:30** — Choose your Vienna evening: the **Prater's
  1897 Riesenrad** ferris wheel (~€14, Third-Man
  vintage) — or tram to a **heuriger** wine tavern in
  Grinzing: new wine, buffet by weight, gemütlichkeit
  by the litre (~€25–35 pp).

## Day 4 — Art or armour, canal or Hundertwasser

- **09:30** — Pick your palace of art: the
  **Kunsthistorisches Museum** (Bruegel's Tower of
  Babel, the Habsburg loot, arguably Europe's most
  beautiful museum café) or the **Albertina's**
  Dürer-to-Monet run (~€18–21 either).
- **13:00** — **MuseumsQuartier** courtyard lounging on
  the famous colored deckchairs; lunch nearby.
- **15:00** — The un-imperial Vienna:
  **Hundertwasserhaus's** wobbly fairy-tale facade,
  then the Danube Canal's street-art mile back to
  centre.
- **19:00** — Farewell-Vienna dinner; pack for the
  lakes. (Trivia your readers enjoy: Vienna tops the
  world's most-liveable-city rankings almost annually —
  four days here explains why.)

## Day 5 — The ferry to Hallstatt

- **09:30** — Train west (ÖBB/Westbahn via
  Attnang-Puchheim, ~3.5 h total, ~€30–40 booked ahead)
  along the Salzkammergut lakes to tiny **Hallstatt
  station** — which is across the water, by design.
- **13:15** — **The ferry "Stefanie"** (timed to every
  train, ~€4, 15 minutes): the village assembles itself
  across the lake as you approach — church spire,
  stacked wooden houses, mountain wall. The single best
  arrival in the Alps.
- **14:00** — Check in (bags roll fine; the village is
  600 m long), then let the afternoon crowds thin.
- **16:30** — The classic lane walk: Marktplatz, the
  swans, the **famous viewpoint** at the north end
  (Gosaumühlstraße) as day-trippers drain away — then
  the tiny **Bone House** (€2.50): 610 hand-painted
  skulls in the chapel, the village's strangest,
  tenderest tradition.
- **19:00** — Lakeside trout dinner (~€25–35 pp),
  evening silence the day-visitors never hear.
- **The respect note:** Hallstatt (pop. ~750) has
  battled overtourism — drones are banned, lanes are
  homes, and overnighting (you, tonight) is exactly the
  visitor the village asks for.

**Where to stay in Hallstatt** (one night — book months
ahead, rooms are few)

- *The lakefront classic:* **Seehotel Grüner Baum** —
  balconies directly over the water on Marktplatz.
  ~€250–350.
- *Heritage:* **Heritage Hotel Hallstatt** — three
  historic houses by the ferry dock. ~€200–280.
- *Value:* the village's family pensions (~€120–180) —
  or Obertraun across the lake at 30% less.

## Day 6 — Dawn photos, salt slides, on to Salzburg

- **06:45** — Sunrise with the village to yourself —
  the photographs of the trip, mist on the lake
  included at no charge.
- **09:30** — Funicular up for the **Salzwelten salt
  mine + Skywalk combo (€49 adult / €23 child, ages
  4+)**: the world's oldest salt mine — 7,000 years of
  "white gold", banked **wooden miners' slides** (the
  photo-speed-trap tells your km/h), the Bronze-Age
  staircase — then the **Skywalk platform** hanging
  360 m over the rooftops. Funicular-only: €29.
- **13:30** — Buses to **Salzburg** via Bad Ischl
  (~2.5 h, ~€20 — scenic the whole way; a
  driver-transfer ~€180 is the luggage-heavy
  alternative).
- **16:30** — Check in, then **Mirabell Gardens** —
  yes, the Do-Re-Mi steps and gnome garden — with the
  fortress framed beyond the roses.
- **19:30** — Old-town dinner (~€20–30) and the
  cathedral quarter lit up.

**Where to stay in Salzburg** (three nights)

- *Riverside icon:* **Hotel Sacher Salzburg** — 4.6/5
  (~2,300 reviews), torte included by osmosis.
  ~€350–500.
- *Boutique:* **Hotel & Villa Auersperg** — 4.7/5
  (~2,000 reviews), garden calm ten minutes' walk in.
  ~€200–280.
- *Value:* **IMLAUER Hotel Pitter** — 4.5/5,
  TripAdvisor's #1 value: rooftop restaurant staring
  at the fortress. ~€150–210.

## Day 7 — Fortress, Mozart, monastery beer

- **09:00** — **Hohensalzburg Fortress** by funicular
  (~€15–17 combined): Europe's largest intact medieval
  castle — the Golden Chamber, marionette museum, and
  the city-and-Alps panorama from the ramparts.
- **11:30** — Down into the lanes: **Getreidegasse's**
  wrought-iron guild signs, **Mozart's Birthplace**
  (~€13 — the boy-wonder's violin, properly moving),
  the cathedral's marble hush.
- **14:00** — Lunch, then **DomQuartier** (the
  prince-archbishops' state rooms) or the Kapuzinerberg
  woodland stairs for the reverse view.
- **18:00** — **Augustiner Bräu Mulln**: the
  monastery-founded beer hall — stone mugs rinsed at
  the fountain, chestnut garden, snack counters
  (radler and alcohol-free exist; the pretzels are
  ecumenical). ~€15–25.

## Day 8 — The hills are alive (or the ice is)

- **09:00** — **The Sound of Music tour** (~€65–70,
  4 h — Panorama/Bob's, both good): the Leopoldskron
  lake terrace, Hellbrunn's **gazebo**, Nonnberg
  Abbey gates, then out to the **Salzkammergut
  lakes** and **Mondsee's wedding church** — with a
  busload singing Edelweiss by the end. Resistance is
  futile; joy is standard. (Fun fact for the bus:
  Austrians largely haven't seen the film.)
- **The alternatives:** **Werfen ice caves** (~€48
  with cable car — the world's largest ice cave,
  jacket weather in August; 40 min south) or
  **Hellbrunn's trick fountains** (€13.50 — the
  archbishop's 400-year-old practical jokes; children
  leave soaked and converted).
- **16:00** — Last old-town hours: Fürst's original
  **Mozartkugel** (the silver-blue ones) for the
  suitcase, riverside walk.
- **19:30** — Farewell: a **fortress dinner-concert**
  (Mozart by candlelight above the city, ~€70–90) or
  St. Peter Stiftskulinarium — serving since 803,
  plausibly Europe's oldest restaurant (~€35–60).

## Day 9 — Out, three ways

- **Morning** — Choose your exit: **Salzburg airport**
  (Europe-hub connections, 20 min); the **Munich
  exit-hack** (~1.5 h by train — more long-haul non-stops, and
  it stitches this trip to our
  [Germany guides](/itineraries/germany-10-days-guide/)); or rail
  back to Vienna (2.5 h) for a VIE departure. Mozart
  balls distributed among hand luggage per convention.

## FAQ

**How does the opera standing-ticket hack actually
work?**
The Staatsoper releases **Stehplätze (~€13–18)** daily
— via the app/online from 10:00 and at the box office
~80 minutes before curtain. Claim your rail spot with
a scarf (honoured custom), dress tidy-casual, and
you're watching a full production from the best
acoustics in the house. With kids: one act is a
victory; leave at the interval guilt-free.

**Is Hallstatt worth it — isn't it overrun?**
Both true, and the overnight resolves the paradox:
10:00–16:00 the lanes belong to coach tours; dawn and
dusk belong to the 750 residents and the handful who
slept there. Day-tripping from Salzburg (the standard
mistake) delivers only the crowded hours. Go gently —
it's a village, not a set — and it repays you with
the trip's best morning.

**Sound of Music tour — even if I've never seen the
film?**
Then swap it without guilt (locals mostly haven't
either): **Werfen's ice caves** or **Hellbrunn's
trick fountains** are the equal-value alternatives.
Fans, though, report it among their favourite
half-days in Europe — the Salzkammergut scenery
carries even the songless.

**Best time — and the December question?**
May–June and September win on weather-per-crowd.
**December is a legitimate rival**: Vienna's
Rathausplatz and Salzburg's cathedral-square markets
are top-tier (our
[Christmas markets guide](/itineraries/germany-christmas-markets-guide/)
already routes a Salzburg day) — pack for 0°C and
gain the glühwein layer. Hallstatt in snow is
absurdly beautiful and requires bus-schedule
patience.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Vienna is one of Europe's easier halal capitals —
the Turkish/Balkan grill scene is everywhere,
Naschmarkt runs on falafel and levantine counters,
and chicken-schnitzel versions solve the national
dish. Salzburg is smaller but covered (old-town
kebab, Indian options). Vegetarians thrive:
käsespätzle, knödel, strudel both savoury and sweet
— and every café's cake counter is meat-free by
definition.

**What's Austria guide #2?**
The **Tyrol Alps** (Innsbruck, Zillertal cable cars,
Swarovski caves) or **Vienna-deep + the Wachau**
(Melk Abbey, Dürnstein's wine terraces by Danube
boat). And Bratislava sits 1 h from Vienna for a
two-country afternoon whenever you're ready.
