Portugal is the Western Europe trip that still feels like a
discovery — and a discount. Lisbon tumbles down seven hills in
a cascade of terracotta roofs and blue-tiled facades, vintage
trams grinding up the middle; **Sintra** stacks
pastel palaces on a green ridge twenty minutes away; and
**Porto**, three hours north by fast train, pairs a
UNESCO riverfront with cellars full of barrels across the
water. Dinner with wine costs what a starter does in Paris.

The practicals: Lisbon is a direct hop from most of Europe
and an ~8 h non-stop from the Gulf, one **Schengen visa**
covers it (Portugal runs its own VFS desks), and the whole
route runs on trains. The
shape: **Lisbon (4 nights, one day for Sintra) → Porto (3
nights, one day for the Douro valley)**, flying home from
Porto's connections or looping back on the €16 promo rail
fare. Figure **{{EUR 1400}} per person
lakh)** including the visa, excluding flights. **Go
March–June or September–October**; July–August is hot,
brilliant and crowded; even winter is mild and gentle here.

## Before you go: the admin

- **Schengen visa, Portugal flavour:** the standard VFS
  drill from our [France](/itineraries/france-9-days-guide/) and
  [Croatia](/itineraries/croatia-8-days-guide/) guides — **€90** +
  service fee at your nearest VFS centre with Portugal as
  main destination: biometrics, proof of residence, bank
  statements, insurance (€30,000), itinerary; apply
  **4–6 weeks out**. Existing multi-entry Schengen
  stickers work.
- **Two timed tickets rule this trip — book them before
  you fly:** **Pena Palace** (timed entry is mandatory;
  summer slots vanish weeks out — book at the official
  Parques de Sintra portal) and the **Lisbon→Porto
  train** (CP releases **promo fares from €16 about 60
  days out** at cp.pt vs €35.70 walk-up on the Alfa
  Pendular).
- **Money:** euros, cards everywhere; keep €30–50 cash
  for pastelarias, kiosks and tram-stop gelado. Decline
  home-currency conversion on terminals, as always.
- **Getting around Lisbon:** buy a **Viva Viagem card**
  (€0.50) and load the **24-hour pass (~€7.10)** —
  covers metro, trams (including the famous 28),
  funiculars and the Santa Justa lift, each of which
  charges tourists €3–6 onboard otherwise.
- **The hills are the gym:** Lisbon and Porto are
  staircase cities on cobbles polished since the
  1755 earthquake — real walking shoes, not sandals
  with opinions.

## Day 1 — Land in Lisbon, ride into the postcard

- **Afternoon** — Emirates lands mid-afternoon; metro
  or Uber (~€15) into town, check in around
  Baixa/Chiado.
- **17:00** — Golden-hour starter: the **Santa Justa
  viewpoint** or **Miradouro de São Pedro de
  Alcântara** (funicular up, covered by the pass) —
  the city stacked to the castle in evening light.
- **19:30** — First dinner in Chiado or Bairro Alto:
  grilled sardines or a proper bacalhau (€12–18
  mains), vinho verde by the jug (€8), and a
  **ginjinha** (cherry liqueur, €1.50, standing at
  the counter) on the walk home. Skip any Baixa
  terrace with a laminated photo menu — one street
  uphill halves the bill.

**Where to stay in Lisbon** (four nights)

- *Luxury-boutique:* **Corpo Santo Historical
  Hotel** — 4.9/5 on TripAdvisor (~4,600 reviews),
  by the river with a Roman wall in the basement
  and staff who win awards for a reason. ~€280+.
- *Mid-range:* **Brown's Central** — 4.5/5 (~1,800
  reviews), stylish Baixa base. ~€140–190.
- *Value:* **Hotel Gat Rossio** — 4.1/5 (~2,200
  reviews), bright rooms off Rossio square.
  ~€95–130.

## Day 2 — Alfama, the castle, tram 28

- **09:00** — **São Jorge Castle** at opening
  (~€15, book online): Moorish ramparts, peacocks,
  and the whole city-and-river map below.
- **11:00** — Get lost DOWNHILL through **Alfama** —
  the medieval quarter that survived the
  earthquake: laundry lines, fado murals, the
  **Miradouro de Santa Luzia's** tiled terrace, the
  cathedral. Lunch at a tasca (grilled fish +
  wine, €10–15).
- **15:00** — **Tram 28**, done honestly: it's a
  beautiful rattling museum piece AND Lisbon's
  pickpocket capital with hour-long queues at
  Martim Moniz. The play: board at **Graça or
  Campo de Ourique** (the quiet ends), ride a
  half-route, zips closed, phone in front pocket.
  Or photograph it on the Alfama curves — the
  outside is the better view anyway.
- **17:30** — **LX Factory** under the bridge
  (creative-factory shops, the bookshop with the
  flying bicycle) or the Time Out Market food
  hall for grazing dinner (€10–20).
- **21:00** — Optional: fado in Alfama — the €40
  dinner shows are fine; the €10 "fado vadio"
  (amateurs, locals, goosebumps) in Mouraria
  taverns is the memory.

## Day 3 — Belém: monks, caravels and the tart

- **09:00** — Tram/train to **Belém**, the launch
  ramp of the Age of Discoveries. **Jerónimos
  Monastery** first (~€18, book the slot): the
  cloister is the single most beautiful room in
  Portugal — Manueline stone carved like rope and
  coral, Vasco da Gama's tomb by the door.
- **11:30** — The waterfront line-up: the
  **Monument to the Discoveries**, the **Belém
  Tower** (~€15 — the honest note: the interior
  is a queue for narrow stairs; the exterior is
  the masterpiece, and skipping inside costs you
  little), and the riverside walk between them.
- **13:00** — The pilgrimage: **Pastéis de Belém**
  (since 1837, the original custard-tart recipe,
  ~€1.40 each) — the queue is for boxes; the
  sit-down halls inside move fast. Order four,
  regret nothing, compare with every pastel de
  nata for the rest of your life.
- **15:30** — MAAT's wave-roof museum or the
  coach museum (world's best golden-carriage
  flex, ~€8), then back for a Cais do Sodré
  riverside evening.

## Day 4 — Sintra: the fairy-tale day

- **08:15** — Train from Rossio station (~€2.50,
  40 min) — beat the ridge's tour buses. At
  Sintra station, bus 434 loops to the palaces
  (day hop ticket ~€13.50) — don't drive; parking
  is a myth.
- **09:30** — **Pena Palace** at your timed slot
  (**€20 palace+park** — book days/weeks ahead):
  a Bavarian-Moorish-Manueline fever dream in
  red and yellow on the ridge top, mist willing.
  Walk up from the gate (25 min through the
  park) or shuttle (€3).
- **12:30** — Picnic or vila lunch, then
  **Quinta da Regaleira** (~€15, at regaleira.pt
  — it's outside the official Sintra bundle):
  the masonic millionaire's garden with the
  **Initiation Well** — the moss-green spiral
  staircase sunk nine storeys into the earth —
  and tunnels that exit behind waterfalls.
  For many, the day's real winner.
- **16:30** — If legs allow: the **Moorish
  Castle** ramparts (€12) — or the wise
  alternative, pastry #2 (travesseiros at
  Piriquita) in the old town.
- **18:30** — Train back; gentle Chiado evening.
  Pack for the north; tomorrow's train is booked
  (right?).

## Day 5 — North to Porto

- **09:30** — **Alfa Pendular from Santa Apolónia
  or Oriente** (2 h 50; €35.70 full / **from €16
  promo booked ~60 days out**; Campanhã arrival —
  your ticket includes the 5-min hop to gorgeous
  **São Bento station**, so take it and arrive
  under 20,000 azulejo tiles).
- **13:00** — Check in, lunch on a francesinha —
  Porto's melted-cheese, meat-stacked,
  beer-sauce sandwich (€12–15, split one, no
  shame) — or the lighter bifana (€3).
- **15:00** — First wander: **Livraria Lello**
  (the neo-Gothic bookshop with the forked
  crimson staircase — **~€10 voucher, redeemable
  against a book; book the entry slot online**,
  and go near closing when the crush thins),
  then the **Clérigos Tower** climb (~€10) for
  the rooftop panorama.
- **18:30** — Down through the Ribeira's
  tumbling lanes to the river: the **Dom Luís I
  bridge** double-decked over the Douro, port
  lodges glowing across the water. Dinner
  riverside (€15–25 pp).

**Where to stay in Porto** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Torel Palace Porto** — 4.9/5
  (~740 reviews), literary boutique palace with
  city views. ~€250+.
- *Mid-range:* **PortoBay Flores** — 4.8/5
  (~1,300 reviews), spa-calm on pedestrian Rua
  das Flores. ~€150–200.
- *Value:* **Moov Hotel Porto Centro** — 4.3/5
  (~3,500 reviews, the city's #1 Best Value),
  by São Bento in an old cinema. ~€75–100.

## Day 6 — Porto: cellars and the six bridges

- **09:30** — Morning icons: São Bento's tiles
  properly, the cathedral terrace, the **Igreja
  do Carmo** azulejo wall, coffee at a
  belle-époque café (the Majestic is beautiful
  and charges for it; Café Guarany does the job
  at half).
- **12:00** — Walk the **top deck of the Dom
  Luís I bridge** (pedestrian, vertiginous,
  free) to **Vila Nova de Gaia**.
- **13:00** — Lunch in Gaia, then the reason
  Porto is a verb: a **port-wine cellar tour**
  (Cálem, Sandeman, Graham's — **€20–28 with
  three tastings**, book the English slot):
  barrels the size of rooms, the difference
  between ruby and tawny finally explained, and
  the discovery that white port + tonic is
  summer's answer.
- **16:30** — **Six Bridges cruise** (~€20,
  50 min) from the Gaia quay — the lazy,
  perfect river hour — then the cable car or
  stairs back up.
- **19:30** — Dinner in the Ribeira backstreets;
  sunset from the Jardim do Morro with half of
  Porto and a guitarist.

## Day 7 — The Douro valley

- **08:00** — Full-day **Douro valley tour**
  (~€100–120 pp with two quinta visits,
  tastings, lunch and usually a 1-h river
  stretch; the DIY train to Pinhão — one of
  Europe's great rail rides along the water —
  runs ~€28 return if you'd rather freelance):
  the world's oldest demarcated wine region,
  terraced like a contour map in green and
  gold. September = harvest chaos, the best
  kind.
- **19:00** — Back by early evening; last
  supper: modern Portuguese tasting rooms run
  €35–60 pp here and outclass twice-the-price
  Lisbon. Toast with a 10-year tawny.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- Porto's airport is 25 min by metro (€2.25).
  Connections via European hubs — or the €16
  promo train back to Lisbon for the Emirates
  non-stop, booked when you booked everything
  else.

## What to pack

- Grippy walking shoes — calçada (the polished
  mosaic pavement) is glass when damp, and both
  cities are staircases.
- Layers + a light rain shell; Atlantic evenings
  cool fast even in June. Sintra's ridge makes
  its own fog — a jacket in the daypack always.
- Sunglasses + SPF for miradouro afternoons.
- A zipped cross-body for tram days (that's the
  entire crime map).
- **European Type C/F adapter.**
- Suitcase space: cork, tinned fish in art-deco
  wrappers, and a bottle of tawny are coming
  home with you.

## FAQ

**Is Portugal safe?**
Among Europe's safest — the honest list is
short: tram 28 and Rossio pickpockets (zips +
the boarding trick above), the fake-drug
"salesmen" of Baixa (comically persistent,
ignorable — it's oregano anyway, say locals),
and ATM-conversion buttons. Families and solo
travellers: entirely easy, late evenings
included.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Lisbon makes it easy — the Martim
Moniz/Mouraria quarter is a halal hub
(Bangladeshi, Pakistani, Nepali kitchens,
several with the certificate on the door), and
kebab houses dot both cities; classic Portuguese
kitchens are pork-and-seafood country, so
seafood-halal eating (grilled dourada,
octopus, bacalhau) is the smooth default.
Vegetarians: better than legend — vegetable
soups everywhere, peixinhos da horta (green-bean
tempura — Portugal taught Japan tempura, true
story), and both cities have strong modern-veg
scenes. "Sem carne, sem peixe" gets warm nods.

**Sintra in one day — Pena AND Regaleira AND
the castle?**
Two palaces is the honest maximum with joy
intact: Pena (timed, early) + Regaleira is this
guide's pairing. Add the Moorish Castle only if
you're fast walkers on a cool day. NEVER
attempt three in August. And take the train —
Sintra's traffic is a cautionary tale.

**Is the Douro tour worth it vs the DIY
train?**
Tour if wine is the point (tastings + lunch +
transport solved, nobody drives); the
**Pinhão train** if landscape is the point —
the last hour hugs the river bank and costs
€28. Photographers and budgeteers: train.
Everyone celebrating something: tour.

**Porto or Lisbon — if we had to choose one?**
Lisbon for breadth (neighbourhoods, Sintra,
beaches, big-city buzz); Porto for
concentrated charm — it's smaller, cheaper,
and the river-plus-port-cellar evening is the
single best cityscape in Iberia. This guide's
answer: don't choose; the train is €16.

**What's Portugal guide #2?**
**The Algarve** — Benagil caves, Lagos cliffs,
sandbar beaches — or the **Madeira** island
week (levada walks, fearless sunsets). Votes
welcome; both on the list.
