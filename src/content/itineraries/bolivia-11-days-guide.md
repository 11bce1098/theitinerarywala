Bolivia is the highest, poorest and most indigenous country in South
America, and the one that rewards effort most. It is also the one
where **altitude is not a footnote but the organising fact of the
trip**. La Paz's airport sits at **4,061 m** — higher than almost any
mountain in Europe — and the Uyuni circuit spends days above 4,000.

What you get is a landscape with no equivalent. The **Salar de
Uyuni** is 10,000 km² of salt flat, flat to within a metre across
its whole width, blinding white in the dry season and a perfect
mirror when a film of water sits on it. South of it, the **Eduardo
Avaroa reserve** has lagoons coloured red and green by minerals and
algae, geysers at 4,900 m, and flamingos that somehow live there.

**Lake Titicaca** at the end is a rest: 3,800 m, blue, and the
Andean world's sacred centre.

## Before you go: altitude first, then the tour operators

### Altitude is the whole plan

Read this before booking anything.

- **El Alto airport serves La Paz at 4,061 m.** You will feel it
  walking to baggage claim. This is not like Cusco or Quito; it is
  substantially higher.
- **Do not fly in and go straight up.** Plan the first two days as
  rest: La Paz's city centre sits lower than the airport, around
  3,600 m, and even that is high.
- **The Uyuni circuit sleeps at 4,200–4,500 m** for two nights. Do
  it after you have acclimatised, never at the start.
- **Symptoms** — headache, breathlessness, poor sleep, nausea —
  are normal for a day or two and should improve. If they worsen,
  descend; that is the only reliable treatment.
- **Coca tea and coca leaves** are legal, ubiquitous and mildly
  helpful. Talk to a doctor about acetazolamide before you fly.
- **Go easy on alcohol** for the first three days.

### Choosing an Uyuni operator

This matters more here than almost anywhere on this site.

- **The standard trip is three days and two nights** from Uyuni
  through the salt flat and south to the lagoons, finishing at the
  Chilean border or looping back.
- **Operators vary enormously.** There is a long history of
  accidents involving fatigue, alcohol and speed on remote tracks,
  and of vehicles breaking down far from help.
- **Do not book on price alone.** Ask specifically about the driver's
  hours, whether there is a second vehicle in convoy, oxygen on
  board, and the age of the 4x4. A reputable operator will answer
  without hesitation.
- **Book in Uyuni or La Paz with an established agency**, read
  recent reviews, and be willing to pay more than the cheapest quote.
- **Nights are in basic salt hotels and refugios** — shared rooms,
  intermittent electricity, and cold. Bring a sleeping bag liner and
  a warm layer.

### Money and getting around

- **The boliviano (BOB).** Cards work in La Paz hotels and larger
  restaurants and almost nowhere else on this route.
- **Draw cash in La Paz.** ATMs in Uyuni exist and run out.
- **Bolivia is the cheapest country in South America** by a clear
  margin.
- **Fly La Paz to Uyuni** — an hour, against ten or more by bus.
  The overnight bus is a common choice and a cold one.
- **La Paz's cable car network, Mi Teleférico**, is the city's
  metro: ten lines up the canyon walls, cheap, and the best view in
  the city.

### Entry

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for 30 to 90 days — the EU,
  UK, Canada, Australia and most of Latin America. **US passports
  require a visa**, obtainable in advance or on arrival with
  documentation and a fee. Check your own case; Bolivia's rules
  change with politics.
- **Yellow fever vaccination** is required if you are arriving from
  certain countries and recommended for the lowlands.

## Day 1 — Land in La Paz, and do nothing

- **Afternoon** — Land at 4,061 m, descend into the city. Drop bags
  and stop.
- **Evening** — Walk slowly, drink water, eat lightly. That is the
  whole plan and it is the right one.

## Day 2 — La Paz, gently

- **Morning** — The **Witches' Market** and **Calle Jaén**, the
  colonial street.
- **Afternoon** — **Mi Teleférico**: ride the red and yellow lines
  up to **El Alto** for the view down into the canyon city.
- **Evening** — If it is Thursday or Sunday, the **El Alto market**
  is one of the largest in South America.

## Day 3 — Moon Valley, or Chacaltaya

- **Option A** — **Valle de la Luna**, eroded clay spires twenty
  minutes from the centre.
- **Option B** — **Chacaltaya** at 5,300 m, a former ski resort on a
  vanished glacier — only if day two went well.
- **Evening** — Fly to **Uyuni**, or take the night bus.

## Day 4 — Onto the salt

- **Morning** — The **train cemetery** outside Uyuni, then out onto
  the **Salar**.
- **Midday** — **Incahuasi island**, a coral outcrop covered in
  giant cactus, standing in a white sea.
- **Afternoon** — The perspective photographs, which everyone takes
  and everyone enjoys.
- **Evening** — A **salt hotel** on the edge of the flat.

## Day 5 — South into the reserve

- **All day** — Driving south through the altiplano: **Laguna
  Cañapa** and **Hedionda** with flamingos, the **Árbol de Piedra**
  rock, and altitudes climbing past 4,500 m.
- **Evening** — **Laguna Colorada**, red with algae and full of
  three flamingo species. The refugio here is basic and very cold.

## Day 6 — Geysers, springs, and back

- **05:00** — **Sol de Mañana** geysers at 4,900 m, at dawn, which
  is when they are most active and most punishing.
- **Morning** — **Polques hot springs**, and **Laguna Verde** under
  the Licancabur volcano.
- **Afternoon** — The long drive back to Uyuni, or transfer to the
  Chilean border for San Pedro de Atacama.

## Day 7 — Back to La Paz

- **Morning** — Fly or bus north.
- **Afternoon** — A shower, a real bed, and lower air.

## Day 8 — To Lake Titicaca

- **Morning** — Bus to **Copacabana**, three to four hours, with the
  ferry crossing at Tiquina where you get out and the bus goes
  separately.
- **Afternoon** — The **basilica** and the hill above the town for
  sunset over the lake.

## Day 9 — Isla del Sol

- **Morning** — Boat to **Isla del Sol**, the Inca creation site,
  with terraces, ruins and no vehicles.
- **Afternoon** — Walk the island, or stay the night, which is
  better if you have it.
- **Evening** — Back to Copacabana.

## Day 10 — Tiwanaku, and La Paz

- **Morning** — Back toward La Paz, stopping at **Tiwanaku**, the
  pre-Inca site with the Gate of the Sun and monoliths that predate
  the Incas by a thousand years.
- **Evening** — Last dinner in La Paz.

## Day 11 — Fly home

- El Alto is above the city; allow for the climb and for altitude
  slowing everything down, including you.

## What to pack

- **A properly warm layer, hat and gloves.** Nights on the Uyuni
  circuit drop well below freezing and the refugios are unheated.
- **A sleeping bag liner** for the same reason.
- **Sunglasses and very high-factor sunscreen.** The salt flat
  reflects everything and the altitude thins the filter.
- **Lip balm**, which sounds trivial and is not at 4,500 m.
- **Cash in bolivianos**, drawn in La Paz.
- **Type A/C plugs** — Bolivia uses both, and voltage varies between
  110V and 220V by city. Check your charger.
- **A head torch** for refugios without reliable power.

## FAQ

**How bad is the altitude?**
Real, and manageable if you respect it. La Paz's airport at 4,061 m
is higher than most people have ever been. Two quiet days first, and
the Uyuni circuit afterwards rather than before, is the plan that
works.

**Is the Uyuni tour safe?**
With a reputable operator, yes. With the cheapest one, not
reliably — there is a genuine history of accidents on these remote
tracks. Ask about driver hours, convoy vehicles and oxygen, and pay
the difference.

**Wet season or dry?**
**December to March** floods the flat with a few centimetres of
water and gives the mirror effect, but can make parts impassable.
**May to October** is dry, gives the white hexagon crust and clear
access to the whole circuit. Both are worth it; they are different
photographs.

**Can I combine it with Chile or Peru?**
Easily. The southwest circuit ends at the Chilean border two hours
from San Pedro de Atacama — see our
[Chile guide](/itineraries/chile-12-days-guide/) — and Titicaca
continues into Peru.
