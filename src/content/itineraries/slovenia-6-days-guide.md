Slovenia is what happens when the Alps, the Mediterranean and
Central Europe are folded into a country half the size of the
UAE — and every fold hides something absurd: a lake with an
island church that rings wishes true, a 700-year-old castle
built INTO a cliff cave, a capital whose bridges are guarded
by art-nouveau dragons, and a cave system so big it has its
own underground train. Nothing is more than two hours from
anything else, which makes it the highest postcard-density
trip on this site.

The route: **Ljubljana (2 nights) → Lake Bled (2 nights,
with Vintgar Gorge and wilder Lake Bohinj) → the Postojna
Cave + Predjama Castle day → Ljubljana (1 night)**. Figure
**{{EUR 1000}} per person** including
the visa, excluding flights — a suspiciously round number the
country genuinely delivers. **Go May–June or September**:
full green, swimmable lakes, and Bled before/after its
July–August crush. October is golden; winter is a snow-globe
with shorter lists.

## Before you go: the admin

### Schengen, Slovenia flavour

- **Schengen visa, Slovenia flavour:** the usual VFS
  drill from our other Schengen guides — **€90** +
  service fee, biometrics, proof of residence, bank
  statements, insurance, itinerary, **4–6 weeks out**
  (check the current Slovenia application handler in
  the UAE when booking — smaller consulates
  occasionally re-route). Any existing multi-entry
  Schengen sticker works.

### Flights — the Venice trick

- **Flights — the Venice trick:** Ljubljana has no UAE
  non-stops; the one-stop is Turkish via Istanbul
  (~US$600–870). The arbitrage this guide loves:
  **fly Emirates/flydubai to VENICE** (often cheaper,
  non-stop) and ride a **GoOpti shared shuttle 2.5 h
  to Ljubljana (€25–45)** — Venice's airport is
  functionally Slovenia's second airport, and you can
  even bolt on a Venice day using our
  [Italy guide](/itineraries/italy-10-days-guide/). Zagreb (2 h
  bus) is the third door.

### Getting around, and money

- **Getting around:** buses cover this exact route
  cheaply (Ljubljana–Bled €7–9, 75 min, hourly;
  Bled–Bohinj €4). A **rental car (€45–60/day)** buys
  flexibility for the Vintgar/Bohinj day but is
  optional — this guide runs car-free with one
  caveat noted on Day 5.
- **Money:** euro, cards everywhere — with one famous
  exception: **the pletna boats at Bled are
  cash-only**. Carry €60–80 in notes for boatmen,
  gorges and mountain huts.

### The official-tickets warning

- **Official-tickets warning:** Postojna Cave sells
  **only via its official site** (postojnska-jama.eu)
  — the resellers charging more are the Balkans'
  politest scam. Book the combo there, done.

## Day 1 — Land, meet the dragon city

- **Afternoon** — Arrive (IST connection or the
  Venice shuttle), drop bags near the old town.
- **17:00** — Ljubljana introduces itself in 200
  metres: **Prešeren Square's** pink church, the
  **Triple Bridge** fanning across the river, the
  **Dragon Bridge's** four green guardians (the
  city's symbol — Jason of the Argonauts allegedly
  slew it here, say locals, entirely uncontested by
  evidence).
- **19:00** — The riverside café mile: Ljubljana
  pedestrianised its heart years ago, and dinner on
  the embankment (€15–25 mains) with buskers and
  bridge lights is the whole pitch. Try štruklji
  (rolled dumplings, sweet or savoury) as the
  opener.

**Where to stay in Ljubljana** (nights 1, 2 and 5)

- *Luxury:* **Grand Hotel Union** — 4.4/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~2,800 reviews), the 1905
  art-nouveau grande dame by the Triple Bridge.
  ~€220+.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Cubo** — 4.8/5 (~1,600
  reviews), sharp boutique two minutes from
  everything. ~€140–180.
- *Value:* **ibis Styles Ljubljana Centre** —
  4.0/5 (~420 reviews), ~€85–110. (Guesthouse
  play: Vila Veselova, 4.3/350, by Tivoli park.)

## Day 2 — Castle hill, market morning, alternative afternoon

- **09:00** — **Ljubljana Castle** by funicular
  (~€18 with entry): the watchtower's red-roof
  panorama to the Alps, the excellent
  time-machine museum, and ramparts kids
  actually enjoy.
- **11:30** — Plečnik's **Central Market**
  colonnade (the architect who single-handedly
  drew this city — his riverside colonnades,
  bridges and lamps are everywhere once you
  notice): fruit, honey shops, and — Fridays
  mid-March–October — **Open Kitchen (Odprta
  kuhna)**, the street-food market where half
  the city lunches (€8–14).
- **14:30** — Pick your Ljubljana: **Tivoli
  Park's** promenades, the National Gallery, or
  **Metelkova** — the ex-barracks autonomous art
  quarter, graffiti-deep and gallery-odd (daytime
  browse; evening it becomes the alt-nightlife
  yard).
- **18:00** — Golden hour back on the bridges;
  gelato at Cacao or Vigò (the town takes sides;
  taste both, file a ruling).

## Day 3 — To Bled: the island, the bell, the cake

- **09:00** — Bus to **Bled** (75 min); check in,
  jaws adjust: the lake really is that green, the
  island really does float a baroque church, the
  clifftop castle really has been up there a
  thousand years.
- **11:00** — **Pletna to the island** (**€20
  return, cash** — the flat-bottomed boats have
  been rowed by the same families for centuries;
  40 minutes ashore): climb the 99 steps, pay the
  **church + bell tower (€12)** and **ring the
  wishing bell** — three rings, one wish, a
  tradition nobody outgrows. *(DIY romance:
  row yourselves for €25/hour — harder than the
  boatmen make it look.)*
- **14:00** — Lakeside lunch, then the mandatory
  paperwork: **Bled cream cake (kremšnita,
  ~€5.50)** at the Park Hotel café that invented
  it — 60 years, 15 million slices, one correct
  order.
- **17:00** — The **Ojstrica viewpoint hike**
  (45 min up from the western shore, steep but
  short): THE Lake Bled photograph — island,
  church, castle, Alps — earned on foot for
  free. Sunset slot recommended; bring the
  headtorch for the way down.
- **20:00** — Dinner in the village (trout,
  žlikrofi pockets — €15–25 mains).

**Where to stay in Bled** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Vila Bled** — 4.4/5 (~550
  reviews), Tito's actual lakeside villa turned
  hotel — presidential terraces, private lido.
  ~€250+. (Grand Hotel Toplice, 4.2/1,200, is
  the 1931 lakefront classic.)
- *Mid-range:* **Garni Hotel Berc** — 4.9/5
  (~860 reviews; the town's quiet superstar),
  chalet comfort five minutes up from the water.
  ~€120–150. (Sister Penzion Berc, 4.8/600,
  next door.)
- *Value:* **Old Parish House** — 4.6/5 (~140
  reviews), simple rooms by St Martin's church
  with lake views at ~€90–110.

## Day 4 — Vintgar's boardwalks, Bohinj's wild twin

- **08:30** — **Vintgar Gorge** early (bus/
  shuttle 15 min; **~€10**, open ~April–
  October): 1.6 km of wooden boardwalks pinned
  to the canyon wall over water so turquoise it
  looks lit from below. By 10:30 it's a queue;
  at 8:30 it's a hymn.
- **12:00** — Bus onward to **Lake Bohinj**
  (40 min, €4): Bled's bigger, wilder,
  crowd-free sibling inside Triglav National
  Park — swim from the church-end beach (alpine-
  fresh, gloriously legal, best July–September),
  or ride the **Vogel cable car (~€28)** up for
  the top-of-the-park panorama; the **Savica
  waterfall** walk (~€4, 25 min up) fills a
  spare hour.
- **17:30** — Back to Bled for **Bled Castle**
  (**~€18**, open till late in summer): ramparts
  1,000 years old, a print-your-own-certificate
  medieval press, and the lake going gold below
  the terrace — the day's second great sunset
  option.
- **20:00** — Farewell-Bled dinner; pack for an
  underground morning.

## Day 5 — Postojna Cave and the castle in the cliff

- **08:30** — Travel logistics, honestly: Bled →
  Postojna is the route's one awkward hop by
  bus (via Ljubljana, ~2.5 h total) — EITHER
  bus back to Ljubljana, drop bags, and take
  the direct Postojna bus (1 h, €7); OR rent a
  car for this one day; OR book the
  Ljubljana-based **cave + castle tour**
  (~€95 pp incl entries) and let someone else
  drive. All three work; the guide budgets DIY.
- **11:00** — **Postojna Cave** (**€34.90; the
  Two Adventures combo with Predjama €49.90 —
  official site only**): an electric train
  rattles you 2 km INTO the mountain, then a
  1.5 km walk through halls of curtains,
  spaghetti ceilings and the five-metre
  "Brilliant" stalagmite — plus the aquarium
  of **olms**, the blind, pale "baby dragons"
  that live a century. **It's 10°C inside
  year-round: bring the jacket.**
- **14:30** — **Predjama Castle** (shuttle or
  9-min drive): the world's largest cave
  castle — a Renaissance fortress grouted into
  a 123-metre cliff mouth, complete with the
  secret tunnel its robber-baron used to
  outlast a siege (his story ends
  mid-toilet-visit; the audio guide tells it
  gleefully).
- **17:30** — Bus back to Ljubljana; final
  riverside evening — Open Kitchen if it's
  Friday, dragon-bridge night photos either
  way.

## Day 6 — Fly home

- Morning coffee on the embankment, airport
  bus (€3–5, 45 min) or the GoOpti shuttle
  back to Venice for the Emirates non-stop.
  Honey, pumpkin-seed oil and a tiny dragon
  in the bag.

## What to pack

- Layers: alpine mornings at 12°C, lake
  afternoons at 27°C, and **a proper jacket for
  the 10°C cave** — the guide's least-ignored
  packing line.
- Walking shoes with grip (Ojstrica's roots,
  Vintgar's boards after spray).
- **Swimwear + a quick towel** — Bled and
  Bohinj are swimming lakes, and regretting
  dry kit at that water colour is a known
  medical condition.
- Cash float for pletna boatmen and mountain
  huts.
- **European Type C/F adapter**, headtorch
  for sunset-hike descents.
- Room in the bag: pumpkin-seed oil and
  forest honey are Slovenia's edible
  souvenirs.

## FAQ

**Is Slovenia safe?**
One of Europe's safest countries, full stop —
solo travellers, families, midnight strolls
along the river: all unremarkable. The
watch-list is nature's, and mild: alpine
weather turns fast above Bohinj, and the
lakes are cold ten metres out. That's the
list.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Vegetarians thrive: štruklji, gibanica
(the layered pastry), mushroom soups in
bread bowls, žlikrofi with cheese, and
Ljubljana's café scene runs deep with
veg-first menus. Halal is thinner —
Ljubljana has kebab-grill reliability and
Bosnian **burek** bakeries (the cheese and
spinach ones are everywhere and excellent),
but certified restaurants are few; the
seafood/veg default plus market
self-catering covers the lakes. Note
trout is the regional pride dish —
pescatarian-halal travellers eat like
royalty.

**Bled or Bohinj — and is Bled 'too
touristy'?**
Bled earns the fame: the composition of
island-church-castle-Alps has no equal, and
mornings/evenings belong to walkers, not
buses. Middays in July–August, yes, it's a
scrum — which is why this guide sleeps
there (own the golden hours) and spends the
midday at Bohinj, which locals will tell
you, quietly, is the one THEY love. Do
both; that's the design.

**Do we need a car?**
No — buses run this entire route, and
Ljubljana/Bled reward the car-free. A
one-day rental (or the €95 tour) solves
the only clumsy leg (Bled→Postojna).
Drivers who do rent get bonus reach: the
Vršič Pass and Soča valley — see guide
#2.

**Can we combine Slovenia with Venice or
Croatia?**
Beautifully — that's the region's gift.
Venice is 2.5 h by shuttle (our
[Italy guide](/itineraries/italy-10-days-guide/) has
the city), Zagreb 2 h, and our
[Croatia guide](/itineraries/croatia-8-days-guide/)'s
Dalmatia is a half-day's drive or short
hop south. One Schengen visa spans
Slovenia–Italy; Croatia's now Schengen
too — the triangle is one trip for the
ambitious.

**What's Slovenia guide #2?**
**The Soča Valley** — the emerald-river
adventure west (Vršič Pass, Kobarid,
rafting the unreal-blue water) — or the
**Kotor add-on** (Montenegro's bay, where
Schengen-visa holders enter visa-free).
Votes welcome; both on the list.
