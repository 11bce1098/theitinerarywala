Senegal is the country where West Africa is easiest to meet on its
own terms. **Dakar** is a real capital — loud, fast, and the music
capital of the region, with a club scene built on *mbalax* that
has produced musicians the rest of the world has heard of.

Twenty minutes offshore sits **Gorée**, a car-free island of
ochre colonial houses and bougainvillea, with the **Maison des
Esclaves** and its Door of No Return at the centre of it. The
scale of what passed through Gorée itself is **genuinely disputed
by historians** — and the island's significance as a place of
memory is not.

North, **Saint-Louis** was the capital of French West Africa and
is now a faded grid on a sandbar between river and ocean. Beyond
it, **Djoudj** receives a million migratory birds that have just
crossed the Sahara. South, the **Sine-Saloum delta** is mangrove,
pirogues and islands built from centuries of discarded shells.

## Before you go: visas, language and the season

### Entry and health

- **Many nationalities enter visa-free** for 90 days, including
  the EU, UK, US and Canada. **Check your own case** — the list
  is generous but not universal.
- **Yellow fever vaccination** is required if arriving from an
  endemic country and is strongly advised regardless.
- **Malaria is present year-round**, with higher transmission in
  the south and in the rainy season. Take prophylaxis.

### Language, and why it matters here

- **French is the official language** and the one you will need —
  Senegal is markedly less anglophone than Ghana, and outside
  hotels very little English is spoken.
- **Wolof is what people actually speak.** Learn *salamaleikum*
  (the greeting), *jërëjëf* (thank you) and *ba beneen* (goodbye)
  and you will be met with genuine warmth.
- ***Teranga*** — hospitality — is the national self-description
  and it is not marketing. Expect to be fed.

### Getting around

- **Hire a car with a driver.** Roads between the main points are
  tarred and fine; Dakar traffic is severe and navigation outside
  the city is not obvious. A driver costs far less than the
  equivalent in Europe.
- ***Sept-places*** — shared Peugeot estates carrying seven — run
  between every town and cost almost nothing. Worth doing once.
- **Dakar traffic is the single biggest time sink.** The new
  Diamniadio motorway helps; the city itself does not.
- **Blaise Diagne (DSS) is 45 km from Dakar**, about an hour, and
  this catches people out on departure day.

### When to go

- **November to May is the dry season** and the time to come.
  **December to February** is the most comfortable — warm days,
  cool nights, and the bird migration at Djoudj at full volume.
- **June to October is the rainy season**: humid, hot, with heavy
  afternoon storms. The delta is green and the mosquitoes are
  serious.
- **The harmattan** can blow dust in December and January, which
  flattens the light.

### Money

- **West African CFA franc (XOF)**, pegged to the euro at a fixed
  rate, which removes any currency guesswork.
- **Cash dominates.** Cards work in Dakar hotels and almost
  nowhere else. Draw CFA in Dakar.
- **Orange Money** is the mobile wallet everyone uses.

## Day 1 — Land in Dakar

- **15:00** — Land at **Blaise Diagne (DSS)**, 45 km out — about
  an hour into the city on the motorway.
- **16:30** — Stay in **Almadies** for calm and the coast, or
  **Plateau** for the centre and the museums.
- **18:00** — The **Corniche** at **Pointe des Almadies**, **the
  westernmost point of mainland Africa**, where the Atlantic
  arrives with nothing between here and Brazil.
- **19:00** — Dinner. ***Thieboudienne*** — rice cooked in tomato
  with fish and vegetables, the national dish and the origin of
  jollof — eaten from a shared platter, with the right hand.
- **21:30** — If it is a weekend, **live music**. Dakar's clubs
  start very late and *mbalax* is played by bands with four
  percussionists. Ask where Youssou N'Dour's **Thiossane** is
  playing.

## Day 2 — Gorée Island

- **09:00** — The **ferry from Dakar port**, 20 minutes, and it is
  a proper little crossing with the city receding behind you.
- **09:30** — **Gorée**. No cars, no tarmac — narrow lanes of
  ochre and pink colonial houses with bougainvillea over the
  walls, which is disarmingly beautiful given what the island is
  for.
- **10:00** — The ***Maison des Esclaves***, built 1776, with its
  holding cells, the weighing room, and the **Door of No Return**
  opening straight onto the sea. **Be aware of the honest
  historical position:** scholars dispute the numbers that passed
  through Gorée specifically, with serious estimates far lower
  than the figures often quoted. What is not disputed is that this
  island was part of the trade, and that it has become the place
  where it is remembered — Mandela came, Obama came, and the curator
  Joseph Ndiaye made it what it is.
- **12:00** — The **Castel**, the basalt hill at the island's end,
  with its colonial gun emplacements and the view back to Dakar.
- **13:30** — Lunch at a terrace restaurant on the sand.
- **15:30** — The **IFAN Historical Museum**, and the artists'
  studios — Gorée has a long-standing community of painters and
  the sand paintings are made here.
- **17:30** — Last ferry back, and check the time when you arrive
  because they stop earlier than you expect.
- **20:00** — Dinner in Dakar.

## Day 3 — Dakar, and the Renaissance

- **09:00** — The ***Monument de la Renaissance Africaine***, a
  52 m bronze on a hill above the city — **taller than the Statue
  of Liberty**, built by North Korean labour, controversial from
  the day it was proposed, and you can go up inside the man's head
  for the view.
- **10:30** — The **Musée des Civilisations Noires**, opened 2018
  and genuinely excellent — a continental rather than national
  collection, and explicit about the objects still held in
  European museums.
- **13:00** — Lunch at the **Marché Kermel**, a circular colonial
  market hall.
- **14:30** — **Marché Sandaga** or the **Village des Arts**,
  where artists work in converted barracks and will talk to you
  for an hour.
- **16:30** — **Lac Rose (Lac Retba)**, an hour out: a lake so
  saline that **algae turn it pink** in the dry season and strong
  afternoon light, with salt collectors standing waist-deep
  shovelling into pirogues. The colour varies and some visits get
  brown water — it is weather-dependent and worth knowing before
  you drive out.
- **19:30** — Back for dinner.

## Day 4 — North to Saint-Louis

- **08:00** — Drive north, about four and a half hours on good
  road through increasingly dry country.
- **12:30** — **Saint-Louis (Ndar)**, founded 1659, **the first
  French settlement in Africa** and the capital of French West
  Africa until 1902. The old town sits on a narrow island in the
  Senegal River, joined to the mainland by the **Pont Faidherbe**,
  a 500 m iron bridge.
- **13:30** — Lunch, then walk the grid: balconied colonial
  houses, many of them beautifully decayed, in a UNESCO-listed
  quarter that is being restored slowly and is still lived in.
- **16:00** — Cross to the **Langue de Barbarie**, the sand spit
  between river and ocean, and **Guet N'Dar** — a fishing village
  of extraordinary density, with painted pirogues drawn up on the
  beach in their hundreds.
- **18:00** — Sunset from the bridge.
- **20:00** — Dinner, and **Saint-Louis is a jazz town** — the
  festival is in May and the bars play it year-round.

## Day 5 — Djoudj, and the river

- **07:00** — **Djoudj National Bird Sanctuary**, an hour north on
  the Mauritanian border. This is **the third most important bird
  reserve in the world** and the first fresh water migrating birds
  reach after crossing the Sahara — **around a million arrive
  each winter**.
- **08:00** — Out by **pirogue** through the channels: **great
  white pelicans in colonies of thousands**, flamingos, herons,
  spoonbills, cormorants and African fish eagles. **Peak is
  November to April**; outside that there is far less.
- **12:00** — Back, and lunch.
- **14:30** — Return to Saint-Louis, or push on to the **Langue de
  Barbarie national park** at the spit's southern end, which has
  turtles and a quieter birdlife.
- **17:00** — The old town again at the good hour, and the
  **Maison Rose** or another restored building for a drink on a
  roof.
- **19:30** — Dinner.

## Day 6 — South to the Sine-Saloum

- **07:30** — The long leg south, five to six hours, skirting
  Dakar on the motorway — the one stretch where traffic may cost
  you an hour.
- **13:00** — Lunch at **Mbour** or **Joal-Fadiouth**.
- **14:30** — **Fadiouth**, and stop here: an island joined to the
  mainland by a wooden footbridge and **built entirely of clam
  shells** — the paths, the walls, the ground underfoot,
  accumulated over centuries. Its **cemetery is shared between
  Muslims and Christians**, side by side on a shell hill, which in
  this region is unremarkable and elsewhere would not be.
- **16:30** — On into the **Sine-Saloum delta**, where the Saloum
  river meets the Atlantic across 180,000 hectares of mangrove,
  creeks and sand islands.
- **18:00** — Check in at a *campement* at **Toubacouta**,
  **Ndangane** or **Palmarin**.
- **19:30** — Dinner, and the delta is very quiet after dark.

## Day 7 — The delta by pirogue

- **07:00** — Out by **pirogue** at first light, which is when the
  birds move. The delta holds pelicans, flamingos, ospreys and
  royal terns, and the **Île aux Oiseaux** colonies are the
  concentration.
- **09:30** — Into the **mangrove channels** — narrow enough that
  the branches close over the boat, with oysters growing on the
  roots, which is how the local economy works.
- **11:30** — A **shell island**, where mounds of discarded shells
  several metres deep record a thousand years of occupation, with
  baobabs growing out of them and burial tumuli among them.
- **13:30** — Lunch on a sandbank, usually grilled fish.
- **15:30** — A village visit, or **Palmarin** for the lagoon,
  where hyena and jackal are sometimes seen at dusk.
- **18:00** — Sunset over the water. The delta at this hour is the
  best hour of the trip.
- **20:00** — Dinner at the campement.

## Day 8 — The delta, slow, and back toward Dakar

- **08:00** — A second pirogue, or **kayaks** through the
  mangroves, which gets you quieter and closer.
- **10:30** — **Toubacouta** and the Niombato villages, or the
  **Fathala reserve** if you want giraffe and rhino, which are
  reintroduced and fenced rather than wild.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then drive north.
- **16:00** — **Somone lagoon** or **Toubab Dialaw** on the Petite
  Côte if you want a last beach afternoon — Toubab Dialaw is a
  cliff village of artists and is the nicer of the two.
- **18:30** — Into Dakar, or sleep near the airport at
  **Diamniadio** to avoid the morning run.
- **20:00** — Last dinner, and buy music — the record shops in
  Dakar are real ones.

## Day 9 — Fly home

- **Morning** — **Allow two hours to the airport** from Dakar.
  DSS is 45 km out and the motorway is reliable, but the city end
  is not.
- **Last stop** — Sand paintings from Gorée, *bissap* syrup, and
  a *boubou* if you have been measured for one.
- **Note** — CFA francs are used across eight West African
  countries, so they are worth keeping if you are travelling on —
  and worthless if you are not.

## What to pack

- **Light, modest cotton.** Shoulders and knees covered away from
  the beach is the norm and is noticed.
- **Serious repellent and long sleeves for dusk**, plus malaria
  prophylaxis.
- **Binoculars** — Djoudj and the delta are the two best bird days
  on this site and they are wasted without them.
- **A hat and sun cream.** There is very little shade anywhere
  north of Dakar.
- **Cash in CFA**, drawn in Dakar, in small notes.
- **European Type C/D/E plugs**, 230V.
- **Some French.** Even a little transforms the trip.

## FAQ

**What is the honest position on Gorée?**
Historians dispute the scale of what passed through this
particular island — serious estimates are far lower than the
figures often repeated by guides — and some argue the island's
role has been overstated relative to Saint-Louis or the Gambia
river. None of that is in dispute: Gorée was part of the trade,
the building is real, and it has become the place where this is
remembered. Go, listen, and read afterwards.

**Senegal or Ghana for a first West African trip?**
**Ghana if you want English** and the heavier, better-documented
slave-trade sites. **Senegal if you want French, better music and
more varied landscape** — the delta and Djoudj have no Ghanaian
equivalent. Both are among the easiest countries in the region.

**Is it safe?**
Yes, for this route, with ordinary city caution in Dakar —
pickpocketing and persistent hustling rather than anything worse.
**The Casamance**, in the far south beyond the Gambia, has had a
long-running separatist conflict and carries advisories; it is not
on this itinerary.

**Do I need French?**
Effectively yes. Senegal is much less anglophone than Ghana, and
outside Dakar hotels you will need it or a driver who translates.

**When exactly for the birds?**
**November to March**, with **December to February** the peak at
Djoudj. Outside that the sanctuary is a fraction of itself.
