America's East Coast is the rare first-timer route that needs no
car, no road-trip stamina and no navigation braver than a subway
map. New York is the world's loudest museum of itself; Washington
DC counters with the world's best free museums, full stop; and
Niagara — a one-hour flight, not the ten-hour drive people
imagine — ends the trip with the continent's great waterfall
thundering an arm's length from the boardwalk.

This is a **train-and-flight route**: Amtrak city-centre to
city-centre, one short hop to Buffalo, and not a single rental-car
form. Ten days, three completely different Americas.

**Go April–June or September–early November.** Summer works but
queues and humidity double; late November–March is a legitimate
(and cheaper) snow-globe version — with one big caveat at Niagara
covered below. All costs are in US dollars (USD).

## Before you go: the visa is the trip's hardest part

Read this section first. If your passport needs a US visa, it now
costs more than the New York hotel and takes longer than everything
else combined; if it doesn't, you can skip most of what follows.

### The B1/B2 visitor visa, priced honestly

- **Application (MRV) fee: ~$185** per person. On approval, most
  nationalities including India also pay the new **Visa Integrity
  Fee of ~$250**, introduced October 2025. Real total: **~$435 per
  person** before you've booked anything.
- The consolation: many nationalities, India among them, are
  typically issued a **10-year, multiple-entry** B1/B2 — brutal up
  front, cheap per trip if America is in your next decade.
- **Visa Waiver Program passports** (UK, EU, Japan, Singapore and
  ~40 others) skip all of this: **ESTA ~$40** online, and ESTA
  travellers are exempt from the Integrity Fee.

### Where you apply, and the 2026 waits

- Interview waits vary enormously by post — weeks at some
  consulates, the better part of a year at others. Check the live
  figure for yours at ustraveldocs.com before you plan anything
  else, because it sets the timeline for the whole trip.
- How bad it can get: routine visa services at the US Embassy Abu
  Dhabi and Consulate Dubai were **suspended in July 2026** over
  regional security, and only limited nonimmigrant appointments
  have been released since — recent published waits are **Dubai
  ~11–12 months, Abu Dhabi ~6 months**. Posts in India run many
  months too, varying by consulate.
- You apply where you live, with proof of residence. Where a
  country has several posts, the quieter one can save months, so
  check them all before booking.
- Whichever post: **start 8–12 months before you want to fly**,
  and treat any agent promising "fast US appointments" as a scam
  until proven otherwise.
- Interview answers that work are boring ones: employment, return
  ticket logic, itinerary. Never fabricate bookings — refundable
  real ones cost nothing extra.

### Money, tipping and the price-tag trap

- **No price in America is the price.** Sales tax (~8.875% in NYC)
  is added at the register, and hotels add taxes and sometimes
  "destination fees" of $25–40/night — always compare the
  *total* price when booking.
- **Tipping is not optional**: 18–20% at sit-down restaurants,
  $1–2 per drink at bars, a few dollars for hotel housekeeping and
  taxi drivers. Budget it like a tax, because functionally it is.
- Cards/phone-tap work everywhere; $100–200 cash for the whole
  trip is plenty.

### SIM, plugs, kids

- eSIMs (~$15–25 for 10 days) beat US carrier tourist SIMs on
  price; coverage is excellent on this whole route.
- Plugs: **Type A/B, 120V** — UAE/India devices need the adapter.
- **Kids:** this route is superb for them — Central Park,
  dinosaur halls, a rocket-filled free museum, and a boat into a
  waterfall. Under-6s ride the Maid of the Mist free, under-6s
  enter SUMMIT free, and kids' menus are a national institution.

## Day 1 — Land in New York, Midtown by night

Stay in **Midtown** (walk to everything, noisier) or **Chelsea**
(calmer, great food, 10 minutes from everything by subway).

- **Afternoon** — Land at JFK (direct 14-hour flights from the
  Gulf). Into Manhattan: **AirTrain ($8.50) + subway ($3)** —
  50–75 min, or a flat-rate yellow-cab ~$70 + tolls + tip.
- **First tap matters:** the subway is **OMNY** — tap any
  contactless card or phone, $3.00 a ride, and the same card caps
  itself at **$34/week** (Mon–Sun), after which rides are free. No
  tourist card needed; just use one card consistently.
- **18:00** — Walk **Fifth Avenue → Rockefeller Center → Times
  Square** as the lights come up. Times Square is best treated
  like a firework: stare, photograph, leave.
- **20:00** — First dinner, New York style: a proper slice joint
  (Joe's Pizza), or the original **Halal Guys cart** at 53rd/6th —
  the $12 platter that launched a thousand imitators.

**Where to stay in New York** (five nights)

- *Luxury:* **Casablanca Hotel by Library Hotel Collection** —
  4.8/5 on TripAdvisor across ~7,800 reviews, a Moroccan-themed
  boutique hiding 50 metres off Times Square with free evening
  wine and cheese. ~$400–550.
- *Mid-range:* **Motto by Hilton New York City Chelsea** — 4.8/5
  (~2,200 reviews), new, compact-smart rooms by the High Line.
  ~$220–300.
- *Budget:* **YOTEL New York Times Square** — 4.1/5 (~15,400
  reviews), pod-style cabins, Hudson Yards' doorstep. ~$150–220.

## Day 2 — Liberty, Ground Zero, Brooklyn Bridge

The downtown day. Book the first ferry — the queue difference
between 9:00 and 11:00 is an hour of your life.

- **08:30** — **Battery Park**, security lines for the **Statue of
  Liberty & Ellis Island ferry** (Statue City Cruises, ~$24 —
  included in CityPASS). **Crown access** is a separate few-dollar
  add-on that sells out **months** ahead — book it the day your
  visa is stamped, or settle happily for the pedestal.
- **09:00–13:00** — Liberty Island, then **Ellis Island's
  Immigration Museum** — where 12 million arrivals were processed;
  unexpectedly the most moving stop of the day.
- **13:30** — Lunch back on Manhattan: **Stone Street's**
  cobbled-lane restaurants, ~$20–25.
- **14:30** — **Wall Street**, the Charging Bull, Federal Hall,
  then the **9/11 Memorial pools** (free, open plaza) and the
  **9/11 Museum** (~$33, CityPASS choice option; allow 2 hours,
  heavy but essential). Look up at the **Oculus** on the way out.
- **17:45** — Subway to Brooklyn's **DUMBO**, the Instagram
  cobblestones, then **walk the Brooklyn Bridge back to Manhattan
  at sunset** — Brooklyn→Manhattan is the correct direction; the
  skyline builds in front of you the whole mile.
- **20:00** — Dinner in Chinatown (Joe's Shanghai soup dumplings)
  or Little Italy, ~$25–35.

## Day 3 — Midtown icons and a Broadway night

- **09:00** — **Grand Central Terminal** (the whispering gallery,
  the celestial ceiling — free) and the **New York Public
  Library's** marble Rose Reading Room, with **Bryant Park**
  between them.
- **11:00** — Pick your first skyline deck (see the box below):
  the classic move is **Empire State Building 86th floor** (~$44,
  CityPASS-included, open till very late).
- **13:00** — Lunch: **Urbanspace or The Hugh food halls**
  (~$15–20) — Midtown's office workers know the value.
- **14:30** — **MoMA** (~$30; Van Gogh's Starry Night, Monet's
  Water Lilies) or, with kids, skip ahead to Central Park's
  southern loop: the Zoo, the Mall, Bethesda Terrace, rowboats.
- **17:00** — **TKTS booth under the red steps, Times Square**:
  same-day Broadway seats at 30–50% off. Musicals sell from ~$70
  this way; The Lion King forgives every dollar.
- **19:00** — Pre-theatre dinner on **Restaurant Row** (46th St),
  then curtain at 20:00. This is the night the city was built for.

**Pick your observation deck — you need one, maybe two, not four**

- **Empire State 86F (~$44):** the icon, open past 22:00 — go for
  the building itself and night views *from* the classic.
- **Top of the Rock (~$40):** the best *view of* the Empire State
  and Central Park. Photographers' choice.
- **SUMMIT One Vanderbilt ($43 weekdays / $49 weekends, +$10
  sunset slots):** the mirrored art-experience — spectacular and
  chaotic; wear trousers, the floors reflect.
- The CityPASS includes Empire State plus Top of the Rock *or*
  EDGE as a choice — doing one included deck plus SUMMIT at sunset
  is the strongest combination.

## Day 4 — Central Park and the museum heavyweights

- **08:30** — Bagel-and-lox breakfast (Ess-a-Bagel or Zabar's,
  ~$8–14) — non-negotiable New York.
- **09:30** — **Central Park's** greatest-hits walk: the Mall →
  Bethesda Fountain → Bow Bridge → Strawberry Fields' *Imagine*
  mosaic. 2–3 unhurried hours; rowboats ~$25/hour in season.
- **12:30** — **The Metropolitan Museum of Art** (~$30,
  CityPASS-included): the Temple of Dendur — an actual Egyptian
  temple — arms and armor for the kids, the rooftop bar in
  season. 3 hours minimum; a lifetime available.
- **16:30** — Cross the park to the **American Museum of Natural
  History** (~$28, CityPASS-included, open to 17:30): the T. rex,
  the blue whale, the space halls. With kids, invert the day and
  do this first.
- **19:30** — Dinner on the Upper West Side, or subway to
  **Harlem** for fried chicken and waffles at a gospel-brunch
  institution's dinner service (~$25–30).

## Day 5 — The High Line, the Village, downtown cool

- **09:30** — Walk the **High Line** end to end (free) — the
  elevated-railway park, Hudson Yards' **Vessel** at the top.
- **11:30** — **Chelsea Market** for lunch stalls (lobster rolls,
  tacos, ~$15–20).
- **13:30** — Wander **Greenwich Village**: Washington Square
  Arch, the *Friends* building on Bedford St, jazz-club doorways,
  then **SoHo's** cast-iron streets for the shopping hour.
- **17:00** — Optional last deck: **EDGE's** glass-floor corner at
  Hudson Yards, or simply the free **Staten Island Ferry** round
  trip past the Statue at golden hour — the best $0 in America.
- **20:00** — Farewell-to-NYC dinner in the West Village
  (~$40–60 with tip) — book anything you've dreamed about two
  weeks ahead.
- Pack tonight; tomorrow the trip changes cities by train.

## Day 6 — Amtrak south, monuments by evening

- **08:00** — **Moynihan Train Hall** (Penn Station's gorgeous new
  concourse): the **Amtrak Northeast Regional** to Washington —
  ~3h20, from **$22–49 booked ~4 weeks ahead** (walk-up fares
  triple; the 2h56 Acela costs $120+ and buys you 25 minutes).
  City centre to city centre, no airport, 18 departures a day.
- **11:30** — Into **Union Station's** great hall; drop bags at
  the hotel (DC Metro: tap the same contactless card, $2–2.65).
- **13:00** — Lunch, then the **National Mall monument loop** on
  foot or by Circulator bus: **Washington Monument** (exterior;
  going up needs a $1 timed ticket released 30 days ahead) →
  **WWII Memorial** → the Reflecting Pool → **Lincoln Memorial**
  up the steps where King spoke → **Vietnam and Korean War
  memorials**.
- **18:30** — **Georgetown** for the evening: federal-era streets,
  the C&O canal, cupcake wars, dinner on M Street (~$25–40).

**Where to stay in Washington DC** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **InterContinental The Willard** — 4.6/5 (~5,800
  reviews), the 1818 grande dame two blocks from the White House
  where the word "lobbyist" was allegedly coined. ~$350–500.
- *Mid-range:* **Motto by Hilton Washington DC Downtown** — 4.6/5
  (~2,100 reviews), sharp new rooms walkable to the Mall.
  ~$160–230.
- *Budget:* **Holiday Inn Washington Capitol** — 3.9/5 (~3,400
  reviews), unbeatable location one block from the Air & Space
  Museum; families rate the rooftop pool. ~$130–190.

## Day 7 — The free museum mile (yes, actually free)

Every **Smithsonian museum is free** — the catch is passes, not
prices.

- **08:30** — Walk-up line at the **National Air and Space
  Museum** if you missed the free timed passes (released ~a month
  ahead online; a same-day batch drops 08:30). Inside: the Wright
  Flyer, Apollo 11's column, touchable moon rock.
- **11:30** — **National Museum of Natural History** (walk-in,
  free): the Hope Diamond and the elephant rotunda.
- **13:00** — Lunch at a food truck row on the Mall or the
  National Gallery's café (~$12–18).
- **14:00** — Choose your afternoon heavyweight:
  - **National Museum of African American History and Culture** —
    the Smithsonian's masterpiece; needs timed passes on weekends,
    but **weekday afternoons walk in** (from 10:00 September–
    February).
  - **National Gallery of Art** (free, walk-in) — Da Vinci's only
    painting in the Americas.
  - With kids: the **National Zoo** (free, but book the free entry
    pass online).
- **17:30** — **White House** north-lawn photo stop (interior
  tours need requests via your embassy months ahead — most
  visitors skip) and the **Capitol** dome from the west terrace.
- **20:00** — Dinner at **The Wharf**, DC's waterfront strip, then
  see the **Lincoln Memorial lit at night** — the monuments after
  dark, uncrowded, are the city's best secret. Pack for an early
  flight-day tomorrow.

## Day 8 — Fly to Buffalo, first sight of the falls

- **09:00** — Optional final DC hour: **Arlington National
  Cemetery** (free; Changing of the Guard on the hour) — it's
  10 minutes from the airport side of the river.
- **12:00–14:00** — **DCA (Reagan) → Buffalo BUF**: ~1h25
  nonstop, from ~$80–150 booked a month out.
- **15:00** — Taxi/Uber from Buffalo airport to **Niagara Falls,
  NY**: ~40 minutes, ~$55–75. (Public NFTA buses exist at a few
  dollars but crawl with luggage.)
- **16:30** — Drop bags, then walk straight into **Niagara Falls
  State Park** — **free entry, open dawn to late** — for the
  first look from **Prospect Point**: the American Falls
  underfoot, the Horseshoe beyond.
- **19:00** — Dinner (see Day 9 picks), then return for the
  **nightly illumination** — the falls floodlit in colour every
  evening, year-round, with fireworks on summer/holiday dates.

**Where to stay at Niagara Falls, USA side** (two nights)

- *Charm/luxury:* **The Red Coach Inn** — 4.6/5 (~2,300 reviews),
  a 1923 English-Tudor inn overlooking the upper rapids, 400 m
  from the brink; its restaurant is the town's best table.
  ~$180–280.
- *Mid-range:* **Sheraton Niagara Falls** — 4.2/5 (~5,100
  reviews), the full-service block opposite the park entrance.
  ~$130–200.
- *Budget:* **Comfort Inn The Pointe** — 4.1/5 (~1,450 reviews),
  closest budget beds to the park gate, breakfast included.
  ~$100–150.

## Day 9 — Into the waterfall

- **08:30** — Walk to **Goat Island** (the park island splitting
  the falls) for empty-morning views from **Terrapin Point** —
  the Horseshoe Falls' brink, arguably the single best free view
  on either side of the border.
- **09:30** — **Maid of the Mist** (from the Observation Tower,
  **$30.25 adult / $19.75 child 6–12 / under 5 free**): the
  1846-vintage boat ride into the Horseshoe's spray. Ponchos
  provided; you get wet anyway; it's the point. **Season: late
  April to early November** (2026: Apr 24–Nov 8) — outside it,
  the boats stop.
- **11:30** — **Cave of the Winds** ($23 summer / $14 winter):
  the elevator into the gorge and — late May to mid-October —
  the **Hurricane Deck**, wooden walkways 6 metres from Bridal
  Veil Falls in 60+ km/h spray. Between the boat and this, this
  is the one that soaks and thrills harder.
- **13:30** — Lunch at the **Top of the Falls** restaurant on Goat
  Island or back on Old Falls Street (~$18–25).
- **15:00** — The quiet hours: **Three Sisters Islands** in the
  rapids, **Luna Island** between the falls, or ride 6 km north
  to **Whirlpool State Park**'s violent turquoise bend in the
  gorge.
- **17:30** — Golden hour back at Prospect Point; the mist makes
  rainbows on cue on sunny evenings.
- **19:30** — Farewell dinner at the **Red Coach Inn** (book a
  window table) — then one last illuminated-falls walk.

**The Canada question, answered honestly:** the famous postcard
panorama is from the Canadian side, and the **Rainbow Bridge is a
full international border**. Plenty of passports cross visa-free,
but many others — India among them — need a separate Canadian
visitor visa, and a US visa does not waive it (weeks to months of
processing). If you can cross, walk over for an afternoon
($1 toll) and the view will justify the paperwork. If not, don't
mourn: the US side is the *touch-the-falls* side — the brink, the
boat and the cave are all here.

## Day 10 — Home via New York

- **Morning** — Last mist-rainbow walk if the flight allows.
- **~11:00** — Taxi to Buffalo airport; fly home with one
  connection — **BUF → JFK/EWR** then the Gulf, on one ticket.
  (Toronto's mega-hub is 1.5 h away but sits across that same
  Canadian border — only useful if you carry the Canada visa.)
- Ten days, three Americas, zero rental cars.

## What to pack

- Real walking shoes: 15,000–20,000-step days are the New York
  standard.
- Layers for all three stops — Niagara runs 5–10°C cooler than
  NYC, and the boat spray is cold even in July.
- Sandals or fast-dry shoes for Cave of the Winds (they issue
  souvenir sandals, but small kids do better in their own).
- Type A/B plug adapter (120V), contactless card set up for
  tap-to-pay (the subway *is* your metro card).
- A dressier layer for the Broadway night — New York notices.
- Winter travellers (Nov–Mar): proper coat, hat and gloves —
  Niagara's frozen mist is magnificent and merciless.

## FAQ

**Is 10 days enough?**
For these three, yes — 5+2+2 with two travel half-days is the
honest minimum that doesn't feel robbed. The classic upgrade is a
Philadelphia stop (1h15 on the same Amtrak line, between NYC and
DC) with an 11th day.

**What's the single biggest planning mistake?**
Underestimating the visa timeline. Appointment waits from the UAE
have run **6–12 months** through 2026 (services were suspended
outright in July 2026 and remain limited), and India's queues are
long too. Everything else on this trip can be booked in a month;
the visa cannot.

**Can we do Niagara in winter?**
Yes, with eyes open: the **Maid of the Mist stops (Nov 8, 2026)**
and the Hurricane Deck comes down, but Cave of the Winds runs a
gorge-level winter version ($14), the illumination continues
nightly, and the frozen falls-scape — ice bridges, crystallised
trees — is genuinely otherworldly. Cheaper hotels, too. Bring
Arctic-grade layers.

**Is New York safe? The subway?**
By big-city standards, yes — Midtown, downtown and the park are
heavily walked at all hours. Standard city sense applies: stay in
the busier carriages late at night, keep phones off the platform
edge, ignore anyone "helping" you buy tickets. Niagara's park is
safe and busy; the city blocks beyond the casino are tired after
dark — stay near the park and you'll never notice.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
New York might be the easiest halal city outside the Muslim world
— the street-cart economy (Halal Guys and a hundred rivals) is
halal by default, and Jackson Heights and Curry Hill add full
Indian menus. DC and Niagara both have solid halal and Indian
options. Vegetarians eat well everywhere — pizza, bagels, food
halls — though at classic diners the answer is grilled cheese.

**Should we add the Canadian side?**
Only if your passport crosses visa-free, or you already hold (or
happily obtain) a Canadian visitor visa — see Day 9. A US visa
does not waive it, and processing is measured in weeks-to-months.
With it, the Canadian
panorama plus the American brink is the complete Niagara. Without
it, you're missing the wide shot, not the experience.

**West Coast instead?**
Different trip entirely — California needs a car and twice the
internal flying. This East Coast triangle is the right *first*
America; the West (and a future guide) is the right second.
