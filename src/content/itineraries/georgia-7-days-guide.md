Georgia is the easiest "real mountains" trip most travellers will
ever plan: 3–4 hour flights from the Gulf and much of Europe, and
prices that make a full week cost less than a long weekend
elsewhere. This route covers the old capital, the high Caucasus,
and the oldest wine region on earth — with drives kept short enough
that the road stays part of the holiday.

All costs are in Georgian lari (GEL), with conversions calculated at
today's exchange rate. Opening hours are approximate — Georgian hours drift with
the seasons, so treat anything marked ~ as a guide, not a contract.

## Before you go: the 15-minute admin

### Visas

- **Visa-free (up to 1 year):** EU, UK, USA, GCC (UAE, Saudi,
  Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman) and ~90 other passports. No forms,
  no fees.
- **Everyone else:** e-visa at [evisa.gov.ge](https://www.evisa.gov.ge)
  — ~USD 20, fully online, ~5 working days. Apply 2+ weeks ahead.
- **The residence-permit shortcut:** a visa-required passport
  (e.g. Indian) + a valid residence permit from a GCC state, US,
  UK, or Schengen country = **visa-free entry, 90 days**. Carry the
  physical permit card; officers check.
- Rules change — confirm on the [Georgian MFA site](https://mfa.gov.ge)
  before booking.

### Mandatory insurance — new for 2026

- Since **1 January 2026**, all foreign visitors must carry travel
  insurance covering health and accidents for the full stay.
- Minimum coverage: **{{GEL 30000}}**; proof can be
  requested at the border, in English or Georgian, paper or PDF.
- In practice: buy any standard travel policy, check it names
  Georgia and your dates, and save the PDF offline on your phone.

### Money

- Currency: lari (GEL). Cards work almost everywhere in Tbilisi.
- Carry cash for: cable car top-up, mountain drivers, markets,
  village cafés.
- Skip airport exchange — use Bank of Georgia / TBC ATMs in the
  city, or the Old Town exchange kiosks (fair rates).
- Tipping: 10%, often already on the bill — check first.

### SIM and data

- **Don't buy at the airport** — 40 GEL for 3 GB there vs 10 GEL
  SIM + 35 GEL unlimited 30-day data in the city.
- **Magti** has the best coverage, including the Kazbegi mountain
  road; eSIMs available. Any city branch, 5 minutes, passport
  needed.
- Want data on landing? A cheap international eSIM for day one +
  Magti in town.

### Getting around

- **Tbilisi:** use the Bolt app — rides across town 5–15 GEL.
  Install and add a card before you fly.
- **Outside Tbilisi:** one private-driver leg (Kazbegi) + two day
  trips beats renting a car — the Military Highway is shared with
  cross-border fuel trucks.
- Confident mountain drivers can rent a car instead; everyone else
  shouldn't learn here.

### Travelling with kids?

- This exact route works with small children — short drives, one
  built-in rest day.
- Bring a lightweight stroller for Tbilisi + a carrier for Narikala
  and Gergeti.
- Order khinkali **fried**, not boiled — less scalding.
- Keep Day 4's empty afternoon empty. That's the trick.

## Day 1 — Land in Tbilisi, Old Town + sulfur baths

Adjust to your landing time; this assumes a morning arrival. Stay
near the Old Town or Sololaki so the whole week starts from your
doorstep.

- **12:00** — Check in, drop bags. Grab a Metromoney card at any
  metro station or the Rike cable car kiosk (you'll need it — the
  cable car takes **no cash and no bank cards**).
- **13:00** — Lunch: **Salobie Bia** (near Liberty Square) — famous
  modern take on lobio and Georgian classics, ~25–40 GEL/person.
- **14:30** — Wander the Old Town lanes: Sioni Cathedral, the
  leaning clock tower, Meidan Bazaar.
- **16:00** — **Rike Park → Narikala cable car** (~11:00–23:00,
  2.5 GEL each way, Metromoney only; closes in strong wind).
- **16:15** — Narikala Fortress walls + ridge walk to the Mother of
  Georgia statue (open access, free). Best light late afternoon.
- **17:30** — Walk down into **Abanotubani**, the sulfur bath
  district.
- **18:00** — **Orbeliani (Chreli Abano) Baths** — the famous
  blue-tiled one. Private rooms 50–80 GEL/hour; **book 1–2 days
  ahead** in season. Budget option: Bath House No. 5 or
  Leghvtakhevi, 15–25 GEL/hour, walk-ins fine. Add the kisa scrub.
- **20:00** — Dinner: courtyard restaurant in the Old Town —
  khinkali, khachapuri, house wine. ~50–80 GEL for two.

**Where to stay in Tbilisi**

- *Splurge:* **Radisson Blu Iveria** — city-centre tower off
  Rustaveli, big modern rooms, sweeping views, 10-min walk to Old
  Town.
- *Mid-range:* **Communal Hotel Sololaki** — small, superbly rated,
  breakfast included, walkable to everything here. Few rooms; book
  early.
- *Budget:* **Ibis Budget Tbilisi Center** — clean chain basics at
  guesthouse prices; a short Bolt ride (not a stroll) from Old Town.

## Day 2 — Mtskheta half-day trip

The ancient capital, 30 minutes away. Taxi/Bolt with waiting time
~80–100 GEL return, or join a small-group tour.

- **9:00** — Depart Tbilisi.
- **9:45** — **Jvari Monastery** (daily, ~9:00–18:00, free) — the
  postcard view over the confluence of two rivers. 30–40 minutes is
  plenty.
- **11:00** — **Svetitskhoveli Cathedral** (daily, ~8:00–18:00,
  free) — burial place of Georgian kings. Shoulders/knees covered;
  scarves at the door.
- **12:00** — Browse the souvenir lanes between cathedral and
  river — churchkhela (walnut-grape "Georgian Snickers") is the
  thing to try.
- **13:00** — Lunch: **Salobie** (on the Tbilisi road just outside
  Mtskheta) — a legendary lobio-and-mchadi institution since Soviet
  times, ~15–25 GEL/person.
- **14:30** — Back in Tbilisi.
- **Evening** — Rustaveli Avenue stroll, then the Sololaki
  natural-wine bars, or dinner at **Café Littera** (celebrated
  modern Georgian, garden setting — reserve ahead).

## Day 3 — Georgian Military Highway to Kazbegi

~3 hours of driving broken into 45–60 minute hops. Book a private
driver (~250 GEL/day) over the shared minivans — you control the
stops.

- **9:00** — Depart Tbilisi.
- **10:00** — **Ananuri Fortress** (grounds open daylight hours,
  free) — 17th-century castle on a turquoise reservoir. 30 minutes.
- **12:00** — Lunch: **Pasanauri** — the village where khinkali was
  born; roadside restaurants here serve the best in the country,
  ~1.5–2 GEL per dumpling. Order 5–8 per person, twist, sip, bite.
- **13:30** — **Gudauri viewpoint + Russia–Georgia Friendship
  Monument** (open access) — mosaic balcony over the valley;
  paragliders overhead, tandem flights bookable on the spot.
- **14:30** — Over the 2,395 m Jvari Pass.
- **15:30** — Arrive **Stepantsminda (Kazbegi)**, check in. On a
  clear day Mount Kazbek fills the window.
- **19:00** — Dinner: **Rooms Hotel Kitchen** (walk-ins for
  non-guests; burger ~34 GEL, terrace view worth it alone) or
  **Maisi** for elevated Khevian mountain cooking.

**Where to stay in Kazbegi**

- *Splurge:* **Rooms Hotel Kazbegi** — the famous
  sanatorium-turned-design-hotel; terrace and pool face Kazbek
  head-on. First to sell out — book the day you book flights.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Porta Caucasia** — well-rated, breakfast,
  mountain views at a fraction of the Rooms price.
- *Budget:* **Guesthouse Anano** — family-run, outstanding ratings;
  expect to be fed.

## Day 4 — Gergeti Trinity Church + the valley

The lone-church-under-a-giant-peak photo you've seen. Go early —
tour buses from Tbilisi arrive from ~10:30.

- **8:30** — Up to **Gergeti Trinity Church** (church open daylight
  hours, free), two ways:
  - *Hike:* 1.5–2 hours up from town through open hillside — the
    best half-day walk in easy reach of Tbilisi. Water + sun cover;
    nothing to buy en route.
  - *4x4:* drivers wait on the main square; 15–20 minutes up.
    Shared ~30–50 GEL/person, private 100–150 GEL — agree price and
    waiting time first.
- **10:30** — Back down; coffee in the square.
- **12:30** — Lunch: **Qondari** — homestyle Georgian at budget
  prices, or **Tiba** for Khevian dishes with mountain views.
- **14:00** — **Gveleti waterfall** — short drive toward the
  Dariali gorge, then a ~30–40 minute walk each way.
- **Evening** — Slow dinner, early night. Cloud hid the peak
  today? Kazbek is clearest at sunrise — set an alarm.

## Day 5 — Back to Tbilisi via the Chronicle of Georgia

- **9:30** — Depart Stepantsminda; catch the viewpoints you skipped
  on the way up.
- **13:00** — Reach Tbilisi's edge: **Chronicle of Georgia** (open
  24/7, free) — a hilltop crown of 30-metre carved pillars above
  the Tbilisi Sea. Almost no crowds; the most dramatic photo stop
  of the trip.
- **14:00** — Late lunch back in town: **Machakhela/Samikitno**
  (Meidan) — reliable khachapuri, open round the clock, ~20
  GEL/person.
- **15:00** — Check in, rest.
- **17:30** — **Funicular up to Mtatsminda Park** (runs ~9:00 until
  late evening) — the best sunset-to-night view of Tbilisi.
- **20:30** — Dinner in Sololaki/Vera: the modern-Georgian and
  natural-wine spots (**Shavi Lomi**, **Café Littera**) book out on
  weekends — reserve ahead.

## Day 6 — Kakheti wine country day trip

Qvevri wine has been made here for 8,000 years. Day tours
(~150–200 GEL/person) bundle the stops; self-drive works too. ~1h45
each way.

- **8:30** — Depart Tbilisi.
- **10:30** — **Bodbe Monastery** (grounds ~9:00–19:00, free) —
  cypress gardens and the resting place of St Nino.
- **11:30** — **Sighnaghi** — Georgia's prettiest town: pastel
  houses, defensive walls with Alazani Valley + Caucasus views.
  Walk the wall towers; compact and unhurried.
- **13:00** — Lunch + tasting: **Pheasant's Tears** — the natural
  wine pioneer that put Sighnaghi on the map; set feasts with amber
  wines you won't find at home. Reserve ahead. Non-drinkers get the
  same vineyards' grape juice.
- **15:30** — One more family winery or the Sighnaghi wall at
  golden hour.
- **19:00** — Back in Tbilisi.

## Day 7 — Souvenirs and fly home

- **10:00** — **Dry Bridge Market** (daily ~10:00–17:00, weather
  permitting; fullest on weekends) — Soviet cameras, horn cups,
  enamel badges, old carpets. Cash, and haggle gently.
- **12:00** — Pick up qvevri wine from a proper wine shop — it
  travels fine in checked luggage wrapped in a jumper.
- **13:00** — Final khachapuri, then out. Evening departures get
  most Gulf and European travellers home at a reasonable hour.

## What to pack

- Layers — Tbilisi at 30°C and Kazbegi at 12°C can be the same
  afternoon.
- Rain shell; mountain weather turns in minutes.
- Walking shoes if you're hiking Gergeti.
- Swimwear for the sulfur baths (optional in private rooms, but
  bring it).
- Scarf / modest cover for churches and monasteries.
- Snacks for the drives — shops are sparse between Gudauri and
  Stepantsminda.
- Motion-sickness tablets if you're prone: the Jvari Pass
  switchbacks are the test.
- Your insurance PDF, saved offline.

## FAQ

**Is Georgia safe?**
Yes — street crime is rare and Tbilisi feels safer at night than
most European capitals. The real risks are uneven pavements and
enthusiastic driving: use Bolt, take care crossing roads. Solo
travellers, including women, generally report feeling comfortable.

**When is the best time to go?**
May–June and September–early October: warm in Tbilisi, clear in the
mountains, no peak-season crowds. Deep winter makes Kazbegi hard to
reach (though Gudauri becomes a ski resort); the Gergeti road can
close after snowfall.

**What about picky or vegetarian eaters?**
Forgiving on both counts. Khachapuri is cheese bread, khinkali are
dumplings, and half the classic dishes are meat-free by tradition —
vegetarians do unusually well here.

**Do I need to rent a car?**
No. Bolt covers Tbilisi, day tours cover Kakheti and Mtskheta, and
a private driver for the Kazbegi leg costs less than two days of
rental plus fuel plus nerves. Rent only to explore beyond this
route.

**How far ahead should I book?**
Flights and the Kazbegi hotel: 4–8 weeks in summer. Orbeliani baths
and Pheasant's Tears: a couple of days. Everything else: a week out
is fine, except in August.

**Can I do this itinerary in reverse?**
Yes, but two slow Tbilisi days first give you a weather buffer
before the mountain leg — the part you most want a clear day for.
