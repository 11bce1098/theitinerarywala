Rwanda is small, green, startlingly clean, and organised in a way
that surprises people who have travelled elsewhere in the region.
The roads are good, the country is safe, plastic bags have been
banned since 2008, and you can drive its whole length in a day.

The reason most people come is one hour long. **Mountain gorillas**
live in the Virunga volcanoes on the northern border, and a permit
buys you sixty minutes with a habituated family, at a distance of a
few metres. There are only about a thousand of these animals left in
the world, all in this small range of mountains.

The rest of the country justifies the trip on its own: **Nyungwe**,
one of Africa's oldest montane rainforests, with chimpanzees and a
canopy walkway strung 50 m above a ravine; **Lake Kivu**, with no
crocodiles or hippos and therefore swimmable; and **Kigali**, whose
genocide memorial is among the most important places in Africa.

## Before you go: the permit, the cost and what to expect

### Entry

- **Rwanda offers visa on arrival to all nationalities**, which is
  unusual and welcome. Many nationalities — including most African,
  Commonwealth and some others — enter **visa-free**. An **eVisa**
  is available for everyone else.
- The **East Africa Tourist Visa** covers Rwanda, Uganda and Kenya
  together if you are combining them.
- **Plastic bags are illegal** and confiscated at the airport,
  including the ones inside your suitcase. Repack before you fly.

### The gorilla permit, honestly

This is the single most important planning fact in this guide.

- **A gorilla permit in Rwanda costs USD 1,500 per person**, for one
  hour with one family. That is not a typo, and it is the highest
  price of the three gorilla countries.
- **Permits are capped at eight visitors per family per day**, which
  is why they are scarce and expensive. The money funds
  conservation and community programmes, and the gorilla population
  has been rising.
- **Book months ahead**, through the Rwanda Development Board or a
  licensed operator. Peak months sell out.
- **Uganda's permits cost substantially less** for the same animals
  in the same mountain range, and the **DRC's less again**. Rwanda's
  advantage is the short, good road from Kigali — under three hours
  — and the general ease of the country.
- **Minimum age is 15.**
- If you are ill — even a cold — **you will not be allowed to go**,
  because gorillas catch human respiratory infections and it kills
  them. There are no refunds for this, and the rule is right.

### What the trek is actually like

- **You are assigned a family on the morning**, based on fitness and
  preference. Some are an hour's walk, some are four hours on steep,
  muddy, stinging-nettle-covered slopes at 2,500–3,000 m.
- **Porters are available and cheap**, and hiring one is both a real
  help and direct local income. Take one.
- **The hour starts when you reach the family** and it goes very
  fast. Masks are required, a distance is enforced, and flash is
  banned.
- **Gaiters and gardening gloves** sound excessive and are not — the
  nettles here are serious.

### Money and getting around

- **The franc (RWF)**, and prices run into the thousands. Cards work
  in Kigali and at lodges; carry cash elsewhere.
- **US dollars are used for permits and some lodges** — bring clean
  notes.
- **Roads are good and drivers are the norm.** A car with a driver
  for the week is the standard way to do this itinerary and is not
  expensive relative to the permit.
- Kinyarwanda, French and English are all official; English is
  widely used in tourism.

## Day 1 — Land in Kigali

- **Afternoon** — Land, drop bags. Kigali is hilly, calm and clean.
- **Evening** — Dinner. The city has a real restaurant scene.

## Day 2 — The memorial, and north

- **Morning** — The **Kigali Genocide Memorial**. It is not an
  optional cultural stop; it is the context for everything else you
  will see, and it is handled with great care. Allow two hours and
  expect to need the rest of the morning afterwards.
- **Afternoon** — Drive north to **Musanze**, under three hours,
  with the Virunga volcanoes rising ahead.
- **Evening** — Early night. Tomorrow starts at dawn.

## Day 3 — Gorillas

- **05:30** — Briefing at **Volcanoes National Park** headquarters,
  family allocation, and the drive to the trailhead.
- **Morning** — The trek: anywhere from one to five hours each way.
  Then **one hour** with the family — adults, juveniles, usually a
  silverback a few metres away, entirely unbothered by you.
- **Afternoon** — Back down. Most people are quiet for a while.
- **Evening** — Certificate, a drink, and the realisation that it is
  over.

## Day 4 — Golden monkeys, or Dian Fossey

- **Option A** — **Golden monkey trek**, a much cheaper permit for a
  rarer and very lively primate in the bamboo.
- **Option B** — The hike to **Dian Fossey's grave** at Karisoke,
  which is steep and historically rich.
- **Afternoon** — Drive to **Lake Kivu** at Gisenyi, about an hour.

## Day 5 — Lake Kivu

- **Morning** — Swim. Kivu has **no crocodiles and no hippos**,
  which makes it one of very few swimmable African lakes.
- **Afternoon** — A boat to the islands, or the **Congo Nile Trail**
  by bike along the shore.
- **Evening** — Fishermen's boats going out at dusk, singing, which
  is the lake's nightly ritual.

## Day 6 — South to Nyungwe

- **All day** — The drive south along Kivu is one of the great
  African road journeys — four to six hours of switchbacks above the
  lake through terraced hills.
- **Evening** — Arrive at **Nyungwe Forest**.

## Day 7 — Chimps and the canopy

- **05:00** — **Chimpanzee tracking**. Louder, faster and more
  chaotic than the gorillas, and a different kind of morning.
- **Afternoon** — The **canopy walkway**, 50 m above a ravine and
  160 m long, which is the only one of its kind in East Africa.
- **Evening** — Last forest night.

## Day 8 — Back to Kigali, and home

- **Morning** — The drive back, around five hours, or a short
  domestic flight if your schedule is tight.
- **Afternoon** — Kigali's craft cooperatives, then the airport.

## What to pack

- **Gaiters and thick gardening gloves** for the gorilla trek.
  The nettles and the thorns are the reason.
- **Waterproofs.** It is called a rainforest for a reason, in every
  month.
- **Boots with grip** for steep mud.
- **A mask** for the gorilla hour — usually provided, bring your own
  anyway.
- **No plastic bags**, which are illegal and will be taken.
- **Warm layer** for altitude evenings.
- **Type C/J plugs**, 230V.

## FAQ

**Is the permit worth USD 1,500?**
People who have done it almost universally say yes, and the money
demonstrably funds the conservation that has grown the population.
If it is out of reach, Uganda offers the same experience for less.

**How fit do I need to be?**
Moderately. Treks range from one to five hours on steep, muddy
ground at altitude. Tell the briefing honestly and you will be
matched to an appropriate family; a porter makes a large difference.

**Is Rwanda safe?**
Yes, and notably so — it is among the safest countries in Africa
for visitors, with low crime and excellent roads.

**When should I go?**
**June to September** and **December to February** are the drier
seasons and the easier treks. The rainy months are green, quieter
and considerably muddier.
