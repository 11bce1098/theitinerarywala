Guatemala has the Maya heartland, thirty-odd volcanoes, and the best
textiles in the Americas, and it sits next to two countries most
people visit instead. That is changing, but slowly enough that Tikal
at dawn is still a quiet place.

**Antigua** is a colonial capital flattened by earthquakes and left
half-ruined, cobbled, low and beautiful, with **Volcán de Fuego**
erupting on the horizon most evenings. **Lake Atitlán** fills a
collapsed caldera ringed by volcanoes, with a dozen Maya villages
around the shore, each with its own dress and sometimes its own
language. **Tikal** is the great Classic Maya city, where temples
push up through a rainforest canopy full of howler monkeys.

Ten days does all three, with one internal flight that saves the
best part of two days.

## Before you go: shuttles, altitude and the honest safety brief

### Entry

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for 90 days — the EU, UK,
  US, Canada, Australia and many others.
- **The CA-4 agreement** means that 90 days is shared across
  Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador and Nicaragua — crossing between
  them does not reset it.
- Passport valid six months.

### Getting around: shuttles, not buses

- **Tourist shuttles** are how this route is done: shared minibuses
  between Antigua, Atitlán, Flores and the rest, booked a day ahead
  through any hostel or agency. They are cheap, direct and
  comfortable enough.
- **Chicken buses** — repainted US school buses — are the local
  network. They are cheap, an experience, and not the sensible way
  to move luggage across the country.
- **Fly Guatemala City to Flores** for Tikal. The road is 8–10
  hours; the flight is an hour. The overnight bus exists and is
  the false economy on this trip.
- **Roads are winding and slow.** Antigua to Atitlán is 3 hours for
  a distance that looks like one.

### Altitude, and the volcano

- **Antigua sits at 1,500 m and Atitlán at 1,560 m** — enough to
  feel on the first day, not enough to cause trouble.
- **Acatenango** is the hike people come for: an overnight climb to
  around 3,970 m, camping on the ridge opposite **Fuego**, which
  erupts through the night. It is hard — six hours up, cold at the
  top, and the altitude is real. It is also the single most
  memorable thing in the country.
- **Pacaya** is the gentle alternative: two to three hours return,
  lava fields, and you can toast marshmallows on the vents.

### The safety brief, plainly

- **On this route, in these places, visitors are generally fine.**
  Antigua, Atitlán's main villages and Flores are well used to
  tourism and calm.
- **Guatemala City is the exception** and this itinerary avoids
  spending time there beyond the airport.
- **Do not travel intercity at night**, including the overnight
  buses. This is the single most repeated piece of local advice.
- **Use shuttles or registered taxis**, not street hails.
- **Take what you need and leave the rest** — the risk is
  opportunistic theft, not anything worse.
- Some volcano trails have a history of robberies; **go with a
  registered guide**, which you would want anyway.

### Money and the small print

- **The quetzal (GTQ).** Cards work in Antigua, larger hotels and
  some restaurants; **everywhere else is cash**.
- **ATMs are in the towns and absent in villages.** Draw in Antigua
  or Panajachel.
- **Spanish is essential** outside hotels — and in the highland
  villages, Spanish is often a second language after Kaqchikel or
  Tz'utujil.
- **Tap water is not drinkable.** Refill from filtered dispensers,
  which every guesthouse has.

## Day 1 — Land, and straight to Antigua

- **Afternoon** — Land at Guatemala City and transfer directly to
  **Antigua**, about an hour. Do not linger in the capital.
- **Evening** — The cobbled grid, the **Santa Catalina arch**, and
  dinner in a courtyard.

## Day 2 — Antigua

- **Morning** — The ruined convents and churches — **Santa Clara**,
  **Las Capuchinas**, the cathedral ruins behind the façade.
- **Afternoon** — **Cerro de la Cruz** for the view of the grid with
  **Agua** behind it, or a coffee finca on the slopes.
- **Evening** — If **Fuego** is active you will see it from the
  rooftops after dark.

## Day 3 — Acatenango, or a gentler day

- **Option A** — The **Acatenango overnight**: up through cloud
  forest to a ridge camp at 3,600 m, with Fuego erupting across the
  valley all night. Down the next morning. This takes days 3 and 4.
- **Option B** — **Pacaya** in the morning, then a textile workshop
  or a chocolate class in the afternoon.

## Day 4 — Down from the volcano, or to the lake

- **After Acatenango** — Rest in Antigua. You will want to.
- **Otherwise** — Shuttle to **Lake Atitlán**, three hours, arriving
  at **Panajachel** and taking a boat to your village.

## Day 5 — The lake villages

- **Morning** — **San Juan La Laguna**: weaving and natural-dye
  cooperatives run by women's collectives, and the best place on the
  lake to buy textiles honestly.
- **Afternoon** — **San Marcos** for the swimming platform and the
  hippie end of the lake, or **Santiago Atitlán** for the most
  traditional village and the shrine of **Maximón**.
- **Evening** — Sunset over the volcanoes from the water.

## Day 6 — Atitlán, active or slow

- **Option A** — The **Indian Nose** ridge at dawn, for sunrise over
  the caldera.
- **Option B** — Kayak the shore, or walk the path between San Juan
  and San Pedro.
- **Evening** — Book tomorrow's shuttle and the Flores flight.

## Day 7 — Chichicastenango, then north

- **Morning** — If it is **Thursday or Sunday**, the
  **Chichicastenango market** is the largest indigenous market in
  Central America and worth reorganising the week for.
- **Afternoon** — Shuttle to Guatemala City and fly to **Flores**,
  an hour.
- **Evening** — **Flores** is a small island town on a lake,
  connected by a causeway, and a pleasant surprise after the
  journey.

## Day 8 — Tikal

- **Pre-dawn** — The **sunrise tour** into the park, when the
  howler monkeys start and the mist sits on the canopy. Whether the
  sun actually appears is a gamble; the sound is not.
- **Morning** — **Temple IV**, the tallest, which you climb to look
  out over the canopy at the tops of other temples. The Great Plaza,
  Temple I and II, the acropolis.
- **Afternoon** — Back to Flores, or stay in the park hotels to
  return in the late afternoon when it is empty again.

## Day 9 — Yaxhá, or the lake

- **Option A** — **Yaxhá**, a smaller site on a lake, far quieter
  than Tikal and with a sunset from the top of a temple.
- **Option B** — A slow day in Flores, swimming off the causeway.
- **Evening** — Fly back to Guatemala City.

## Day 10 — Fly home

- Most international departures leave in the morning from Guatemala
  City. Sleep near the airport rather than returning to Antigua.

## What to pack

- **Warm layers for Acatenango**, which is near freezing on the
  ridge at night even though you started the day in shorts.
- **A rain layer** — the wet season runs May to October and rains
  most afternoons.
- **Walking shoes with grip** for volcanic scree.
- **Cash in quetzales** for villages and markets.
- **Insect repellent** for Tikal and the Petén lowlands.
- **Type A/B plugs**, 120V.

## FAQ

**Is Guatemala safe?**
On this route, with the habits above, yes — and it is a far calmer
country to travel than its reputation suggests. Avoid intercity
travel after dark, skip Guatemala City, and use registered guides
on volcano trails.

**Is Acatenango worth it?**
If you are reasonably fit, it is the best thing in the country — an
active volcano erupting across a valley all night, from a camp you
walked to. If you are not, Pacaya gives you lava for a fraction of
the effort.

**Should I fly to Flores or take the bus?**
Fly. The road is eight to ten hours each way and the flight is one.
On a ten-day trip the bus costs you two days to save a modest sum.

**When should I go?**
**November to April** is the dry season and the reliable choice.
**May to October** is green and wet, usually with clear mornings and
afternoon rain, and noticeably cheaper.
