Jaisalmer is where Rajasthan runs out of road. Eight hundred years
old and carved entirely from golden sandstone, its fort is not a
museum — four thousand people still live inside the walls — and
beyond the city there is only the Thar: dunes, abandoned villages,
and finally the Pakistan border. This is the desert-deep trip our
Rajasthan circuit deliberately left out (it's 5+ hours past
Jodhpur), built around the two things you come for: the living fort
by day, and a night in the dunes under more stars than you've seen.

**Go October–March** — the only humane season; the **February
Desert Festival** (full-moon dates) is spectacular and books out
months ahead. April–June exceeds 45°C and many camps close. Desert
winter nights drop near 5°C — the camp is cold and the stars are
the payment.

All costs are in Indian rupees (INR), with conversions calculated
at today's exchange rate. Foreigner prices quoted; **Indian and
OCI passports pay domestic rates.**

## Before you go: the short version of the admin

Visas, UPI One World, SIMs and the scam list are in our Golden
Triangle guide; the car-and-driver pattern is in the Rajasthan
circuit. Jaisalmer specifics:

- **Getting there:** Jaisalmer's airport (JSA) runs **seasonal
  winter directs** (Delhi and select cities, roughly October–
  March — check current schedules); otherwise fly to **Jodhpur**
  and drive ~5 h, or take the comfortable **overnight train** from
  Delhi/Jaipur/Jodhpur. Coming off our Rajasthan circuit, it
  slots after Jodhpur naturally.
- **Stay outside the fort — a genuine ask.** The living fort has
  documented drainage and subsidence problems, worsened by water
  use from guesthouses inside the walls; conservation bodies have
  long urged visitors to sleep outside. Conveniently, the
  best-rated heritage hotels are all outside anyway, with rooftop
  fort views the inside can't offer. Visit the fort all day;
  sleep facing it.
- **Book the dune camp direct** (not through touts at Sam's gate),
  confirm exactly what's included, and reserve 2–3 months out for
  peak weeks. Arrange your transport back — camp roads are dark.
- **The border day needs your passport** (foreigners) and, for the
  final BP 609 border-pillar viewpoint, BSF permission your
  driver/operator arranges — Longewala and Tanot themselves are
  open access.

### Travelling with kids?

- Camels, forts, sand to infinity — small kids think Jaisalmer was
  built for them. Camel rides suit ages ~4+ (shared saddle);
  dune-bashing jeeps are rougher — gentle-drive on request.
- Desert cold at night is the real management item: pack proper
  warm layers for everyone at the camp.
- The Longewala day is long (300 km round trip) — school-age and
  war-museum-curious kids do fine; toddlers won't.

## Day 1 — Arrive at the edge of the map

- **Afternoon** — Arrive by seasonal flight, Jodhpur drive or the
  overnight train; check in outside the fort walls.
- **17:00** — First look: walk up through **Gadisar Lake**'s
  gateway for the classic across-the-water fort view as the
  sandstone turns from gold to amber.
- **19:30** — Rooftop dinner facing the lit fort — ker sangri
  (desert beans and berries) and gatte ki sabzi are the local
  plates; the fort at night from a rooftop is the trip's first
  keeper photo.

**Where to stay in Jaisalmer** (four city nights)

- *Luxury:* **Taj Gorbandh Palace** — superbly rated sandstone
  grandeur with lawns and pool; the famous desert-luxury
  alternative outside town is Suryagarh, for those ending the
  trip on a splurge.
- *Mid-range:* **Shahi Palace Hotel** — the beloved carved-stone
  haveli with a 180° fort-view rooftop; book early, everyone
  reads the same reviews.
- *Budget:* **Open Road Hostel & Café** — superbly rated,
  sociable, cheap.

## Day 2 — The living fort

- **8:30** — Enter **Jaisalmer Fort** through its four gates
  (entry free — people live here) while the lanes still belong to
  residents and cows.
- **9:00** — **Fort Palace Museum** (~9:00–18:00; foreigners
  ~₹600 including a good audio guide) — the maharawal's palace
  stacked above the ramparts; the rooftop view over the golden
  city is the ticket's justification.
- **11:00** — The **Jain temples** inside the fort (mornings,
  small fee; modest dress, no leather) — 15th-century carving as
  dense as Ranakpur's in pocket size.
- **12:30** — Lunch on a fort-wall rooftop; the lassi shops by the
  main square are institutions.
- **14:30** — Down into the old town: **Patwon ki Haveli**
  (~9:00–18:00; foreigners ~₹250) — five merchant mansions of
  impossibly fine stone lacework — then the facades of **Nathmal
  ki Haveli** and **Salim Singh ki Haveli** (peacock roof).
- **17:30** — **Gadisar Lake** at sunset — catfish swarms,
  pigeons, and the ghats going gold; optional rowboat.
- **20:00** — Dinner in the old town; pack a small bag for
  tomorrow's camp night (leave main luggage at the hotel — every
  hotel expects this).

## Day 3 — Cenotaphs, a cursed village, and into the dunes

- **9:00** — **Bada Bagh** (~₹100–300) — the ridge of royal
  cenotaphs with wind turbines behind: 500 years of Rajasthan in
  one frame.
- **10:30** — **Kuldhara** (~₹50/person + vehicle fee) — the
  village abandoned overnight ~200 years ago and never resettled;
  walk the roofless lanes and enjoy Rajasthan's best ghost story
  responsibly.
- **12:30** — Back to town for lunch, then the afternoon drive to
  your **dune camp**. The honest fork:
  - **Sam** (~40 km): the big dunes and the big scene — hundreds
    of camels, sunset crowds, full carnival. First-timers usually
    want this at least once.
  - **Khuri** (~48 km): smaller dunes, village quiet, half the
    camps — couples and second-timers choose it and don't look
    back.
- **16:30** — **Camel ride into the dunes for sunset** (included
  in most packages; ₹500–2,000 solo) — slow, creaking, exactly
  right. Jeep "dune bashing" (~₹1,500–2,000/jeep) is the
  loud alternative.
- **19:00** — Camp evening: Kalbeliya dancers and Manganiyar
  musicians around the fire, dinner under canvas
  (Swiss-tent camps with private bathrooms run **₹2,500–4,000 per
  person** with meals and activities; luxury tiers to ₹8,000+;
  the ₹1,200 gate deals deliver exactly what they cost).
- **22:00** — Walk 100 m past the last light and look up. The
  Thar's sky is the trip's actual headliner. Nights are genuinely
  cold — wear everything.

## Day 4 — Dune sunrise, slow golden afternoon

- **6:45** — Sunrise from the dune crest with chai — the desert's
  best hour, crowds still asleep.
- **9:30** — Back to the city; shower, regroup.
- **12:00** — Lazy lunch, then Jaisalmer's crafts done right:
  the leather-and-silver lanes, patchwork textiles, and the
  yellow-stone carvers — prices here beat Jaipur's for the same
  work.
- **17:00** — Sunset from **Vyas Chhatri**'s cenotaphs (small
  fee) — the sunset-point of choice over the golden skyline — or
  the fort ramparts one more time.
- **20:00** — Dinner; if the **Desert Culture Centre**'s evening
  puppet show is running (~19:30, small ticket), it's a charming
  hour.

## Day 5 — The border day: Longewala and Tanot

The 1971 war pilgrimage — ~300 km round trip through empty desert;
full day with your driver (~₹4,500–6,000 for the car). Carry
passports.

- **8:00** — West through the Thar — the emptiness is the point;
  India thins out with every kilometre.
- **10:30** — **Longewala War Memorial** (8:00–18:00, free) — the
  battlefield where 120 Indian soldiers held off a tank column in
  December 1971 (the film *Border* tells it): the Vijayanta tank,
  captured Pattons, the museum, the eternal flame. Moving whether
  or not you know the story going in.
- **13:00** — Packed lunch or the canteen; on to **Tanot Mata
  temple** (free) — the border temple famous for the unexploded
  shells displayed in its courtyard, run by the BSF itself.
- **15:00** — With pre-arranged BSF permission, the **BP 609
  border pillar** viewpoint — the fence itself; without it, Tanot
  is the turnaround.
- **18:30** — Back in Jaisalmer; easy dinner. (Alternative day for
  the war-uninterested: **Desert National Park**'s morning safari
  for chinkara and — with luck — the critically endangered Great
  Indian Bustard, or simply a second slow city day.)

## Day 6 — Depart

- **Morning** — Last rooftop breakfast against the fort; fly out
  on the seasonal direct, or drive/train to Jodhpur — where our
  Rajasthan circuit is waiting to be run in reverse if this was
  your opening act.

## What to pack

- Real warm layers for the camp night — desert winter nights sit
  near 5°C and tents are canvas: jacket, hat, thick socks.
- Sun kit for the same 24 hours — the desert does both extremes
  daily.
- A scarf/shemagh — sun, sand and camel-ride photos, triple duty.
- Closed shoes for dunes and ruins (sand gets everywhere anyway;
  accept it).
- Torch or headlamp for the camp; power banks — tents charge
  slowly if at all.
- Passport on the border day, and the no-leather kit if you'll
  enter the fort's Jain temples.

## FAQ

**Is Jaisalmer worth the extra distance?**
That's the honest question, and the answer is the desert: if the
dunes-and-stars night and the living fort call to you, nothing in
Rajasthan replaces them. If you're monument-first and
time-limited, the Jodhpur–Udaipur circuit is the better spend —
which is exactly why we published it first.

**One camp night or two?**
One, for most: the arc — sunset ride, fire, stars, sunrise — is
complete in 18 hours, and camp comfort is charming rather than
luxurious. Two suits stargazers and the truly unhurried; ask for
a quieter second-night activity mix.

**Sam or Khuri, really?**
Sam for the spectacle and the biggest dunes, Khuri for quiet.
Reading reviews of the *specific camp* matters more than the
village choice — both places have gems and both have conveyor
belts.

**Is the fort really at risk?**
Its drainage was never built for modern water use, and parts of
the walls and bastions have collapsed over the years. Visiting is
unambiguously fine — tourism sustains the families inside — but
sleeping outside the walls is the small, real contribution a
visitor controls. The rooftop fort views are better from outside
anyway.

**Can I combine this with the Rajasthan circuit?**
Perfectly: run our circuit Jaipur → Udaipur, fly or drive
Udaipur–Jodhpur, then this guide — or insert Jaisalmer after
Jodhpur (Days 4–5 of the circuit) as a 3-night detour if six days
is too many. Either way, ~14 days covers the whole desert
kingdom.

**Vegetarian and halal food?**
Same picture as the circuit guide: vegetarian Rajasthan is superb
and the practical daily default; the camp dinners are buffet-style
with veg always the majority. Confirm halal specifics with camps
ahead — most accommodate with notice.
