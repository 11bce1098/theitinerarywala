Rio de Janeiro has the most dramatic setting of any large city
anywhere: granite peaks dropping straight into the Atlantic, a
rainforest inside the city limits, and beaches that function as the
municipal living room. You could spend the whole trip there and not
feel short-changed.

But Brazil rewards two more stops. **Paraty**, four hours down the
coast, is a colonial port of white houses and cobbles too rough for
cars, with schooners that take you out to the islands. And
**Iguaçu**, where 275 waterfalls straddle the Argentine border and
the Brazilian side gives you the panorama.

Ten days, two internal flights, and one long coastal drive. The
country is enormous — this is a sliver of it, chosen because the
three parts are close enough to combine without spending the trip in
the air.

## Before you go: visas, safety and the calendar

### Entry, and the visa that came back

- **Visa requirements changed recently.** Brazil reintroduced visa
  requirements for some nationalities — including US, Canadian and
  Australian passports — after years of waiving them, with an
  **eVisa** process online. The EU, UK and many others remain
  visa-free for 90 days. **Check your own case close to booking**;
  this is the single fastest-moving fact in this guide.
- **Proof of yellow fever vaccination** is required for arrivals from
  some countries, and recommended for parts of Brazil regardless.
  Check the current requirement for your route and your origin.
- Passport valid six months.

### The safety brief, honestly

Rio's reputation is neither fair nor entirely wrong, and a plain
version helps more than either reassurance or alarm.

- **Take what you need and nothing more.** Carry one card, some
  cash, and leave the rest at the hotel. A phone in your hand on a
  quiet street is the most common opportunity.
- **Use ride-hailing apps** rather than hailing. They work well,
  they are cheap, and they remove the negotiation.
- **Beaches: take the minimum.** Locals bring a towel, some cash and
  nothing else, and that is the model to copy.
- **Avoid favelas without a guide**, and be sceptical of tours that
  present them as an attraction rather than a neighbourhood.
- **After dark, move by car**, not on foot, outside the main
  beachfront strips.
- Paraty and the Iguaçu towns are notably calmer than Rio.

### Carnival changes everything

- **Carnival moves with Easter** and falls in February or early
  March. During it, Rio's prices multiply, accommodation books out
  a year ahead, and the city stops working normally.
- Going for Carnival is a legitimate choice and a completely
  different trip. **Going near it by accident is not.** Check the
  date before booking anything.
- **The rest of the summer** — December to March — is hot, humid and
  lively. **May to September** is Rio's dry season: cooler, clearer,
  and the better choice for sightseeing.

### Money and getting around

- **The real (BRL).** Cards work nearly everywhere, including
  taxis and beach kiosks. Carry a little cash for the smallest
  vendors.
- **Decline the terminal's offer to charge you in your home
  currency.**
- **Internal flights** link Rio and Iguaçu in about two hours. The
  alternative is a bus of well over twenty.
- **Rio → Paraty is four hours by road** — a comfortable coach or a
  private transfer along a genuinely beautiful coastal highway.
- Portuguese, not Spanish. English is limited outside hotels; a
  translation app earns its keep here.

## Day 1 — Land in Rio

- **Afternoon** — Land, transfer to **Copacabana** or **Ipanema**.
- **Evening** — Walk the beachfront promenade, which is busy and
  safe and the best introduction to how the city uses itself.

## Day 2 — Sugarloaf and the south

- **Morning** — **Sugarloaf** by cable car in two stages, ideally
  late afternoon for the light — but go early if the forecast is
  poor, because cloud ruins it.
- **Afternoon** — **Ipanema** and the **Arpoador** rock, where
  people applaud the sunset.
- **Evening** — Dinner in Ipanema or Leblon.

## Day 3 — Christ, and the forest

- **Early** — **Christ the Redeemer** on the first train up, before
  the cloud and the crowds. Book the timed ticket ahead.
- **Afternoon** — **Tijuca** forest, the urban rainforest, or the
  **Botanical Garden** below it.
- **Evening** — **Lapa** for the arches and live music, by car both
  ways.

## Day 4 — Down the coast to Paraty

- **Morning** — Four hours south along the Costa Verde.
- **Afternoon** — Arrive in **Paraty**: whitewashed colonial houses,
  cobbles laid to flood at high tide, no cars in the old town.
- **Evening** — Dinner in the historic centre, which is lit by
  lamplight and looks it.

## Day 5 — Islands and beaches

- **All day** — A **schooner trip** around the bay, stopping at
  islands and swimming beaches. This is what Paraty is for.
- **Evening** — A cachaça distillery tasting; the region invented
  the good stuff.

## Day 6 — Paraty slow

- **Morning** — The **gold trail**, a Portuguese-era stone road
  through the rainforest, or a waterfall walk.
- **Afternoon** — The town itself, unhurried.
- **Evening** — Back toward Rio, or an early night before the
  transfer.

## Day 7 — Fly to Iguaçu

- **Morning** — Transfer to Rio and fly to **Foz do Iguaçu**, about
  two hours.
- **Afternoon** — Settle. The falls are 20 minutes from town.
- **Evening** — Early night; the park is best at opening.

## Day 8 — The falls, Brazilian side

- **Morning** — The **Brazilian park**: a single panoramic trail
  along the gorge that ends on a walkway into the spray beneath the
  Devil's Throat. Two to three hours, and the view that appears on
  every poster.
- **Afternoon** — **Parque das Aves**, the bird park opposite,
  which is much better than it needs to be.
- **Evening** — Dinner in Foz.

## Day 9 — The Argentine side, or the dam

- **Option A** — Cross to **Argentina** for the upper and lower
  circuits, which put you among the falls rather than opposite them.
  Check whether your passport needs an Argentine visa first.
- **Option B** — **Itaipu dam**, one of the largest in the world,
  and a genuinely impressive piece of engineering.
- **Evening** — Last night.

## Day 10 — Fly home

- Fly Foz → Rio or São Paulo and connect. Allow a long layover;
  domestic-to-international transfers here are not quick.

## What to pack

- **Light clothes and a rain layer.** It is hot, and tropical rain
  is sudden.
- **Beachwear you are happy to walk in** — Rio wears it as normal
  dress near the sand.
- **Reef-safe sunscreen and a hat.** The sun here is fierce.
- **A cheap day bag** rather than anything that looks valuable.
- **Type N plugs** — Brazil uses its own standard, and voltage
  varies by city between 127V and 220V. Check your charger's range.
- **Waterproof phone pouch** for Iguaçu and the schooner day.

## FAQ

**Is Rio safe?**
With the habits above, yes for most visitors. Carry little, use
cars after dark, and keep your phone out of sight on quiet streets.
Millions visit each year without incident, and the precautions are
ordinary city sense applied more consistently.

**Which side of Iguaçu should I choose?**
Brazil for the panorama, Argentina to be among the falls. This
itinerary is based in Brazil and treats the crossing as an option,
because it needs a visa check first.

**Do I need a visa?**
Possibly, and this has changed recently — Brazil reinstated
requirements for several nationalities that were previously exempt.
Check yours close to booking rather than relying on older advice.

**Is ten days enough for Brazil?**
For this route, yes. Brazil is a continent — the Amazon, Salvador
and the northeast are all separate trips, each needing another flight
of several hours.
