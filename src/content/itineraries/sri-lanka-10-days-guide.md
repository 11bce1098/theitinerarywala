Sri Lanka fits a continent's worth of trip into an island you
can drive across in a day. In one 10-day loop: a rock-top royal
palace older than most countries, caves of a thousand Buddhas,
wild elephants by the hundred, a blue train winding through tea
gardens into cloud, leopards padding past safari jeeps, a Dutch
fort city on the sea, and the largest animals that have ever
existed surfacing off the morning boat.

It is also among the easiest trips on this site to arrange:
**the visa is free for most visitors**, the island is small
enough to cross in a morning, the gates give **SAARC discounts**
to South Asian passports, and — for readers who grew up with the
Ramayana — half the hill country comes with an epic already
attached.

**Go December–March** for this route (it runs the west, centre
and south — their dry season). April and August–September are
good shoulders (August–September adds Minneriya's great
elephant Gathering); **May–September is the southwest
monsoon** — that's east-coast season, a different guide. All
costs are in Sri Lankan rupees (LKR).

## Before you go: the free visa and the driver decision

- **The visa is free.** Under the scheme expanded in May 2026 a
  long list of nationalities, India included, get a **free ETA —
  30 days, double entry**: apply at the official **eta.gov.lk** (approval
  usually inside 24–48 h; no documents to upload), carry a
  passport valid 6+ months, return ticket and hotel list for
  the immigration desk. Third-party "eta" sites charge for
  what the government gives free.
- **SAARC pricing is real money:** hold a South Asian passport
  — India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, the Maldives or
  Afghanistan — and the Cultural Triangle counters charge the
  SAARC tier: **Sigiriya drops from US$35 to US$20**. Carry the physical passport everywhere;
  copies don't unlock it.
- **The driver-car is how Sri Lanka works:** a car with an
  English-speaking driver runs **~US$55–70/day all-in**
  (fuel, his meals and lodging included) — split by two it
  beats every alternative on an island of winding roads. He
  repositions your luggage on train day, knows which safari
  jeeps don't rattle, and by Day 3 is family. Book via
  well-reviewed operators; tip LKR 1,000–1,500/day at the
  end. In towns, the **PickMe app** is tuk-tuk Uber —
  meters without the negotiation theatre.
- **The respect rules (taken seriously here):** never pose
  with your **back to a Buddha statue** — and visible
  Buddha tattoos have caused genuine deportations. Cover
  shoulders and knees at temples, shoes and hats off, white
  clothing is appreciated at the Tooth Temple. **Poya
  (full-moon) days** are monthly holidays: temples glow,
  alcohol sales pause everywhere.

## Day 1 — Land, breathe, beach warm-up

- **Evening** — Land CMB (4.5 h from the Gulf; barely 1.5 h
  from south India). Your driver meets arrivals; 20 minutes
  to **Negombo** — the sensible first night by the airport.
- **20:00** — Toes-in-sand dinner: lagoon crab or devilled
  prawns (~LKR 2,500–4,500 for two), first wood-apple
  juice, early night. Tomorrow the island begins.

## Day 2 — Into the Cultural Triangle

- **08:00** — Drive north-east (4–5 h with stops): coconut
  arcades, spice-garden pauses, king-coconut roadside
  stands (LKR 200 of pure hydration).
- **14:00** — **Dambulla Cave Temple** (**~US$6**): five
  candle-lit caves under a vast rock, 150+ Buddhas painted
  and gilded across 2,000 years. One barefoot,
  gold-flickering hour.
- **16:30** — Check in near Sigiriya; first sight of the
  Rock across the paddies.
- **19:30** — Village-style dinner: **rice & curry** proper
  — one plate, eight bowls, coconut sambol doing the
  talking (~LKR 1,200–2,500 pp).

**Where to stay in Sigiriya** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Water Garden Sigiriya** — 4.9/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~1,300 reviews), villas with plunge pools facing the
  Rock. ~LKR 90,000+.
- *Mid-range:* **Aliya Resort & Spa** — 4.6/5 (~4,000
  reviews), TripAdvisor's #1 value here: infinity pool with
  a Sigiriya view. ~LKR 30,000–45,000.
- *Value:* **Hotel Sigiriya** — 4.5/5 (~4,200 reviews),
  gardens, pool, Lion Rock from the breakfast table.
  ~LKR 18,000–28,000.

## Day 3 — The Lion Rock, then wild elephants

- **06:45** — **Sigiriya** at gate-opening (**US$20 SAARC /
  US$35 others**; 1,200 steps): the 5th-century sky-palace —
  mirror wall, cloud-maiden frescoes, the giant lion paws,
  and the water gardens' geometry from the summit. Two
  cool-morning hours; by 10 it's a hot staircase queue.
  (The budget-and-photographers' alternative: **Pidurangala
  Rock**, ~LKR 1,000 — the view *of* Sigiriya at sunrise.)
- **11:00** — Pool recovery; lunch.
- **14:30** — **Minneriya or Kaudulla safari** (jeep
  ~LKR 25,000–30,000 + tickets ~US$18 pp; your driver books
  the right park for that week's herds): wild elephants by
  the dozen — and during **August–September's "Gathering",
  by the hundred** — around the ancient reservoir at
  golden hour. One of Asia's great wildlife spectacles.
- **19:30** — Dinner back at base. (History-completists can
  swap the safari for **Polonnaruwa's** ruined medieval
  capital, US$30 — cycling among 12th-century stupas.)

## Day 4 — Kandy: the Temple of the Tooth

- **08:30** — South through spice country (2.5–3 h);
  optional Matale spice-garden stop (free tours, gentle
  sales pitch).
- **12:30** — Kandy: lunch above the lake.
- **14:30** — **Sri Dalada Maligawa — Temple of the Sacred
  Tooth Relic** (**LKR 2,000**): Buddhism's holiest shrine
  on the island, drumming at puja times (morning/evening —
  aim for the 18:30 ceremony), gold-roofed shrine over the
  relic chamber. Dress white-ish and covered; expect
  devotion, not museum quiet.
- **17:00** — Lake loop walk, gem-museum dodge, and the
  viewpoint over the city bowl.
- **19:30** — Dinner; pack a small bag for the train — the
  driver takes the big luggage to Ella by road tomorrow.

**Where to stay in Kandy** (one night)

- *Colonial icon:* **Queen's Hotel** — the 1844 dame on the
  lake beside the Temple; creaking floors, mighty
  atmosphere. ~LKR 25,000–35,000.
- *Modern value:* **Grand Serendib** — TripAdvisor's #1
  value in town, infinity-pool views over the hills.
  ~LKR 20,000–30,000.
- *Temple-close:* **The Radh** — boutique rooms two minutes
  from the Tooth. ~LKR 28,000–38,000.

## Day 5 — THE train: Kandy to Ella

- **08:30** — Board the blue train for the ride every list
  calls the world's most beautiful: **~6.5–7 hours** climbing
  through tea terraces, cloud forest, tunnels and
  waterfall-sides via Nanu Oya and the summit at Pattipola.
- **The ticket truth:** **reserved seats (1st/2nd class,
  roughly LKR 1,500–4,000 by class and agent) open ~30 days
  ahead and sell out** — book via your driver/agent or
  12go-style platforms the day the window opens. Sold out?
  **Unreserved 3rd class (~LKR 500)** is the doors-open,
  locals-and-backpackers adventure — genuinely fun for the
  hardy. The popular pick is the **right-hand side leaving
  Kandy**; honestly, both sides win for hours.
- **12:00** — Tea, samosas and window-lean photography;
  the famous open-door shots are done *sitting* in the
  doorway, not hanging off it — every year someone learns
  this the hard way.
- **15:30** — Into **Ella**, backpack-town-in-the-clouds;
  driver and luggage waiting. Sunset from your balcony or
  a first look at the Nine Arch viewpoint cafés.
- **19:30** — Kottu roti dinner — the chopped-roti
  stir-fry percussion solo you'll hear before you see
  (~LKR 800–1,500).

**Where to stay in Ella** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **98 Acres Resort & Spa** — 4.6/5 (~3,900
  reviews), chalets inside a working tea estate facing
  Little Adam's Peak. ~LKR 70,000+.
- *Boutique:* **EKHO Ella** — 4.9/5 (~1,600 reviews),
  mountain-view rooms and morning yoga. ~LKR 35,000–50,000.
- *Value:* **Ella Flower Garden Resort** — 4.3/5 (~1,800
  reviews), balconies at Little Adam's foot; Ella's
  homestays (LKR 8,000–15,000) are also superb.
  ~LKR 18,000–28,000.

## Day 6 — Ella's greatest hits

- **06:30** — **Little Adam's Peak** (free): 45 gentle
  minutes to a 360° dawn over the gap — the effort-to-view
  bargain of the island.
- **09:30** — **Nine Arch Bridge**: the colonial viaduct
  curving through tea — time it for a train crossing (your
  guesthouse knows today's times; mid-morning usually
  obliges), coffee at a viewpoint café.
- **12:00** — **Tea-factory tour** (Halpewatte or Uva-side
  estates, ~LKR 1,000–2,000 with tasting): pluck-to-packet
  in 24 hours, and the difference between BOP and dust
  finally explained.
- **14:30** — **Ravana Falls** photo stop — and, for
  Ramayana-trail travellers, **Ravana's cave** above it:
  local tradition holds Sita was hidden in these hills
  (the Seetha Amman temple near Nuwara Eliya is the
  detour-day version — see FAQ).
- **16:30** — Flying-Ravana zipline (~US$20) for the
  adrenaline delegation, or third pot of estate tea for
  the rest.
- **19:30** — Farewell hill dinner; early pack — safari
  dawn tomorrow.

## Day 7 — Down to leopard country, on to the sea

- **05:30** — Drive down-country (2.5 h) for a **Yala
  National Park half-day safari** (jeep ~LKR 25,000–35,000
  + tickets/fees ~US$25 pp + service): the world's densest
  leopard population, plus sloth bears, crocs, painted
  storks and elephant crossings. **The honesty:** Block 1
  gets leopard-jam crowded in season — a good driver-
  tracker combo (and a dawn start) is the difference;
  elephant-first families can swap in quieter
  **Udawalawe** en route instead.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then the coast road west (2.5–3 h)
  through Tangalle's coves.
- **17:00** — Check in **Mirissa**; first swim, first
  king-coconut on the sand.
- **19:30** — Beach-barbecue dinner: the day's catch
  chalked on boards (~LKR 3,000–6,000 for two).

**Where to stay on the south coast** (three nights,
Mirissa base)

- *Clifftop splurge:* **Cape Weligama** — the headland
  resort next bay over; villas and that crescent infinity
  pool. ~LKR 120,000+.
- *Boutique:* **The Spice House Mirissa** — 4.8/5 (~800
  reviews), garden calm five minutes from the sand.
  ~LKR 25,000–38,000.
- *Value:* **Triple O Six** — 4.6/5, "cloud-like beds"
  by Coconut Tree Hill. ~LKR 15,000–22,000.

## Day 8 — Blue whales before breakfast

- **06:00** — **Whale watching from Mirissa harbour**
  (**~LKR 12,000–18,000 pp**, 3–5 h, **season
  November–April**): this coast is one of Earth's most
  reliable places to see **blue whales** — plus sperm
  whales, spinner dolphins by the hundred, the odd orca
  cameo. Choose a licensed, whale-respecting operator
  (they keep distance and don't chase); take the
  seasickness tablet *before* boarding, not after.
- **11:00** — Recovery brunch; hammock hours.
- **16:00** — **Coconut Tree Hill** golden hour (free, the
  palm-grove headland of every Sri Lanka reel), then
  **Parrot Rock** at sunset.
- **19:30** — Seafood again because you can; Mirissa's
  lanes hide excellent halal-friendly grills and
  rotti shops.

## Day 9 — Galle Fort day

- **09:00** — Coast road to **Galle** (45 min): stilt-
  fishermen poles at Koggala (posed-for-payment these
  days — photograph honestly or skip), turtle-hatchery
  ethics check (release-focused ones only).
- **10:00** — **Galle Fort** (free, UNESCO): the
  17th-century Dutch walled town — ramparts walk from
  Flag Rock to the lighthouse, frangipani lanes, the
  Dutch Reformed Church, gem shops and gelato. Lunch in
  a fort courtyard café (~LKR 1,500–3,000 pp).
- **15:00** — Optional: Unawatuna's Jungle Beach swim or
  a lace-and-mask browse; cricket on the Galle green if
  the gods smile.
- **18:00** — Ramparts sunset — the whole fort turns out
  for it — then back to Mirissa for the farewell feast:
  lagoon crab curry, the island's masterpiece
  (~LKR 4,000–8,000 for two).

## Day 10 — Home along the expressway

- **Morning** — Last swim; the Southern Expressway runs
  Mirissa→CMB in ~2.5–3 h (your driver times it to the
  flight). Cinnamon, tea and a wooden elephant in the
  bag; SAARC savings spent on the crab. Fly home.

## What to pack

- Temple kit: shoulders/knees cover, easy-off footwear,
  a white-ish outfit for the Tooth; **no Buddha-image
  clothing or visible Buddha tattoos** (cover them —
  this is enforced).
- Layers: Ella nights hit 12–15°C while Mirissa steams —
  one fleece earns its space.
- Safari neutrals, binoculars, dust buff; dawn starts
  mean the warm layer rides in the jeep.
- Reef-safe sunscreen, serious repellent (dusk,
  everywhere), rehydration salts, motion tablets for
  the whale boat and hill bends.
- Grippy sandals for 1,200 rock steps and rampart walls.
- Passport on your person daily — it *is* the SAARC
  discount.

## FAQ

**Is the visa really free — and until when?**
Free ETA, 30 days, double entry, under the scheme
expanded in **May 2026** — no published end date, but
schemes change: confirm on **eta.gov.lk** (only) the
week you book. Approval lands in a day or two by email.

**How does the SAARC discount work?**
Cultural Triangle tickets (Sigiriya, Polonnaruwa,
Anuradhapura) have a SAARC tier — **Sigiriya US$20
instead of US$35** — issued on sight of an original South
Asian passport at the counter. It doesn't apply to
national parks or the whale boats, but the triangle
savings alone cover a dinner.

**Yala or Udawalawe?**
Leopards vs certainty. **Yala** has the world's densest
leopard population and the crowds that follow; a dawn
half-day with a sharp tracker usually delivers.
**Udawalawe** guarantees elephants in open country with
half the jeeps. Photographers pick Yala; young families
and elephant-first hearts pick Udawalawe — this route
accommodates either without changing hotels.

**The Ramayana trail — worth building in?**
For many South Asian travellers it's the emotional spine of
the island: **Seetha Amman Temple** (Nuwara Eliya, at
the traditional Ashok Vatika site), **Ravana Falls and
cave** at Ella (on this route already), Divurumpola
(the Agni Pariksha tradition) and more. Add one
Nuwara Eliya day between Kandy and Ella to do it
properly — or ask us for the dedicated Ramayana-trail
guide, which is firmly on the list.

**Is Sri Lanka stable now?**
Yes — the 2022 crisis is past tense on the ground:
tourism is booming, fuel queues are history, and the
island runs normally. Standard-issue cautions (ocean
rips outside flagged areas, night buses, PickMe over
haggled tuks) are the real list.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Comfortable both ways: Muslim communities across the
island mean **halal signs are common in every town**
(Colombo, Kandy, the east especially; Mirissa's
grills include Muslim-run spots), seafood is
everywhere, and Sri Lankan cooking is a vegetarian's
friend — dal, jackfruit curry, pol sambol, string
hoppers, egg hoppers if eggs qualify. Ask "halal
thiyanawada?" and shops answer honestly.

**What's Sri Lanka guide #2?**
Two live candidates: the **east-coast summer version**
(Trincomalee, Pasikuda, Arugam Bay — for May–September
when this route drips) and the **Ramayana trail
special**. Reader demand will pick.
