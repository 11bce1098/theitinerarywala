South Korea compressed a century of change into fifty years and
kept the receipts: Joseon palaces stand across the street from
robot baristas, monks chant uphill from e-sports stadiums, and
the food — barbecue smoke, market alleys, convenience-store
midnight feasts — may be the best value eating in the developed
world. Your teenagers already know the soundtrack.

This first Korea guide runs the classic pair: **Seoul**, six
days deep (palace-and-hanbok day, DMZ day, three neighbourhoods
of food), then the **KTX bullet train** south to **Busan** —
Korea's salty, candy-coloured second city — before looping home.

**Go late March–April (cherry blossom) or late October–
November (foliage)** — Korea's two jackpot seasons; May–June
and September are the reliable lovely middle. July brings
jangma monsoon humidity; winter is -5 to -10°C, neon-bright
and cheap. All costs are in Korean won (KRW; ₩10,000 ≈
US$7.2).

## Before you go: the visa file and the app truth

### The visa, or the K-ETA

- **Check which one applies to you, because they are very
  different.** A long list of nationalities enters visa-free
  and needs only the **K-ETA** — ~US$7, online, approved in
  days. Everyone else, India included, needs the **C-3-9
  tourist visa**: there is no visa-on-arrival, and K-ETA is
  not available to passports that need a visa.
- **If it's the visa:** apply through a Korean embassy or a
  KVAC centre in the country you live in — typically **7–10
  working days**, and the fee lands around **US$65–90** with
  service charges (single entry, up to 90 days).
- The file Korea likes: 6 months of statements showing
  **steady salary and settled balances** ("averages, not
  last-week top-ups"), employment and leave evidence, proof
  of residence if you are applying outside your own country,
  itinerary and refundable bookings. Apply 3–4 weeks out.

### The app truth (read before landing)

- **Google Maps cannot route you in Korea** — mapping-data
  export laws neuter it. Download **Naver Map and Kakao Map**
  (both have English) before flying; they are the national
  navigation, bus-arrival and everything apps.
- **Papago** beats Google Translate for Korean — menus,
  signs, conversations.
- **T-money** (card ₩3,000–4,000 at any convenience store,
  or phone versions) taps every metro, bus and taxi in the
  country; fares from ~₩1,400. Kakao T hails taxis
  (honest meters, no negotiation culture).
- Cards work almost everywhere; keep ₩50,000 in cash for
  street stalls and markets. **No tipping, anywhere, ever** —
  it can genuinely confuse people.

## Day 1 — Land in Seoul, Myeongdong by night

Stay in **Myeongdong** (shopping + food + airport express
convenience) or **Insadong/Jongno** (palace quarter charm).

- **Afternoon** — Land ICN (~9 h direct from the Gulf).
  **AREX express** to Seoul Station (₩11,000, 43 min),
  T-money loaded, check in.
- **18:30** — **Myeongdong street-food alley** as the
  griddles light: tornado potatoes, cheese-scallion skewers,
  hotteok (brown-sugar pancakes) for dessert — dinner by
  instalments (~₩15,000–25,000 pp).
- **20:30** — First skincare-shop reconnaissance (the haul
  comes later; resistance now builds character). Early
  night — hanbok waits.

**Where to stay in Seoul** (five nights + one at the end)

- *Luxury:* **JW Marriott Seoul** — 4.9/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~2,300 reviews), Express-Bus-Terminal connected, pool
  with a skyline. ~₩450,000+.
- *Boutique:* **Hotel28 Myeongdong** — 4.8/5,
  cinema-themed calm in the middle of everything.
  ~₩220,000–300,000.
- *Value:* **Hotel Skypark Myeongdong 3** — steps from the
  night market; TripAdvisor's value shortlist regular.
  ~₩130,000–180,000.

## Day 2 — Hanbok day: palaces for free

- **09:00** — Rent a **hanbok** near Gyeongbokgung
  (₩10,000–15,000 for 4 hours, hair-braiding extra) — and
  here's the trick: **hanbok wearers enter the royal
  palaces free**.
- **09:40** — **Gyeongbokgung** (**₩3,000 without hanbok;
  ₩0 with; CLOSED TUESDAYS**): position for the **10:00
  royal guard-changing ceremony** at Gwanghwamun Gate,
  then Geunjeongjeon throne hall and the lake pavilion —
  the photos take themselves.
- **12:30** — Return the hanbok; lunch in **Insadong**
  (bibimbap, ~₩10,000–15,000).
- **14:00** — **Bukchon Hanok Village**: 600-year-old
  tiled-roof lanes between the palaces — the famous view
  down alley 31. **Etiquette matters:** people live here —
  quiet hours are enforced, whisper and wander.
- **16:30** — Insadong's **Ssamziegil** spiral market and a
  traditional tea house (omija tea, ₩8,000, worth every
  antioxidant).
- **18:30** — **Cheonggyecheon stream** walk at lamp-light
  into **Gwangjang Market**: mung-bean pancakes
  (bindaetteok, ₩5,000), "drug" kimbap, tteokbokki — eat
  standing at the grandmothers' counters (~₩15,000 pp
  buys a feast).

## Day 3 — The DMZ: staring into North Korea

- **07:30** — **DMZ day tour** (~₩100,000–120,000 pp,
  passport mandatory, most tours closed Mondays): the
  world's tensest border as a day trip — **the 3rd
  Infiltration Tunnel** (walk into the dug-under-the-
  border tunnel itself), **Dora Observatory** (telescopes
  across the wire into a North Korean village and its
  giant flagpole), Imjingak's rusted locomotive and
  ribbon-covered fence.
- **The honesty:** the famous **JSA blue huts** (Panmunjom)
  have been suspended/limited for tourists since 2023 —
  standard tours don't include them, whatever old blogs
  say; and tours can cancel on short notice when tensions
  rise. It remains the most sobering, fascinating day in
  Korea. Dress plainly; jokes travel badly here.
- **16:30** — Back in Seoul; decompress the modern way:
- **18:00** — **Hongdae** — buskers and K-pop dance crews
  on the walking street, vintage shops, then **norebang**
  (private karaoke, ₩20,000–30,000/hour of glory) and
  Korean fried chicken (~₩22,000 a bird, halal versions
  exist — see FAQ).

## Day 4 — The secret garden and the two Gangnams

- **10:00** — **Changdeokgung Palace + the Secret Garden**
  (₩3,000 + ₩5,000 timed garden tour — book online;
  **closed Mondays** — note the alternation: Gyeongbokgung
  rests Tuesdays, Changdeokgung Mondays): the Huwon's
  pavilion-and-lotus-pond valley is the palaces' quiet
  masterpiece.
- **12:30** — **Ikseondong** hanok lanes — the old houses
  turned café-bakery wonderland (brunch ~₩15,000).
- **15:00** — Metro south of the river: the **Starfield
  Library** at COEX (free — the two-storey book
  cathedral), the tongue-in-cheek **Gangnam Style hands**
  statue, or **Bongeunsa temple** doing serene resistance
  across from the mall.
- **18:30** — **K-BBQ initiation, done right**: samgyeopsal
  or galbi on the table grill — staff wield the scissors,
  you wrap (lettuce + meat + ssamjang + garlic, one bite),
  and the side dishes refill free (~₩25,000–40,000 pp).
  Beef-only grills abound for the pork-averse.

## Day 5 — The choice day (and the tower)

Pick your Seoul:

- **The K-drama pilgrimage:** **Nami Island + Petite
  France** day (~₩70,000–90,000 tours, or DIY by ITX) —
  the tree avenues and lakeside lanes half your favourite
  dramas filmed on.
- **The theme-park day:** **Lotte World** (indoor+outdoor,
  ~₩62,000) or Everland (bigger, further) — kids vote,
  parents comply.
- **The Seoul-classics day:** morning **Namsan** walk to
  **N Seoul Tower** (observatory ~₩21,000; the
  love-locks terrace is free and arguably better), War
  Memorial (free, superb), then **Itaewon** — the Seoul
  Central Mosque and the city's **halal restaurant row**
  (see FAQ) for dinner.
- **20:30** — Whichever day you chose: Han River hour —
  convenience-store ramyeon at a Hangang park bench,
  cooked at the store's hot-water station, city lights
  across the water. Seoul's cheapest perfect moment
  (~₩5,000).

## Day 6 — KTX south, into the rainbow village

- **09:00** — Seoul Station → **Busan by KTX**: 2h40,
  **₩59,800** standard class (book seats on Korail's
  app days ahead; right side for late-route sea
  glimpses).
- **12:30** — Check in Haeundae or Gwangalli; market
  lunch.
- **14:00** — **Gamcheon Culture Village**: the hillside
  refugee settlement repainted into a Lego-bright maze —
  buy the **stamp map (₩2,000)**, hunt the murals, queue
  briefly for the **Little Prince ledge photo**, get lost
  on purpose (2–3 hours).
- **18:00** — Down to **Nampo-dong**: BIFF Square's film-
  festival handprints and street snacks (ssiat hotteok —
  the seed-stuffed Busan upgrade), then **Jagalchi fish
  market** — pick your fish downstairs, they grill it
  upstairs (~₩30,000–50,000 for two; the grilled-mackerel
  route needs no adventurousness).

**Where to stay in Busan** (three nights)

- *Beachfront luxury:* **Grand Josun Busan** — 4.9/5
  (~900 reviews), the pool deck floating over Haeundae
  sand. ~₩350,000+.
- *Central classic:* **Lotte Hotel Busan** — 4.8/5
  (~2,600 reviews), Seomyeon's metro-hub polish.
  ~₩200,000–280,000.
- *View value:* **Hotel Aqua Palace** — 4.7/5,
  Gwangalli-front rooms staring at the lit bridge.
  ~₩130,000–180,000.

## Day 7 — Sea temple, sky capsule

- **09:00** — Bus/taxi 40 min east to **Haedong
  Yonggungsa** (free): the rare Korean temple built ON
  the sea rocks — dragons, lanterns, waves exploding
  below the Buddha. Go early; coaches arrive by 11.
- **12:00** — Back to **Haeundae**: raw-fish alley or
  dwaeji gukbap (Busan's pork-rice soup — the city's
  soul dish, ~₩10,000) for the initiated.
- **14:00** — **Blueline Park**: ride the **sky capsule**
  along the clifftop rail to Cheongsapo (couple capsule
  ~₩60,000–70,000 one-way — book a slot; the pastel
  capsules over the sea are Busan's postcard) and the
  **beach train** back (~₩7,000 pp). Daljmaji hill cafés
  if time floats.
- **17:00** — Haeundae beach hour; sandcastle-grade sand,
  proper swimming in season.
- **19:30** — **Gwangalli Beach** evening: the
  diamond-lit bridge, café-row neon — and on **Saturday
  nights, the free drone show** writes whales and hearts
  over the water (10 minutes, check that week's
  schedule). Dinner on the strip (~₩20,000–35,000 pp).

## Day 8 — Cliffs, soup and the bathhouse question

- **09:30** — Choose your coast: **Taejongdae** cliffs
  (Danubi train loop, lighthouse over jade water) or the
  **Songdo cable car** (crystal-floor cabins over the
  bay, ~₩20,000 return).
- **12:30** — **Seomyeon** for lunch and Korea's best
  underground shopping maze.
- **15:00** — The cultural deep-end (optional but
  memorable): **Spa Land Centum** — the famous
  jimjilbang (Korean bathhouse; ~₩25,000): note honestly,
  the bath floors are nude and gender-split (universally
  observed, briefly startling, quickly liberating), while
  the shared sauna zones are pyjama-clothed and
  family-friendly. Alternatives: **Huinnyeoul culture
  village's** cliff lane — Busan's quieter, whiter
  Gamcheon.
- **19:00** — Farewell-Busan dinner: hoe (Korean sashimi)
  on Millak's raw-fish floors, or one more KBBQ with the
  bridge lights.

## Day 9 — Back up the line, last Seoul evening

- **11:00** — Final Haeundae stroll; 13:00 **KTX north**
  (₩59,800).
- **16:00** — Seoul re-entry for the finale: the
  **Myeongdong/Olive Young haul** (the skincare suitcase
  event this trip was partly always about) and any K-pop
  merch pilgrimage still outstanding.
- **19:30** — Last supper by temperament: a splurge on
  **temple cuisine** (Balwoo Gongyang-style tasting,
  ~₩50,000–80,000 — vegan Buddhist fine dining, book
  ahead), or Gwangjang round two because you've thought
  about that bindaetteok for a week.
- Overnight near Myeongdong/Seoul Station for the
  morning train to the plane.

## Day 10 — Home from Incheon

- **Morning** — AREX to ICN (leave 3.5 h — the airport
  is its own city; the Korean Cultural Street and
  transit spa are genuinely good). Long-haul flight
  home, hand luggage 40% sheet masks by volume.

## What to pack

- Walking shoes rated for 20,000-step days and palace
  gravel; slip-on-ability helps (floors, fitting rooms,
  temple halls).
- Layers: spring/autumn swing 8–20°C daily; winter needs
  the real coat; summer needs the smallest clothes you
  own plus a rain shell (jangma).
- Modest-enough cover for temples; socks without holes
  (you'll be shoeless more than you think).
- An empty duffel *inside* the suitcase — the
  skincare/merch haul is not a hypothesis.
- Power bank (the apps run the day), T-money card kept
  from arrival, ₩50,000 street-food float.
- Kids: the DMZ has minimum-age quirks by tour (check);
  Lotte World compensates.

## FAQ

**How strict is the Korean visa for Indians?**
Document-strict, decision-fair: the embassy wants a
clean 6-month statement story, employer letters and a
credible itinerary. UAE-based applicants report
7–10-working-day turnarounds routinely. No VoA exists,
and **K-ETA is not available to passports that need a visa** —
don't buy one from a copycat site.

**Is the DMZ safe — and worth it?**
Statistically as safe as any escorted tour in Korea
(the machinery around it is the point), and yes —
standing in a tunnel North Korea dug under the border
recalibrates the evening news forever. Know the 2026
reality: **JSA/Panmunjom visits remain suspended for
general tourists**; the 3rd Tunnel + Dora Observatory
format is the real product. Tours cancel when politics
twitch — book early-trip, keep a swap day.

**When exactly for cherry blossoms or foliage?**
Blossoms: **late March (Busan first) to mid-April
(Seoul)** — Jinhae's festival near Busan is the
national epicentre. Foliage: **late October–early
November**, palaces and Namsan glowing. Both windows
sell hotels out a season ahead; this itinerary works
identically in either.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Better than reputation, with a map: **Itaewon is halal
HQ** (Seoul Central Mosque, certified Korean fried
chicken and BBQ, kebab row), Myeongdong keeps several
halal-certified Korean kitchens, Busan is thinner
(Haeundae's Indian/Pakistani spots carry it).
Vegetarians need the phrases — broth and fish sauce
hide everywhere — but bibimbap ("gogi ppae juseyo" —
no meat), japchae, kimbap variants and **temple
cuisine** (Korea's 1,700-year vegan tradition) make it
genuinely rewarding. Note: traditional kimchi often
contains fermented seafood — ask, or enjoy the
mul-kimchi.

**Language barrier?**
Lower than feared: metro and signs are bilingual,
menus have photos, Papago handles the rest, and
politeness ("annyeonghaseyo" + a small bow) opens
every door. The apps matter more than the alphabet —
though hangul takes literally one afternoon to learn
and turns every sign into a puzzle you can solve.

**What's Korea guide #2?**
**Jeju Island** (Hallasan, lava tubes, haenyeo divers,
the beaches) or the **seasonal special** (a
blossom/foliage-chasing route timed to the week). And
for the K-pop households: a dedicated fandom-Seoul
day-by-day is quietly under consideration.
