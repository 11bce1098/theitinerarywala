Transylvania is not what the films made of it. It is a high plateau
ringed by the Carpathians, settled eight hundred years ago by German
speakers who built fortified churches and walled towns, and left
behind some of the best-preserved medieval streetscapes in Europe.
Then it is villages of hay ricks and horse carts that look like a
century ago, because in parts they still work that way.

**Brașov** sits under a forested mountain with a Gothic church so
soot-blackened it is called the Black Church. **Sighișoara** is a
walled citadel with a clock tower and a house where Vlad Țepeș was
born. **Sibiu** has pastel squares and roofs with eyes.

And yes, **Bran Castle** is marketed as Dracula's. The honest
version of that is below — it is worth visiting anyway.

Romania is also the best-value country in the European Union by a
clear margin, which makes eight days here cost what four would in
the west.

## Before you go: driving, Dracula and the Schengen question

### Entry, and where Romania now sits

- **Romania joined the Schengen area** — air and sea borders first,
  land borders following — so the same visa rules apply as the rest
  of the area. The process is in our
  [Schengen visa guide](/guides/schengen-visa-how-it-works/).
- **It is not in the euro.** Romania uses the **leu (RON)**, and
  will for some years yet.
- Visa-free nationalities need only a passport.

### Drive, and here is why

- **Hire a car.** This is the rare European route where the car
  genuinely beats the train: the Saxon villages, the fortified
  churches and the mountain roads are where the trip lives, and none
  of them is on a rail line.
- **Roads are good between cities and rough in villages.** A normal
  car is fine; drive in daylight.
- **Horse carts, dogs and unlit cyclists** share rural roads. Speeds
  are low for good reason.
- **The Transfăgărășan** — the mountain road built on Ceaușescu's
  orders, hairpinning to over 2,000 m — is **only open roughly July
  to October**, weather permitting. Outside that window it is
  closed by snow and there is no argument to be had with it. The
  **Transalpina** nearby has the same seasonality.
- If you would rather not drive, trains link Bucharest, Brașov,
  Sighișoara and Sibiu, slowly but cheaply, and you lose the
  villages.

### The Dracula question, honestly

- **Bram Stoker never visited Romania.** He took a name from a
  history book and a setting from a travelogue.
- **Vlad III Țepeș** was a real fifteenth-century ruler, brutal by
  modern standards and a national hero for resisting the Ottomans.
  He was born in Sighișoara.
- **Bran Castle has almost no connection to either of them.** It is a
  genuine, handsome fourteenth-century fortress that was assigned
  the Dracula story for tourism. Go for the castle, not the myth.
- **Corvin Castle** at Hunedoara is the more spectacular building if
  castles are why you came.

### Money and the practicalities

- **The leu**, and prices will feel low. A good restaurant meal costs
  what a sandwich does in western Europe.
- Cards are widely accepted in cities; carry cash for villages,
  church donations and small guesthouses.
- **Tipping** around 10% is normal.
- **Guesthouses (*pensiuni*)** are the best accommodation on this
  route — family-run, often with breakfast and dinner included, and
  far nicer than the hotels at the same price.

### When to go

- **May to September** is the season. **July to October** if you
  want the Transfăgărășan open.
- **September** is the sweet spot: warm, harvest-time, and the
  mountain road still open.
- Winter is cold and rural roads become difficult, though Brașov's
  Christmas market is lovely.

## Day 1 — Land in Bucharest

- **Afternoon** — Land, collect the car or leave it until you head
  north.
- **Evening** — The **Old Town** for dinner, and the sheer scale of
  the **Palace of the Parliament** from outside — the heaviest
  building in the world, and a monument to what it cost.

## Day 2 — Bucharest, then north

- **Morning** — The **Village Museum**, an open-air collection of
  rural houses from across the country, which sets up everything you
  will see later.
- **Afternoon** — Drive north to **Brașov**, about three hours
  through the mountains.
- **Evening** — **Piața Sfatului**, the council square, ringed by
  cafés.

## Day 3 — Brașov and Bran

- **Morning** — The **Black Church**, **Rope Street** — one of the
  narrowest in Europe — and the cable car up Tâmpa for the view.
- **Afternoon** — **Bran Castle**, 30 minutes away, and
  **Râșnov** fortress on the way back, which is emptier and has
  better views.
- **Evening** — Back in Brașov.

## Day 4 — Peleș, and the Saxon villages

- **Morning** — **Peleș Castle** at Sinaia, a neo-Renaissance royal
  palace of carved wood and stained glass, and the most beautiful
  building in Romania.
- **Afternoon** — East into the **Saxon villages** — **Viscri** or
  **Biertan** — for the fortified churches, UNESCO-listed and
  frequently empty.
- **Evening** — A village *pensiune*, with dinner cooked by your
  hosts.

## Day 5 — Sighișoara

- **Morning** — Drive to **Sighișoara**, a walled citadel still
  lived in, with the **Clock Tower** and the covered stairway up to
  the church on the hill.
- **Afternoon** — The citadel's lanes, which take an afternoon and
  no plan.
- **Evening** — Dinner inside the walls.

## Day 6 — Sibiu

- **Morning** — Drive to **Sibiu**, about two hours.
- **Afternoon** — The **Large and Small Squares**, the **Bridge of
  Lies**, and the roofs with eyelid dormers that watch the street.
- **Evening** — Sibiu eats well; it was a European Capital of
  Culture and has not forgotten.

## Day 7 — The mountain road, or the gorge

- **In season (July–October)** — The **Transfăgărășan**: hairpins to
  2,042 m, Bâlea Lake at the top, and a tunnel through the ridge.
  Allow the whole day.
- **Out of season** — **Corvin Castle** at Hunedoara instead, which
  is the castle Bran pretends to be.
- **Evening** — Back toward Bucharest, or sleep en route.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- Drive back to Bucharest — around four hours from Sibiu — and
  return the car with time in hand.

## What to pack

- **Layers.** Transylvania sits high and the evenings cool sharply
  even in August.
- **Walking shoes** for citadel cobbles and church hills.
- **A rain jacket** — mountain weather changes fast.
- **European Type C/F plugs.**
- **Cash in small notes** for village churches, which often ask a
  few lei from a caretaker with a key.

## FAQ

**Do I have to drive?**
Not strictly — the trains connect the cities — but the Saxon
villages and the mountain roads are the best of the country and the
train does not reach them.

**Is Bran Castle worth it?**
As a castle, yes. As a Dracula site, it is a marketing exercise. Go
knowing that and you will enjoy it; go expecting Stoker and you
will not.

**Is the Transfăgărășan open when I am there?**
Only roughly July to October. Check before building a day around it,
and have Corvin Castle as the alternative.

**Is Romania cheap?**
It is the best value in the EU by a distance. Expect good food and
comfortable guesthouses at a fraction of western European prices.
