The Canadian Rockies are what happens when glaciers spend two
million years sculpting and then fill their own quarries with
meltwater: **Lake Louise** and **Moraine Lake** glow an
un-Photoshopped turquoise (rock flour suspended in the water —
science, not filters), the **Icefields Parkway** strings 230
kilometres of them together, and elk wander through town like
they pay rates.

Two pieces of homework decide this trip. First, **the visa**:
processing commonly runs **30–60 days**, and longer from
some countries — this is a book-months-ahead trip, though
there's an **eTA shortcut if you hold a valid US visa**
(details below). Second, **Moraine Lake**: private cars are banned
for good; the shuttle booking game has rules, and this guide
plays them for you.

The route: **Calgary → Banff (3 nights) → the Parkway →
Jasper (2 nights) → back**, self-drive, **June–September**
(the Moraine road itself runs 1 June–12 October). Figure
**{{CAD 2600}} per person**
excluding international flights — with 2026's pleasant
surprise that **park entry is free all summer** under the
Canada Strong Pass.

## Before you go: the admin

- - **The visitor visa (TRV), honestly:** **CAD 100 + CAD 85
  biometrics** per person at your nearest visa centre, filed like a
  Schengen-plus (proof of residence, bank statements, employment
  letter, ties, itinerary) — and processing commonly runs **30–60
  days, longer from some countries, plus passport-return time**.
  Translation: **apply 3–4 months before summer dates.** The prize
  is generous: TRVs are usually **multiple-entry, valid up to 10
  years**.
- **The eTA shortcut (check this first):** Indian
  citizens who **hold a valid US non-immigrant visa
  or have held a Canadian visa in the past 10 years**
  can instead apply for a **CAD 7 eTA** — minutes
  online — **valid for arrival by AIR only**. If your
  US visa from the [USA guides](/itineraries/usa-east-coast-guide/)
  is current, this trip's paperwork just became a
  web form. Verify eligibility on canada.ca before
  relying on it.
- **Flights:** no non-stops from the Gulf to Calgary — one-stop
  via Toronto (Emirates DXB–YYZ + Air Canada),
  London, or Frankfurt; ~US$1,100–1,750 return,
  16–20 h door to door.
- **Park entry:** normally **CAD 12.25/adult/day
  (family/group CAD 24.50)** or the annual Discovery
  Pass (family CAD 167.50) — but in **2026, 19 June–
  7 September, admission is FREE under the Canada
  Strong Pass**. Outside that window, the family
  daily rate wins for this trip length.
- **Driving:** most foreign licences in English work
  for visits; roads are wide, calm and moose-aware.
  Fuel ~CAD 1.50–1.75/L; **fill up in Banff or
  Saskatchewan River Crossing — the Parkway has one
  expensive pump**.
- **Wildlife rules (they're serious):** never feed or
  approach; 30 m from elk, 100 m from bears; a
  "bear jam" means stay IN the car; carry bear spray
  (~CAD 50, rentable) for any real hike and know how
  to use it. Fines are large and the animals' lives
  depend on the rules more than yours does.

## Day 1 — Land in Calgary, drive into the wall

- **Afternoon** — Land at YYC, collect the car
  (book months ahead for summer; ~CAD 85–100/day),
  grocery-run at a Calgary superstore (Banff prices
  will thank you).
- **17:00** — The TransCanada west: prairie flatness
  for 45 minutes, then the Rockies stand up on the
  horizon like a special effect. Check in — Calgary
  tonight (cheaper) or straight to Banff if you
  land early.
- Dinner note for the audience: Calgary's northeast
  is one of Canada's great desi food quarters —
  a proper halal shawarma-and-karahi send-off
  before mountain prices begin.

## Days 2–4 — Banff

- **Day 2 — Town + the classics:** Banff Avenue
  with its postcard mountain backdrop; **Banff
  Gondola** up Sulphur Mountain (~CAD 70–85 online,
  dynamic — the boardwalk summit ridge is the
  view); Bow Falls and the **Fairmont Banff
  Springs** castle photo stop; soak at the **Banff
  Upper Hot Springs** (CAD 17.50) as the alpenglow
  arrives. Evening: elk on the golf-course road,
  reliably.
- **Day 3 — THE lakes day (the one you planned
  months ago):**
  - **Moraine Lake:** private vehicles banned —
    book the **Parks Canada shuttle (CAD 12.75
    adult + CAD 3.50 booking fee)** the moment
    your 40%-advance window opens (**15 April
    2026** for the season) or fight for the
    **48-hours-before release of the remaining
    60%** at 8 am Mountain Time. Sunrise
    obsessives: the **Alpine Start shuttles (4 &
    5 am)** or private sunrise buses (~CAD 60–90)
    with hotel pickup.
  - The shuttle ticket includes the **Lake
    Connector**, so do both: Moraine's Valley of
    the Ten Peaks first (the old $20 bill view),
    then **Lake Louise** — walk the shoreline to
    the far end where the crowds evaporate, or
    the 3.5 km teahouse trail to **Lake Agnes**
    (cash for tea and scones at the 1905
    teahouse).
- **Day 4 — Choose your Banff:** paddle Lake
  Minnewanka or Vermilion Lakes (canoe ~CAD 65/h);
  the Johnston Canyon catwalk falls (go before 9
  or after 5); or Yoho's **Emerald Lake +
  Takakkaw Falls** over the BC line — the
  connoisseur's add-on. Evening: Bow Valley
  Parkway wildlife loop at dusk.

**Where to stay in Banff** (three nights — book
first, before flights; summer sells out by spring)

- *Luxury:* **Fairmont Banff Springs** — 4.4/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~9,900 reviews), the 1888 "castle
  in the Rockies". CAD 800+.
- *Mid-range:* **Moose Hotel & Suites** — 4.6/5
  (~1,700 reviews), rooftop hot pools facing the
  peaks. ~CAD 350–450.
- *Value:* **Banff Aspen Lodge** — 4.2/5 (~2,600
  reviews), walkable, hot tubs, breakfast add-on.
  ~CAD 240–300. (True budget: HI-Banff Alpine
  Centre from ~CAD 60/bed.)

## Day 5 — The Icefields Parkway

- **08:00** — Fuelled, snacked, camera charged:
  the **230 km Banff→Jasper Parkway**, routinely
  called one of the world's great drives. The
  stop list that matters: **Bow Lake** (mirror
  mornings), **Peyto Lake** (10-min walk to the
  wolf-head turquoise overlook), Saskatchewan
  Crossing (fuel), the Weeping Wall, and the big
  bend up Sunwapta Pass.
- **13:00** — **Columbia Icefield**: the
  **Ice Explorer + Skywalk combo (~CAD 126
  adult / 82 child, book 2+ weeks out for the
  price and the slot)** — a monster-truck bus
  onto the **Athabasca Glacier** (stand on 200 m
  of ice, fill a bottle with glacier melt) then
  the glass horseshoe 280 m over the Sunwapta
  valley. Touristy and glorious; afternoon slots
  after 3:30 run ~20% cheaper.
- **16:30** — Waterfall dessert: **Sunwapta
  Falls** and **Athabasca Falls** roadside stops,
  then into Jasper by evening — bighorn sheep
  committee likely en route.

**Where to stay in Jasper** (two nights) — with
the honest note: the **2024 wildfire** took a
bite of the town's hotel stock; rebuilt Jasper is
open and welcoming, but **inventory is tighter
and prices firmer — book these nights first**,
and forgive the odd construction corner.

- *Splurge:* **Fairmont Jasper Park Lodge** —
  the lakeside cabin resort across the water
  (check current reviews/rates; CAD 600+).
- *Mid-range:* **Forest Park Hotel** — 3.7/5
  (~3,600 reviews), the dependable big lodge.
  ~CAD 280–360.
- *Value:* **Jasper Inn & Suites** — 3.8/5
  (~3,100 reviews), kitchenette rooms.
  ~CAD 220–280.

## Day 6 — Jasper: Maligne country

- **08:30** — **Maligne Lake** (48 km of
  moose-prone road; eyes up): the **Spirit
  Island cruise (~CAD 95–135, 90 min, book
  ahead)** to the tiny treed island that stands
  for the whole Rockies in a million calendars.
  *(Budget swap: skip the boat, kayak the
  lakefront or walk Moose Loop free.)*
- **13:00** — **Maligne Canyon**: the karst slot
  gorge by bridges 1→4 (go bottom-up to dodge
  the coaches); picnic by the Athabasca.
- **16:00** — Town afternoon: Jasper's
  main-street brewery and fudge economy, or
  **Pyramid Island's** boardwalk sunset.
- **Dark** — Jasper is a **Dark Sky Preserve**
  (the world's second largest): drive five
  minutes out, kill the lights, and let the
  Milky Way do the encore. October's Dark Sky
  Festival is the seed of a future trip.

## Day 7 — Parkway southbound, Calgary landing

- **08:30** — The Parkway in reverse — morning
  light flips every view; stop at whatever you
  rationed (Mistaya Canyon deserves its 20
  minutes), brunch-picnic at Bow Lake.
- **14:00** — Optional last hit: **Johnston
  Canyon** if missed, or Canmore's café strip —
  Banff's cheaper, quieter sibling.
- **17:30** — Roll into Calgary, drop bags;
  farewell dinner in the northeast's desi
  quarter or 17th Ave's global row.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- Morning YYC return (fuel the car before the
  airport ramp), one-stop home. Maple syrup and
  smoked-salmon packs at the airport cost +40%
  — you did that shopping at the Calgary
  superstore on Day 1, obviously.

## What to pack

- Layers for genuine ranges: July valley
  afternoons hit 25°C; the glacier and dawn
  lakes sit near 5°C. Fleece + shell always in
  the daypack.
- Proper walking shoes (teahouse trail,
  canyons), sandals for hot springs.
- Sunscreen + sunglasses (altitude + lake
  glare), bug spray for June–July dusk.
- Binoculars for the bear jams (from the car!),
  zoom lens, polariser filter — turquoise water
  rewards it.
- Refillable bottles (glacier-fed taps), cooler
  bag for the grocery system.
- **Type A/B (US-style) plugs** — most of the world
  needs an adapter.
- Swimwear for hot springs; a warm hat for the
  Skywalk even in August.

## FAQ

**Is the visa really the hard part?**
It's the long part. The file itself is standard
(steady employment + travel history helps);
the queue is the issue — hence the 3–4 month
runway, and hence the **eTA check first**: a
valid US visit visa in your passport converts
this whole section into a CAD 7, ten-minute
form (air arrivals only). Families mixing
statuses: everyone needs their own
authorisation.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Calgary is genuinely strong — one of Canada's
big halal cities (northeast Calgary's
Pakistani/Afghan/Arab strips, plus downtown
shawarma). In **Banff and Jasper**, formal
halal thins out fast: the working pattern is
seafood/veg dining plus the grocery system
(both towns' supermarkets stock basics; Calgary
superstores stock everything, halal meat
included — hence the Day 1 shop). Vegetarians
do fine everywhere: mountain towns love a
veggie burger, and Indian restaurants exist in
both Banff and Jasper.

**Moraine Lake with zero shuttle luck — any
way in?**
Yes, three: the **48-hour release** (60% of all
seats, 8:00 am MT, two days out — set the
alarm, it works), **Roam public transit route
10** from Lake Louise village, and **private
sunrise shuttles** (~CAD 60–90 with pickup) —
which are also the only sane sunrise play.
What doesn't work: driving up to "see" — the
road gate is enforced and turnaround chaos is
real.

**When exactly should we go?**
**Late June–early September** for full access
(Moraine road, teahouses, glacier tours all
open) — July–August is peak everything,
including prices. The connoisseur pick:
**mid-September** — golden larches above Lake
Louise, thinner crowds, sharp light — accepting
that the free-entry window has closed and
first snow can dust the passes. Winter is a
magnificent, different (ski) trip.

**Is Jasper fully open after the 2024 fire?**
Open, rebuilt in large part, and visibly
grateful for visitors — tourism IS the
recovery. About a third of the town's
buildings were lost; some hotels are new-build
fresh, some views carry burn scars (the
Parkway approach shows regrowth already
greening). Book early, tip well, and know
Maligne, the canyon and the lakes were
untouched.

**What's Canada guide #2?**
**The eastern classic** — Toronto, Niagara,
Montréal and Québec City by rail — or
**Vancouver + Sea-to-Sky** joined to this very
route as the 12-day western epic. Votes
welcome; both on the list.
