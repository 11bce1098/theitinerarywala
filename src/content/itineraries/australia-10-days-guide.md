Australia is the far one — 13 hours from the Gulf, a fare that
stings, a visa that rewards patience — and then you're standing
at Circular Quay watching the Opera House glow while ferries
fan across the harbour, and the maths stops mattering. This
guide runs the definitive first pass: **Sydney** (the world's
most beautiful working harbour), **Cairns** (the only address
on earth with the Great Barrier Reef out front), and
**Melbourne** (coffee, laneways, and the coast road every car
ad rents).

Three cities, two internal flights, ten days — brisk but
proven. And one consolation for the costs: **nobody tips in
Australia**, prices include tax, and half the best things
(Bondi's clifftop walk, Melbourne's free trams, penguins at
St Kilda pier) cost nothing.

**Go September–November or March–May** — the overlap windows
when Sydney and Melbourne are mild and Cairns is dry-season
gorgeous. December–February is southern summer (glorious
cities, wet-season Cairns); June–August flips it (whales off
Sydney, dry warm Cairns, chilly Melbourne). All costs are in
Australian dollars (AUD).

## Before you go: the visa needs a head start

- **Two routes in, and your passport fixes which one you get.**
  The EU, UK, US, Canada, Japan, Singapore and a number of
  others clear an **ETA or eVisitor** online in minutes. For a
  long list of others, India included, no such shortcut exists —
  those need the **Visitor visa (subclass 600)**: **AUD 200**,
  applied entirely online through **ImmiAccount**, with
  biometrics at a VFS centre if requested. Check yours before
  you plan around a date.
- **The honest timeline:** most tourist grants come in a few
  weeks, but processing is case-by-case and can stretch —
  **apply 2–3 months before travel** and don't buy
  non-refundable flights first. Grants are commonly 3, 6 or
  12 months, often multi-entry.
- **What refusals look like:** weak or unexplained bank
  funds, vague itineraries, inconsistent employment info,
  thin home ties. The file that works: 3–6 months of
  statements, employer NOC + leave approval, day-by-day
  itinerary (this page is one), refundable bookings, and proof
  of residence if you are applying from a country other than
  your own.
- **Border strictness is real:** Australia's biosecurity is
  the world's fussiest — **declare every food item**
  (yes, the snacks in your bag and the sweets from home) on the
  incoming card. Declared = usually fine; undeclared =
  AUD 300+ fines. When in doubt, tick yes.

## Money, sun and sea — the local rules

- **Tap your card on everything**: Sydney's Opal system and
  Melbourne's trams take contactless bank cards directly;
  Sydney fares **cap daily (~AUD 18–19)** — the Manly ferry
  and Blue Mountains train ride inside the cap, which is
  cheating, legally.
- **No tipping. Anywhere.** Menu price = final price
  (weekend/holiday surcharges of 10–15% are printed on the
  menu). Australia is expensive *honestly*.
- **Slip, slop, slap:** the UV index here breaks
  instruments — SPF50+, hat and sunglasses are equipment,
  not accessories, even on cloudy days.
- **Swim between the red-and-yellow flags** — rips kill
  more visitors than every animal combined. Flags =
  lifeguards watching that exact stretch.
- Cairns extras: **stinger season (Nov–May)** means netted
  beaches and free lycra suits on reef boats (wear one);
  **croc signs are not decorative** — no wild-river swims.
- eSIM ~US$15–25; Telstra coverage rules outside cities.

## Day 1 — Land in Sydney, harbour hello

Stay around **Circular Quay/The Rocks** (splurge-worthy,
everything on foot) or **Darling Harbour** (families, value).

- **Afternoon** — Land SYD (direct ~13.5 h from the Gulf).
  Airport train in (~$17), check in, shower off the flight.
- **17:00** — The walk that resets the jet lag: **Circular
  Quay → Opera House forecourt → Royal Botanic Garden's
  Mrs Macquarie's Chair** — the postcard angle with bridge
  and sails together.
- **19:30** — Dinner in **The Rocks** (1788's cobbled
  quarter): pub classics under the bridge pylons
  (~$30–45 pp). Early night; the harbour will still be
  there tomorrow.

**Where to stay in Sydney** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Langham Sydney** — 4.7/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~2,000 reviews), Observatory Hill quiet above The
  Rocks. ~$450+.
- *Harbour-view value:* **Shangri-La Sydney** — 4.2/5
  (~8,800 reviews), TripAdvisor's #1 value: Opera
  House-and-bridge panoramas from the high floors.
  ~$280–380.
- *Value:* **Kith Hotel Darling Harbour** — 4.5/5, fresh
  rooms by the water's family precinct. ~$180–250.

## Day 2 — Opera House inside, Manly by ferry

- **09:00** — **Sydney Opera House guided tour** (**$49
  adult / $30 child**, 1 h — book by 5pm the day before;
  ~300 stairs): under the sails, into the Concert Hall,
  and the story of the world's greatest planning-permission
  saga.
- **11:00** — Walk the **Harbour Bridge** footpath (free)
  for the reverse view — or bank the splurge decision:
  **BridgeClimb** runs ~$300–400 by slot (the **Pylon
  Lookout, ~$30**, delivers 80% of the photo for 10% of
  the price).
- **13:00** — **The Manly ferry** (tap Opal card, ~30 min):
  the world's best commute — harbour heads, sailboats,
  skyline receding. Fish and chips on the Corso, beach
  hour, the Shelly Beach snorkel loop.
- **17:30** — Ferry back at golden hour (sit left) — the
  Opera House lights up on approach.
- **20:00** — Dinner around Circular Quay or Barangaroo's
  waterfront strip (~$35–60 pp).

## Day 3 — Bondi to Coogee, the clifftop classic

- **09:00** — Bus/Uber to **Bondi Beach**: watch the
  sunrise-surf crowd, breakfast at a beach café (~$20–28 —
  avocado toast in its natural habitat).
- **10:30** — The **Bondi–Coogee coastal walk** (6 km,
  free, 2–3 unhurried hours): sculpted sandstone cliffs,
  Tamarama and Bronte's surf bowls, Waverley Cemetery's
  absurdly scenic headstones, ocean pools at every turn.
  Swim at flagged Bronte or Clovelly midway.
- **14:00** — Coogee lunch, bus back via **Bondi
  Icebergs** for *that* pool photo (entry ~$10 if you
  swim it).
- **17:00** — **Darling Harbour** evening: families hit
  the SEA LIFE aquarium option, everyone else hits the
  waterfront bars; Saturday nights often end in
  fireworks.
- **20:00** — Chinatown/Spice Alley dinner (~$20–35 pp —
  Sydney's cheap-eats pressure valve).

## Day 4 — The Blue Mountains

- **07:15** — Train from Central to **Katoomba** (~2 h,
  inside the Opal daily cap — the great Sydney day-trip
  bargain). Or a small-group tour (~$110–170) if you'd
  rather be driven.
- **09:45** — **Echo Point**: the **Three Sisters** over
  the Jamison Valley's blue-eucalyptus haze (the blue is
  real — oil mist). Walk the Giant Stairway's first
  section or the clifftop track toward Katoomba Falls.
- **12:00** — **Scenic World** (~$60ish unlimited rides):
  the world's steepest railway (52°!) down into the
  rainforest, boardwalks under tree ferns, cableway back
  up. Touristy and terrific.
- **14:30** — Leura village for pie-and-bookshop charm,
  or Wentworth Falls lookout if legs remain.
- **18:30** — Back in Sydney; pack for the tropics.
  Farewell harbour dinner (~$35–60 pp).

## Day 5 — Fly north into the tropics

- **08:30** — SYD → **Cairns** (~3 h; Qantas/Virgin/Jetstar
  **~$160–260** booked ahead, bags counted on the budget
  carriers).
- **12:30** — Land in the wet-tropics air, check in on the
  Esplanade.
- **14:00** — **Cairns Esplanade Lagoon** (free, lifeguarded,
  stinger-proof — the town's answer to no-swim beaches),
  boardwalk stroll, fig-tree flying foxes at dusk.
- **18:00** — **Night Markets** browse + mud-crab or barra
  dinner (~$30–45 pp). Book tomorrow's reef boat seats
  confirmed; early night — the reef starts early.

**Where to stay in Cairns** (two nights)

- *Resort:* **Crystalbrook Riley** — oceanfront pool scene,
  TripAdvisor's #1 value here. ~$280–380.
- *Mid:* **Pullman Reef Hotel Casino** — 4.6/5 (~2,700
  reviews), marina-front polish. ~$220–300.
- *Value:* **Il Palazzo Boutique** — 4.5/5, quiet, by the
  night markets. ~$140–190.

## Day 6 — The Great Barrier Reef

- **07:30** — Check in at the Reef Fleet Terminal for a
  **full-day OUTER-reef cruise** — the honest sweet spot
  runs **$260–325 pp** (pontoon or roaming-boat operators;
  snorkel gear, lycra suit, buffet lunch and reef levy
  included). Outer reef = clearer water, healthier coral —
  worth it over cheap inner-reef runs.
- **10:30** — First drop: parrotfish crunching coral,
  clouds of fusiliers, a turtle if the day is kind.
  **Intro dives** (~$100–160 add-on, no licence needed)
  are the upgrade people remember at 80; glass-bottom and
  semi-sub loops carry the non-swimmers.
- **The climate honesty:** bleaching events are real and
  recurring; what survives is still the largest living
  structure on Earth and utterly worth seeing — go with
  eco-certified operators, wear reef-safe sunscreen
  (provided suits do most of the work), touch nothing.
- **16:30** — Back ashore, salt-crusted and quiet.
  Recovery gelato; easy dinner; horizontal by nine.

## Day 7 — Rainforest morning, Melbourne midnight

- **08:30** — **Kuranda day, compressed:** ride the
  **Skyrail cableway** over the World-Heritage rainforest
  canopy up, and the 1891 **Kuranda Scenic Railway**
  through 15 tunnels and past Barron Falls down (combo
  **~$130–150**): butterflies, didgeridoo galleries,
  market stalls in the village between.
- **15:30** — Back to Cairns, airport.
- **18:00** — CNS → **Melbourne** (~3.5 h, **~$180–280**);
  land in a city that considers 11pm dinner-time
  negotiable. SkyBus or train in; check in; sleep.

**Where to stay in Melbourne** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Langham Melbourne** — 4.6/5 (~8,800
  reviews), Southbank river views. ~$380+.
- *Heritage mid:* **Treasury on Collins** — 4.8/5 (~2,200
  reviews), 1876 bank building, suites with kitchenettes,
  dead-centre. ~$220–300.
- *Apartment value:* **Quest St Kilda Road** — 4.7/5,
  self-catering calm on the tram line. ~$160–220.

## Day 8 — The Great Ocean Road

- **07:00** — Small-group day tour (**~$120–180**, 12 h —
  the correct call over self-driving your first
  left-hand-side day): the surf coast through Torquay and
  Bells Beach, koala-spotting at Kennett River, rainforest
  at Maits Rest, Apollo Bay lunch.
- **15:30** — **The Twelve Apostles**: limestone stacks in
  Southern-Ocean spray (eight remain; the ocean is
  renegotiating), Loch Ard Gorge's shipwreck cove, Gibson
  Steps down to sea level. Reverse-itinerary tours hit the
  Apostles near sunset — worth choosing.
- **21:00** — Back in Melbourne; late bowl of ramen or
  pasta in a laneway that never closed (~$20–30).

## Day 9 — Melbourne: laneways, markets, penguins

- **08:30** — Coffee is the religion; attend a service
  (flat white, ~$5.50) — then **Hosier Lane's** street-art
  canyon, **Centre Place** and **Degraves Street's**
  espresso alleys. The **free City Circle tram** (and the
  whole **CBD Free Tram Zone**) is your day pass.
- **11:00** — **Queen Victoria Market** (closed Mon/Wed):
  jam doughnuts from the van, cheese hall, souvenir sweep.
- **13:30** — **Fed Square + NGV** (free galleries), a
  Yarra riverside walk, and the **Shrine of Remembrance**
  view down St Kilda Road.
- **17:30** — Tram 96 to **St Kilda**: pier sunset, cake
  on Acland Street — and at dusk, the **free little-
  penguin colony on the breakwater** (volunteers point
  them out; no flash) — Phillip Island's famous parade
  (~$150+ by tour) is the bigger show, but this one costs
  a tram fare.
- **20:30** — Farewell dinner: Lygon Street Italian,
  Chinatown dumplings, or a rooftop over the grid
  (~$35–60 pp).

## Day 10 — Home from Melbourne

- **Morning** — SkyBus to MEL, and fly home
  (~14 h). You've done the harbour, the reef and the
  laneways — the other 95% of the continent (see FAQ) is
  the reason people come back.

## What to pack

- SPF50+, real hat, sunglasses — the UV here is a
  contact sport; reef-safe sunscreen for Cairns.
- Layers for Melbourne ("four seasons in a day" is the
  city's own slogan) and one warm layer for reef-boat
  wind.
- Swimwear ×2, quick-dry towel; the lycra stinger suit
  is provided on boats.
- Walking shoes for 6-km cliff paths and laneway miles.
- **No undeclared food in the bags** — biosecurity reads
  this list too. Declare the snacks; keep the fine.
- Type I plug adapter (the slanted-pin one — different
  from UK/UAE/US).

## FAQ

**How early should the visa really be filed?**
Two to three months before travel is the safe lane: many
grants land in 2–4 weeks, but the tail is long and the
department doesn't rush for itineraries. Strong funds
paper, employer letters and a coherent plan (borrow this
page) do the persuading. Nothing non-refundable until
the grant email.

**Is 10 days enough for Australia?**
For *this* triangle, yes — briskly. It's a sampler by
design: two internal flights, three bases, zero rest
days. With 12–13 days, stretch Cairns to 3 nights (add
the Daintree) and Sydney to 5. The continent's other
icons — see below — are separate trips, and that's not
a flaw; it's geography.

**Is the reef still worth it — really?**
Yes, with clear eyes: bleaching has hurt sections, and
operators now steer to the healthiest sites — which
remain hallucinatory. Book eco-certified boats, go
outer reef, snorkel don't stand, and understand you're
seeing something both magnificent and mortal. Nobody
comes back underwhelmed; many come back changed.

**BridgeClimb or not?**
It's ~$300–400 per person for 3 hours and a jumpsuit —
unforgettable if the budget laughs, skippable if it
doesn't: the **Pylon Lookout (~$30)** and the free
bridge footpath deliver the views without the harness.
Put it in the same mental box as the Teotihuacán
balloon.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
The big two cities are easy: Sydney's Lakemba/Auburn
and Melbourne's north are halal heartlands, the
**halal snack pack** is a genuine Melbourne
institution, and kebab-and-charcoal-chicken shops dot
every suburb; Cairns is thinner (a few Indian/Malay
halal spots — the night markets help). Vegetarians
coast everywhere; even pubs carry real options now.

**What about Uluru, Perth, Tasmania, New Zealand…?**
All separate trips by honest distance: **Uluru + the
Red Centre** (2N, Field of Light) pairs with this
route as a +3-day splurge and leads our Australia #2
shortlist, with **Sydney + New Zealand's South
Island** the other contender. Perth and Tasmania are
their own journeys entirely.
