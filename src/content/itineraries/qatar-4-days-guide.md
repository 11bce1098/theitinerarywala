Doha is the long weekend hiding in plain sight. One hour from
Dubai, **visa-free for Indian passports**, and transformed by
two decades of ambition into a city that rewards a proper
look: the **Museum of Islamic Art** on its own peninsula (I.M.
Pei's final masterpiece, built at 91), a **National Museum**
shaped like a 350-metre desert rose, a souq that still trades
falcons and oud — and, an hour south, the **Inland Sea**,
where Qatar's dunes pour directly into a turquoise gulf.

It's also the gentlest possible "abroad" for a first family
trip or a reset weekend: the metro is spotless and QAR 2 a
ride, karak costs one riyal, everything is halal by default,
and the whole city runs on Gulf rhythms — late evenings, and
an indoor middle of the day.

The shape: **three nights, one base** (souq-side or
Msheireb), museums and souq in the middle, the desert on day
two. Figure **{{QAR 1800}} per person** excluding flights — the smallest budget on this
site. **Go November–March**; Doha summer is a 45°C
indoor-city event.

## Before you go: the admin

### Visa-free, with one quirk

- **Visa-free, with one quirk worth knowing:** Indian
  passports get **30 days free on arrival** (extendable
  once to 60). The file: passport 6+ months, return
  ticket, funds card — and officially, **a hotel booked
  through the Discover Qatar platform
  (discoverqatar.qa)** rather than third-party sites.
  Two clean workarounds: the **Hayya A1 tourist e-visa
  (QAR 100, 24–48 h)**, which frees you to book hotels
  anywhere — or, for this site's readers, the **Hayya
  A2 for GCC residents: QAR 100, multiple entries, 60
  days**, the smoothest option and the one that turns
  Doha into a repeatable weekend. Apply at
  hayya.qa; approvals are quick.

### Flights and the metro

- **Flights:** Dubai/Abu Dhabi/Sharjah → Doha is a
  ~70-minute hop on Qatar Airways, Emirates, Etihad,
  flydubai and Air Arabia — commonly **US$140–330
  return**; fare-watch midweek dates.
- **Getting around:** the **Doha Metro** is the trick —
  gleaming, driverless, **QAR 2 standard / QAR 6 day
  pass**, and it links the airport, souq, museums,
  Katara and Lusail. Uber/Careem and Karwa taxis fill
  the gaps cheaply. No car needed.

### Money and manners

- **Money & manners:** riyal ≈ dirham (1:1 mental
  maths), cards everywhere. Modest dress around the
  souq and mosques (shoulders/knees — the UAE
  standard); alcohol only in hotel venues; Friday
  mornings run quiet until ~13:00 — plan museums
  after souq breakfasts that day.

## Day 1 — Corniche, the Pei masterpiece, the souq

- **Morning hop** — Land by late morning; metro from
  the airport, drop bags.
- **13:00** — Lunch at **Souq Waqif** to set the tone
  (Qatari machboos or a Yemeni mandi house,
  QAR 35–60), then walk it off along the
  **Corniche** — seven kilometres of bay with the
  West Bay skyline stacked across the water and
  wooden dhows moored in the foreground.
- **15:30** — **Museum of Islamic Art (QAR 50,
  under-16s free)**: I.M. Pei circled the world for
  inspiration, then demanded his own island so
  nothing could ever crowd the building. Inside:
  1,400 years of calligraphy, astrolabes, Mughal
  jades and carpets, lit like jewellery. The
  five-storey atrium window IS the skyline photo;
  the park behind it is sunset-picnic central.
- **18:30** — Back to **Souq Waqif** as it wakes:
  the **Falcon Souq** (birds on perches, QR-coded
  pedigrees, a dedicated falcon hospital —
  wander in, it's welcomed), the spice and oud
  alleys, street sketchers.
- **20:00** — Dinner in the souq's lantern-lit
  lanes (Parisa's mirrored Persian dining room for
  the occasion, QAR 90–130 pp; the shawarma
  benches for QAR 15) — then **karak (QAR 1–2)**
  standing at the counter like a local. First of
  many.

**Where to stay in Doha** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Mandarin Oriental, Msheireb** — 4.8/5
  on TripAdvisor (~460 reviews), the polished heart
  of the regenerated downtown, metro at the door.
  QAR 1,300+. (The Ned, 4.9/1,100, is the
  members-club-glam alternative.)
- *Mid-range/heritage:* **Al Najada Doha by
  Tivoli** — 4.7/5 (~1,100 reviews), a restored
  courtyard quarter two minutes from the souq —
  the location that makes this itinerary walkable.
  ~QAR 450–650.
- *Value:* **ibis Doha** — 4.9/5 (~750 reviews;
  yes, an ibis with those numbers), clean and
  metro-connected at ~QAR 250–350.

## Day 2 — The desert-rose museum, then the Inland Sea

- **09:00** — **National Museum of Qatar (QAR 50,
  under-16s free)**: Jean Nouvel's building — 539
  interlocking discs like a giant desert rose —
  wraps a walk-through film of Qatar's story, from
  pearl-diving hunger to gas-boom vertigo. The
  pearl-carpet room and the oil-era gallery are
  the keepers. 2–2.5 hours.
- **12:00** — Ten minutes to **Msheireb Downtown**:
  the world's densest smart-city rebuild, home to
  four small **free heritage museums** (Bin
  Jelmood House on the region's slavery history
  is quietly essential), a free tram loop, and
  good coffee.
- **14:30** — Pickup for the **desert safari to
  Khor Al Adaid — the Inland Sea** (half-day
  shared 4x4 ~**QAR 300–350 pp**; private jeeps
  ~QAR 1,200): dune-bashing down roller-coaster
  ridges, a camel-and-sandboard stop, and the
  payoff UNESCO shortlisted — **dunes meeting
  open water**, with Saudi Arabia shimmering
  across the channel. Sunset here is the trip's
  photograph.
- **20:00** — Back to town; light dinner at
  Msheireb or room-service recovery — sand in
  unexpected places is part of the package.

## Day 3 — Katara, the Pearl, and a dhow at dusk

- **09:30** — Metro north to **Katara Cultural
  Village** (free): the golden-tiled mosque, the
  pigeon towers, a Roman-style amphitheatre
  facing the sea, and galleries between —
  planned as the city's culture campus and
  best in the morning cool. Beach add-on for
  kids (small fee).
- **12:30** — Lunch at Katara's institutions
  (Chapati & Karak, QAR 15 and iconic, or the
  Lebanese terraces at QAR 60–90).
- **14:00** — **The Pearl-Qatar**: yacht-marina
  loops, Porto Arabia's gelato-and-columns
  promenade — Doha doing Monaco. *(Swap for
  sports families: the **3-2-1 Olympic & Sports
  Museum, QAR 50** — genuinely one of the
  world's best sports museums — or Lusail's
  boulevard and marina.)*
- **17:30** — Back to the Corniche for a
  **traditional dhow cruise** (~QAR 50–80 pp
  for the sunset hour): the skyline lighting up
  from the water, tea on deck — the QAR-50
  version of a superyacht evening.
- **20:00** — Farewell dinner: modern Qatari at
  Jiwan (in the NMoQ, QAR 150+ pp) for the
  occasion, or the souq's grills one last time.

## Day 4 — Slow morning, one-hour flight home

- **09:30** — Pick your finale: **Mathaf** (the
  Arab Museum of Modern Art — free, superb,
  ignored), the souq's morning quiet for
  spice-and-oud shopping, or nothing at all
  beyond a final karak.
- **13:00** — Metro to Hamad (allow the airport
  its scale — it's a destination with gates),
  and the 70-minute hop home. Weekend complete;
  passport barely warm.

## What to pack

- Light layers + one warmer layer November–
  February (desert evenings drop to 14–18°C —
  the safari sunset gets genuinely cool).
- Modest daywear for souq and mosques
  (UAE-standard covers it); a scarf in the
  daypack.
- Comfortable walking shoes — Corniche +
  museums = 15,000-step days — and sandals
  that survive dune sand.
- Sunglasses, SPF, refillable bottle (fountains
  and cheap water everywhere).
- Swimwear if your hotel pools or Katara beach
  call.
- Plugs: **UK-style Type G — UAE chargers work
  as-is.** The rare guide with zero adapters.

## FAQ

**Is it really visa-free — what's this Discover
Qatar hotel rule?**
Yes — 30 days, zero riyals, on arrival. The
fine print says the visa-free route wants your
hotel booked via the official Discover Qatar
platform; enforcement is inconsistent but the
rule is real. The stress-free answers: book
one night through the platform, or spend
QAR 100 on a **Hayya A1/A2 e-visa** and book
however you like — A2 (GCC residents) is
multi-entry, which for UAE readers turns Qatar
into a season ticket.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
The easiest FAQ on the site: **everything is
halal**, and vegetarians are looked after by a
huge Indian community — Chapati & Karak is a
national institution, pure-veg thali houses
dot the old town, and every Levantine menu is
half mezze anyway. The struggle is only
choosing.

**Is Doha worth it versus 'it's just like
Dubai'?**
Different project entirely: Doha spent its
money on museums and public space rather than
records — MIA and NMoQ outclass anything
between Cairo and Singapore, the souq is a
functioning old market rather than a themed
one, and the whole city runs at half Dubai's
speed and two-thirds its prices. As a
weekend contrast, that's precisely the point.

**When exactly should we go?**
**November to March** — 22–28°C days, cool
desert evenings, and the calendar stacks up
(Doha's winter is festival season). April and
October are shoulder-warm; May–September is
indoor Qatar: doable (museums + malls +
metro are all arctic), but the Inland Sea
sunset becomes a sauna.

**Souq-side or West Bay/Lusail — where to
base?**
Souq/Msheireb, and it isn't close: you can
WALK to the souq, MIA and NMoQ, with the
metro for everything else. West Bay's towers
and Lusail's glitz are better visited than
slept in — unless a beach-pool resort day is
the actual holiday, in which case Katara-side
resorts flip the answer.

**What's Qatar guide #2?**
The **Qatar Airways stopover guide** — what
12/24/48 hours from Hamad can honestly
deliver (the airline's stopover hotel deals
start at $14) — or the **Dubai→Doha drive**
through the Saudi corridor for the road-trip
crowd. Votes welcome; both on the list.
