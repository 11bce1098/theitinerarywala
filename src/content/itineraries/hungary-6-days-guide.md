Budapest is two cities glaring at each other across a river —
hilly, castled **Buda** and flat, café-crammed **Pest** — and
the glare is the best urban view in Europe: Parliament's
Gothic wedding cake on one bank, the castle and bastion on the
other, seven bridges stitching them together. Underneath it
all, 120 hot springs bubble up, which is why this is the only
capital where "let's go to the baths" is a Tuesday plan.

The sums are friendly: low-cost carriers reach Budapest from across
Europe and the Gulf (**Wizz Air flies Abu Dhabi–Budapest direct**),
Hungary keeps its own **forint** (so prices undercut the euro zone),
and one **Hungarian Schengen visa** — same drill as our
[France](/itineraries/france-9-days-guide/) and
[Czech](/itineraries/czech-republic-7-days-guide/) guides — opens
the whole zone.

The shape: **five nights in Pest's centre**, with the
castle, the baths, the ruin bars, a night cruise — and a
day up the **Danube Bend** to Szentendre's painted lanes.
Figure **{{HUF 310000}} per person**
including the visa, excluding flights. **Go April–June or
September–October**; December is market-lit and cold,
July–August hot and busiest.

## Before you go: the admin

- - **Schengen, Hungary flavour:** apply via **your nearest VFS
  Global centre** with Hungary as main destination — **€90** +
  service fee, biometrics, proof of residence valid past your
  return, bank statements, employment letter, insurance (€30,000),
  full itinerary. Apply **4–6 weeks out**; summer slots go fast.
  Already holding a Schengen visa from our other guides' trips? It
  works here — one zone, one sticker.
- **Flights:** **Wizz Air AUH–BUD direct** (~AED
  600–1,400 return if you pack light and book early —
  the site's cheapest Europe airfare trick) or
  **flydubai DXB–BUD**; Emirates connections via
  European hubs otherwise.
- **Money:** Hungary uses the **forint (HUF)** — not
  euros, whatever the tourist-strip menus imply. Cards
  tap everywhere; when the terminal offers to charge in
  AED, **choose HUF** (dynamic conversion skims 5–10%).
  ATMs: bank-branded only, decline conversion; avoid the
  blue Euronet boxes.
- **Taxis:** never hail from the street. **Bolt** (or
  official yellow Főtaxi) is cheap and honest; the
  airport's **100E bus** runs to the centre for
  ~HUF 2,200.
- **Getting around:** the metro alone is worth a ride —
  Line M1 is continental Europe's oldest underground,
  all wood and brass. **BudapestGO app**: 24h/72h
  travelcards (~HUF 2,500/5,500) cover metro, trams
  (tram 2 along the river is a sightseeing tour in
  disguise) and buses.

## Day 1 — Land, walk the river at night

- **Afternoon** — Land, 100E bus in, check in around
  Deák Ferenc tér.
- **17:00** — Orientation the classic way: walk the
  **Danube Promenade** past the poignant **Shoes on
  the Danube** memorial (bronze shoes for those shot
  into the river in 1944–45 — pause properly) to
  **Chain Bridge**, the lion-guarded 1849 original.
- **19:30** — First Hungarian dinner: goulash (it's a
  soup here, not a stew — first honesty of the guide),
  chicken paprikash with nokedli, ~HUF 4,500–7,000 pp
  off the tourist strips (rule: **nothing on Váci
  utca with a picture menu**).
- **21:00** — The river at night from Chain Bridge:
  Parliament lit like a chandelier. Tomorrow you're
  inside it.

**Where to stay in Budapest** (five nights, Pest side)

- *Luxury:* **Anantara New York Palace** — 4.6/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~5,700 reviews), the palazzo whose
  ground floor is the **New York Café**, self-styled
  "most beautiful café in the world". ~HUF 160,000+.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Moments, Andrássy Avenue** —
  4.9/5 (~5,400 reviews), the boulevard address at
  boutique prices, breakfast famous. ~HUF 55,000–75,000.
- *Value:* **ROOMbach Hotel** — 4.7/5 (~2,700
  reviews), clean-lined rooms tucked by the Rumbach
  synagogue, ruin-bar district on foot. ~HUF
  32,000–42,000.

## Day 2 — Parliament, the basilica, a night cruise

- **09:00** — **Hungarian Parliament tour** — booked
  1–3 weeks ahead (it sells out): 45 minutes under
  the gilded dome with the Crown of St Stephen. The
  honest price note: **non-EU adults pay HUF 23,150
  (~€60)** — steep, and still the best interior in
  the city. Budget-savers: skip inside, shoot the
  facade from Batthyány tér across the river and
  spend the difference on tonight's cruise.
- **11:30** — **Kossuth tér** and the changing of
  the guard, then tram 2 south along the river (the
  free sightseeing ride).
- **13:00** — Lunch at **Central Market Hall**
  (Nagyvásárcsarnok): lángos — fried dough, sour
  cream, cheese — upstairs (~HUF 2,000), paprika
  garlands downstairs for the suitcase.
- **15:00** — **St Stephen's Basilica**: hall
  HUF 2,600, or the **all-access HUF 6,800** with
  the 360° dome terrace (lift or 300 steps) and the
  treasury — plus the mummified right hand of King
  Stephen himself, Hungary's oddest relic.
- **19:30** — **Danube night cruise** (~HUF
  7,000–10,000 with a drink): Parliament, castle
  and bridges floodlit from the water — the reason
  every Budapest photo you've ever seen exists.
  Book the 21:00 sailing in summer for full dark.

## Day 3 — Buda: castle hill done right

- **08:30** — Cross Chain Bridge and beat the
  crowds uphill (funicular ~HUF 4,000 return — or
  the pretty 10-minute walk up the castle gardens,
  free).
- **09:00 sharp** — **Fisherman's Bastion**: the
  fairy-tale white turrets are **free before 9 am**
  (after that, upper terraces HUF 1,700 — arriving
  early is the site's favourite kind of discount).
  Parliament across the water in morning light is
  the picture of the trip.
- **10:00** — **Matthias Church** (~HUF 3,500,
  majolica roof and painted interior), then the
  castle courtyards, the President's palace guard,
  and the medieval lanes of Úri utca.
- **13:00** — Lunch in the Víziváros below
  (~HUF 3,500), or ruin-pub brunch back in Pest.
- **15:00** — **Gellért Hill**: the 30-minute climb
  past the Liberty Statue for the second great
  panorama (free), descending to the art-nouveau
  **Gellért Baths** facade.
- **19:00** — Dinner in the Jewish Quarter, then
  the institution: **Szimpla Kert**, the original
  ruin bar — a condemned tenement filled with
  fairy lights, bathtub seats and twenty years of
  creative clutter. Go 19:00–21:00 for the magic
  without the 23:00 crush; Tuesday–Thursday beats
  weekends.

## Day 4 — Széchenyi: the bath day

- **09:30** — **Széchenyi Baths**, City Park: 15
  pools indoors and the famous canary-yellow
  outdoor trio steaming at 27–38°C, chess players
  mid-soak. **Weekday locker ticket HUF 13,200**
  (weekends 14,800; online "fast-track" prices run
  higher in euros). The house economics: **bring
  your own towel and flip-flops** — rentals run
  HUF 6,900 and 4,200 — and go weekday morning for
  space. Two to three hours is the sweet spot;
  more and you're soup.
- **13:00** — Recover with lunch toward **Heroes'
  Square** and the Millennium monuments;
  Vajdahunyad Castle's architectural mixtape sits
  in the park behind.
- **15:30** — **Andrássy Avenue** amble: the Opera
  House (guided tours ~HUF 9,000 if the interior
  calls), the M1 metro ride beneath it, ending
  with cake — either the New York Café spectacle
  (queue, ~HUF 4,500 with coffee, worth it once)
  or a classic cukrászda without one.
- **19:30** — Modern-Hungarian dinner night
  (~HUF 8,000–12,000 pp at the bistronomy end) —
  Budapest's kitchen scene quietly outruns its
  prices.

## Day 5 — The Danube Bend: Szentendre

- **09:00** — **HÉV suburban train (H5)** from
  Batthyány tér to **Szentendre** (40 min,
  ~HUF 700–1,000 each way on top of the
  travelcard): a Serbian-Orthodox-flavoured
  artists' village of ochre lanes, gallery
  courtyards and riverside promenade.
- **10:00** — Wander: the Main Square's crooked
  charm, **Margit Kovács ceramics museum**
  (~HUF 2,400 — quietly wonderful), church-hill
  viewpoint, and the compulsory **marzipan
  museum** for anyone under 12.
- **13:00** — Riverside lunch (~HUF 4,000), then
  either the summer boat back down the Danube
  (seasonal, ~HUF 4,000 — the scenic return) or
  the HÉV again.
  *(Castle-hungry swap: Visegrád's citadel above
  the Bend's hairpin, by bus — bigger views,
  fewer galleries.)*
- **17:30** — Back in town: last-light walk on
  **Margaret Island** — the traffic-free park
  island with the musical fountain.
- **20:00** — Farewell dinner + a final ruin-bar
  round or a concert at the basilica.

## Day 6 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Kürtőskalács (the chimney cake —
  Hungarian-Transylvanian and proudly so) at the
  market, paprika and Tokaji into the suitcase,
  100E bus out. Wizz's afternoon rotation suits
  the slow morning; check your times.

## FAQ

**Is Budapest safe?**
Yes — comfortably so for families and solo
travellers. The full watch-list: pickpockets on
crowded trams and in the market, unofficial
taxis (solved by Bolt), "0%-commission" exchange
booths and Euronet ATMs (use bank ATMs, pay in
HUF), and the ancient tourist-bar bill-padding
trick on Váci utca (eat one street over). Ruin
bars are rowdy-friendly, not rough.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Halal: a real and growing scene — Turkish,
Middle Eastern and Pakistani kitchens cluster
around the centre and the Jewish Quarter's edges
(look for displayed certification; classic
Hungarian places cook with pork fat by default,
so ask). Vegetarian: easier than the
paprika-and-pork stereotype — lángos, túrós
csusza without bacon, main-course cheese
dumplings, plus a strong modern-veg scene
(Budapest does Europe's best-value tasting
menus, veg included). Say "disznó nélkül" —
without pork — and smile.

**Which bath — Széchenyi, Gellért or Rudas?**
Széchenyi for the icon (big, yellow, social,
chess); **Gellért** for art-nouveau tiles and
calm; **Rudas** for the 16th-century Ottoman
dome plus a rooftop pool IN the Danube view —
best at night. Doing two is normal; doing three
is a lifestyle. Etiquette everywhere: shower
first, flip-flops always, keep voices low
indoors.

**Is the Parliament tour worth €60 for non-EU
visitors?**
If state rooms thrill you — the dome hall and
crown genuinely do — yes, once. If you're
budget-tuned, the honest answer: the exterior
(from the river, at night, from Batthyány tér)
delivers 80% of the awe for free, and the
basilica dome + cruise combo costs less than
one Parliament ticket.

**Budapest with kids?**
Excellent: bath pools (14+ at Széchenyi — take
younger kids to Palatinus lido on Margaret
Island instead), the marzipan museum, castle
funicular, chairlift up the Buda Hills and the
**Children's Railway** (staffed by actual
children, gloriously), plus chimney cakes as a
food group.

**What's Hungary guide #2?**
**Lake Balaton in summer** — Tihany's lavender
abbey, Balatonfüred's promenade, wine hills
above the "Hungarian sea" — or the **Eger wine
country** long weekend. Votes welcome; both on
the list.
