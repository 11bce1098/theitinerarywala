Namibia is the trip where the driving *is* the holiday. Two and a
half million people in a country twice the size of Germany, roads
that run straight to the horizon, and almost no traffic on any of
them. You will spend hours seeing nothing, and then something
extraordinary.

Three landscapes carry the route. **Sossusvlei**, where 300-metre
red dunes stand over a white clay pan and the dead camelthorn trees
of Deadvlei have not rotted in eight centuries. **Swakopmund**, a
German colonial seaside town with cold Atlantic fog rolling over the
dunes behind it. And **Etosha**, where you do not chase animals —
you park at a waterhole and let them come.

Be clear about one thing before booking: **this is a self-drive
country**, and the roads are mostly gravel. That is not a problem,
but it is a skill and a set of rules.

## Before you go: the roads, the fuel and the car

### Entry, and when to go

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for up to 90 days — the EU,
  UK, US, Canada, Australia, South Africa, India and many others.
  Namibia has broadened this in recent years; check your own case
  and the current fee position, which has changed.
- **Passport validity of six months** and blank pages for stamps.
- **May to October is the dry season** and the right answer: cooler
  days, no rain, and animals concentrated at waterholes because
  there is nowhere else to drink. **June to August** are coldest —
  desert nights near freezing.
- **November to April** is hot, occasionally wet, and the game
  scatters. Green, but harder.

### The gravel-road rules

This is the part that matters most, and the part most guides skip.

- **Most of this route is gravel.** It is well graded and perfectly
  drivable, at the right speed.
- **Slow down.** The single biggest cause of trouble is driving
  gravel at tarmac speed. Sixty to eighty km/h is sensible; the
  accidents happen above that.
- **Two spare tyres**, not one. Punctures are routine, help is far
  away, and every experienced operator here insists on it.
- **Never drive at night.** Wildlife on unfenced roads, no lighting,
  and no one coming past for hours.
- **Fuel where you can, not where you must.** Stations are far
  apart and some take cash only. Fill up at every town whether you
  need it or not.
- **Tell someone your route.** Mobile coverage disappears for long
  stretches.
- **A 2WD high-clearance car covers this itinerary**; a 4x4 is
  required only for sand driving and some far-north routes. Rent
  from an operator who explains the tyre rules without being asked.

### Distances, honestly

- **Windhoek → Sossusvlei**: about 5 hours.
- **Sossusvlei → Swakopmund**: about 5 hours.
- **Swakopmund → Etosha**: about 6 hours.
- **Etosha → Windhoek**: about 5 hours.

These are driving hours at safe gravel speeds, before stops. They
are why this is a ten-day trip and not a seven-day one.

### Money and the practicalities

- **The Namibian dollar (NAD)** is pegged one-to-one with the South
  African rand, and **rand is accepted everywhere**. Namibian dollars
  are not accepted in South Africa, so spend them before you leave.
- Cards work in towns and lodges. **Carry cash** for fuel stations,
  park entry and small settlements.
- **No malaria risk at Sossusvlei or Swakopmund.** Etosha and the
  north carry risk in the wet season — take advice before you go.
- Tap water is safe in towns and at lodges on this route.

## Day 1 — Land in Windhoek

- **Afternoon** — Land, collect the car, check the spares yourself
  before leaving the lot.
- **Evening** — Sleep in Windhoek. Shop for water and snacks
  tonight; tomorrow's drive has little along it.

## Day 2 — South to the dunes

- **Morning** — The long drive to **Sesriem**, about five hours,
  through the Khomas Hochland passes.
- **Afternoon** — Arrive, settle. **Sesriem Canyon** is a short
  walk and a good leg-stretch.
- **Evening** — Stars. There is almost no light pollution here and
  the sky is genuinely astonishing.

## Day 3 — Sossusvlei before sunrise

- **Pre-dawn** — Be at the gate when it opens. Staying inside the
  park gets you in an hour before outside guests, which is the
  difference between a photograph and a crowd.
- **Sunrise** — **Dune 45**, climbed in the cool.
- **Morning** — Drive to the 2x4 car park, then the shuttle or a
  deep-sand walk to **Deadvlei** — the white pan, black trees, red
  dunes and blue sky that is Namibia's signature image.
- **Midday** — Out before the heat. Afternoon is for lying still.

## Day 4 — The desert road to the coast

- **Morning** — North-west toward **Walvis Bay** and
  **Swakopmund**, about five hours through the Namib.
- **Afternoon** — Arrive on the coast, where it will be
  unexpectedly cold and foggy. That is the Benguela current, and it
  is why this desert exists.
- **Evening** — Swakopmund's German colonial streets, and a very
  good bakery or two.

## Day 5 — Sand, sea and fog

- **Morning** — **Walvis Bay lagoon**: flamingos, pelicans, and a
  boat trip where seals climb aboard.
- **Afternoon** — **Sandwich Harbour** by 4x4 where the dunes drop
  straight into the Atlantic, or quad-biking and sandboarding for
  something less scenic and more fun.
- **Evening** — Oysters, which are better here than they have any
  right to be.

## Day 6 — North toward Etosha

- **Morning** — Long drive north, roughly six hours, with the
  optional stop at **Spitzkoppe** or the **Cape Cross** seal colony.
- **Evening** — Sleep near **Okaukuejo** or the southern gate.

## Day 7 — Etosha: the waterhole day

- **Early** — Into the park at gate opening. Drive slowly between
  waterholes and stop at each; this is not a chasing safari.
- **Midday** — Rest at camp. Animals do.
- **Evening** — The **floodlit waterhole** at Okaukuejo after dark,
  which is the best wildlife hour in Namibia and costs nothing.

## Day 8 — Etosha, east

- **All day** — Work east across the pan toward **Halali** or
  **Namutoni**, waterhole to waterhole. Elephant, giraffe, zebra and
  springbok are near-certain; rhino and lion are luck and patience.

## Day 9 — South to Windhoek

- **Morning** — Leave early. The drive back is about five hours.
- **Afternoon** — Windhoek. Wash the car of half the country's dust,
  spend your remaining Namibian dollars.

## Day 10 — Fly home

- Morning flight out. Allow time for the rental inspection; gravel
  chips are normal and usually covered, but check what you signed.

## What to pack

- **Warm layers for the desert nights.** June to August drops near
  freezing after dark, which surprises people who packed for Africa.
- **Serious sun protection** for the days, which are the opposite.
- **A head torch** for camps and the night waterhole.
- **Binoculars**, essential at Etosha's waterholes.
- **Type D and M plugs** — Namibia uses the large South African
  round-pin. A standard universal adapter often does not fit; buy
  the right one before you go.
- **A dust-proof bag** for cameras. Gravel dust gets into everything.

## FAQ

**Is self-driving really safe?**
Yes, with the rules above: slow on gravel, two spares, never at
night, fuel early. Thousands of visitors do it every year. Speed on
gravel is what causes the incidents.

**Do I need a 4x4?**
Not for this route. A high-clearance 2WD covers all of it. A 4x4 is
for Sandwich Harbour, the far north-west and sand driving — and
Sandwich Harbour is better done with a guide anyway.

**Is ten days enough?**
For these three landscapes, yes, and cutting it to seven means
spending most of it driving. Adding days is best spent on Damaraland
or the Caprivi, not on stretching this loop.

**When is the wildlife best?**
**August to October**, at the end of the dry season, when the
waterholes are the only water left and the animals have no choice
but to come to you.
