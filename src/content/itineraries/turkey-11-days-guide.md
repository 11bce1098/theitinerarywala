Turkey packs four different trips into one country: a city that was
capital to two empires, a volcanic dreamscape you float over at
sunrise, the best-preserved Roman city in the Mediterranean, and a
mountain of white travertine pools. This route strings all four
into a clean flight triangle — Istanbul → Cappadocia → the Aegean →
back — with no leg repeated.

A note on money before anything: Turkish inflation makes lira
prices stale within months, and the major sites now charge foreign
visitors **in euros** anyway. So this guide budgets in EUR, quotes
lira only where tickets are still set in it, and begs you to
re-check numbers close to travel — they move here faster than
anywhere else we cover.

**Go April–June or September–October.** July–August is 35°C+ at
Ephesus and Pamukkale (both shadeless); winter is moody-beautiful
in Istanbul and Cappadocia but balloon cancellations rise.

## Before you go: the 15-minute admin

### Visas — check your exact case

- **Visa-free (90 days in most cases):** most European passports,
  the UK, Japan, Korea, GCC states including the UAE, and many
  others. Verify your passport on the official
  [evisa.gov.tr](https://www.evisa.gov.tr) checker — the list has
  shifted several times recently.
- **Indian passports — read carefully:** the e-visa (~USD 50–55,
  30-day single entry, issued in 1–2 days) is **conditional**: you
  must hold a valid visa or residence permit from the **Schengen
  Area, USA, UK or Ireland**. A UAE residence visa does **not**
  qualify — unlike Georgia or Armenia. Without a qualifying visa,
  the route is the **sticker visa** through a Turkish application
  centre or agency: costlier (~USD 200 all-in) and 4–10 working
  days, so start early.
- Passport validity: 6 months beyond arrival, as ever.

### Money

- Cards are accepted almost everywhere, including street-food
  windows; carry modest lira cash for markets, taxis and tips.
- Withdraw lira from bank ATMs (skip the airport exchange);
  several big sites price tickets in euros and take cards
  directly.
- **The honest scam list, Istanbul edition:** use **BiTaksi or
  Uber** rather than flagging taxis (meter games are the city's
  main tourist tax); ignore the "dropped shoe brush" trick; and
  never order in a Taksim-area club without seeing prices. That's
  90% of Turkey's tourist trouble avoided in one bullet.

### SIM and data

- Airport tourist SIMs (Turkcell, Vodafone) are expensive
  (~€25–35); an **international eSIM** is usually cheaper for
  under two weeks. Turkcell has the best coverage on the
  Cappadocia and Aegean legs if you go physical.

### Getting around

- **Istanbul:** an **Istanbulkart** (from machines, ~small fee +
  credit) covers trams, ferries, metro and funiculars — the T1
  tram and the Bosphorus ferries do most of this guide's work.
- **Between regions:** three short domestic flights (Turkish
  Airlines, Pegasus, AJet, SunExpress; €30–80 a leg booked
  ahead) — Istanbul→Cappadocia, Cappadocia→Izmir,
  Denizli→Istanbul. Book all three when you book international
  flights.
- **Aegean ground legs:** Izmir airport → Selçuk is ~1 h
  (train or shuttle); Selçuk → Pamukkale ~3 h by bus or transfer.

### Travelling with kids?

- Turks adore children and the logistics are easy; the two
  pinch-points are Ephesus's shadeless marble (go at opening, hats
  and water) and balloon age minimums — most operators take
  children from age 6.
- Cave hotels charm kids; check stair-heavy properties with a
  stroller.
- The travertines' barefoot walk is a highlight for small feet —
  shallow, warm, supervised.

## Day 1 — Land in Istanbul, first night in the old city

Stay in **Sultanahmet** (steps from the big four sights) or
**Galata/Karaköy** (better food and evenings, one tram ride away).

- **15:00** — Check in; get the Istanbulkart.
- **17:00** — Walk Sultanahmet Square at dusk: **Blue Mosque** and
  **Hagia Sophia** facing each other across the fountains, gulls
  circling the minarets at the evening call to prayer — the
  arrival scene.
- **19:30** — Dinner nearby, then early sleep — tomorrow is the
  heavyweight day.

**Where to stay in Istanbul** (four nights)

- *Splurge:* **Pera Palace Hotel** — the 1892 Orient Express
  grande dame above the Golden Horn; Agatha Christie's room is
  upstairs.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Sultania** — superbly rated Ottoman-style
  boutique in the old city, rooftop views.
- *Budget:* **The And Hotel** — Sultanahmet's best-value pick,
  minutes from everything.

## Day 2 — Imperial Sultanahmet

Note the closure days when you plan: **Topkapi closes Tuesdays**,
the **Grand Bazaar closes Sundays**.

- **9:00** — **Topkapi Palace + Harem** (~9:00–18:00, closed Tue;
  ~2,750 TL) — four courtyards of Ottoman power: the treasury, the
  kitchens, the Bosphorus terraces. Do the Harem; it's the best
  part. 3 hours.
- **12:30** — Lunch off the tourist strip — the lokantas
  (steam-tray canteens) around Sirkeci beat any square-side menu.
- **14:00** — **Hagia Sophia** (visitor gallery ~9:00–19:30;
  **€25** for foreign visitors) — 1,500 years of empire under one
  dome: mosaics upstairs, prayer hall below. The euro ticket stings
  and it is still non-negotiable.
- **15:30** — **Blue Mosque** (free; closed to visitors during the
  five daily prayers) — modest dress, scarves provided.
- **16:30** — **Basilica Cistern** (~9:00 till evening; ~1,950 TL
  daytime) — the drowned column forest under the city; the two
  Medusa heads hide at the back.
- **18:00** — **Grand Bazaar** (closed Sun; until ~19:00) — 4,000
  shops of glorious commerce. Haggle from half, walk away once,
  enjoy the theatre.
- **20:00** — Dinner in Sultanahmet or tram to Karaköy for the
  livelier table.

## Day 3 — The Bosphorus and the new city

- **9:00** — Tram + walk across **Galata Bridge** (fishermen up
  top, breakfast below); Karaköy's café lanes.
- **10:30** — **Bosphorus ferry cruise** — take the public
  Şehir Hatları short circular (cheap, ~90 min) over the private
  "yacht tours": same strait, fraction of the price. Palaces,
  fortresses and two continents from the water.
- **13:00** — Fish sandwich (balık ekmek) at the Eminönü stands or
  lunch in Karaköy.
- **14:30** — **Dolmabahçe Palace** (closed Mon; ~2,000 TL) — the
  Ottomans' last, Versailles-scale home — or skip in favour of
  slow Karaköy and the Istanbul Modern.
- **17:00** — Up through Galata's lanes; the **Galata Tower** view
  is €30 — honest verdict: a rooftop café with the tower *in* the
  view beats the queue for less.
- **18:30** — **İstiklal Avenue** with the nostalgic tram, into
  the Çiçek Pasajı arcades.
- **20:00** — Meyhane night: mezes, raki and noise — the Asmalımescit
  or Karaköy taverns. Book weekends.

## Day 4 — Two continents, one hamam

- **9:30** — Ferry to **Kadıköy** on the Asian side (20 min,
  Istanbulkart) — the market streets, Moda's seafront cafés, and a
  Tuesday-to-Sunday food scene locals prefer to Europe's side.
  Lunch here — çiğ köfte, midye dolma, künefe.
- **14:30** — Ferry back; **Süleymaniye Mosque** (free) — Sinan's
  masterpiece, calmer and to many finer than the Blue Mosque, with
  the Golden Horn spread below its terrace.
- **16:00** — **Spice Bazaar** (daily) — saffron, lokum,
  pistachios; prices better in the streets just behind it.
- **17:30** — The **hamam**: Kılıç Ali Paşa (Sinan-built,
  beautifully restored) or Çemberlitaş (1584) — full scrub-and-foam
  service ~€60–90. Fly to Cappadocia scrubbed of the city.
- **20:30** — Last Istanbul dinner; pack for an early flight.

## Day 5 — Fly to Cappadocia, first valleys

- **8:00** — Fly Istanbul → **Kayseri (ASR)** or **Nevşehir (NAV)**
  (~1h20); transfer to **Göreme** (~1 h from ASR — book the
  shuttle with your hotel).
- **12:00** — Check into your cave hotel; lunch in Göreme.
- **14:00** — **Göreme Open Air Museum** (~€20; ~8:00–18:30) —
  Byzantine cave churches with frescoes still bright after a
  millennium; the Dark Church add-on is worth it.
- **16:30** — Walk into **Pigeon Valley** or **Love Valley**
  (free) — the fairy-chimney landscape on foot.
- **18:30** — Sunset at the Göreme viewpoint or **Red Valley**
  (park entry ~€25 where charged) — the rock glows exactly as
  advertised.
- **20:00** — Dinner: testi kebab — the meat stew cracked open
  from its sealed clay pot (~€10–15).
- **Early night** — the balloons fly at dawn, and so do you.

**Where to stay in Göreme** (three nights, cave hotels — book the
famous ones months ahead)

- *Splurge:* **Sultan Cave Suites** — the rooftop the whole
  internet photographs at balloon hour.
- *Mid-range:* **Chelebi Cave House** — superbly rated, cosy,
  stunning terrace.
- *Budget:* **Aydinli Cave Hotel** — real cave rooms, panoramic
  views, remarkable value.

## Day 6 — Balloon dawn, then the north

**Balloon strategy, honestly:** standard shared sunrise flights run
**€120–250 per person** by season and demand. Weather cancels
roughly one morning in four — so book the flight for your **first**
morning, giving two rebooking chances. Licensed operators only;
if the price looks too good, so is the safety margin.

- **4:45** — Pickup in the dark; tea while the balloon inflates.
- **5:45–7:00** — **The flight** — a hundred balloons rising over
  the valleys at first light. It costs real money and remains one
  of travel's best hours.
- **8:00** — Champagne-and-certificate ritual, back for the
  rooftop breakfast you've seen in every photo.
- **10:30** — The northern circuit (self-drive, taxi-tour or "Red
  tour"): **Uçhisar Castle** (~€9) for the highest viewpoint,
  **Paşabağ**'s mushroom chimneys, **Devrent**'s imagination
  valley, Avanos pottery town on the river.
- **17:00** — Nap. You were up at 4:45.
- **19:30** — Dinner in Göreme or Uçhisar with a valley view.

## Day 7 — Underground cities and the green south

- **9:30** — The southern circuit ("Green tour", ~€60–75/person
  with lunch and guide — worth taking guided for the distances):
- **10:30** — **Derinkuyu underground city** (~€13) — eight
  storeys deep, ventilation shafts and rolling stone doors: whole
  civilisations hid here. Not for the claustrophobic; unmissable
  for everyone else.
- **13:00** — **Ihlara Valley** — a green canyon hike along a
  river between cave churches; lunch by the water.
- **15:30** — **Selime Monastery** — a cathedral carved into a
  mountain.
- **18:30** — Back in Göreme. If your balloon got weather-cancelled
  yesterday, tomorrow dawn is the final retry — this buffer is why
  Cappadocia gets three nights.
- **20:00** — Farewell Cappadocia dinner; pack for the Aegean.

## Day 8 — To the Aegean: Selçuk

- **Morning** — Fly Cappadocia → **Izmir** (SunExpress direct most
  days, otherwise via Istanbul; check when booking), then train or
  shuttle to **Selçuk** (~1 h) — the friendly farm town at
  Ephesus's gate.
- **15:00** — Check in; climb to the **Basilica of St John**
  (~€6) — the apostle's hilltop tomb with castle views — and the
  lone standing column of the **Temple of Artemis** (free), one of
  the Seven Wonders reduced to a stork's nest pedestal. Both are
  better the evening before Ephesus: context first.
- **19:00** — Dinner in Selçuk's plane-tree square — köfte,
  gözleme, Aegean greens in olive oil.

**Where to stay in Selçuk** (two nights)

- *Boutique:* **Ayasoluk Boutique Hotel** — superbly rated, pool,
  the town's best restaurant terrace.
- *Mid-range:* **Ephesus Centrum** — spotless and central.
- *Budget:* **Tuncay Pension** — courtyard breakfasts and warm
  hosts, the classic backpacker-family pick.

## Day 9 — Ephesus at opening, Şirince by afternoon

- **7:45** — Be at Ephesus's **upper gate at the 8:00 opening**
  (entry **€40**) and walk downhill through the marble city ahead
  of the heat and the cruise buses (they land ~10:00). Curetes
  Street → Hadrian's Temple → the **Library of Celsus** nearly
  alone → the 25,000-seat theatre.
- **9:30** — Add the **Terrace Houses** (€15, separate ticket) —
  the frescoed homes of Ephesus's rich under a protective roof;
  most skip it, which is exactly why you shouldn't.
- **11:30** — Exit lower gate; optional **House of the Virgin
  Mary** (~€12.50) in the hills, or the **Ephesus Museum** in town
  (€10) for the artefacts the site is missing.
- **13:30** — Drive up to **Şirince** (20 min) — a hillside Greek
  village of stone houses, fruit wines and long lunches. Eat here,
  taste there, amble.
- **18:00** — Back to Selçuk; sunset from the castle walls,
  quiet dinner.

## Day 10 — Pamukkale's white terraces

- **9:00** — Bus or transfer to **Pamukkale** (~3 h). Check into a
  village hotel below the terraces (small family-run places with
  pools; pick by current reviews), light lunch.
- **14:30** — **Pamukkale + Hierapolis** (€30; ~8:00–20:00) —
  enter from the village gate and climb the travertines
  **barefoot** (rule, not suggestion — carry your shoes). Know
  before you go: only rotating sections hold water, by design —
  dry terraces are conservation, not disappointment.
- **16:30** — **Hierapolis** on the plateau: the vast necropolis,
  the theatre — and, if you want the classic indulgence,
  **Cleopatra's Antique Pool** (extra ticket) to swim over
  submerged Roman columns.
- **18:30** — **Sunset on the terraces** — the white turns rose
  and gold, day-trippers have gone, and staying the night pays
  for itself in this hour.
- **20:00** — Village dinner — gözleme and Denizli kebab.

## Day 11 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Optional: tandem paragliding over the terraces
  (Pamukkale is one of the cheapest places anywhere to fly), or a
  last barefoot hour on the travertines before the buses arrive.
- **12:00** — Transfer to **Denizli airport (DNZ,** ~1 h**)**,
  fly to Istanbul (~1h10, several daily) and connect home —
  Gulf travellers land the same night.

## What to pack

- Modest-cover layers for mosques (scarves provided, nicer to
  bring); shoulders/knees covered generally in Sultanahmet.
- Real walking shoes — Ephesus is 3 km of marble, Cappadocia's
  valleys are dusty trails.
- A warm layer for balloon dawn (cold aloft even in summer) and
  Cappadocia nights.
- Sun kit + refillable bottle: Ephesus and Pamukkale are
  shadeless.
- A small bag for shoes on the travertines, and swimwear for
  Cleopatra's pool.
- Space for lokum, spices, ceramics and the carpet you swore you
  wouldn't buy.

## FAQ

**Is Turkey safe right now?**
The tourist circuit in this guide — Istanbul's centre, Cappadocia,
the Aegean — is heavily visited and feels it: safe, policed,
practiced. Standard big-city awareness in Istanbul (and the scam
list above) covers the realistic risks. Check your government's
advice for anything beyond this route, especially the far
southeast.

**Will my balloon actually fly?**
About 3 mornings in 4. The civil aviation authority grounds all
flights in wind or rain — nobody flies when anyone doesn't. Book
morning one of three, and treat a cancellation refund as the
system working.

**Is Istanbul enough of a reason to go alone?**
Absolutely — it's the anchor. But the domestic flights are so
cheap that skipping Cappadocia to "save time" is the most-regretted
decision in Turkey travel. If you must cut this route, cut
Pamukkale first, Ephesus second, Cappadocia never.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Halal is effectively the default across Turkey. Vegetarians eat
superbly without trying: mezes, gözleme, imam bayıldı, çiğ köfte
(the modern version is meatless), lentil soup with every meal.

**Euros or lira?**
Both, knowingly: big attractions price in euros and take cards;
street life runs on lira. Carry modest lira cash, pay cards
elsewhere, and re-check all prices near travel — inflation moves
them quarterly.

**Can I add the beach?**
Easily — from Selçuk, Kuşadası and Pamucak beach are 20 minutes;
serious beach weeks continue south to Bodrum or the Turquoise
Coast. That's the natural 14-day extension of this exact route.
