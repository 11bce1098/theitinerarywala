Puerto Rico packs an improbable amount into an island 160 km long.

**Old San Juan** is a walled colonial quarter founded in 1521 —
blue cobbles, pastel facades, and two enormous Spanish forts on
the headland that the US Navy shelled in 1898 and that are now
national park.

**El Yunque** is the **only tropical rainforest in the United
States national forest system**, half an hour from the city, with
waterfalls you swim in and 500 mm of rain a month in places.

And **Mosquito Bay on Vieques** is the **brightest bioluminescent
bay ever recorded** — dinoflagellates so concentrated that your
paddle leaves a blue wake and fish streak away underneath you like
flares.

For US travellers there is no passport, no visa and no currency
change. For everyone else it is a US entry, which means a US visa
or ESTA — and that is the one piece of admin to get right.

## Before you go: entry, the bay and hurricane season

### Entry — read this first

- **Puerto Rico is a US territory.** US citizens need no passport
  and travel domestically.
- **Everyone else enters the United States**: the same **visa or
  ESTA** as the mainland, with the same rules. There is no
  separate Puerto Rican entry.
- **US dollars, US plugs, US mobile networks** — if you have a US
  plan it works here at no extra cost.
- **Spanish is the dominant language**; English is widely spoken
  in San Juan and the tourist areas, less so in the interior.

### The bio bay, and how not to waste it

- **Mosquito Bay on Vieques** is the brightest of the three
  bioluminescent bays here, and the brightness depends almost
  entirely on **how dark the sky is.**
- **Book for the nights around the new moon.** A full moon washes
  it out completely and people come away disappointed having paid
  the same money.
- **Kayak tours only** — motorboats are banned, and swimming in it
  is prohibited to protect the organisms. **Sunscreen and
  repellent must be rinsed off** before you go in; both kill
  dinoflagellates.
- **It is cancelled in rain and high wind.** Build in a spare
  night on Vieques if you possibly can.

### Hurricane season, honestly

- **June to November is hurricane season**, with **August to
  October** the peak. **Hurricane Maria in 2017** devastated the
  island and the recovery took years; infrastructure is better now
  but power outages still happen.
- **December to April** is the dry season and the peak — best
  weather, highest prices, and winter escape traffic from the US.
- **May and early June** are the value window: warm, mostly dry,
  and much cheaper.

### Getting around

- **Hire a car.** San Juan has some transit; the rest of the
  island effectively has none, and El Yunque, Ponce and the west
  coast all need one.
- **Driving is on the right**, signs are in Spanish, **distances
  are in kilometres but speed limits in miles per hour**, which
  catches everyone out once.
- **Vieques is reached by ferry from Ceiba** (about 45 minutes,
  cheap, book online, and it sells out) or a short **flight from
  San Juan**. **Leave the hire car on the mainland** and rent a
  jeep on Vieques — the mainland companies forbid taking cars over.

## Day 1 — Land in San Juan

- **13:00** — Land at **Luis Muñoz Marín (SJU)**, 20 minutes from
  Old San Juan.
- **14:30** — **Stay in Old San Juan** rather than the Condado
  strip. The quarter is seven blocks square and the point is being
  in it in the evening.
- **16:00** — Walk **Calle del Cristo** and **Calle Fortaleza**,
  with the **blue cobblestones** underfoot — ballast from Spanish
  ships, cast from furnace slag, and they are the quarter's
  signature.
- **17:30** — **Paseo de la Princesa** along the city wall, out to
  the **Raíces** fountain and round beneath the ramparts.
- **19:30** — Dinner. ***Mofongo*** — mashed green plantain with
  garlic and crackling — and a *piña colada*, which **was invented
  in this city** and is claimed by two separate bars that both
  have plaques.
- **21:00** — **La Factoría** for cocktails if you want the
  late version; it is a series of unmarked rooms running into each
  other.

## Day 2 — The forts and the quarter

- **08:30** — **Castillo San Felipe del Morro** at opening, on the
  headland. Six levels of Spanish fortification begun in 1539,
  with walls 5 m thick, built to hold the harbour mouth against
  Drake, the Dutch and eventually the United States. The lawn in
  front is where the whole city flies kites at weekends.
- **10:30** — Walk the wall to **Castillo San Cristóbal**, the
  larger of the two and the biggest Spanish fort in the Americas,
  with tunnels and a sentry box out on the point.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then the **Catedral de San Juan Bautista**,
  which holds **Ponce de León's tomb**.
- **15:00** — **Museo de las Américas** in the old barracks, or
  **La Fortaleza** — the oldest executive mansion in continuous
  use in the Americas.
- **17:00** — **Cementerio Santa María Magdalena**, the white
  marble cemetery wedged between the fort and the sea.
- **19:00** — **Santurce** for dinner and street art — **La Placita
  de Santurce** is a market square that becomes an open-air bar
  after dark, and it is where San Juan actually goes.

## Day 3 — El Yunque

- **07:30** — Drive east, 45 minutes. **Reserve your entry permit
  online in advance** — El Yunque now caps vehicles and turning up
  without one does not work.
- **08:30** — **El Yunque National Forest**: 11,000 hectares, the
  **only tropical rainforest in the US national forest system**,
  and it rains most days — which is the point rather than a
  problem.
- **09:00** — **La Mina Falls** or **Juan Diego**, depending on
  which trails are open — storm damage closes sections for long
  periods and the park's own site is the only reliable source.
- **10:30** — **Yokahú Tower** for the view over the canopy to the
  coast, and **Mount Britton** tower, a steep 45-minute climb
  usually into cloud.
- **12:00** — Listen for the ***coquí*** — a thumb-sized tree frog
  whose two-note call is the national sound. They are everywhere
  and almost impossible to see.
- **13:30** — Lunch at the **Luquillo kiosks** — sixty-odd food
  stalls in a row by the beach, which is a Puerto Rican
  institution.
- **15:00** — **Luquillo beach** or **Playa Azul** for the
  afternoon.
- **18:00** — Back to San Juan, or sleep near **Ceiba** for the
  morning ferry.

## Day 4 — To Vieques, and the bay

- **08:00** — The **ferry from Ceiba to Vieques**, about 45
  minutes. **Book online days ahead** — residents have priority
  and the passenger sailings fill.
- **09:30** — Vieques is 34 km long with about 8,000 people. **Two
  thirds of it was a US Navy bombing range until 2003**, and that
  land is now a **national wildlife refuge** — which is precisely
  why the beaches are empty and undeveloped.
- **10:30** — Rent a **jeep**. The refuge roads are dirt and the
  best beaches are at the end of them.
- **12:00** — **Playa Caracas (Red Beach)** and **Playa La Chiva
  (Blue Beach)** in the refuge — white sand, no facilities, almost
  nobody.
- **15:00** — **Wild horses** wander the island freely and are
  genuinely wild; do not approach them.
- **17:00** — Check in, rinse off all sunscreen and repellent.
- **20:00** — **Mosquito Bay by kayak.** Paddling lights the water
  blue; trailing a hand leaves a glowing wake; fish moving below
  look like torpedoes. **It is the brightest bio bay on record**
  and on a moonless night it is genuinely difficult to believe.

## Day 5 — Vieques, slow

- **09:00** — **Playa Negra**, a black-sand beach reached by a
  short walk up a dry streambed — volcanic sand on a limestone
  island, which is unusual.
- **11:00** — **Esperanza** on the south coast: a single
  waterfront strip, the **malecón**, and the little **Vieques
  Conservation and Historical Trust** museum, which explains the
  bay and the Navy years properly.
- **13:00** — Lunch on the malecón.
- **14:30** — **Sun Bay (Sombé)**, a long public crescent, or back
  into the refuge for whichever beach you have not done.
- **17:00** — **Fort Conde de Mirasol** above Isabel Segunda —
  **the last fort Spain built in the Americas**, finished in 1855.
- **19:00** — Dinner in Esperanza.
- **Note** — If the bay was rained off last night, tonight is your
  second chance, which is why the spare night matters.

## Day 6 — Back west, and Ponce

- **08:30** — Ferry back to Ceiba, collect the car.
- **11:00** — Drive south-west to **Ponce**, about two hours —
  **the island's second city**, and nothing like San Juan.
- **13:00** — Lunch on the **Plaza Las Delicias**.
- **14:00** — The ***Parque de Bombas***, a red-and-black striped
  wooden firehouse from 1882 that is the most photographed
  building in Puerto Rico, and the cathedral beside it.
- **15:30** — The **Museo de Arte de Ponce**, which holds a
  genuinely important European collection — including Leighton's
  *Flaming June*, which is on this island and surprises people.
- **17:00** — **Hacienda Buena Vista**, a restored 19th-century
  coffee estate with working water-powered machinery, by tour and
  booked ahead.
- **19:00** — Dinner in Ponce, or drive on to the west coast.

## Day 7 — The west coast

- **08:30** — **Rincón**, the surf town on the western tip, about
  90 minutes from Ponce.
- **09:30** — **Domes Beach** and **Maria's** if there is swell —
  **December to March** is the season and Rincón hosted the world
  championships in 1968, which is what put it on the map.
- **11:30** — The **Punta Higüero lighthouse**, where **humpback
  whales pass close inshore between January and March**.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then choose:
- **14:30 — Option A:** **La Parguera**, the island's third
  bioluminescent bay, which permits swimming — dimmer than
  Mosquito Bay but you can get in.
- **14:30 — Option B:** **Cabo Rojo** — the pink salt flats, the
  cliffs at **Los Morrillos** with its 1882 lighthouse, and
  **Playa Sucia**, which despite the name is one of the best
  beaches on the island.
- **18:00** — Sunset at Rincón, which faces west and makes the
  most of it.
- **20:00** — Dinner.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Rincón to **SJU** is about two and a half hours
  across the island on the toll road. Leave margin; the San Juan
  end backs up.
- **If you have time** — **Arecibo** on the north coast, and the
  **Camuy caves**, one of the largest cave systems in the western
  hemisphere, if they are open.
- **Note** — Returning to the US mainland is a **domestic flight**
  with no immigration. Everyone else departs internationally.

## What to pack

- **Rain gear for El Yunque.** It is a rainforest and it will rain
  on you.
- **Reef shoes and water shoes** for the river pools and the
  refuge beaches.
- **Biodegradable sunscreen**, and plan to **rinse completely**
  before the bio bay — this is enforced and it matters.
- **A dry bag** for the kayak.
- **Repellent**, applied before you leave for the bay rather than
  at it.
- **US-style Type A/B plugs**, 120V — the same as the mainland.
- **Some Spanish**, which goes a long way outside San Juan.

## FAQ

**Do I need a visa?**
If you are a US citizen, no — it is a domestic trip. **Everyone
else is entering the United States** and needs the same ESTA or
visa as for New York. There is no separate Puerto Rican entry
route, and people are caught out by this.

**When should I book the bio bay?**
**Around the new moon**, and check the lunar calendar before you
book flights if this is a priority. A full moon drowns it out
entirely. Rain cancels tours, so leave a spare night on Vieques.

**Is it safe?**
Yes, for this itinerary, with ordinary city caution in parts of
San Juan after dark. The island is a US jurisdiction with US
policing, and the tourist areas are straightforward.

**Has it recovered from the hurricanes?**
Largely, but not evenly. Tourism infrastructure is back; **trail
closures in El Yunque and occasional power outages still
happen**, and the park's own website is the only reliable source
for what is open on the day.

**Vieques or Culebra?**
**Vieques** has the bio bay and the refuge beaches, and is in
this itinerary for the first reason. **Culebra** has Flamenco
Beach, which is regularly rated among the best in the world, and
no bio bay. With more days, do both.
