The Dalmatian coast is what happens when Venice, Rome and a
thousand islands share a sea: white-stone towns polished by
centuries of feet, water in Maldives blues but with
2,000-year-old bell towers behind it, and evenings that smell
of grilled fish and pine. Croatia joined **Schengen and the
euro** in 2023, so the trip runs on one visa and one currency
— and since **flydubai flies Dubai–Dubrovnik in season**, the
Adriatic starts four hours from home.

The classic shape, done open-jaw: **Dubrovnik (3 nights)** — the
walled city — then the coastal road north to **Split (4 nights)**,
using it as a base for a **Hvar island day** and a **Krka waterfalls
day**. Figure **{{EUR 1300}} per person** including the visa,
excluding flights. **Go May–June or September–early October**: sea
warm, ferries running, crowds humane. July–August works, but you'll
share every quay with the cruise calendar — this guide's timings
exist to beat it.

## Before you go: the admin

- **Schengen visa, Croatia flavour:** the same VFS drill
  as our [France](/itineraries/france-9-days-guide/),
  [Czech](/itineraries/czech-republic-7-days-guide/) and
  [Hungary](/itineraries/hungary-6-days-guide/) guides — **€90** +
  service fee via your nearest VFS centre with Croatia as
  main destination: biometrics, proof of residence, bank
  statements, employment letter, €30,000 insurance, full
  itinerary. Apply **4–6 weeks out** (more before
  summer). An existing multi-entry Schengen from another
  trip works here — one zone, one sticker.
- **Flights:** **flydubai Dubai–Dubrovnik**, roughly
  April–October, plus Zagreb links; otherwise one-stop
  on Turkish/Pegasus via Istanbul or EU hubs. Book
  **into Dubrovnik, out of Split** — open-jaw costs
  little more and saves a 4-hour backtrack.
- **Money:** the **euro**, cards everywhere; carry
  ~€50–100 cash for konobas, sobe landladies and ferry
  kiosks. When a terminal offers to bill you in your home
  currency, decline — always,
  as always.
- **The bus is the intercity system:** Dubrovnik→Split
  coastal buses run all day (**€20–30, ~4–4.5 h**,
  sea-side seats on the LEFT northbound) — the ride
  itself is a highlight. Passports handy: some routings
  nip through Bosnia's 9 km Neum corridor (the new
  Pelješac bridge routes avoid it).

## Day 1 — Land in Dubrovnik, meet the marble

- **Afternoon** — Land, bus/taxi to the old town area,
  check in.
- **17:30** — First entry through **Pile Gate** as the
  day-trippers drain away: the **Stradun** — the
  marble main street polished to a mirror — Onofrio's
  fountain, the Rector's Palace facades, alleys
  climbing in staircases either side.
- **20:00** — Dinner in a side-alley konoba (black
  risotto or grilled catch, €18–30 pp) — Stradun-front
  tables charge double for the same squid.

**Where to stay in Dubrovnik** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Hotel Excelsior** — 4.6/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~880 reviews), sea-view rooms with the old town
  filling the window, ten minutes' walk to Ploče Gate.
  €400+ in season.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Kompas**, Lapad bay — 4.7/5
  (~1,800 reviews), pool + beach + the €2 bus into
  town. ~€140–200.
- *Value:* **Hotel Lapad** — 4.3/5 (~3,200 reviews),
  marina-front classic ~€90–130 — though Dubrovnik's
  real value plays are the old town's **sobe/private
  rooms and apartments (€70–110)**: staying inside
  the walls means owning the town at dawn and
  midnight.

## Day 2 — The walls at opening, Lokrum in the afternoon

- **07:45** — Be at the Ploče entrance queue for the
  **City Walls at opening (8:00 in season; €35–40,
  and worth every cent)**: 2 km of ramparts, 1.5–2
  hours, orange roofs one side and open Adriatic the
  other. The honesty that organises the whole day:
  **cruise groups own the walls 10:00–15:00** —
  at 8 am you'll share them with a dozen
  photographers, at noon with two thousand day
  packs. Hydrate; there's no shade.
- **10:30** — Coffee in the old harbour, then the
  interiors: the Rector's Palace (~€15), the
  Franciscan monastery's cloister and Europe's
  third-oldest pharmacy (~€8), the cathedral's
  treasury.
- **14:00** — Boat from the old harbour to **Lokrum**
  (~€27 return incl. the reserve fee): the pine-and
  -peacock island where you swim — the "Dead Sea"
  salt lake for warm floating, the rocky south shore
  for the brave. **Sea-urchin rule, coast-wide: pack
  swim shoes.** Botanical gardens, a small GoT
  throne, olive-shaded paths — the anti-crowd
  afternoon.
- **19:00** — Last boat back; sunset drink at a
  **buža bar** — the hole-in-the-wall bars hanging
  off the sea-facing cliffs of the walls
  (€6 drinks, million-euro view).

## Day 3 — Srđ mountain and Dubrovnik at your pace

- **09:00** — **Cable car up Srđ** (~€30 return —
  or hike the zigzag path free, 1 h): the postcard
  view of the walled town as a coin dropped in blue
  velvet, plus the Homeland War museum in the
  Napoleonic fort (~€5) — sobering, recent,
  clarifying: those roof tiles are new because the
  city was shelled in 1991–92.
- **12:30** — Lunch in town, then the pick-your-own
  afternoon: **Game of Thrones corner** (walk
  Fort Lovrijenac's King's Landing ramparts, ~€15 or
  covered by the walls ticket same-day) for the
  fans; **Banje beach** swim with wall views;
  or the Ploče-side kayak sunset tour (~€35).
- **20:00** — Farewell-Dubrovnik dinner; pack for
  the coast road — buy tomorrow's bus seats
  tonight in season.

## Day 4 — The coastal road to Split

- **09:00** — **Dubrovnik → Split bus (€20–30,
  ~4–4.5 h)**: over the Pelješac bridge, past
  oyster-farm bays and the Makarska Riviera's
  mountain-wall coast — left-side window seats,
  snacks in the daypack.
- **14:00** — Check in and walk straight into
  **Diocletian's Palace** — not a museum: a Roman
  emperor's 1,700-year-old retirement palace with
  3,000 people still living inside it. Laundry
  lines between Corinthian columns; a sphinx from
  Egypt older than Rome itself watching the
  **Peristyle** square.
- **17:00** — Climb the **St Domnius bell tower**
  (~€15 combo with cathedral/crypt): vertigo stairs,
  harbour-to-mountains reward.
- **19:30** — The **Riva** promenade hour (Split's
  nightly catwalk), dinner in the palace lanes
  (€15–25 pp), gelato non-negotiable.

**Where to stay in Split** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Judita Palace Heritage Hotel** — 4.8/5
  (~900 reviews), a 16th-century palazzo INSIDE the
  palace walls. ~€250+.
- *Mid-range:* **Cornaro Hotel** — 4.7/5 (~1,400
  reviews), rooftop bar two minutes from the Golden
  Gate. ~€130–180.
- *Value:* **Divota Apartment Hotel** — 4.7/5 (~590
  reviews), design rooms scattered through the old
  Veli Varoš fishermen's quarter. ~€80–120.

## Day 5 — Hvar: the island day

- **08:00** — Fast catamaran to **Hvar Town**
  (Jadrolinija or Krilo, **~€18–22, 1 h — book
  online days ahead; July–August sailings sell
  out**, and note the trap: the cheap car ferry
  goes to Stari Grad, 30 minutes from Hvar Town).
- **09:30** — Hvar before the yachts wake: the
  Venetian piazza and cathedral, then the zigzag
  climb to the **Spanish Fortress** (~€13) —
  lavender in the air, the **Pakleni islands**
  scattered below like green stepping stones.
- **12:30** — Swim: walk the shore path west to the
  pebble coves (Pokonji Dol) or ride a €10 taxi
  boat to the Pakleni's Palmižana bay.
- **15:00** — Long lunch (grilled fish, gregada
  stew — €20–35 pp; Hvar prices carry a yacht
  tax), backstreet gelato, last swim.
- **17:30** — Catamaran home; Riva sundowner.
  *(Alternative island: Brač's Zlatni Rat beach —
  cheaper, beachier, less scene.)*

## Day 6 — Krka: the waterfall day

- **08:00** — **Krka National Park** — organised
  day tour €60–90 pp incl. transfer and often the
  Skradin boat, or DIY: bus to Skradin (~1 h 15)
  + entry. **Entry is fiercely seasonal: €40 in
  June–September, €20–25 in shoulder months,
  under €10 in winter** — one more argument for
  a September trip.
- **10:00** — The boardwalk circuit at
  **Skradinski Buk**: seventeen travertine
  terraces stacked into one great green staircase
  of water. **Honesty update: swimming here has
  been banned since 2021** — the emerald-pool
  photos you've seen are pre-ban; you come for
  the walkways, mills and mist now, and swim
  later at Skradin's town beach.
- **13:00** — Lunch in Skradin (riverside
  konoba, €15–20), optional park boat to
  Visovac monastery-island (~€15–20).
- **17:00** — Back to Split; easy evening —
  Marjan hill's first viewpoint (15-min climb
  from Varoš) for sunset over the palace.
- *(Waterfall purists' swap: Plitvice Lakes is
  the national icon but 3+ hours each way from
  Split — it belongs to the Zagreb guide,
  seeded below.)*

## Day 7 — Split's own day

- **09:00** — **Marjan peninsula** morning: rent
  bikes or walk the shore path to Bene beach,
  swim off the rocks under the pines (urchin
  shoes again), coffee at the Vidilica terrace.
- **13:00** — Lunch in Veli Varoš's stone lanes.
- **15:00** — Palace basements (**Diocletian's
  cellars**, ~€8 — the substructures that held
  the palace up for 17 centuries), the Golden
  Gate's giant **Grgur Ninski statue** (rub the
  toe, make the wish), green market browsing.
- **18:00** — Optional class move: sunset from
  **Matejuška** pier with locals and a bakery
  burek (€3) — or the rooftop-bar version above
  the Peristyle.
- **20:30** — Farewell dinner: peka (slow
  fire-baked lamb or octopus — order 24 h ahead
  at a proper konoba, €25–30 pp) — the Dalmatian
  send-off.

## Day 8 — Fly home from Split

- **Morning** — One last Riva coffee; airport
  bus (30 min). Open-jaw complete — no
  backtracking, all coast.

## What to pack

- **Swim shoes** — Dalmatia's beaches are pebble
  and rock, and sea urchins are real; this is the
  guide's most-thanked line.
- Reef-safe sunscreen, hat, refillable bottle
  (fountains run cold and drinkable).
- One modest layer for cathedral interiors;
  smart-casual covers every konoba.
- Walking shoes with grip: marble streets +
  fortress stairs; the Stradun after rain is an
  ice rink.
- Light jumper for May/September evenings and
  catamaran air-con.
- **European Type C/F adapter**, power bank for
  ferry days.

## FAQ

**Is Croatia safe?**
One of Europe's safest coasts — the honest hazard
list is environmental: sun, urchins, marble
streets, and August ferry queues. Pickpocketing
exists at Split's station and on packed Stradun
afternoons at a fraction of Barcelona levels.
Solo travellers and families: entirely
comfortable.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Plan a little. Halal-certified restaurants are
few (a handful in Split and Dubrovnik — kebab
houses are the reliable fallback), but the
coast's default is **seafood**: grilled fish,
octopus salad, black risotto — easy
seafood-halal eating without paperwork.
Vegetarians: blitva (chard + potato), grilled
veg platters, pasta/gnocchi, soparnik pie, and
strong pizza everywhere; say "bez mesa" (without
meat). Self-catering apartments + green markets
make it trivial.

**Dubrovnik and the crowds — is it still worth
it?**
Emphatically, IF you play the clock: walls at
8:00, old town before 10:00 and after 17:00,
Lokrum or Srđ 11:00–16:00 while the cruise
groups hold the Stradun. Check the port's cruise
calendar (public online) and give the heaviest
day to Lokrum. May–June and September make the
whole problem halve.

**Hvar or Brač — which island day?**
Hvar for the glamour, fortress views and Pakleni
swimming; **Brač (Zlatni Rat)** for the famous
shape-shifting beach and family ease at lower
prices. Ferry-schedule reality decides for many:
book whichever fast boat fits your morning.

**Should we squeeze in Plitvice?**
Not from Split in a day — 6+ hours of bus for a
park that deserves a slow morning. This route's
Krka delivers 80% of the waterfall wonder at 30%
of the transit. Plitvice anchors the Zagreb
guide below.

**What's Croatia guide #2?**
**Zagreb + Plitvice Lakes** — the capital's
café culture and the 16-lake national icon —
or the **Istria week** (Rovinj, Pula's arena,
truffle hills). Votes welcome; both on the
list.
