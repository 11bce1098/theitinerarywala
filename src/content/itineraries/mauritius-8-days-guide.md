Mark Twain reported that heaven was copied from Mauritius,
and the brochure has coasted on the quote ever since. What
the brochure undersells: this is the most **Indian** island
outside India — two-thirds of Mauritians trace their roots to
indentured workers who landed at Port Louis's Aapravasi Ghat,
Bhojpuri still floats around the markets, and the street food
is dholl puri at thirty rupees — paradise with a kitchen
worth flying for.

The practicalities are as friendly as the food: **visa-free
on arrival for most nationalities — 60 days, zero fee, Indian
passports included**, non-stops from the Gulf in ~6½ hours
and from Europe in around 12, and one free online form is the
entire paperwork. This guide does the
island properly with **two bases and a hire car**: the lively
north (**Grand Baie, 4 nights**), then the cinematic
southwest under Le Morne mountain (**3 nights**).

Costs are in Mauritian rupees — figure **{{MUR 43000}} per
person** excluding flights. **Best
windows: April–June and September–November** — between the
southern-winter winds and the January–March cyclone lottery.

## Before you go: the admin (it barely exists)

- - **Visa-free, free:** most passports get **60 days on arrival, no
  fee** (Indian passports included) — carry the return ticket, hotel
  bookings and a bank card as your never-asked-for proof of funds
  (~US$100/day is the official benchmark). Extensions to 6 months
  per year are possible in-country.
- **The one form:** the **Mauritius All-in-One Travel
  Digital Form** at **safemauritius.govmu.org**, within
  **72 hours before arrival** — free, instant QR;
  screenshot it, airlines ask at check-in.
- **Passport** valid beyond the stay; that's the whole
  file.
- **Driving:** Mauritius drives on the **left** — a jolt
  if you are used to the right, second nature if not.
  Your Indian licence (in English) is accepted for
  tourist stays — carry an IDP if you like belt AND
  braces. Roads are good, distances small (the whole
  island is 65 × 45 km), speed cameras plentiful.
- **Money:** cards widespread, but keep MUR 2,000–3,000
  cash for street food, markets and beach vendors. ATMs
  everywhere in towns.

## Day 1 — Land, drive north, exhale

- **Morning** — The overnight flight lands early.
  Collect the car (from ~MUR 1,300–1,700/day for a
  compact — book ahead in season) and drive north
  (~1 h) to **Grand Baie**, the island's sociable bay.
- **14:00** — First swim at **Pereybere**, the
  turquoise pocket-cove two minutes up the coast;
  gateau piment and alouda from the beach kiosk
  (MUR 100 covers you both).
- **17:30** — Sunset at **La Cuvette** beach, dinner
  in Grand Baie (Creole fish curry or a grill,
  MUR 400–700 pp) — and note how many menus read
  half like home.

**Where to stay in Grand Baie** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **LUX* Grand Baie** — 4.9/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~1,400 reviews), the island's glossiest rooftop-bar
  address. ~MUR 25,000+. (Royal Palm Beachcomber, 4.9,
  is old-money grandeur.)
- *Mid-range:* **Mauricia Beachcomber** — 4.6/5
  (~7,100 reviews, the bay's #1 Best Value),
  all-inclusive on the beach. ~MUR 9,000–12,000.
- *Value:* **Ocean V Hotel, Pereybere** — 4.6/5
  (~1,300 reviews), rooftop infinity pool at
  guesthouse money. ~MUR 3,500–5,000.

## Day 2 — Port Louis and the ancestors

- **09:00** — **SSR Botanical Garden, Pamplemousses**
  (~MUR 300): giant Amazon water lilies, 85 varieties
  of palm, and the pond Indian grandmothers
  photograph for an hour. Guides at the gate
  (~MUR 200) earn their fee.
- **11:30** — Into **Port Louis**: start at the
  **Aapravasi Ghat** (UNESCO, small/free entry) — the
  immigration depot steps where half a million
  indentured workers from India first stood between
  1834 and 1920. For most Indian visitors this quiet
  courtyard is unexpectedly the trip's heaviest
  moment: the island's story starts on these sixteen
  steps.
- **13:00** — **Central Market** lunch the correct
  way: **dholl puri** (MUR 15–30 each — the national
  dish is basically a Bihari roti that emigrated),
  plus pineapple with chilli-salt.
- **15:00** — The **Citadel (Fort Adelaide)** for the
  harbour panorama, then Caudan Waterfront's umbrella
  street if souvenirs call.
- **17:00** — Back north for a Mont Choisy sunset
  walk — 3 km of casuarina-backed sand.

## Day 3 — Île aux Cerfs: the catamaran day

- **07:30** — Full-day **catamaran to Île aux Cerfs**
  from Trou d'Eau Douce (shared boats **MUR
  2,500–3,500 pp with BBQ lunch**; booking with
  island-wide hotel transfer pushes it toward
  MUR 4,500 — driving yourself to the jetty is the
  saving): sails up along the east coast, a snorkel
  stop on the reef, grilled fish and sega drums on
  deck, then two hours on the island's swimming-pool
  lagoon and white spits.
- **The honest note:** Île aux Cerfs is beautiful AND
  busy — it's the island's day-trip catwalk. Go for
  the sailing-day whole of it, not a deserted-island
  fantasy; the deserted version is tomorrow's north
  coast.
- **19:30** — Easy dinner back in Grand Baie;
  sunburn audit.

## Day 4 — The lazy north (your beach day)

- **09:30** — Pick your sand: **Trou aux Biches**
  (calm, shallow, family-perfect) or a boat morning
  to the **northern islets** (Gabriel/Flat Island
  cats run ~MUR 2,500 pp if the lagoon is calling
  again).
- **13:00** — Beach-shack grilled catch (MUR
  300–500).
- **15:30** — **Cap Malheureux**: the red-roofed
  chapel with Coin de Mire island floating behind —
  the north's postcard — then Grand Gaube's quiet
  coves.
- **19:00** — Grand Baie last night: Sunset
  Boulevard browsing, seafood platter splurge or
  street-food crawl (boulettes — Sino-Mauritian
  dumpling soup — MUR 150).

## Day 5 — South over the spine: tea, the sacred lake, Le Morne

- **09:00** — Check out, drive south into the hills.
  Stop 1: **Bois Chéri tea factory & tasting**
  (~MUR 400) on the misty plateau.
- **11:30** — **Ganga Talao (Grand Bassin)** — free:
  the crater lake Hindu Mauritius holds sacred, water
  from the Ganges ritually mingled in, guarded by a
  **33-metre Shiva** and a matching Durga across the
  road. During Maha Shivaratri (Feb/Mar) half a
  million pilgrims walk here barefoot from across
  the island — the largest Hindu gathering outside
  India. Cover shoulders; feed no monkeys.
- **13:30** — Into **Black River Gorges National
  Park** (free): the **Alexandra Falls** and **Gorges
  viewpoints** — 6,500 hectares of green crumpled
  mountains with tropicbirds drawing white lines
  below.
- **16:00** — Down the hairpins via the **Maconde
  viewpoint** to the **Le Morne** coast; check in
  and watch the mountain — a UNESCO World Heritage
  site as a monument to escaped slaves who hid on
  its cliffs — go copper at sunset.

**Where to stay around Le Morne** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **The St. Regis Le Morne** — 4.7/5
  (~3,900 reviews), plantation-house polish directly
  under the mountain. ~MUR 28,000+. (Dinarobin
  Beachcomber, 4.8/~5,000, shares the peninsula.)
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Riu Turquoise** — 4.2/5
  (~980 reviews), all-inclusive on Le Morne beach —
  the sensible family engine room. ~MUR 9,000–12,000.
- *Value:* **Villa Seewoo, La Gaulette** — 4.9/5
  (~350 reviews), a guesthouse with breakfast and
  mountain views ten minutes from the beach.
  ~MUR 2,500–3,500.

## Day 6 — Chamarel: earth in seven colours

- **09:00** — **Chamarel Seven Coloured Earth
  Geopark** (**MUR 400 adult / 200 child**, includes
  the **100 m Chamarel waterfall** viewpoint and the
  giant-tortoise pen): volcanic dunes striped
  red-brown-violet-blue that never mix, best in
  low morning sun.
- **11:30** — Optional grown-up stop: the **Rhumerie
  de Chamarel** distillery tour + tasting
  (~MUR 650) — or coffee at a planteur's café with
  valley views for the abstainers.
- **13:00** — Lunch in Chamarel village (table
  d'hôte Creole spreads, MUR 500–800).
- **15:00** — Back down for **Le Morne public
  beach**: filao trees, kite-surfers streaking the
  lagoon, the mountain looming — many call it the
  island's best free afternoon.
- **19:00** — La Gaulette's local restaurants do the
  island's best-value seafood (ourite — octopus
  curry — MUR 350).

## Day 7 — Dolphins at dawn, slow goodbye

- **06:30** — From Black River/Tamarin, the
  **dolphin cruise** (catamaran ~MUR 1,800–2,500 pp,
  speedboat less): spinner and bottlenose pods
  commute along this coast most mornings.
  **Ethics check (house rule for wildlife on this
  site):** choose operators who keep distance and never
  "guarantee swimming ON the pods" — chasing boats
  are the coast's known problem; reward the patient
  ones.
- **11:00** — **La Preneuse** beach and the Martello
  tower, or the lower **Le Morne trail** (the summit
  push needs a licensed guide, ~MUR 2,000 pp — 3–4 h,
  worth it for the fit).
- **16:00** — Pool, pack, one last lagoon float.
- **19:30** — Farewell dinner under the mountain.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- **Morning** — It's ~45 minutes from Le Morne to
  the airport (SSR International, southeast). Drop
  the car, board with sand in every zip. Duty-free
  vanilla and Bois Chéri tea cost half what the
  resort shop asked.

## What to pack

- Reef-safe sunscreen + rash vests (the lagoon
  tricks you: cool water, equatorial UV).
- Reef shoes for rocky entries, snorkel set if
  you're particular.
- A light jumper for May–September evenings — the
  southern winter is real at 19°C — and for
  Chamarel's uplands anytime.
- Modest cover for Ganga Talao (shoulders/knees).
- **UK-style Type G plugs** — UK and Gulf chargers work
  unchanged (some older hotels also keep two-pin C).
- Motion tablets for catamaran days, mosquito
  repellent for dusk.

## FAQ

**Is Mauritius safe?**
Among the safest destinations on this site — solo
women road-trip it routinely. The full caution
list: don't leave valuables visible in the parked
car at trailheads/beaches, watch the lagoon
current at unmonitored beaches, and respect
cyclone-warning classes if travelling
January–March (hotels handle it well; it mostly
means a pool day indoors).

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Both are natively easy. Mauritius is ~17% Muslim —
**halal butchers and restaurants are normal
fixtures**, especially in Port Louis and the north.
Vegetarians eat like royalty: dholl puri, gateau
piment, farata, seven-curry temple meals and a
Hindu-majority food culture mean the question is
"which veg thing", never "is there one". Fine-dining
resorts handle both with a day's notice.

**When exactly should we go?**
**April–June and September–November** are the
island's grins: 24–28°C, low rain, calm lagoons.
July–August is dry but windy (the east and south
blow — kite-surfers cheer, swimmers move north);
December is lovely and pricey; **January–March is
hot, humid and carries the cyclone lottery** —
insurable, but know it.

**Which side of the island should we base on?**
This guide's answer is both — but if forced: the
**north** for swimming, eating out and doing
things nightly; the **southwest** for scenery,
hikes and the feeling of being somewhere. The east
(Belle Mare) has the dreamiest resort beaches and
the least around them — perfect for the honeymoon
edition below.

**Mauritius or Seychelles or Maldives?**
The site now has all three, so: **Maldives** for
pure overwater luxury doing nothing beautifully
([guide](/itineraries/maldives-7-days-guide/)), **Seychelles**
for dramatic granite scenery and short wild hikes
([guide](/itineraries/seychelles-8-days-guide/)), **Mauritius**
for the most COUNTRY per day — road trips, food
culture, festivals, history — at the friendliest
prices of the trio. Families and first-timers:
Mauritius wins more often than the brochures admit.

**What's Mauritius guide #2?**
**The honeymoon edition** — Belle Mare/Le Morne
resort pairing, seawalk and submarine days, private
catamarans — or the flight to **Rodrigues**, the
"Mauritius of 50 years ago". Votes welcome; both
are on the list.
