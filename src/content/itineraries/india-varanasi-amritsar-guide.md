This is the India trip about meaning rather than monuments. Varanasi
is one of the oldest living cities on earth — Hindus come to its
ghats to pray, to bathe, and to die, and the visitor's job is to
witness it respectfully from a rowing boat at dawn. Amritsar is its
counterweight: the Golden Temple's white marble and gold at the
centre of a pool of stillness, a free kitchen that feeds a hundred
thousand people a day, and — twenty minutes away — the
foot-stamping theatre of the Wagah border ceremony. One week, two
cities, and more of India understood than most month-long trips
manage.

Fair warning, given honestly: **Varanasi confronts.** The old city
is intense — narrow, crowded, sacred and profane shoulder to
shoulder — and the burning ghats mean you will see cremation. Most
travellers leave calling it the most powerful place they've ever
been; a few just want to leave. Read Day 2's etiquette notes and
decide with open eyes.

**Go October–March.** Summer is brutal in both cities; winter dawns
on the Ganges are misty and cold (5–10°C) — part of the magic,
pack for it.

All costs are in Indian rupees (INR), with conversions calculated
at today's exchange rate. **Indian and OCI passports pay domestic
rates at ticketed sites.**

## Before you go: the short version of the admin

Visas, UPI One World, SIMs and the general scam list live in our
Golden Triangle guide. Route specifics:

- **Flights:** into Varanasi (VNS) via Delhi from most origins;
  between the cities, Varanasi → Amritsar usually connects
  **via Delhi** (~4–5 h door to door; occasional seasonal directs
  exist — check). Amritsar (ATQ) has **direct Gulf connections**
  (Dubai/Sharjah), so Gulf-based readers should fly home from
  there rather than backtracking.
- **Both cities are walking-and-rickshaw places** — the old
  quarters are vehicle-free or close to it. Autos and cycle
  rickshaws by agreed fare; Ola/Uber work outside the old cores.
- **Dress for sacred spaces all week:** shoulders and knees
  covered everywhere, head covering mandatory at the Golden Temple
  (free scarves at the gates) and useful at every Varanasi temple.
- **The Varanasi hustle:** boat touts, "priests" offering blessings
  that become invoices, and karma-donation collectors at the
  burning ghats. The Golden Triangle guide's rules apply doubled —
  agree every price first, and a firm, smiling no is a complete
  sentence.

### Travelling with kids?

- Doable but think twice: Varanasi's lanes are intense, edges are
  unfenced, and cremation is public — parents of teens report
  profound trips; parents of toddlers report stress.
- Amritsar, by contrast, is wonderful with children — the temple
  community adores them, langar is an adventure, and Wagah is
  pure pantomime.
- If bringing young kids: boat mornings (life jackets are
  mandatory now), calmer Assi Ghat as your base, and skip the
  burning-ghat lanes.

## Day 1 — Arrive Varanasi, first fire ceremony

- **Afternoon** — Land (via Delhi), taxi to the ghats-side hotel;
  the last stretch is on foot or cycle-rickshaw — the old city
  excludes cars, which is why it still exists.
- **17:00** — First walk south along the ghats — 80+ stone
  stairways to the river, each with its own life: laundry, cricket,
  yoga, buffalo.
- **18:00 winter / 18:45 summer-ish** — **Ganga aarti at
  Dashashwamedh Ghat** (free from the steps; arrive 45–60 min
  early for a spot) — seven priests, fire lamps, bells and
  20,000 voices. Tonight watch from the steps, inside the sound;
  tomorrow you'll see it from the water.
- **20:00** — Dinner at your hotel rooftop — the lanes at night
  are for day three's confidence, not day one's.

**Where to stay in Varanasi** (three nights, on or above the ghats)

- *Heritage splurge:* **BrijRama Palace** — the 18th-century
  palace on Darbhanga Ghat, arrival by boat; Varanasi's one great
  luxury address.
- *Mid-range:* **Amritara Suryauday Haveli** — riverside haveli,
  courtyard calm, rooftop Ganges views.
- *Budget:* **Ganpati Guest House** — the long-running
  travellers' favourite with river-view rooms and rooftop café.

## Day 2 — Dawn on the river, the old city, the corridor

- **5:45** — **Sunrise boat ride** (book the evening before,
  ~20:00, for fair rates: shared rowboat ₹150–250/person, private
  ₹1,500–2,500 — sunrise carries a premium; life jackets are
  mandatory). Drift from Assi toward Manikarnika as the sun lifts
  out of the far bank and the ghats fill with bathers and
  yoga — the single greatest hour in Indian travel.
- **7:30** — Passing **Manikarnika, the burning ghat**, from the
  water: fires burn around the clock and have for centuries.
  The etiquette is absolute — **no photographs of the pyres, ever**,
  no pointing, and on land later, politely refuse anyone offering
  "closer views" for donations.
- **8:30** — Kachori-and-jalebi breakfast in the lanes; the famous
  hole-in-the-wall lassi shops (served in clay cups) open
  mid-morning.
- **10:00** — **Kashi Vishwanath temple and corridor** — the
  rebuilt riverfront complex around Shiva's great shrine. Entry is
  free; queues are long, phones/bags go into lockers, and
  non-Hindu visitors should check current inner-sanctum rules at
  the counter (the corridor and outer complex welcome all).
- **13:00** — Lunch, then the wise Varanasi move: retreat, rest,
  process.
- **17:30** — Evening round two, your choice: the **aarti from a
  boat** (stationary on the water facing Dashashwamedh —
  spectacular, book ahead) or the smaller, more intimate **Assi
  Ghat aarti** among locals.
- **20:00** — Dinner; try Banarasi specialities — baati chokha,
  malaiyyo in winter, and paan to finish, because Banaras invented
  the art.

## Day 3 — Sarnath, silk and the slow goodbye

- **6:00** — Optional: **Subah-e-Banaras** at Assi Ghat — the
  dawn programme of Vedic chants, ragas and open-air yoga
  (~5:00–5:30 start by season; free) — gentler than another boat,
  and locals outnumber tourists.
- **9:30** — **Sarnath** (~30–40 min): where the Buddha preached
  his first sermon. The great **Dhamek Stupa** and monastery ruins
  (foreigners ~₹300), the excellent **Archaeological Museum**
  (small fee; **closed Fridays**) holding the Ashoka lion capital —
  India's national emblem — in the flesh. Two–three calm hours;
  the contrast with the old city is the point.
- **13:00** — Lunch back in town.
- **15:00** — **Banarasi silk**: the weaving quarter's looms and a
  reputable showroom (your hotel knows the honest ones) — a real
  Banarasi sari or stole is the souvenir of the trip. Watch the
  handloom work before buying; that's what you're paying for.
- **18:00** — Last ghat walk at golden hour; sit on the steps and
  let the city do its thing.
- **20:00** — Pack for an early flight; rooftop farewell dinner.

## Day 4 — Fly to Amritsar, first sight of gold

- **Morning** — Fly Varanasi → Amritsar via Delhi (~4–5 h door to
  door). Land, check in on the pedestrianised **Heritage Street**.
- **16:00** — Walk the heritage corridor to the temple complex.
  Shoes into the free cloakroom, feet through the wash, head
  covered (free scarves at the gate) — and then through the
  gateway: the **Golden Temple** floating on its pool, gold on
  water, kirtan hymns on loudspeakers that somehow soothe rather
  than blare. All faiths welcome, 24 hours, free, forever.
- **17:30** — First slow parikrama (clockwise circuit) of the
  pool; join the queue for the inner sanctum if it's short, or
  save it for dawn.
- **19:30** — **Langar** — the world's largest free kitchen:
  sit in rows on the floor with everyone from beggars to
  billionaires, eat dal-roti-kheer served by volunteers. Then do
  the thing that makes the visit: **volunteer 30 minutes** rolling
  chapatis or washing plates — walk-ins welcome, no signup.
- **21:30** — The temple at night is, many say, more beautiful
  still — stay for the illuminated reflection, or return for the
  **Palki ceremony** (the scripture's nightly procession, ~21:45
  winter).

**Where to stay in Amritsar** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Taj Swarna** — the city's five-star benchmark,
  superb Punjabi kitchen.
- *Mid-range:* **Fortune Inn Heritage Walk** — superbly rated,
  walking distance to the temple.
- *Budget:* **SureStay Heritage Walk** — three minutes from the
  gates, excellent value; the SGPC's own pilgrim guesthouses are
  the free/nominal alternative.

## Day 5 — Dawn gold, hard history, border theatre

- **5:30** — (Or 3:30, for the brave: the **amrit vela** hours are
  transcendent.) The temple at dawn — mist on the pool, the first
  hymns, short sanctum queue. This is the visit you'll remember.
- **8:30** — Breakfast on Heritage Street.
- **9:30** — **Jallianwala Bagh** (nominal entry; grounds all
  day) — the walled garden of the 1919 massacre: the preserved
  bullet holes, the martyrs' well. Fifteen minutes' walk and a
  century of weight.
- **11:00** — **The Partition Museum**, Town Hall (foreigners
  ~₹200; ~2 h; typically closed Mondays) — the world's first
  museum of the 1947 Partition, told through survivors' objects
  and voices. In this city, on this border, it lands hard and it
  should.
- **13:00** — Lunch: Amritsari kulcha, properly — **Kulwant Singh
  Kulchian Wala** or the Lawrence Road institutions (₹80–120 of
  butter-blistered perfection).
- **14:30** — Drive to **Wagah-Attari border** (30 km; shared
  transport ~₹150/seat, private car ~₹1,800 round trip). Arrive
  ~2 h before the ceremony — seating is first-come and free;
  guards wave **foreigners, women and children** toward better
  blocks (carry passports). Big bags stay in the car.
- **16:15 winter / 17:15 summer** — **The Beating Retreat
  ceremony**: high-kicking soldiers, roaring grandstands on both
  sides of the gate, the synchronized flag-lowering — one hour of
  nationalist opera that ends, disarmingly, with a handshake.
- **19:30** — Back in town; dinner at **Kesar Da Dhaba** (since
  1916 — the dal makhani that launched a thousand imitations).

## Day 6 — The food city, the fort, the last hour of gold

- **9:00** — Breakfast like Amritsar means it: puri-chole or
  another kulcha round, then **Gurdas Ram Jalebi Wala**
  (est. 1884) while they're hot.
- **11:00** — The old-city bazaars: phulkari embroidery, juttis,
  papad-warian lanes; or the **Gobindgarh Fort** complex
  (ticketed, part theme-park these days — go for the walls and
  skip the add-ons).
- **13:30** — Clay-pot **lassi** at the century-old akharas —
  spoon-thick, ₹50–70, lunch replaced.
- **16:00** — Rest, shop, or one more museum (the Sikh Museum
  inside the temple complex, free, moving).
- **19:00** — Farewell dinner — Amritsari fish or the vegetarian
  thali blowout.
- **21:00** — Last visit to the Golden Temple at night. Sit by the
  water until they sing the day closed. No better ending exists in
  India.

## Day 7 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Amritsar airport: direct to the Gulf, or connect
  via Delhi. If the flight is late, one more dawn at the temple
  costs nothing — literally.

## What to pack

- A scarf/head-cover per person — used daily in both cities.
- Modest layers throughout; winter dawn on the river is 5–10°C —
  real jacket, hat for the boat.
- Slip-on shoes (removed constantly) + socks for cold marble
  parikramas.
- Hand sanitiser and the standard India stomach kit; the street
  food is half the trip — ease in, choose busy stalls.

  the rest.

## FAQ

**Is Varanasi too intense for a first India trip?**
It's the deep end. If this is your first India, do our Golden
Triangle first and add this route second — you'll navigate the
lanes with far more ease. Hardy first-timers do land straight
here and thrive; know your travel temperament.

**Can I photograph the cremation ghats?**
No. From the river at a respectful distance, wide shots of the
ghat are tolerated by most boatmen's guidance; on land, cameras
stay down entirely near the pyres. When unsure, don't — no
photograph is worth the intrusion, and touts "authorising" photos
for money have no authority to sell.

**Is the Golden Temple really open to everyone?**
Completely — every faith, no ticket, 24 hours, and the langar
feeds all comers as a founding principle. Cover your head, go
sober and shoeless, and you are a guest of honour like everyone
else.

**Is Wagah worth the hype?**
As spectacle, absolutely — it's the world's most choreographed
border. Go once, cheer along, and read the Partition Museum the
same day; the pairing turns pantomime into perspective.

**Vegetarian and halal food?**
Varanasi's old city is heavily vegetarian (and largely
alcohol-free near the ghats) — a vegetarian paradise. Amritsar's
langar is vegetarian by rule; the city's Muslim quarter and many
dhabas serve halal — though the iconic dhabas are veg — so both
diets travel well here.

**What pairs with this route?**
Amritsar sits 4–5 h by road from **Dharamshala/McLeod Ganj** (the
Tibetan world — a natural +3 days), and Varanasi connects onward
to **Bodh Gaya** for the Buddhist circuit. Within our guides, the
Golden Triangle bolts on via Delhi at either end.
