Sarajevo has a line in the pavement marking where West met East —
Ottoman bazaar on one side, Austro-Hungarian boulevards on the
other — and within a few hundred metres a mosque, a synagogue, a
Catholic cathedral and an Orthodox church. It is one of the most
interesting cities in Europe and one of the least visited.

It is also a city that was besieged for nearly four years in the
1990s, the longest siege of a capital in modern history, and that
is visible everywhere once you know what you are looking at: the
"Sarajevo roses" where mortar scars in the pavement have been filled
with red resin, and the tunnel under the airport that kept the city
supplied.

South, **Mostar**'s bridge was destroyed in 1993 and rebuilt from
the original stones raised out of the river. **Blagaj** has a
sixteenth-century dervish house beneath a 200 m cliff where the
Buna river emerges fully formed. **Kravice** is a horseshoe of
waterfalls you can swim in.

Seven days, and one warning that is not optional.

## Before you go: the history, the landmines, and the money

### Entry

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for 90 days — the EU, UK,
  US, Canada, Australia and many others.
- **Bosnia is not in the EU or Schengen**, so time here does not
  count against a Schengen allowance.
- **The convertible mark (BAM)**, pegged to the euro. Euros are
  sometimes accepted at a poor rate; pay in marks.

### The one warning that matters

- **Landmines from the 1990s remain in parts of the countryside.**
  Clearance is ongoing and the affected areas are mapped and
  marked, but they have not all been cleared.
- **Stay on marked paths and roads**, do not wander into abandoned
  buildings or unmarked fields, and take any warning sign at face
  value.
- **This does not affect the towns or normal tourist routes at all.**
  Sarajevo, Mostar, Blagaj and Kravice are entirely fine. It is
  rural hiking and exploring ruins where it applies.

### The history, handled honestly

- **The war is recent, personal, and talked about openly.** Most
  guides lived through it. Ask respectfully and you will get
  extraordinary conversations.
- **The Siege of Sarajevo** ran 1992–96. The **Tunnel of Hope**
  museum, dug under the airport runway, is the essential site.
- **Srebrenica** is three hours from Sarajevo and is a genocide
  memorial, not a day out. Go if you mean to; go with a guide.
- **Do not treat the damage as scenery.** Photographing bullet holes
  is one thing; treating a neighbourhood as a backdrop is another.
- **The country has three constituent peoples and a complicated
  political structure.** You will notice different flags and
  alphabets in different places. It is not your argument to have.

### Getting around

- **Buses connect Sarajevo and Mostar** in about 2.5 hours and are
  cheap and frequent.
- **The train between them is slower and far more scenic**, running
  through the Neretva canyon. Take it in one direction if the
  timetable allows; the service has been intermittent.
- **A car helps for Blagaj, Kravice and Počitelj**, which cluster
  near Mostar and are awkward without one. Day tours from Mostar
  cover all three cheaply if you would rather not drive.
- **Roads are winding.** Distances are short and journeys are long.

### When to go

- **May to June and September to October** are ideal: warm, green,
  and quiet.
- **July and August** are hot in Mostar — 40°C is normal — and
  busier with day trips from the Croatian coast.
- **Winter** is cold; Sarajevo has genuine ski resorts from the 1984
  Olympics, twenty minutes from the city and remarkably cheap.

## Day 1 — Land in Sarajevo

- **Afternoon** — Land, drop bags near **Baščaršija**, the Ottoman
  bazaar.
- **Evening** — Coffee in the bazaar, done properly in a džezva,
  and ćevapi for dinner.

## Day 2 — Sarajevo's layers

- **Morning** — The **Meeting of Cultures** line, then **Gazi
  Husrev-bey Mosque**, the **Old Orthodox Church**, the
  **cathedral** and the **synagogue**, all within a short walk.
- **Afternoon** — The **Latin Bridge**, where Franz Ferdinand was
  shot in 1914, and the City Hall, burned in 1992 and rebuilt.
- **Evening** — The **Yellow Fortress** for sunset over a valley of
  minarets and white gravestones.

## Day 3 — The siege, and the hills

- **Morning** — The **Tunnel of Hope**, out by the airport. It
  explains the siege better than any book.
- **Afternoon** — The **cable car** to Trebević, the old Olympic
  mountain, and the abandoned **bobsleigh track** now covered in
  graffiti.
- **Evening** — Last Sarajevo night.

## Day 4 — South to Mostar

- **Morning** — Train or bus through the **Neretva canyon**, which
  is the reason to take the train.
- **Afternoon** — **Stari Most**, the rebuilt bridge, and the
  cobbled bazaar either side of it. Divers jump from it in summer,
  for money, and it is 24 m down.
- **Evening** — The bridge lit, from a restaurant on the riverbank.

## Day 5 — Blagaj, Počitelj and Kravice

- **Morning** — **Blagaj Tekija**, the dervish house under the
  cliff where the Buna emerges from a cave, which is one of the most
  striking places in the Balkans.
- **Midday** — **Počitelj**, an Ottoman village stacked up a
  hillside above the river.
- **Afternoon** — **Kravice waterfalls**, a horseshoe of falls into
  a pool you can swim in.
- **Evening** — Back to Mostar.

## Day 6 — Mostar, or over the border

- **Option A** — Mostar slowly: the **Muslibegović House**, the
  old Turkish houses, and the **sniper tower** if you want the
  harder history.
- **Option B** — **Kotromanić** and the Neretva valley north, or a
  day trip to **Dubrovnik**, which is two and a half hours away.
- **Evening** — Last dinner.

## Day 7 — Fly home

- Sarajevo and Mostar both have small airports; **Dubrovnik and
  Split** are within three hours and often have better connections.

## What to pack

- **Shoes for cobbles and hills.** Both cities are steep.
- **Modest cover for mosques** — shoulders and knees, and
  headscarves are lent at the door.
- **Layers.** Sarajevo sits in a bowl at 500 m and is cool; Mostar
  is Mediterranean and hot.
- **Swimwear** for Kravice.
- **European Type C/F plugs.**
- **Cash in marks** for markets and small places.

## FAQ

**Is it safe?**
Yes, for visitors, entirely so in the towns. The one real caveat is
landmines in some rural areas — stay on marked paths and out of
abandoned buildings, and it does not affect this itinerary.

**Is talking about the war appropriate?**
Generally yes, if you ask respectfully. Most people are willing and
many guides are explicit that they want visitors to understand it.
Follow their lead on how much detail they want to give.

**Can I do it as a day trip from Croatia?**
Mostar is done that way by thousands of people and it is a poor
version of the country. Sarajevo is the reason to come and needs two
nights minimum.

**How does it compare to Montenegro or Albania?**
It has no coastline to speak of and far more history. If you want
beaches, go next door. If you want the most interesting city in the
Balkans, come here — see our
[Montenegro guide](/itineraries/montenegro-7-days-guide/) for the
coast, which combines easily.
