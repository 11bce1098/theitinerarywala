For decades, the biggest country in the Gulf was the one you
couldn't see. Then Saudi Arabia opened the doors, spent
astonishing money on its heritage, and quietly became the most
interesting new destination on Earth — with a **one-year,
multiple-entry eVisa** waiting at the end of a web form for
most of the world, and a short hop from anywhere in the Gulf.

This trip pairs the two showpieces. **Riyadh**: a capital
mid-transformation, where the mud-brick birthplace of the
Saudi state has been rebuilt into an open-air museum quarter
and a sandstone cliff an hour away drops off the edge of the
world. **AlUla**: the oasis valley where the Nabataeans —
Petra's people — carved 111 monumental tombs into golden
outcrops at **Hegra**, Saudi's first UNESCO site, standing in
silence Petra hasn't known for fifty years.

Costs are in Saudi riyals. Figure **{{SAR 5300}} per person**
excluding international flights. **This is an October–March
trip**: AlUla in summer is a 45°C furnace, and
in winter it's the Gulf's best-weather escape, with Riyadh
Season's carnival running the same months.

## Before you go: the admin

- **The eVisa is the whole story:** citizens of some
  60 countries — most of Europe, the UK, the US, Japan,
  Australia and more — apply directly at
  **visa.visitsaudi.com**: **SAR 535 all-in** (includes
  mandatory insurance), approval usually **24–48 hours,
  often minutes**, and you get a **one-year,
  multiple-entry visa with 90-day stays**. One form covers
  this trip and every weekend return.
- **Not on that list?** Two routes open the same eVisa to
  any nationality — Indian passports very much included:
  **residence in a GCC country valid 3+ months**, or a
  **valid US, UK or Schengen visa** that has been used at
  least once. A visa-on-arrival lane (~SAR 480) exists for
  both categories, though the online route is saner.
- The tourist eVisa also permits **Umrah** (outside Hajj
  season). **Makkah remains
  closed to non-Muslims**; mixed groups plan around it.
- **Book with the calendar:** a **seasonal Dubai–AlUla
  non-stop** (3× weekly — Sun/Tue/Thu, late October to late
  March) makes a perfect open-jaw if you route through the
  Gulf: **fly into Riyadh, home direct from AlUla**.
  Outside that window, and from everywhere else, you
  connect back through Riyadh or Jeddah.
- Domestic leg **Riyadh → AlUla**: Saudia and flynas,
  ~1 h 50, **SAR 250–550** — book early; AlUla's winter
  flights fill with Riyadh weekenders.

## The culture brief (honest, and mostly good news)

- **Dress:** modest, not costumed — no abaya required for
  visitors. Shoulders and knees covered does it;
  loose linen is your friend anyway. At mosques and
  heritage sites, scarves for women are respectful.
- **Alcohol: none, anywhere, full stop.** The Kingdom's
  evening currency is instead saffron coffee, mint
  lemonade and some of the Gulf's best karak. Our usual
  wine notes take this guide off.
- **Prayer times:** shops pull shutters for ~20–30 min
  at prayer (restaurants mostly keep serving seated
  guests). The Athan app or Google prayer times turns
  this from surprise into rhythm. Friday before ~1pm is
  the quiet zone; plan museums after.
- **Getting around:** **Uber/Careem run everything** in
  Riyadh (cheap, plentiful), and the gleaming new
  **Riyadh Metro** costs SAR 4 a ride. Women travel,
  drive and dine solo freely — the transformation is
  real, and visitors describe feeling conspicuously
  safe.
- **Manners:** photograph people only with permission
  (especially women), skip government buildings, keep
  PDA minimal. Hospitality will find you — accept the
  coffee.

## Day 1 — Riyadh: arrival and the Sky Bridge

- **Morning flight** in (~2 h from the Gulf). Land, grab an
  eSIM, Careem to Olaya — Riyadh's glass-tower spine.
- **13:00** — Lunch the local way: a mandi or mathbi
  house (fragrant rice + smoked chicken, SAR 30–45) or
  the food halls at Via Riyadh/Centria if you want
  polish.
- **16:30** — **Kingdom Centre Sky Bridge** (~SAR 65):
  the bottle-opener tower's 300-metre bridge, with the
  whole city grid going gold at sunset.
- **19:30** — **Boulevard City / Riyadh Season** (in
  season, roughly late Oct–Mar): the Gulf's biggest
  entertainment sprawl — fountains, concerts, restaurant
  rows. Entry tickets vary by zone (~SAR 30–100); it's
  Riyadh's living room in winter.

**Where to stay in Riyadh** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Ritz-Carlton Riyadh** — 4.6/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~1,100 reviews): a palace that happens to
  take bookings, gardens included. ~SAR 1,600+.
- *Mid-range:* **Hyatt Regency Riyadh Olaya** — 4.5/5
  (~790 reviews), walkable to Kingdom Centre.
  ~SAR 550–750.
- *Value:* **Holiday Inn Al Qasr** — 4.6/5 (~1,360
  reviews), dependable comfort at ~SAR 350–450.

## Day 2 — Old Riyadh and the reborn Diriyah

- **09:30** — **The National Museum** (Murabba): the
  Kingdom's story from meteorites to unification,
  genuinely world-class and near-free. Pair with
  **Masmak Fortress** (the 1902 raid that started the
  state; look for the spearhead in the door) and haggle
  practice in **Souq Al Zal** — carpets, oud,
  daggers.
- **13:00** — Najdi lunch at Al Mamlaka-style heritage
  restaurants (jareesh, kabsa; SAR 40–70).
- **16:00** — **Diriyah**: book the **Diriyah Pass
  (SAR 50–100, kids under 12 free — and the fee comes
  back as dining credit)** at diriyah.sa. Wander
  **At-Turaif**, the UNESCO mud-brick capital of the
  first Saudi state — lamplit ochre palaces, museums in
  the walls, the Wadi Hanifah palms below. Golden hour
  here is the photo of the Riyadh leg.
- **19:30** — Dinner on **Bujairi Terrace** facing the
  floodlit citadel — your pass money back on the table
  (SAR 150–250 pp at the good tables; booking ahead
  essential in season).

## Day 3 — The Edge of the World

- **08:00** — Full-day 4x4 to **Jabal Fihrayn — the
  Edge of the World** (group tours from ~SAR 160–200,
  small-group ~SAR 250–350, ~6 h): the Tuwaiq
  escarpment snaps off into a 300-metre void over an
  ancient seabed, camel caravan trails threading the
  plain below. You walk (carefully) to fins of rock
  jutting over nothing. No barriers, no ticket booth —
  just supervise kids like a hawk and wear grippy
  shoes. Access is by tour/4x4 only; saloon-car
  self-drives get turned back at the graded track.
- **16:30** — Recovery: hammam hour or the pool.
- **20:00** — Last Riyadh night: Souq Al Zal by lamplight
  or Boulevard round two. Pack for the desert.

## Day 4 — Fly to AlUla: old town and the Elephant

- **09:00** — **Riyadh → AlUla** (1 h 50). The airport
  is 30 min from town; hire a car at arrivals
  (~SAR 180–230/day — AlUla's sites sprawl and taxis
  are scarce; driving here is easy and the roads are
  cinematic).
- **13:00** — Check in; lunch in the **AlJadidah arts
  district** (SAR 40–80 — date-syrup everything).
- **16:00** — **AlUla Old Town** (free, guided walks
  optional): 900 mud-brick houses stacked against the
  oasis for eight centuries, abandoned in the 1980s,
  now half-restored into lantern-lit lanes of craft
  shops and cafés. Climb to Harrat viewpoint road for
  the oasis-in-a-canyon overview.
- **18:00** — **Elephant Rock (Jabal AlFil)** — free:
  a three-storey sandstone elephant standing in a
  natural amphitheatre of dunes, ringed by sunken
  fire-pit cafés. Sunset here, karak in hand, is
  AlUla's signature evening.

**Where to stay in AlUla** (three nights) — and the
honesty first: **AlUla hotels are the trip's shock**.
December–February nightly averages run four figures in
riyals; October/November and midweek dates cost half.

- *Luxury:* **Banyan Tree AlUla** — 4.9/5 (~470
  reviews), tented villas folded into the Ashar Valley
  rocks. SAR 3,500+.
- *Boutique splurge:* **Our Habitas AlUla** — 4.7/5
  (~570 reviews, the valley's #1 "Best Value"),
  infinity pool facing the canyon, e-bikes between
  villas. SAR 2,200+.
- *Value:* **Cloud7 Residence AlUla** — 4.3/5 (~50
  reviews), modern apartments on the town's edge from
  ~SAR 700–950 — or the town's guesthouse/apartment
  scene from ~SAR 400 midweek.

## Day 5 — Hegra: the other Petra

- **08:30** — **Hegra** (book the official tour at
  experiencealula.com — **from SAR 95 including entry,
  coach and a rawi storyteller-guide**, 2–3 h from
  Winter Park; vintage Land Rover and private versions
  cost more): 111 Nabataean tombs carved into golden
  outcrops across an open plain — the monumental
  **Tomb of Lihyan son of Kusa**, alone on its rock
  island, is the Kingdom's Treasury moment. You'll
  share it with dozens of people, not thousands. Morning
  light, fewer buses.
- **13:00** — Lunch back in town; siesta like a local
  (everything pauses 13:00–16:00 anyway).
- **16:00** — **Dadan & Jabal Ikmah** (**from SAR 60
  guided**): the cliff-tomb capital of the kingdoms
  that predate the Nabataeans, then the canyon dubbed
  Saudi's "open library" — hundreds of inscriptions in
  scripts up to 2,500 years old, UNESCO-listed in
  their own right.
- **19:30** — Dinner in Old Town's lanes (SAR 60–120
  pp) — try the AlUla speciality: dates every way,
  citrus from the oasis, saleeg under the lanterns.

## Day 6 — Canyon morning, stars at night

- **08:00** — Pick one: the **Harrat Uwayrid
  viewpoint** hike above the black lava plateau, a
  **hot-air balloon over Hegra at dawn** (the splurge:
  ~SAR 900–1,200 pp, winter mornings, books out), or
  simply the pool at the resort you're paying for.
- **13:00** — AlJadidah's cafés and design shops;
  buy dates by the box (Barni dates are the local
  pride, SAR 20–40/kg vs triple at the airport).
- **17:00** — Golden-hour drive through the **Ashar
  Valley** (camels, canyon walls, the mirrored Maraya
  concert hall — the world's largest mirrored
  building, startling in the rock).
- **20:00** — **Gharameel stargazing** (~SAR 200–300
  pp): 4x4s out past the light pollution to a plain of
  black rock pinnacles under a Milky Way the Gulf's
  cities forgot existed. Blankets, fires, dates, tea —
  the trip's quiet finale.

## Day 7 — Home from the desert

- **Winter magic:** the **flydubai direct AlUla →
  Dubai** (Sun/Tue/Thu, late Oct–late Mar) gets you
  home in ~2½ hours, no backtrack. Other dates: hop
  via Riyadh or Jeddah. Either way — window seat over
  the harrat lava fields.

## What to pack

- Layers: winter desert = 25°C days, **8°C nights** —
  the stargazing fleece is not optional.
- Modest daywear (linen trousers, sleeves), scarf in
  the daypack, one nicer outfit for Bujairi.
- Grippy closed shoes for the Edge (sand-polished
  rock + 300 m drops), sandals for town.
- Sunscreen, lip balm, refillable bottle — desert
  air drinks you quietly.
- Power bank for tour days; Saudi uses **Type G**
  (UK-style) sockets.
- Your driving licence (most are accepted for rentals
  here) + passport copies.

## FAQ

**Is it actually open and easy? Any minders or
restrictions?**
Open, easy, and eager. Tourists move freely — no
guides required, no chaperones, women travel and
drive solo, and the tourist eVisa's one-year validity
is built for repeat weekends. The genuine
restrictions: **Makkah is for Muslims only** (Madinah
city welcomes all visitors, with the Prophet's Mosque
core reserved for Muslims), alcohol is fully illegal,
and drones need permits. That's the list.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
The site's usual FAQ, inverted: **everything is halal
by law** — zero checking, ever. It's **vegetarians**
who need the plan: Najdi cuisine is meat-forward, so
lean on the enormous Indian restaurant scene (Riyadh
and even AlUla have plenty, courtesy of a huge desi
community), Lebanese mezze (fattoush, moutabal,
falafel), and hotel breakfasts. You will not go
hungry; you may eat a lot of paneer.

**Is Hegra really comparable to Petra?**
Different flavour, same civilisation. Petra is
bigger, carved into canyon walls, and heaving; Hegra
is tombs standing on an open desert plain, restored
obsessively, and **nearly empty** — you photograph
the Lonely Castle with nobody in frame. If Petra is
a stadium concert, Hegra is the soundcheck — same
band, front-row seat.

**When exactly should we go?**
**Late October to early March.** AlUla's events
season (Winter at Tantora music, festivals) and
Riyadh Season both run then, the flydubai direct
flies then, and daytime temps sit at 22–28°C. April
is the shoulder gamble; May–September is genuinely
punishing in AlUla.

**Can we combine this with Umrah?**
Yes — the tourist eVisa permits Umrah outside Hajj
season, and AlUla sits in Madinah province (the
holy city is ~3.5 h by road or a short hop, with
direct AlUla–Jeddah flights too). Plenty of visitors
run exactly that loop. Plan Makkah logistics
separately given entry rules for non-Muslim
companions.

**What's Saudi guide #2?**
**Jeddah + the Red Sea**: Al-Balad's coral-house
old town, the corniche, diving that rivals Egypt
without the crowds — and the door to Taif's rose
farms. On the list.
