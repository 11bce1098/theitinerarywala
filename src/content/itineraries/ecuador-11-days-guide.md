The Galápagos are the reason, and they deserve it. Six hundred miles
off the mainland, the animals evolved without land predators and
never developed a reason to run. A sea lion will share the dock with
you. A marine iguana will not move off the path. Blue-footed boobies
nest at the edge of a trail and continue nesting.

What makes Ecuador a good trip rather than a good excursion is that
it is small. **Quito**'s colonial centre is one of the best preserved
in the Americas and sits at **2,850 m**, in a valley with volcanoes
on both sides. **Mindo**'s cloud forest is two hours away and holds
several hundred bird species. You can have all three in eleven days
without a long internal flight beyond the islands.

Two decisions shape the cost and the trip: **cruise or island-hop**,
and **which islands you actually need**.

## Before you go: the fees, the two ways to do it, and altitude

### Entry, and the fees nobody mentions

- **Most nationalities enter Ecuador visa-free** for 90 days — the
  EU, UK, US, Canada, Australia and most of Latin America.
- **The Galápagos charge separately, twice.** There is a **transit
  control card** bought at the mainland airport before you fly, and a
  **national park entrance fee** paid on arrival in the islands. The
  park fee was increased substantially in 2024 and is now
  **USD 200 for most adult foreign visitors**, payable in cash.
- **Bring that in cash, in clean US dollars.** Card facilities at
  the island airports are unreliable and the queue does not care.
- Your bags are inspected for organic material on the way in. Do not
  carry seeds, fruit or plants.

### Cruise or island-hopping

This is the decision that shapes everything.

- **A cruise** sleeps aboard and sails overnight, which is the only
  way to reach the outer islands — **Genovesa**, **Española**,
  **Fernandina** — where the rarer species are. Four, five and eight
  night itineraries are standard, and they are expensive.
- **Island-hopping** bases you in towns on **Santa Cruz**, **San
  Cristóbal** and **Isabela**, with day boats to nearby sites. It
  costs a fraction of a cruise, you sleep on land, and you see a
  great deal — just not the outer islands.
- **Day boats are limited by distance**, and inter-island ferries
  are small, fast and rough. Take something if you are prone.
- **This itinerary assumes island-hopping**, because it is what most
  people can actually afford and it is genuinely good.

### The altitude, and the order

- **Quito sits at 2,850 m**, which is higher than most European ski
  resorts and enough to feel on arrival.
- **Give yourself the first day gently.** This guide starts in Quito
  rather than rushing to the islands, partly for that reason.
- **The Galápagos are at sea level**, so the islands are a rest from
  the altitude rather than an addition to it.

### Money and getting around

- **Ecuador uses the US dollar** as its own currency, which removes
  a whole category of problem. Bring small denominations; change for
  large notes is scarce.
- Cards work in Quito and larger island hotels; **carry cash** for
  boats, guides, fees and small restaurants.
- **Flights to the Galápagos** leave from Quito and Guayaquil and
  take around two hours. Book early.
- Spanish is essential outside tourism; English is common among
  island guides.

## Day 1 — Land in Quito

- **Afternoon** — Land, transfer into the city. Sleep in the
  **Centro Histórico** or **La Floresta**.
- **Evening** — Take it easy. You are at altitude and the first
  night matters.

## Day 2 — Old Quito

- **Morning** — The **Centro Histórico**: **La Compañía**, gilded
  floor to ceiling, and **San Francisco**, the oldest church in the
  city.
- **Afternoon** — The **TelefériQo** cable car to 4,000 m for the
  view along the valley, weather permitting.
- **Evening** — **La Ronda**, the restored colonial street, for
  dinner and canelazo.

## Day 3 — The Equator, then fly

- **Morning** — **Mitad del Mundo** and the neighbouring
  **Intiñan** museum, which has the more accurate line and the
  better demonstrations.
- **Afternoon** — Fly to **Baltra** or **San Cristóbal**, about two
  hours. Pay the park fee on arrival.
- **Evening** — Your first sea lions, which will be on the pavement.

## Day 4 — Santa Cruz

- **Morning** — The **Charles Darwin Research Station** and the
  giant tortoise breeding programme.
- **Afternoon** — **Tortuga Bay**, a 45-minute walk from town to a
  white beach with marine iguanas and sharks in the shallows.
- **Evening** — The fish market on the dock, where pelicans and sea
  lions queue with the customers.

## Day 5 — The highlands and a day boat

- **Morning** — **Santa Cruz highlands**: giant tortoises in the
  wild on farmland, and the lava tunnels.
- **Afternoon** — A day boat to **North Seymour** for frigatebirds
  and blue-footed boobies, or **Santa Fé** for snorkelling with sea
  lions.

## Day 6 — Ferry to Isabela

- **Morning** — The two-hour speedboat to **Isabela**, the largest
  and least developed of the inhabited islands.
- **Afternoon** — **Concha de Perla** for snorkelling straight off a
  boardwalk, with sea lions and turtles.
- **Evening** — The flamingo lagoon behind the town.

## Day 7 — Sierra Negra, and the tunnels

- **Morning** — **Sierra Negra volcano**, a walk to one of the
  largest calderas in the world, often in cloud and worth it anyway.
- **Afternoon** — **Los Túneles**, lava arches in shallow water,
  with white-tipped reef sharks, turtles and seahorses.

## Day 8 — Back to Santa Cruz, or San Cristóbal

- **Morning** — Ferry back.
- **Afternoon** — **Kicker Rock** off San Cristóbal if your route
  takes you there — an eroded tuff cone with a channel full of
  sharks and rays.
- **Evening** — Last island night.

## Day 9 — Fly to the mainland, on to Mindo

- **Morning** — Fly back to Quito, two hours.
- **Afternoon** — Drive to **Mindo**, two hours north-west and
  1,600 m lower, into cloud forest.
- **Evening** — Frogs, and rain on the roof.

## Day 10 — Birds, and the forest

- **Dawn** — **Birding** with a guide. Mindo and its valley hold
  several hundred species, and the hummingbird feeders are
  ridiculous.
- **Afternoon** — The **tarabita** cable car over the gorge and the
  waterfall trails, or a chocolate tour, since the cacao here is
  excellent.
- **Evening** — Back toward Quito.

## Day 11 — Fly home

- Quito's airport is an hour from the city, further in traffic.

## What to pack

- **Cash in US dollars for the park fee**, in clean notes.
- **Reef-safe sunscreen**, a rash vest and your own snorkel mask if
  you have one.
- **Motion sickness tablets** for the inter-island ferries, which
  are genuinely rough.
- **Layers for Quito**, which is cool at 2,850 m, and light
  clothing for the islands.
- **Waterproofs for Mindo**, which is a cloud forest in every month.
- **Type A/B plugs**, 120V, as in the US.

## FAQ

**Cruise or island-hopping?**
Island-hop if budget matters — you will see sea lions, iguanas,
tortoises, boobies, sharks and turtles, and sleep on land. Cruise if
you want Genovesa, Española and Fernandina, which day boats cannot
reach.

**Is eleven days enough?**
Yes, for this shape. Adding days is best spent on more islands
rather than more mainland — though Cotopaxi and Baños are both
excellent if you want the Andes properly.

**When should I go?**
**Two seasons, both good.** December to May is warmer with calmer
seas and better snorkelling visibility. June to November is cooler
and rougher, with more nutrients in the water and therefore more
marine activity — and it is when the albatross are on Española.

**Will the altitude in Quito be a problem?**
Mildly, for a day. Take the first afternoon slowly, drink water, and
do not plan anything strenuous before day two.
