The Galápagos are the reason, and they deserve it. Six hundred miles
off the mainland, the animals evolved without land predators and
never developed a reason to run. A sea lion will share the dock with
you. A marine iguana will not move off the path. Blue-footed boobies
nest at the edge of a trail and continue nesting.

What makes Ecuador a good trip rather than a good excursion is that
it is small. **Quito**'s colonial centre is one of the best preserved
in the Americas and sits at **2,850 m**, in a valley with volcanoes
on both sides. **Mindo**'s cloud forest is two hours away and holds
several hundred bird species. You can have all three in eleven days
without a long internal flight beyond the islands.

Two decisions shape the cost and the trip: **cruise or island-hop**,
and **which islands you actually need**.

## Before you go: the fees, the two ways to do it, and altitude

### Entry, and the fees nobody mentions

- **Most nationalities enter Ecuador visa-free** for 90 days — the
  EU, UK, US, Canada, Australia and most of Latin America.
- **The Galápagos charge separately, twice.** There is a **transit
  control card** bought at the mainland airport before you fly, and a
  **national park entrance fee** paid on arrival in the islands. The
  park fee was increased substantially in 2024 and is now
  **USD 200 for most adult foreign visitors**, payable in cash.
- **Bring that in cash, in clean US dollars.** Card facilities at
  the island airports are unreliable and the queue does not care.
- Your bags are inspected for organic material on the way in. Do not
  carry seeds, fruit or plants.

### Cruise or island-hopping

This is the decision that shapes everything.

- **A cruise** sleeps aboard and sails overnight, which is the only
  way to reach the outer islands — **Genovesa**, **Española**,
  **Fernandina** — where the rarer species are. Four, five and eight
  night itineraries are standard, and they are expensive.
- **Island-hopping** bases you in towns on **Santa Cruz**, **San
  Cristóbal** and **Isabela**, with day boats to nearby sites. It
  costs a fraction of a cruise, you sleep on land, and you see a
  great deal — just not the outer islands.
- **Day boats are limited by distance**, and inter-island ferries
  are small, fast and rough. Take something if you are prone.
- **This itinerary assumes island-hopping**, because it is what most
  people can actually afford and it is genuinely good.

### The altitude, and the order

- **Quito sits at 2,850 m**, which is higher than most European ski
  resorts and enough to feel on arrival.
- **Give yourself the first day gently.** This guide starts in Quito
  rather than rushing to the islands, partly for that reason.
- **The Galápagos are at sea level**, so the islands are a rest from
  the altitude rather than an addition to it.

### Money and getting around

- **Ecuador uses the US dollar** as its own currency, which removes
  a whole category of problem. Bring small denominations; change for
  large notes is scarce.
- Cards work in Quito and larger island hotels; **carry cash** for
  boats, guides, fees and small restaurants.
- **Flights to the Galápagos** leave from Quito and Guayaquil and
  take around two hours. Book early.
- Spanish is essential outside tourism; English is common among
  island guides.

## Day 1 — Land in Quito

- **14:00** — Land at **Mariscal Sucre (UIO)**, which is 40 km east
  of the city in the Tababela valley — an hour to the centre, more
  in traffic, and around USD 30 by taxi.
- **15:30** — Sleep in the **Centro Histórico** for the churches on
  your doorstep, or **La Floresta** for the restaurants. The airport
  sits at 2,400 m and the city at **2,850 m**, so you arrive higher
  than you landed.
- **17:00** — Walk a few blocks, slowly. Quito is higher than most
  people have ever slept and the first evening is not the time to
  climb anything.
- **18:30** — *Locro de papa* — potato and cheese soup with avocado
  — and a **canelazo**, hot cane spirit with cinnamon, which is the
  correct drink at this altitude on a cold evening.
- **20:00** — Early night. Ecuador uses US dollars, so there is no
  money to change; get small notes, because nobody has change for a
  twenty.

## Day 2 — Old Quito

- **09:00** — **La Compañía de Jesús**, gilded floor to ceiling in
  seven tonnes of gold leaf and the finest baroque interior in South
  America. Half an hour, and photography is not allowed.
- **10:30** — **San Francisco**, the oldest church in the city,
  begun in 1534 within weeks of the Spanish founding, and its
  cloister.
- **12:00** — **Plaza Grande** and the **Basílica del Voto
  Nacional** — climb the tower if the ladders and open walkways do
  not bother you, and note the gargoyles, which are Galápagos
  animals rather than monsters.
- **14:00** — Lunch on **Calle La Ronda**.
- **15:30** — The **TelefériQo** to **4,050 m** on Pichincha's
  flank. Go on a clear afternoon or not at all; the queue is long
  and the cloud wins often. The walk up toward Rucu Pichincha from
  the top station will wind you completely.
- **19:00** — **La Ronda** again after dark, when the restored
  colonial street fills up, for dinner and another canelazo.

## Day 3 — The Equator, then fly

- **08:30** — **Mitad del Mundo**, the monument on the line the
  French Geodesic Mission calculated in 1736. They were about 240 m
  out, which is remarkable for the instruments they had.
- **10:00** — The neighbouring **Intiñan museum** sits on the GPS
  line and does the demonstrations — the water swirl is a trick, the
  egg-on-a-nail is skill, and it is all enjoyable if you hold it
  lightly.
- **12:00** — To the airport.
- **14:00** — Fly to **Baltra** or **San Cristóbal**, about two
  hours. At check-in your bags are inspected for organic material
  and you buy the **INGALA transit card**; on landing you pay the
  **national park fee in cash**, which rose sharply in 2024. Budget
  for both and carry dollars.
- **16:30** — Land. Cross to **Santa Cruz** by bus, ferry and bus —
  about 90 minutes to **Puerto Ayora**.
- **18:30** — Your first sea lions. They will be on the pavement,
  on the benches, and occasionally on the boat you wanted.

## Day 4 — Santa Cruz

- **07:30** — **Charles Darwin Research Station** at opening, before
  the heat and the tour groups: the giant tortoise breeding
  programme, and the corral of Española tortoises whose species was
  brought back from fourteen individuals.
- **10:00** — Walk back through town and pick up snorkel gear if you
  did not bring your own.
- **11:30** — **Tortuga Bay**: a 45-minute walk on a paved path from
  town, and then two kilometres of white sand. The first beach has
  surf and marine iguanas; walk to the far end for **Playa Mansa**,
  the sheltered lagoon with turtles, rays and small sharks in
  water you can stand up in.
- **15:00** — Back, out of the midday sun.
- **17:30** — The **fish market** on the dock, where pelicans and a
  sea lion queue with the customers and the woman filleting does
  not look up. It is the best free show in the islands.
- **19:30** — Dinner on **Charles Binford**, the street that closes
  to become an open-air kitchen.

## Day 5 — The highlands and a day boat

- **07:00** — The **Santa Cruz highlands**, 20 minutes up and
  several degrees cooler: giant tortoises grazing wild on farmland
  at **El Chato**, entirely unbothered by you, and the **lava
  tunnels** you walk through beneath them.
- **10:30** — Back down, or straight to the dock.
- **11:00** — A **day boat**. **North Seymour** for frigatebirds
  inflating their red throat pouches and blue-footed boobies doing
  the foot-lifting dance; or **Santa Fé** for snorkelling with sea
  lions, which is the single best in-water hour available here.
- **16:30** — Back into Puerto Ayora, sunburnt.
- **19:00** — Dinner, and book the Isabela speedboat for the
  morning — they sell out.

## Day 6 — Ferry to Isabela

- **07:00** — The speedboat to **Isabela**, two hours. It is open
  ocean in a small boat: take the tablet if you are prone, sit at
  the back, and expect to be wet.
- **09:30** — **Puerto Villamil**, sand streets, no rush, and the
  least developed of the inhabited islands.
- **11:00** — **Concha de Perla**: a boardwalk through mangrove to
  a natural pool where you snorkel straight off the steps, with sea
  lions, turtles and penguins. Free, and twenty minutes from town.
- **14:00** — Lunch, then the **Centro de Crianza** tortoise centre
  on the edge of town.
- **16:30** — The **flamingo lagoon** behind the village — Ecuador's
  largest population, and quiet at this hour.
- **18:30** — Sunset from the beach bar with the swing seats, which
  is exactly as good as it sounds.

## Day 7 — Sierra Negra, and the tunnels

- **07:00** — **Sierra Negra volcano**: a 45-minute drive, then a
  16 km round walk to a caldera 10 km across — one of the largest
  in the world. It is often in cloud and worth doing anyway; the
  Volcán Chico extension crosses fumaroles and lava fields from the
  2018 eruption.
- **13:00** — Back to town, and lunch.
- **14:30** — **Los Túneles**, an hour by boat: collapsed lava
  arches in shallow clear water. You snorkel among white-tipped
  reef sharks sleeping under ledges, turtles, rays and seahorses,
  and walk over the arches between swims. The best excursion in the
  archipelago, and it does not run when the sea is up.
- **18:30** — Back. Last Isabela evening.

## Day 8 — Back to Santa Cruz, or San Cristóbal

- **06:00** — The speedboat back, two hours. Mornings are calmer
  than afternoons.
- **09:00** — If you are routing out via **San Cristóbal**, a
  second boat; otherwise a slow day in Puerto Ayora.
- **11:00** — **Kicker Rock (León Dormido)** off San Cristóbal: an
  eroded tuff cone split by a channel you swim through, with
  Galápagos sharks, hammerheads in season, and rays beneath you in
  very deep water. Not a beginner's snorkel.
- **15:00** — **La Lobería** or **Playa Mann** for the last sea
  lion colony.
- **18:30** — Last island night. Repack — your bags are inspected
  again on departure.

## Day 9 — Fly to the mainland, on to Mindo

- **09:00** — Fly back to Quito, two hours, and gain an hour: the
  islands run one hour behind the mainland.
- **13:00** — Drive to **Mindo**, two hours north-west and 1,600 m
  lower — over the pass and down into the **Chocó** cloud forest,
  which is a different climate and a different set of birds.
- **15:30** — Arrive. It is warm, wet and loud.
- **17:00** — The **hummingbird feeders** at a lodge café: a dozen
  species at arm's length, including the booted racket-tail, and
  they hold still for nobody.
- **19:30** — Dinner, then listen to the frogs. Rain on the roof is
  the normal soundtrack.

## Day 10 — Birds, and the forest

- **05:30** — **Birding with a guide**, which is the whole reason to
  be here. The Mindo valley holds over 500 species; a good guide
  with a scope finds toucans, tanagers and — if you have booked it
  — a **cock-of-the-rock lek** at dawn, where the males display in
  scarlet.
- **09:30** — Breakfast back at the lodge.
- **11:00** — The **tarabita**, a hand-hauled cable car over the
  gorge, then the waterfall trails — six falls, and you can swim in
  the **Nambillo**.
- **14:30** — A **chocolate tour** at one of the small producers:
  bean to bar in two hours, on cacao grown in the valley, and
  genuinely good.
- **17:00** — Drive back toward Quito, or stay and drive in the
  morning.

## Day 11 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Back to Quito if you stayed in Mindo — two hours,
  and add an hour for the airport on the far side of the city.
- **Allow three hours** at UIO. It is an hour from the centre in
  light traffic and considerably more at rush hour, and the road
  has one realistic alternative.
- **Spend your coins.** US currency is legal tender here, but the
  Ecuadorian *centavo* coins in your pocket are not, anywhere else.

## What to pack

- **Cash in US dollars for the park fee**, in clean notes.
- **Reef-safe sunscreen**, a rash vest and your own snorkel mask if
  you have one.
- **Motion sickness tablets** for the inter-island ferries, which
  are genuinely rough.
- **Layers for Quito**, which is cool at 2,850 m, and light
  clothing for the islands.
- **Waterproofs for Mindo**, which is a cloud forest in every month.
- **Type A/B plugs**, 120V, as in the US.

## FAQ

**Cruise or island-hopping?**
Island-hop if budget matters — you will see sea lions, iguanas,
tortoises, boobies, sharks and turtles, and sleep on land. Cruise if
you want Genovesa, Española and Fernandina, which day boats cannot
reach.

**Is eleven days enough?**
Yes, for this shape. Adding days is best spent on more islands
rather than more mainland — though Cotopaxi and Baños are both
excellent if you want the Andes properly.

**When should I go?**
**Two seasons, both good.** December to May is warmer with calmer
seas and better snorkelling visibility. June to November is cooler
and rougher, with more nutrients in the water and therefore more
marine activity — and it is when the albatross are on Española.

**Will the altitude in Quito be a problem?**
Mildly, for a day. Take the first afternoon slowly, drink water, and
do not plan anything strenuous before day two.
