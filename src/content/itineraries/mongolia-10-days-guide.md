Mongolia has three and a half million people in a country the size
of western Europe, and half of them live in the capital. What is
left is the least densely populated place on earth: steppe to the
horizon in every direction, herders moving with the seasons, and
almost no fences.

The trip is mostly driving, and that needs saying at the start.
**There are few paved roads outside the main corridors.** You travel
in a Russian van or a 4x4 across tracks that the driver knows and no
map shows, for six or eight hours a day, and that is not a failure
of planning — it is the country.

What you get for it: the **Gobi**, which is not sand but gravel
plain with dunes at one end and red cliffs at another; the **Orkhon
Valley**, the seat of the Mongol and earlier empires; and nights in
**gers** with families who have been doing this for a very long time.

## Before you go: the season, the driving and the ger

### Entry

- **Many nationalities enter visa-free** for 30 days — the US, most
  of the EU, Canada, Japan, Korea and others — and Mongolia has been
  expanding the list. Others need an **eVisa**, which is
  straightforward.
- Check your own case; this list has changed repeatedly in recent
  years.
- Passport valid six months.

### The season is short and the shoulders are harsh

- **June to early September** is the season, and **July and August**
  are the reliable months: warm days, cold nights, everything open.
- **Naadam**, the national festival of wrestling, archery and horse
  racing, falls in **mid-July** and is worth building a trip around —
  but book everything far ahead if you do.
- **Winter is genuinely extreme.** Ulaanbaatar is the coldest
  capital city in the world and the countryside reaches −40°C. The
  Golden Eagle Festival in the far west is in October and is a
  different, harder trip.
- **Even in July, nights on the steppe are cold.** The ger stove is
  lit at dusk and goes out by three in the morning.

### The driving is the itinerary

- **You hire a driver and a vehicle**, almost always with a cook or
  guide, arranged through a Ulaanbaatar operator. Self-drive is
  possible and inadvisable without serious experience.
- **Six to eight hours a day of off-road driving** is normal on a
  Gobi loop. Distances look modest and take all day.
- **A Russian UAZ van** is the classic choice — slow, agricultural
  and repairable anywhere. A Land Cruiser is faster and more
  comfortable.
- **There are no fuel stations for long stretches.** Your driver
  will plan for this; trust them.
- **Domestic flights** to Dalanzadgad cut the Gobi drive
  substantially and are worth considering if your time is short.

### Ger camps, and staying with families

- **Tourist ger camps** have beds, a restaurant and shared shower
  blocks. **Family stays** have none of that and are the better
  experience.
- **There is no plumbing** in most of the countryside. Long-drop
  toilets, a basin, and a shower every few days at best.
- **Ger etiquette matters**: enter on the left, move clockwise, do
  not step on the threshold, accept what you are offered with your
  right hand, and never turn down the first bowl of tea.
- **Airag** — fermented mare's milk — will be offered. Take a sip at
  least; it is a courtesy, and it is sour rather than alcoholic in
  any serious sense.

### Money and practicalities

- **The tugrik (MNT)**, and the numbers are very large. Cards work
  in Ulaanbaatar and nowhere else on this route.
- **Draw all your cash in Ulaanbaatar.** There are no ATMs in the
  countryside.
- **No mobile signal for long stretches.** Tell people at home.
- Cyrillic script, Mongolian language; English is common among
  guides and rare otherwise.

## Day 1 — Land in Ulaanbaatar

- **Afternoon** — Land, drop bags. The capital is a Soviet grid with
  glass towers dropped into it and ger districts on the hills around.
- **Evening** — **Sükhbaatar Square**, then dinner. Confirm your
  tour, your driver and your cash.

## Day 2 — Ulaanbaatar, and south

- **Morning** — **Gandan Monastery**, the working Buddhist centre
  that survived the purges, and the **National Museum**, which is
  genuinely excellent on the empire.
- **Afternoon** — Drive south toward the Gobi. The city ends
  abruptly and then there is nothing.
- **Evening** — First ger camp.

## Day 3 — Into the Gobi

- **All day** — Driving, with stops. The **Baga Gazriin Chuluu**
  granite formations or **Tsagaan Suvarga**'s eroded cliffs,
  depending on your route.
- **Evening** — Steppe sunset, which takes an hour and covers the
  whole sky.

## Day 4 — Yolyn Am, the ice gorge

- **Morning** — **Yolyn Am**, a narrow gorge in the Gobi Gurvan
  Saikhan mountains that holds ice into July — a glacier in a desert.
- **Afternoon** — The walk up the gorge, with pikas and lammergeiers.
- **Evening** — Ger camp near Dalanzadgad.

## Day 5 — Khongoryn Els, the singing dunes

- **Afternoon** — **Khongoryn Els**: dunes up to 300 m high running
  for 100 km, which make a low booming sound as the sand shifts.
- **Evening** — Climb one for sunset. It is much harder than it
  looks and worth every step.
- **Optional** — Camel ride, which here is a two-humped Bactrian and
  a working animal.

## Day 6 — The Flaming Cliffs

- **Morning** — **Bayanzag**, the red sandstone cliffs where Roy
  Chapman Andrews found the first dinosaur eggs known to science in
  1923. Still producing fossils.
- **Afternoon** — North out of the desert.
- **Evening** — Ger camp on the steppe.

## Day 7 — North to the Orkhon Valley

- **All day** — The long drive north into greener country, with
  herds and gers increasingly visible.
- **Evening** — The **Orkhon Valley**, a UNESCO landscape and the
  heart of successive steppe empires.

## Day 8 — The valley

- **Morning** — **Orkhon waterfall**, and a horse ride, which is the
  correct way to cover ground here.
- **Afternoon** — A family ger visit: milking, felt-making, and
  however much of the day they want to give you.
- **Evening** — Stay with the family if your operator arranges it.

## Day 9 — Kharkhorin, and back

- **Morning** — **Kharkhorin**, the site of Karakorum — Genghis
  Khan's capital — and **Erdene Zuu**, the monastery built from its
  stones and ringed by 108 stupas.
- **Afternoon** — Drive back to Ulaanbaatar, around six hours on
  mostly paved road.
- **Evening** — A shower, and a restaurant.

## Day 10 — Fly home

- The airport is an hour south of the city on a good road.

## What to pack

- **A proper warm layer and a hat**, even in July. Steppe nights
  drop close to freezing and the ger stove goes out.
- **Wet wipes and dry shampoo.** Showers are occasional.
- **A head torch.** No electricity in most family gers.
- **All your cash, in tugrik, drawn in Ulaanbaatar.**
- **Sun protection** — high, dry, and no shade anywhere.
- **Dust protection for cameras**, which is not optional on Gobi
  tracks.
- **Type C/E plugs**, 220V, where there is power at all.

## FAQ

**Is it really all driving?**
A lot of it, yes — six to eight hours on many days. The landscape is
the compensation and it does not repeat. Flying to Dalanzadgad cuts
the Gobi section if that is too much.

**Can I do it independently?**
Not easily. There is no intercity bus network to the places in this
itinerary and few roads. Almost everyone hires a vehicle with a
driver, alone or in a group.

**How basic are the ger stays?**
Tourist camps have beds and shared showers. Family stays have a
felt tent, a stove, a long-drop and great hospitality. Both are
worth doing; neither is a hotel.

**When should I go?**
**July and August.** Naadam in mid-July is the national festival
and worth planning around, if you book well ahead.
