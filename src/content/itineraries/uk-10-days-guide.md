The United Kingdom packs two capitals of completely different
temperament into one four-hour train ride. London is the layered
giant — Roman walls under glass towers, a working monarchy, the
world's best free museums and its most bookable wizard warehouse.
Edinburgh is the theatrical one: a castle on a volcanic crag, a
medieval mile sloping down to a royal palace, and the Highlands
starting practically at the last bus stop.

This guide runs the classic 6+3: London deep enough to breathe,
Edinburgh long enough for a castle day *and* a Highlands day. No
car, no internal flight — one famous railway line up the east
coast.

**Go May–June or September** for the odds (there are no
guarantees — see the weather box). July–August is peak-price and
fine; **August in Edinburgh is the Fringe** — the world's biggest
arts festival, electric and triple-priced, book five months out
or aim elsewhere. December is lights, markets and 3:45pm
sunsets. All costs are in pounds sterling (GBP).

## Before you go: the admin

### The ETA or the visa

- **Almost every visitor now needs one or the other, so check
  first.** Nationals of the EU, the US, Canada, Australia, Japan,
  the Gulf states and many others travel on an **Electronic
  Travel Authorisation (~£16)**, applied for online and usually
  approved within days. Everyone else — India included — needs
  the **UK Standard Visitor visa**: **~£127** for six months
  multiple entry, with 2, 5 and 10-year versions costing more.
  The difference between the two routes is £16 and a few days
  against £127 and a month, so confirm which applies to your
  passport at gov.uk before you book anything.
- **If it's the visa:** apply online at gov.uk, then give
  biometrics at a VFS Global centre in the country you are
  applying from. **Processing runs ~3 weeks** from biometrics,
  with priority (~5 working days) and super-priority (next day)
  available for meaningful extra fees. Decisions arrive as a
  **digital eVisa** — check your status online before flying;
  there's no sticker to admire.
- The file that works: 3–6 months of bank statements, employment
  and salary evidence, itinerary and refundable bookings. Apply
  4–6 weeks before travel, earlier in summer.
- Either way, the tiers below work identically.

### Money and the service-charge decoder

- Contactless everywhere, including every bus and Tube gate —
  cash is nearly ceremonial (keep £20–40 for markets).
- **Tipping, decoded:** most London restaurants add a **12.5%
  "service charge" to the bill automatically** — that *is* the
  tip; add nothing on top. Where it's not added, 10–12% is
  generous. Nobody tips at pubs or for pints.
- Prices include VAT — the number on the menu is the number you
  pay (a relief after our USA guides).
- **The free-museum trick** that resets a London budget: the
  British Museum, National Gallery, both Tates, Natural History,
  Science Museum and the V&A — all **free**, all world-class.
  London's paid icons are pricey; its £0 tier is unmatched
  anywhere.

### Weather honesty, transport, kids

- **It rains whenever it likes.** Any month, any forecast:
  carry the packable rain layer daily and plan one indoor swap
  per day (see: free museums). Four seasons before lunch is a
  lifestyle, not a saying.
- **London transport:** tap the same contactless card or phone
  on everything — Tube, buses, Elizabeth line — and it
  **auto-caps around £9/day (zones 1–2)**. From Heathrow, the
  Elizabeth line (~£13–14, 35 min) beats the Heathrow Express
  on price and the Piccadilly on time.
- **Intercity trains price like airlines:** LNER
  London–Edinburgh **Advance singles from ~£35–60** appear
  ~12 weeks out and climb steeply — book the day plans fix.
- **Kids:** under-11s ride London transport free with an
  adult, under-5s enter almost everything free, and between
  the Crown Jewels, the wizard warehouse and a castle with a
  daily cannon, this may be the easiest kids' trip on the
  site.

## Day 1 — Land in London, Westminster at golden hour

Stay around **Covent Garden/Strand** (walk everywhere, theatres
at the door) or **South Bank/Westminster** (the view side).

- **Afternoon** — Land LHR, which takes non-stops from
  practically anywhere.
  **Elizabeth line** into town (~£13–14, 35 min), check in.
- **17:30** — The single best first walk in Europe:
  **Westminster Bridge → Big Ben and Parliament → Whitehall →
  Trafalgar Square**, then back to the river as the lamps come
  on along the **South Bank**.
- **20:00** — First dinner: a proper pub — pie and mash,
  fish and chips (~£18–26) — or straight to Dishoom's Covent
  Garden queue (yes, it earns the hype; more on Day 4).

**Where to stay in London** (six nights)

- *Boutique-luxury:* **The Resident Covent Garden** — 4.9/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~2,900 reviews), kitchenette-smart rooms two
  streets from the piazza. ~£280–380.
- *Mid-range:* **Strand Palace** — 4.6/5, the 1909 grande dame
  opposite the Savoy at a quarter of its bill. ~£180–250.
- *Value:* **Premier Inn London County Hall** — 4.4/5, inside
  the historic hall beside the London Eye; families' favourite
  for a reason. ~£120–180.

## Day 2 — Royal day: the Abbey, the Guard, a show

- **09:15** — **Westminster Abbey** at opening (**£31 adult /
  £14 child**, superb multimedia guide included; **closed
  Sundays** to tourists): coronations since 1066, Newton and
  Darwin underfoot, Poets' Corner. Two hours.
- **11:00** — Through **St James's Park** (pelicans, palace
  views) for the **Changing of the Guard** at Buckingham
  Palace — held at 11:00 on most days in a rotating schedule
  (bands, bearskins, 45 min; check the Household Division
  calendar and claim the Victoria Memorial steps by 10:30).
- **13:30** — Lunch around St James's or a picnic in the park
  (~£10–18).
- **15:00** — **The National Gallery** (free): Van Gogh's
  Sunflowers, Turner, da Vinci — 90 minutes of highlights —
  then Covent Garden's street-performer hour.
- **19:30** — **West End theatre night**: the TKTS booth on
  Leicester Square sells same-day seats at 25–50% off (from
  ~£25–45); The Lion King and Les Mis forgive every pound.

## Day 3 — The Tower, the Bridge, the dome

- **09:00** — **Tower of London** at opening (**£37 adult /
  £18.50 child**, online): straight to the **Crown Jewels**
  before the queue forms, then a **Yeoman Warder tour**
  (included, deadpan-hilarious, every 30 min) — a thousand
  years of executions, ravens and armour. Three hours.
- **12:30** — Walk over **Tower Bridge** (the walkway views
  are the free version), then Thames path to **Borough
  Market** for lunch — toastie legends, curry stalls, salted
  caramel doughnuts (~£8–15).
- **15:00** — **St Paul's Cathedral** (~£26 online, closed
  Sun to tourists): Wren's dome, the Whispering Gallery
  climb, city views from the Golden Gallery — or the free
  alternative: **Sky Garden** (book the free slot ~3 weeks
  out).
- **19:30** — **Brick Lane** for the East End curry-and-
  bagel evening, or Shoreditch's street-art-and-supper
  version of the same postcode (~£20–30).

## Day 4 — The wizard warehouse (or the museum mile)

- **08:00** — **Warner Bros. Studio Tour — The Making of
  Harry Potter**: adult tickets **from ~£53** and they
  genuinely **sell out weeks (school-holiday months) ahead** —
  this is the trip's book-first ticket. Getting there:
  Euston → Watford Junction (~20 min) + the liveried shuttle.
  Inside: the real Great Hall, Diagon Alley, butterbeer,
  3–4 hours of the actual sets. Under-4s free.
- **14:30** — Back in town: the **British Museum** (free) —
  Rosetta Stone, the mummies, the Parthenon marbles — 90
  minutes of the greatest hits.
- **17:00** — **Oxford and Regent Street** sweep (or its
  antidote: Daunt Books and Marylebone), Carnaby for the
  photogenic version.
- **19:30** — **Chinatown** dinner and Soho wander (~£18–30).
- *Non-Potter households:* swap the morning for South
  Kensington's twin free giants — the **Natural History
  Museum** (the blue whale hall) and the **V&A** — with
  Harrods food halls between them.

## Day 5 — A castle out of town: Windsor (or the Stonehenge day)

- **09:00** — Paddington → **Windsor** (~35 min): **Windsor
  Castle** (**~£30–33**, book the slot) — the oldest occupied
  castle on earth: St George's Chapel (the late Queen's
  resting place), the State Apartments, the dolls' house.
  Eton's high street across the bridge for lunch.
- **15:30** — Back to London for the afternoon you haven't
  had yet: **Notting Hill's** pastel mews and Portobello
  Road, tea-and-scones somewhere unapologetic (~£15–30).
- **19:30** — Thames-side dinner on the South Bank; the Eye
  by night if you're riding it (**~£30–40 online** — views
  are best at dusk slots).
- *The alternative day:* coach tours run **Stonehenge + Bath**
  (~£95–110 with entries, 11 h) — the 5,000-year-old circle
  plus Georgian Bath's Roman spa. Superb, long; families with
  under-8s usually prefer Windsor.

## Day 6 — Village London and the meridian

- **09:30** — **Camden Market** as it opens: food stalls
  firing up, canal-side seats — then walk the towpath to
  **Primrose Hill** for the skyline framed by park.
- **13:00** — **Greenwich** by river: ride the **Uber Boat**
  downstream (tap contactless, ~£9–12 — London's best
  commute): the **Cutty Sark**, the market, and the hill up
  to the **Royal Observatory** to stand on the **Prime
  Meridian** (~£20, or straddle the free line outside the
  gate; the view is £0).
- **17:30** — Back by boat at golden hour — the Tower and
  Bridge from the water is the shot.
- **20:00** — Farewell-London supper: Dishoom (the breakfast
  naan argument can now be settled at dinner), a chophouse,
  or the pub you've been eyeing since Day 1. Pack — the north
  calls at nine.

## Day 7 — The East Coast line to Edinburgh

- **08:30** — **King's Cross**: photograph the **Platform
  9¾** trolley (the queue moves fast at this hour), then the
  09:00 **LNER Azuma** north — **4h20, Advance singles from
  ~£35–60**. Sit left for the sea: after York and Newcastle
  the line runs along cliffs into Scotland, and Berwick's
  bridges are the overture.
- **13:30** — Arrive **Waverley** — the only station named
  after a novel — and walk up into the **Old Town**.
- **15:00** — First **Royal Mile** descent: St Giles'
  cathedral's crown spire, the closes (the alley-veins off
  the Mile — duck into every one), the outside of
  Holyroodhouse at the bottom.
- **18:00** — **Victoria Street's** curved candy-coloured
  shops (a Diagon Alley claimant with receipts) down into
  the **Grassmarket** under the castle rock for dinner —
  haggis-neeps-tatties for the brave, superb curry houses
  for the homesick (~£18–30).

**Where to stay in Edinburgh** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Balmoral** — 4.6/5 (~5,800 reviews), the
  clock-tower landmark over Waverley (its clock runs 3 min
  fast so no Scot misses a train). ~£350+.
- *Mid-range:* **Market Street Hotel** — 4.7/5, Old Town
  ramparts location, skyline breakfasts. ~£180–250.
- *Value:* **Premier Inn Edinburgh City Centre (Princes
  Street)** — 4.4/5 (~4,400 reviews), castle views from the
  lucky rooms at chain prices. ~£90–150.
- **August warning:** Fringe month triples rates and sells
  the city out — book by March or come in September.

## Day 8 — Castle, closes and the little mountain

- **09:30** — **Edinburgh Castle** (**~£22 online, timed
  entry — book ahead in season**): the Scottish Crown
  Jewels and the Stone of Destiny, St Margaret's 12th-century
  chapel, the war memorial, and the **One O'Clock Gun** if
  you time it. Two-and-a-half hours on the crag.
- **13:00** — Lunch off the Mile (Makars Gourmet Mash for
  the theme, ~£12–18), then **The Real Mary King's Close**
  (~£25 — the buried 17th-century street under the Royal
  Exchange; book) or the free **National Museum of
  Scotland** rooftop terrace.
- **16:00** — **Arthur's Seat**: the extinct volcano in the
  city's back garden — 45–60 minutes up the easy spine from
  Holyrood, the whole city, firth and bridges below. (Calton
  Hill is the 10-minute version with 80% of the view.)
- **20:00** — Old Town evening: a **ghost tour** among the
  vaults (~£20–35) or a **whisky tasting** flight
  (~£25–40) — Edinburgh does darkness and drams equally
  well.

## Day 9 — The Highlands in a day

- **07:45** — The big one: **Loch Ness, Glencoe & the
  Highlands day tour** — **£65 adult (~£63 child), 12.5
  hours** with a storyteller-driver: Glencoe's brooding
  valley (the Skyfall country), Ben Nevis views, the full
  length of **Loch Ness** (Urquhart Castle + monster-water
  cruise ~£20 extra and worth it), Cairngorms edges, and the
  contractually-guaranteed **hairy coos**.
- **20:30** — Back in Edinburgh: late fish supper or the
  hotel bar's dram — you've crossed half of Scotland today.
- *DIY alternative:* ScotRail to **North Berwick** or
  **Stirling Castle** for a gentler £15 day — but for
  Highlands-in-a-day, the tour genuinely beats the maths of
  doing it yourself.

## Day 10 — Home from Edinburgh

- **Morning** — If the flight allows: **Calton Hill** at
  sunrise (10 minutes up, the postcard of the Mile,
  Balmoral clock and Arthur's Seat) or the **Dean Village**
  photo stop. Tram to EDI (~£7.50, 35 min); direct Gulf
  flights home. Open-jaw (into Heathrow, out of Edinburgh)
  closes the line without backtracking.

## What to pack

- The packable waterproof — daily carry, non-negotiable, in
  every month including July.
- Layers over a coat: 18°C-and-sunny to 11°C-and-sideways
  can share a single afternoon.
- Comfortable waterproof-ish shoes: London is 18,000 steps a
  day; Edinburgh adds cobbles at a gradient.
- One smart-casual outfit (theatre night, nicer dinners) —
  jeans-fine, gym-wear-not.
- A contactless card set up for tap (it *is* your transport
  ticket), UK Type G adapter.
- Sunglasses. Yes, seriously — and when the sun appears,
  join the locals in the park immediately; it's the law.

## FAQ

**How strict is the UK visa, really?**
Documented and predictable more than harsh: the file that
sails through shows stable UAE employment, 3–6 months of
statements that support the trip's cost, and a coherent
itinerary. Refusals cluster around unexplained deposits and
vague plans. Apply 4–6 weeks out (longer before summer), and
remember the decision is a digital eVisa — verify it online
before you fly.

**Is the London Pass worth buying?**
Usually not for this itinerary: the free-museum tier carries
two of the six days, and the paid icons here total ~£150 pp —
about what the pass costs before you've sprinted to justify
it. Buy attractions à la carte, online, in advance (they're
£3–5 cheaper than gate everywhere).

**When exactly — and what about the Fringe?**
May–June and September are the sweet spots: longest light,
kindest odds. **August is the Edinburgh Fringe** — 3,000
shows a day, the world's greatest cultural pile-on, and
hotel rates to match; go *for* it deliberately (book by
March) or avoid the month entirely. December's markets and
lights are magical and dark by 16:00.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
London is arguably the easiest halal capital in the West —
Edgware Road, Whitechapel and Brick Lane anchor it, Dishoom
does halal chicken and famous veg dishes, and every high
street has options; Edinburgh keeps solid halal curry houses
and Turkish grills around the Old Town. Vegetarians coast
everywhere — even pubs now default to a veggie pie. The one
trap: traditional fish-and-chip shops fry in shared oil —
ask.

**Should we add Paris by Eurostar?**
Tempting — 2h16 from St Pancras — but it needs a **Schengen
visa** on top of the UK one (see our
[France guide](/itineraries/france-9-days-guide/)), and a day-tripped
Paris shortchanges both cities. Do it as its own trip; the
train will still be there.

**What's next for the UK?**
Two pull hardest: the **Cotswolds + Bath + Oxford** honeyed-
village England (gentle, no-car-able), and **Skye + the
Highlands proper** (the dramatic one, self-drive). One of
them is guide #2.
