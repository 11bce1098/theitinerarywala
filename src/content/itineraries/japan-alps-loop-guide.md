Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka is Japan's front door. This trip is the country
behind it: Kanazawa, the samurai city the bombs never reached;
Shirakawa-go's steep-roofed farmhouses under the mountains;
Takayama, an Edo merchant town that still smells of cedar and sake;
and Matsumoto, home to Japan's most beautiful surviving original
castle. It runs as one clean loop from Tokyo — shinkansen out,
mountain buses across, an alpine express back — with no flights and
no backtracking.

If Japan is new to you, do our 10-day Golden Route first. This is
the second helping — slower, cheaper, and with a fraction of the
crowds.

All costs are in Japanese yen (JPY), with conversions calculated at
today's exchange rate. Opening hours are approximate — treat
anything marked ~ as a guide, not a contract.

## Before you go: the short version of the admin

Visas, money, Suica and eSIMs are identical to our first Japan
guide, where they're covered in depth. The route-specific
essentials:

- **Visas:** ~70 passports visa-free (EU, UK, USA, UAE and more);
  Japan's eVISA covers all nationalities resident in the UAE and
  everyone resident in India, via accredited agencies. Fees rose in
  July 2026 (~¥15,000 single-entry); start 3–4 weeks out.
- **No JR Pass needed** — this loop's transport bought
  point-to-point totals ~¥35,000 per person, far under the ¥50,000
  pass.
- **Reserve the mountain buses.** The Kanazawa → Shirakawa-go →
  Takayama highway buses are all-reserved seating and **sell out
  on autumn-foliage and winter weekends** — book on Japan Bus
  Online / Nohi Bus as soon as dates are fixed.
- **Suica note:** IC cards cover the cities, but the mountain
  buses and some rural lines are ticket- or cash-based — keep
  ¥15,000+ in cash on this route, more than you'd need in Tokyo.
- **Luggage:** the takkyubin trick again — forward big bags
  Tokyo → Takayama (or straight to your last Tokyo hotel) and
  travel the loop with an overnight bag. Buses have small holds;
  everyone on them is grateful they packed light.

### Travelling with kids?

- An easy route with children: short legs, castle to climb,
  markets to graze, deer-free.
- The one long sit is the Takayama–Matsumoto bus (~2.5 h) — time
  it with naps.
- Matsumoto Castle's steep ladder-stairs are a genuine six-storey
  climb: fine for school-age kids, a carrier job for toddlers.

## Day 1 — Land in Tokyo

- **15:00** — Check in near a Yamanote line station (Shinjuku or
  Tokyo Station side is ideal for tomorrow's Shinkansen). Set up
  mobile Suica if you haven't.
- **17:00** — Keep it light: an evening in Shinjuku or wherever
  your first trip left gaps — this loop returns you here for two
  nights at the end.
- **19:00** — Dinner and early night; tomorrow the trip actually
  starts.

**Where to stay in Tokyo** (bookends: 1 night now, 2 at the end)

- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Gracery Shinjuku** — central, reliable,
  under the Godzilla head.
- *Budget:* **APA Hotel Shinjuku** and siblings — small, spotless,
  cheap.

## Day 2 — Hokuriku Shinkansen to Kanazawa

- **9:00** — **Hokuriku Shinkansen** from Tokyo Station (~2.5 h,
  ~¥14,000 reserved) — through the mountains of Nagano and along
  the Sea of Japan.
- **12:00** — Arrive under Kanazawa Station's great wooden
  Tsuzumi-mon drum gate; check in, lunch near the station.
- **14:00** — **Higashi Chaya district** — the largest of
  Kanazawa's preserved geisha teahouse quarters: latticed wooden
  facades, craft shops, and the city's signature **gold-leaf ice
  cream** (Kanazawa makes 99% of Japan's gold leaf; a whole sheet
  goes on the cone, ~¥1,000).
- **16:30** — Walk the Asano River bank to Kazue-machi, the
  quieter riverside teahouse row.
- **19:00** — Dinner in **Katamachi**, the local nightlife grid —
  Kanazawa izakaya run on Sea of Japan seafood and jibuni duck
  stew.

**Where to stay in Kanazawa** (two nights)

- *Upscale:* **Hyatt Centric Kanazawa** — modern, by the station,
  rooftop bar.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Nikko Kanazawa** — the classic tower at the
  station; top-floor bar, big breakfasts.
- *Budget:* **Via Inn Kanazawa** — clean and soundproofed, inside
  the station complex.

## Day 3 — Kanazawa: garden, castle and samurai lanes

- **7:30** — **Kenroku-en** (~7:00–18:00 Mar–mid-Oct, from 8:00 in
  winter; ~¥320) — one of Japan's three great gardens, and at
  opening hour you share it with herons and gardeners, not tour
  groups. The two-legged Kotoji lantern is the postcard.
- **9:30** — **Kanazawa Castle Park** next door (grounds free;
  restored halls ~¥320) — the rebuilt turrets show off the
  castle's odd, beautiful lead-white roof tiles.
- **11:30** — **Omicho Market** (~9:00–17:00ish, many stalls
  closed Sun/Wed) — 300 years old and still the city's kitchen:
  the move is a **kaisen-don** seafood bowl lunch (~¥2,000–3,500).
- **13:30** — **21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art** (closed
  Mon; ~10:00–18:00; grounds free, exhibitions ~¥450+) — home of
  the famous "swimming pool" you can stand inside.
- **15:30** — **Nagamachi samurai district**: earthen walls,
  canals, and the **Nomura-ke** samurai house (~¥550) with its
  tiny perfect garden.
- **18:30** — Dinner: Kanazawa's conveyor sushi is famously the
  country's best of the genre — the station-building branches of
  the local chains (Morimori, Kurothe) run ¥2,000–3,500 and queue
  fast; put your name in early.

## Day 4 — Shirakawa-go's thatched village, sleep in Takayama

- **8:00** — Breakfast at Omicho, bags collected.
- **9:30** — Reserved highway bus **Kanazawa → Shirakawa-go**
  (~75 min, ¥2,800 — book ahead).
- **11:00** — **Ogimachi village, Shirakawa-go** (UNESCO): 100+
  gassho-zukuri farmhouses, their steep thatched roofs built like
  praying hands against the snow. Shuttle or 15-min walk up to the
  **Shiroyama observation deck** (shuttle ¥200 cash) for the
  postcard over the whole valley, then inside **Wada House**
  (~9:00–17:00, ¥400) to see the silk-attic engineering.
- **13:00** — Lunch in the village — hoba miso (miso grilled on a
  magnolia leaf) with mountain vegetables.
- **15:30** — Reserved bus onward **Shirakawa-go → Takayama**
  (~50 min, ¥2,800).
- **17:00** — Check into your Takayama ryokan; soak first, then
  dinner in — **Ryokan Tanabe**'s Hida-beef kaiseki is the reason
  to book it.

**Where to stay in Takayama** (two nights)

- *Splurge:* **Ryokan Tanabe** — central, warmly run, famed for
  its Hida beef dinners and afternoon sake.
- *Mid-range:* **Mercure Hida Takayama** — onsen baths, big
  breakfast, mountain views.
- *Budget:* **Tokyu Stay Hida-Takayama Musubi no Yu** — by the
  station, with public baths and in-room laundry.

## Day 5 — Takayama: markets, sake and old-town wandering

- **7:30** — **Miyagawa Morning Market** (~7:00–12:00, from 8:00
  in winter) along the river: pickles, miso, apples, crafts, and
  breakfast on a stick.
- **9:00** — **Sanmachi Suji** — three lanes of dark-timber Edo
  merchant houses. Do the **sake brewery crawl** (six breweries,
  cedar-ball signs over the doors; tastings ¥200–500) and the
  **Hida beef sushi stands** — seared wagyu nigiri on a rice
  cracker plate, ¥900–1,500, worth every queue minute.
- **12:00** — **Takayama Jinya** (~8:45–17:00, ~¥440) — the last
  surviving Edo-era provincial government house, tatami rooms and
  a rice-tax storehouse.
- **14:00** — Pick one: **Hida no Sato** open-air village
  (~8:30–17:00, ¥700) — more gassho houses in a hillside setting,
  quieter than Shirakawa-go — or the **Higashiyama walking
  course** through temple-lined lanes.
- **18:30** — Hida beef, round two: teppan or sukiyaki in town
  (~¥4,000–7,000/person) — or your ryokan's second-night menu.

## Day 6 — Over the mountains to Matsumoto

- **9:40** — Highway bus **Takayama → Matsumoto** (~2.5 h, ~¥3,500,
  reservation advised) — up through the Hida mountains past
  Kamikochi's peaks; in clear weather this is the most scenic
  public-transport ride in central Japan.
- **12:30** — Arrive Matsumoto; check in, soba lunch (Nagano's
  signature — the station area alleys do it well, ~¥1,000).
- **14:00** — **Matsumoto Castle** (~8:30–17:00, last entry 16:30;
  ¥700) — the "Crow Castle": black, moated, and one of only twelve
  original-keep castles left in Japan. Climb the six storeys of
  ship-steep stairs; the top floor's mountain view is the reward.
- **16:30** — **Nakamachi Street**'s white-walled storehouses and
  **Nawate "Frog" Street**'s riverside stalls.
- **19:00** — Dinner in Nakamachi — sansai (mountain vegetable)
  izakaya food and local Shinshu sake.

**Where to stay in Matsumoto** (one night)

- *Traditional:* **Marumo Ryokan** — creaking, atmospheric,
  riverside by Nakamachi.
- *Modern:* **Dormy Inn Matsumoto** — rooftop onsen and the
  chain's famous free late-night ramen.

## Day 7 — A post town on the Nakasendo, then back to Tokyo

- **9:00** — Local JR Chuo line to **Narai-juku** (~35 min) — the
  best-preserved post town on the old Nakasendo highway: a
  kilometre of Edo-period wooden inns between forested mountains,
  and a fraction of Magome/Tsumago's visitors. Coffee in a
  200-year-old house; walk toward the Torii Pass trailhead if legs
  ask for more.
- **13:00** — Local train back to **Shiojiri**, board the **Azusa
  limited express** to Shinjuku (~2.5 h, ~¥6,000).
- **16:30** — Back in Tokyo; check in.
- **19:00** — Dinner wherever your first trip's list left off —
  you've earned the neon again after a week of wooden lanes.

## Day 8 — Tokyo flex day

- Use it for whatever the Golden Route trip missed: a **Nikko** or
  **Kamakura** day trip, the Ghibli Museum if you won the ticket
  race, a teamLab rematch, or shopping with the foldable extra
  bag. Structured suggestion if you want one: Nikko's Toshogu
  shrine complex (~2 h by train each way) is the natural finale to
  a trip themed on old Japan.

## Day 9 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Airport (Narita Express / Skyliner ~¥1,500–3,000),
  konbini breakfast, done. Departure tax (¥3,000) is already in
  your ticket.

## What to pack

- Everything from the Golden Route list (slip-on shoes, coin
  purse, foldable bag), plus:
- A warmer layer than the season suggests — Takayama and
  Shirakawa-go sit high, and evenings run 5–10°C below Tokyo.
- Real winter kit December–March: the valley snow is
  metres-deep and pavements ice over; boots with grip.
- More cash than city Japan needs — rural sights, buses and
  market stalls.
- An onsen-ready attitude: two ryokan nights means yukata, shared
  baths and dinner at a set hour. It's the point of the trip.

## FAQ

**Is this doable as a first Japan trip?**
Yes mechanically, but you'd be skipping Kyoto to see old streets —
odd sequencing. Do the Golden Route first; this loop is the reward
trip, and the contrast is what makes it land.

**Which direction should I run the loop?**
As written (Kanazawa first) puts the Shinkansen's fixed timetable
at the start and the flexible Azusa at the end — better for
jet-lagged day one. Reversed works identically if hotel prices
favour it.

**When is the best time?**
October–November foliage is the loop at its finest and its
fullest — book two months ahead. April–May and September are the
easy sweet spots. Winter is magical in Shirakawa-go and quiet
everywhere else, but carry proper boots and watch bus timetables.

**Can I add the Alpine Route or Kamikochi?**
In season, yes: the Tateyama-Kurobe Alpine Route (mid-April–
November) slots between Kanazawa/Toyama and Nagano, and Kamikochi
(mid-April–mid-November) is a bus detour from the
Takayama–Matsumoto leg. Both add a day each; both are
weather-dependent. They're the natural upgrades to this exact
loop.

**Is Shirakawa-go worth it if I've seen Hida no Sato, or vice
versa?**
They're different pleasures: Shirakawa-go is a living village in a
dramatic valley (with crowds to match, 10:00–15:00); Hida no Sato
is a curated, quiet museum. This itinerary does both a day apart —
if cutting one, keep Shirakawa-go and go before 10am.

**Halal / vegetarian food on this route?**
Thinner than Tokyo: ryokan kitchens can adapt with advance notice
(tell them at booking, not check-in), soba and sansai dishes carry
vegetarians far, and Kanazawa has a handful of halal-listed
restaurants. Rural Japan runs on dashi — strict diets should brief
every accommodation ahead.
