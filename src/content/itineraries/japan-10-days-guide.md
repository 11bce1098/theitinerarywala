Japan is the trip that over-delivers: the trains really do run to
the second, a convenience-store meal really is good, and the jump
from Tokyo's neon canyons to a silent moss garden in Kyoto takes
one train ride. This is the classic first-timer arc — the Golden
Route — with an onsen ryokan night in Hakone between the two big
cities, a day among Nara's bowing deer, and a food-first finish in
Osaka.

Two things date most Japan guides: the JR Pass stopped being good
value for this route after its 2023 price rise, and the big Tokyo
attractions now sell out online weeks ahead. Both are handled
below.

All costs are in Japanese yen (JPY), with conversions calculated at
today's exchange rate. Opening hours are approximate — treat
anything marked ~ as a guide, not a contract.

## Before you go: the 15-minute admin

### Visas

- **Visa-free (90 days for most):** ~70 passports including the
  EU, UK, USA, Australia, Singapore and the UAE. No forms, no
  fees.
- **eVisa:** Japan's eVISA covers many others — including **all
  nationalities resident in the UAE, and everyone resident in
  India** — applied through Japan's accredited agencies rather
  than directly. So an Indian passport with UAE residence
  qualifies; apply via an accredited agency in the UAE with your
  Emirates ID, bank statements and itinerary.
- Visa fees rose sharply in **July 2026** — single-entry is now
  around ¥15,000 — and processing runs 8–10 working days, so start
  3–4 weeks out.
- Check the official [MOFA eVISA list](https://www.mofa.go.jp/j_info/visit/visa/visaonline.html)
  for your passport; it's revised periodically.

### The 2026 changes to know

- **Skip the JR Pass for this route.** The 7-day pass costs
  ¥50,000; every train in this itinerary bought individually
  totals roughly ¥30,000. The pass only pays off if you add
  Hiroshima or ride 5+ Shinkansen legs.
- **Kyoto's lodging tax jumped in March 2026**: now ¥400–¥10,000
  per person per night depending on your room rate (¥400 at
  mid-range levels), collected in cash/card at the hotel — it
  can't be prepaid online, so don't be surprised at check-in.
- **Japan's departure tax rose to ¥3,000** — normally baked into
  your air ticket, just budget for it.
- **Book-ahead attractions sell out**: teamLab (sales open ~2
  months ahead), Shibuya Sky (~2 weeks ahead; sunset slots go
  first), Ghibli Museum (10th of each month, 10:00 JST, for the
  following month — a genuine lottery-speed scramble).

### Money

- Japan is now largely cashless in cities — foreign Visa/
  Mastercard work almost everywhere — but keep **¥10,000–20,000
  in cash** for shrines, small restaurants, markets and lockers.
- **7-Eleven ATMs** take foreign cards, are everywhere, and have
  English menus. Exchange nothing at home; withdraw there.
- **No tipping. Anywhere.** It causes confusion, not gratitude.
  Put money on the small tray by the till, not into hands.

### Transport cards and data

- **iPhone:** add **Suica to Apple Wallet before you fly** — no
  deposit, top up from your foreign card, and one tap covers
  trains, metros, buses, konbini and lockers nationwide.
- **Android (foreign phones):** grab a physical **Welcome Suica**
  at the airport (no deposit, 28-day validity) and top up with
  cash.
- **Data:** a travel eSIM (Ubigi, Airalo and similar) costs
  ~¥1,500–3,000 for the trip and activates instantly — no rental
  pocket-wifi needed. Google Maps works perfectly for transit.
- **Luggage trick that makes Day 5 work:** forward your big bags
  hotel-to-hotel with **takkyubin** (~¥2,000–2,500 per bag,
  arranged at any hotel desk) — send them Tokyo → Kyoto and carry
  one overnight bag to Hakone. Everyone who knows Japan does this.

### Travelling with kids?

- Japan is one of the easiest countries on earth with children:
  spotless, safe, stroller-friendly stations (elevators are
  signed), and konbini food solves every meltdown.
- Kids love teamLab (barefoot water rooms), the Hakone pirate
  ship, and Nara's deer — but hold small hands there: the deer
  headbutt for crackers.
- Under-6s ride trains free; the Romancecar's front-window seats
  are the best ¥1,110 you'll spend on a child.

## Day 1 — Land in Tokyo, ease in at Asakusa

Assumes a daytime arrival. Base yourself in Shinjuku (transport
king) or Asakusa (atmosphere, value) — this guide works from
either.

- **14:00** — Airport → hotel (Narita Express / Skyliner from NRT,
  or the monorail/Keikyu line from Haneda; ¥1,500–3,000pp). Set up
  Suica if you haven't.
- **16:00** — **Senso-ji Temple, Asakusa** (grounds open always;
  main hall ~6:00–17:00, free) — Tokyo's oldest temple behind the
  great red Kaminarimon lantern. The Nakamise shopping lane is
  touristy and fun; the temple at dusk, half-lit and quiet, is the
  real arrival moment.
- **18:00** — Walk to the Sumida River for the Skytree view.
- **19:00** — Dinner around Asakusa — a low-key izakaya, or
  tempura (the district's specialty, ~¥1,500–2,500/person).
  Early night; jet lag is a tool if you sleep now.

**Where to stay in Tokyo**

- *Splurge:* **Park Hyatt Tokyo** (Shinjuku) — the *Lost in
  Translation* hotel: library calm, skyline views, the New York
  Bar.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Gracery Shinjuku** — under the Godzilla
  head in the middle of everything; compact, well-run, walk home
  from dinner.
- *Budget:* **APA Hotel Shinjuku** (and its many siblings) —
  small, spotless business-hotel rooms; Japan's budget tier
  embarrasses other countries' mid-range.

## Day 2 — Market sushi, teamLab, Shibuya at sunset

Book teamLab and Shibuya Sky before your trip (see admin).

- **8:00** — **Tsukiji Outer Market** — breakfast standing up:
  tamagoyaki skewers, tuna bowls, grilled scallops (~¥2,000–3,000
  well spent). Go early; stalls wind down after noon.
- **11:00** — **teamLab Planets, Toyosu** (~8:30–22:00; from
  ¥3,600, dynamic pricing; barefoot, knee-deep water — wear
  rollable trousers) — 2–3 hours inside the internet's favourite
  art museum, and it deserves the hype.
- **14:30** — **Ginza** stroll — Uniqlo's flagship, the depachika
  food basements under the department stores (lunch idea: buy a
  ¥1,500 bento masterpiece and eat it in Hamarikyu Gardens).
- **16:30** — **Shibuya**: cross the Scramble, find Hachiko, then
  up **Shibuya Sky** for your booked sunset slot (advance ¥2,700
  before 15:00 / ¥3,400 after; sunset sells out ~2 weeks ahead) —
  Fuji silhouette on clear evenings, and Tokyo's lights switching
  on beneath you.
- **19:30** — Dinner in Shibuya — conveyor sushi (Uobei/Genki
  Sushi, ~¥1,500) or ramen (Ichiran's solo booths are a rite of
  passage, ~¥1,200).

## Day 3 — Shrines, Harajuku and Shinjuku nights

- **9:00** — **Meiji Shrine** (sunrise–sunset, free) — a forest of
  100,000 trees in the middle of the city; the giant torii and
  sake-barrel wall are the photo stops. Weekend mornings often
  bring traditional weddings.
- **11:00** — **Harajuku**: Takeshita Street's chaos (crepes,
  ¥100 shops), then the calmer architecture-and-boutiques walk
  down **Omotesando**.
- **13:00** — Lunch around Harajuku/Omotesando.
- **15:00** — **Shinjuku Gyoen** (closed Mon; ~9:00–17:30ish by
  season, ¥500) — the city's most beautiful garden: cherry
  blossoms in spring, fiery maples in November.
- **17:30** — **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building
  observatory** (free, ~9:30–22:00, closed some Mondays) — the
  best free skyline view in Japan; Fuji at dusk in winter.
- **19:00** — **Omoide Yokocho** — the lantern-lit yakitori alley
  by Shinjuku Station: smoke, counters for six, skewers and beer
  (~¥2,000–3,000). Adults can nightcap in **Golden Gai**'s
  shoebox bars (cover charges posted at the door).

## Day 4 — Old Tokyo: Ueno, Akihabara and the Imperial moat

- **9:00** — **Ueno Park**: pick one museum — the **Tokyo National
  Museum** (closed Mon; ~9:30–17:00, ¥1,000) for samurai swords
  and kimono, or the science museum with kids.
- **12:00** — Walk **Ameyoko market** for street food lunch —
  post-war black-market alley turned snack strip.
- **14:00** — **Akihabara** — electric town: retro game arcades,
  anime floors, gachapon halls. Even non-gamers give it an hour.
- **16:30** — **Imperial Palace East Gardens** (closed Mon/Fri,
  free) and the Tokyo Station red-brick facade.
- **19:00** — Free evening: return to whatever pulled you —
  Shibuya's back streets, Asakusa at night, or a baseball game if
  the Giants are home. Pack for Hakone; send big bags to Kyoto by
  takkyubin from the hotel desk tonight.

## Day 5 — Hakone: the mountain loop and an onsen night

The **Hakone Free Pass** (2-day, ¥7,100 from Shinjuku) covers the
whole loop below — trains, cablecar, ropeway, pirate ship, buses.
Add ¥1,110 each way for the Romancecar's reserved panoramic seats.

- **8:30** — **Romancecar from Shinjuku** to Hakone-Yumoto
  (~85 min).
- **10:15** — **Hakone Tozan Railway** — the little mountain train
  switchbacking up through forest to Gora.
- **11:15** — Cablecar to Sounzan, then the **Hakone Ropeway**
  over the steaming sulfur valley of **Owakudani** — stop for the
  famous black eggs, boiled in the springs (one adds seven years
  to your life, says the legend). Note: the ropeway occasionally
  closes for volcanic gas; replacement buses run.
- **13:30** — Ropeway down to Lake Ashi and the gloriously
  unnecessary **pirate ship cruise** to Moto-Hakone.
- **15:00** — **Hakone Shrine** — the red torii standing in the
  lake; the queue for the photo moves fast. On clear days Mt Fuji
  owns the horizon (winter mornings are most reliable; summer
  afternoons are cloud roulette — honest odds: about 50/50).
- **16:30** — Check into your **ryokan**: yukata on, then kaiseki
  dinner course by course, then the onsen under the night sky.
  This night is the trip's centrepiece — budget for it.

**Where to stay in Hakone** (one night, book early)

- Ryokan with dinner+breakfast run ~¥25,000–45,000 for two at
  mid-range and climb steeply from there; the Gora and
  Miyanoshita areas put you on the loop. Tattoos: check bath
  policy or book a room with a private bath — plenty offer them.

## Day 6 — Shinkansen to Kyoto, temples at dusk

- **8:30** — One more onsen soak. You've earned nothing yet; do it
  anyway.
- **10:00** — Bus/train down to **Odawara**, then the
  **Shinkansen to Kyoto** (~2 h, ~¥12,000 reserved; buy via the
  SmartEX app or station machines).
- **13:00** — Arrive Kyoto, check in (bags from Tokyo are waiting
  — takkyubin magic), lunch near the station.
- **14:30** — **Southern Higashiyama on foot**: **Kiyomizu-dera**
  (~6:00–18:00, ¥500) and its wooden veranda over the maples, then
  down the preserved lanes of **Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka**.
- **17:30** — **Gion at dusk** — the wooden teahouse streets of
  Hanamikoji. Etiquette matters here: some private alleys are
  closed to tourists and photographing geiko/maiko without consent
  draws fines — admire, don't chase.
- **19:00** — Dinner on **Pontocho Alley** — the lantern-lit lane
  along the river; in summer, riverside kawadoko terraces.

**Where to stay in Kyoto**

- *Splurge:* **The Westin Miyako Kyoto** — hillside gardens and
  woodland trails above Higashiyama; or spend the splurge on a
  second ryokan night instead.
- *Mid-range:* **The Celestine Kyoto Gion** — quietly elegant,
  walking distance to Higashiyama's best.
- *Budget:* **Miyako Hotel Kyoto Hachijo** — plain, spotless, and
  directly at the station: perfect for the Nara and Osaka legs.
- Remember the **lodging tax** (¥400pp/night at mid-range rates
  since March 2026), paid at the desk.

## Day 7 — Ten thousand torii, then Nara's deer

Fushimi Inari and Nara sit on the same southbound JR line — the
classic combination day.

- **7:00** — **Fushimi Inari Taisha** (open 24/7, free) — the
  vermilion torii tunnels up the sacred mountain. At 7am you'll
  have whole stretches to yourself; by 10 it's a procession. Climb
  at least to the Yotsutsuji viewpoint (~45 min up).
- **10:30** — JR Nara line onward to **Nara** (~45 min).
- **11:30** — **Nara Park**: 1,200 sacred deer who bow for
  shika-senbei crackers (~¥300 a bundle — hold them high and bow
  back). **Todai-ji** (~7:30–17:30, ¥800) houses the 15-metre
  bronze Great Buddha in one of the world's largest wooden
  buildings.
- **14:00** — Lunch in Naramachi's old merchant quarter.
- **15:00** — **Kasuga Taisha**'s lantern-lined forest paths, then
  amble back through the park.
- **17:00** — Train back to Kyoto.
- **19:00** — **Nishiki Market**'s evening tail end for snacks,
  then dinner in the Kawaramachi grid — Kyoto obanzai (home-style
  small plates) or ramen.

## Day 8 — Gold, zen and bamboo

- **8:45** — **Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion** (9:00–17:00,
  ¥500) — be at the gate for opening; the first 30 minutes are the
  calm ones. It photographs like a painting because it basically
  is one.
- **10:30** — **Ryoan-ji** (~8:00–17:00, ¥600) — the fifteen-stone
  zen garden, ten minutes away. Sit; count; you'll only ever see
  fourteen.
- **12:30** — Bus/taxi across to **Arashiyama**; lunch by the
  river (yudofu — Kyoto's tofu hotpot — is the local order).
- **14:00** — **Arashiyama**: the **bamboo grove** (open always,
  free — thinner crowds after 15:00), **Tenryu-ji**'s garden
  (~8:30–17:00, ¥500), and the Togetsukyo Bridge river scene.
  % Arabica's riverside espresso bar earns its queue.
- **17:30** — Optional: the Sagano lanes north of the grove —
  where Arashiyama gets quiet again.
- **19:30** — Last Kyoto dinner — book something special tonight;
  Kyoto kaiseki at a counter is the city's art form (lunch
  kaiseki, ~¥6,000, is the smart way to taste it if dinner prices
  frighten).

## Day 9 — Osaka: castle, markets, Dotonbori

- **9:00** — Local train Kyoto → **Osaka** (~30 min, ¥580 — no
  Shinkansen needed). Drop bags in Namba.
- **10:30** — **Osaka Castle** (~9:00–17:00, ¥600) — the donjon is
  a modern museum inside; the moats, walls and park are the real
  spectacle.
- **13:00** — **Kuromon Ichiba Market** — Osaka's kitchen: grilled
  scallops, wagyu skewers, fatty tuna by the piece. This is lunch.
- **15:00** — **Shinsekai** — the retro district under Tsutenkaku
  Tower: kushikatsu (deep-fried skewers) at **Daruma**, the
  original — and obey the one rule: **no double-dipping** in the
  communal sauce.
- **17:30** — Namba back-streets: Hozenji Yokocho's moss-covered
  temple alley, then into the lights.
- **19:00** — **Dotonbori** — the Glico running man, the giant
  crab, and a takoyaki crawl (Wanaka and Kukuru are the names to
  find, ~¥600 a boat). Osaka's motto is *kuidaore* — "eat until
  you drop." Comply.

**Where to stay in Osaka** (one night)

- *Upscale:* **Swissotel Nankai Osaka** — directly over Namba
  Station; airport train downstairs.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel the Leben Osaka** — stylish, superbly run,
  in the Minami action.
- *Budget:* **First Cabin Midosuji Namba** — capsule-plus comfort
  for solo travellers.

## Day 10 — Fly home from Kansai

- **Morning** — One last konbini breakfast (you'll miss them), and
  if time allows, the **Umeda Sky Building** observatory or last
  shopping in Shinsaibashi.
- **Airport** — The **Nankai Rapi:t** train runs Namba → Kansai
  Airport in ~40 min (~¥1,500). KIX has direct Gulf and Asian
  connections, so book **open-jaw flights** (into Tokyo, out of
  Osaka) — it costs little more and saves the ¥28,000 and half-day
  of backtracking to Tokyo.

## What to pack

- Your best walking shoes — this itinerary is 15,000+ steps a day,
  and they must slip off easily (temples, ryokan, some
  restaurants).
- Socks without holes. You will be showing them daily.
- A coin purse — cashless Japan still rains ¥100 coins on you.
- Trousers you can roll up (teamLab's water rooms) and a compact
  umbrella any season.
- Layers for Hakone — the mountains run 5–10°C cooler than Tokyo.
- Deodorant and any specific medicines from home — both are
  surprisingly hard to buy in Japan (some common decongestants are
  actually banned; check before flying).
- A separate foldable bag for the souvenirs. This is not optional;
  you'll see.

## FAQ

**When is the best time to go?**
March–April (cherry blossoms) and November (autumn colour) are
peak Japan — book hotels 3–6 months out and add ~20% to prices.
May and October are the smart compromise: superb weather, thinner
crowds. June is rainy, July–August hot and humid, winter clear,
cold and underrated (best Fuji visibility).

**Is Japan expensive?**
Less than its reputation since the yen weakened. Food is the
bargain of the developed world — a ¥1,000 ramen or a ¥500 konbini
onigiri-and-coffee breakfast are both excellent. Trains and
book-ahead attractions are where the money goes; hotels sit
between Georgia-cheap and Europe-dear.

**Do I need the JR Pass?**
For this route, no — the maths stopped working when the pass rose
to ¥50,000. Buy the two Shinkansen legs individually (~¥30,000
total per person for everything in this guide, including the
Hakone pass).

**Is language a barrier?**
Minimal. Signage is bilingual, station staff are practiced, and
Google Translate's camera handles menus. Learn "sumimasen"
(excuse me/sorry/thanks) and "oishii" (delicious) — they carry
you far.

**Halal / vegetarian food?**
Both need light planning: true vegetarian dishes often hide dashi
(fish stock), so use apps like HappyCow and look for shojin ryori
(Buddhist temple cuisine) in Kyoto. Halal-certified restaurants
cluster in Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka — search "halal gourmet Japan"
listings — and Kuromon market has halal-friendly seafood
everywhere.

**Can I swap a day?**
The classic swaps: Day 4 for a **Kamakura** day trip (Great
Buddha, coastal train) or the **Ghibli Museum** if you win the
ticket scramble; in Osaka, families often trade the castle for
**Universal Studios Japan** (book express passes ahead). The
Hakone night is the one thing not to cut.
