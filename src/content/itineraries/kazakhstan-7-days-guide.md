Almaty is the trip that makes people check their currency
converter twice. Four hours from the Gulf sits a green,
café-crowded city with the snowy Tian Shan stacked at the end
of every south-facing street — and beyond it, a canyon
striped like Arizona, alpine lakes the colour of kerosene
glass, and a drowned spruce forest rising out of turquoise
water. Then the bill arrives, in tenge, and it's half what
you braced for.

The paperwork is the other draw: **most Western passports
enter visa-free for 30 days**, and **Indian passport holders
for 14 days per visit** (up to 42 in any 180) — on nothing
but a passport, hotel booking and return ticket. Air Arabia,
flydubai and Air Astana fly it non-stop from the Gulf.

The shape: **five nights in Almaty** wrapped around a **2-day,
1-night run east** to Charyn Canyon and the Kolsai–Kaindy lakes.
Costs are in tenge — figure **{{KZT 285000}} per person (≈ US$550)**
excluding flights. **September–October is the golden-autumn sweet
spot**; June–August is green alpine summer; December–March turns the
same cable cars into Central Asia's biggest ski trip.

## Before you go: the admin

- **Visa-free, official:** the EU, UK, US, Japan and some
  fifty others get **30 days**; since Government Decree
  464 (2022) Indian citizens get **14 days per entry,
  capped at 42 per 180**. Passport 6+ months
  valid with two blank pages; carry the return ticket and
  hotel PDFs for the counter. No forms, no fees, no
  registration for tourist stays this length.
- **Flights:** the Gulf hubs → Almaty ~4 h, commonly
  **US$250–490 return** (Air Arabia is often the
  steal). Overnight departures land you in Almaty at
  dawn — day one starts immediately.
- **Money:** the tenge (roughly KZT 515 to the US dollar). Cards
  and Kaspi-QR run the city, but **the mountains and
  park gates are cash country** — carry KZT 20,000–30,000
  in notes. ATMs everywhere in town.
- **Getting around:** download **Yandex Go** before you
  land — Almaty's Uber, absurdly cheap (most city hops
  KZT 700–2,000), and the etiquette answer to
  unofficial taxi touts: just don't. The metro is one
  line, gorgeous, and KZT 120 a ride.
- **Language:** Kazakh and Russian; English is patchy
  outside hotels and tour circles. Google Translate's
  Russian pack + menu-photo mode = fluent enough.

## Day 1 — Land at dawn, ease into Almaty

- **Morning** — Land, eSIM at the airport (Beeline/Tele2,
  ~KZT 3,000–5,000), Yandex to the centre (~KZT 3,000),
  drop bags.
- **11:00** — **Panfilov Park**: the candy-coloured
  **Zenkov Cathedral** — one of the world's tallest
  wooden buildings, built without nails and rebuilt in
  pastel glory — and the colossal Soviet war memorial
  beside it.
- **13:00** — First plov and samsa at a chaikhana
  (KZT 2,500–4,000 pp, portions built for shepherds).
- **15:30** — The **Green Bazaar**: pyramids of dried
  apricots, kurt cheese balls, honey rows, horse-meat
  counters (label-reading matters here — more below),
  and Almaty's famous **aport apples** — the city's
  name means "father of apples", and this is the
  species' genetic homeland.
- **18:30** — Golden-hour stroll on **Panfilov Street's
  pedestrian mile**, dinner at a modern Kazakh
  restaurant (KZT 6,000–9,000 pp at the fancy end).

**Where to stay in Almaty** (five nights, city centre)

- *Luxury:* **The Ritz-Carlton, Almaty** — 4.7/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~550 reviews), top floors of the
  Esentai tower with the whole Tian Shan wall in the
  window. ~KZT 180,000+.
- *Mid-range:* **Mercure Almaty City Center** — 4.5/5
  (~190 reviews), new, central, mountain-view rooms.
  ~KZT 45,000–60,000. (Rixos, 4.5/~1,000 reviews, is
  the grand-dame upgrade.)
- *Value:* **Park Hotel Almaty** — 4.5/5 (~290
  reviews, the city's #1 Best Value), breakfast in.
  ~KZT 28,000–38,000.

## Day 2 — Medeu, Shymbulak and the 3,180 m lunch

- **09:00** — Yandex (or bus 12) up the gorge to
  **Medeu** — the world's highest Olympic-size ice
  rink, a Soviet legend at 1,691 m (skating in winter,
  ~KZT 3,000 + skates; a photogenic amphitheatre in
  summer).
- **10:00** — The **gondola to Shymbulak** (2,260 m):
  sightseeing tickets run **KZT 4,000–12,000 return
  depending how high you ride** — take it all the way
  up the **Combi lifts to Talgar Pass at 3,180 m**,
  where the air thins, the marmots whistle and the
  city feels like a rumour. Ten-minute acclimatisation
  rule: walk slowly, drink water.
- **13:00** — Lunch on the Shymbulak deck (mountain
  mark-up, still ~KZT 4,000–6,000 pp) with glacier
  views.
- **16:30** — Down to the city; recovery coffee in a
  hipster kofeynya (Almaty's café scene genuinely
  surprises).
- **18:30** — **Kok-Tobe** hill: the cable car from
  Dostyk Ave (~KZT 4,000 return) to the fairground,
  zoo-lite, and THE city-lights panorama — plus
  Central Asia's only bronze Beatles bench,
  inexplicably and delightfully.

## Day 3 — Big Almaty Lake and the Arasan Baths

- **09:00** — Half-day to **Big Almaty Lake**
  (2,511 m): a turquoise bowl under three peaks, 40
  minutes' drive up. Logistics honestly: it sits in a
  **border zone (Kyrgyzstan is over the ridge) — carry
  passports**, and it's the city's drinking reservoir,
  so **no swimming, no shoreline picnics** — you admire
  from the viewpoints, and it's still worth every
  tenge. Yandex won't wait; book a return taxi
  (~KZT 25,000–35,000 for the car) or a small-group
  tour.
- **14:00** — Lunch back down, then the **Central
  Mosque** (gleaming, welcoming; scarves at the door)
  or the Kasteyev art museum.
- **17:00** — The Almaty institution: **Arasan
  Baths** — a Soviet-brutalist cathedral of steam
  (Russian, Finnish and Turkish halls, ~KZT
  6,000–12,000 for a session + felt hat purchase you
  won't regret). The correct end to an altitude day.
- **20:00** — Dinner; pack a small overnight bag —
  the mountains want you at 07:00.

## Days 4–5 — The big loop: canyons, Kolsai, the sunken forest

Book the **2-day, 1-night group tour** (the standard
full package — transport, English-speaking guide,
**park entrance fees, guesthouse night in Saty village
and four meals — runs ~US$160 (≈ KZT 83,000) per
person**; private 4x4 versions cost multiples).
Self-driving is possible but the Kaindy track
genuinely needs a 4x4 and local nerve.

- **Day 4** — Out east across the steppe (3–4 h with
  breakfast stop): the **Moon and Black canyons'**
  badlands overlooks, then up into the spruce zone to
  **Kolsai-1 (1,818 m)** — a fjord-green lake with a
  boardwalk trail along its flank (walk 30–90 min;
  horses for hire ~KZT 5,000/hr). Night in a **Saty
  village guesthouse**: home cooking, samovar tea,
  Milky Way overhead — rustic and warm, adjust
  expectations and enjoy it.
- **Day 5** — Morning 4x4 (or horseback) up to **Lake
  Kaindy (2,000 m)**: the 1911 earthquake drowned a
  spruce forest here, and the bleached trunks still
  stand in blue-green water — one of Central Asia's
  strangest, loveliest sights. Then down to **Charyn
  Canyon** for the **Valley of the Castles** walk
  (entry ~KZT 730–1,500, cash; 2–3 km down the
  formations to the Charyn river, eco-shuttle back
  KZT 500 if knees vote no). Golden hour in the
  canyon, back in Almaty ~21:00.

## Day 6 — Slow Almaty: metro art, last bazaars

- **10:00** — Ride the **Almaty Metro** end to end
  (KZT 120): Soviet-marble stations with apple
  mosaics — Zhibek Zholy and Almaly are the stars.
  Surface for the **Republic Square** ensemble and
  the Golden Warrior monument.
- **13:00** — Farewell-food decisions: **beshbarmak**
  is the national dish (hand-pulled pasta under
  slow-boiled meat — traditionally horse; beef/lamb
  versions everywhere), manty the size of fists,
  or a Georgian khachapuri detour.
- **15:30** — Last shopping: Green Bazaar round two
  for dried fruit, honey and kurt (vacuum-packed
  for the flight), or Esentai Mall if the tenge
  maths made you brave.
- **19:00** — Sunset from Kok-Tobe round two or a
  rooftop café; toast with **shubat or kymyz** if
  adventurous (fermented camel/horse milk — the
  guide's honesty: order one glass, share it).

## Day 7 — Fly home

- Morning Yandex to the airport (~KZT 3,500, 30
  min), board with apples in the carry-on and the
  strong suspicion you'll be back for ski season.

## What to pack

- Layers with real intent: Almaty at 25°C and Talgar
  Pass at 5°C happen the same afternoon. Fleece +
  windshell minimum, gloves outside July–August.
- Broken-in walking shoes (canyon 3 km, Kolsai
  boardwalks); trainers fine, boots better.
- **Cash tenge for the mountains**, passport ON YOU
  for Big Almaty Lake day.
- Sunscreen + sunglasses (altitude UV), lip balm,
  motion tablets for the steppe drive's last winding
  hour.
- Swimwear for Arasan (and a sense of humour for
  its dress codes), flip-flops.
- Power: **Type C/F European plugs** — pack the
  adapter unless you are coming from Europe.
- Winter trip? Thermal everything; the city runs
  warm indoors, the mountains do not.

## FAQ

**Is Kazakhstan safe? Any Central Asia nerves to
settle?**
Settle them: Almaty is a calm, prosperous,
espresso-drinking city where the biggest tourist
hazards are icy pavements (winter) and over-ordering
at the bazaar. Standard city sense applies at night;
solo women travellers report comfortable trips. Use
Yandex instead of street taxis and you've handled
90% of it. The mountains' real risks are altitude
pace and weather turns — go with the guides.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Kazakhstan is Muslim-majority, so **halal is the
default assumption at most Kazakh and Uzbek
kitchens** (signage is common; pork exists mainly in
Russian/European restaurants — ask "svinina yest?").
The flip side is the site's usual one, reversed
again: **vegetarians work a bit** — the national
cuisine is proudly meat-forward — but manty come in
pumpkin versions, lagman does vegetable, Georgian
restaurants carry the cheese course, and Indian
restaurants dot the centre. One honest warning for
everyone: **horse meat (kazy, beshbarmak) is
beloved and ubiquitous** — if that's a line for
you, learn the word *konina* and ask.

**When exactly should we go?**
**September–early October** is the answer we'd book:
golden aspens at Kolsai, warm days, snow dusting
the peaks. June–August for green valleys and the
full hiking menu (also peak local-tourist season at
the lakes); **December–March flips the whole guide
into a ski trip** — Shymbulak runs, Medeu ice,
half-price hotels. April–May is melt and mud at
altitude; the city's still lovely.

**Can we do Charyn and the lakes as day trips
instead of overnight?**
There are 16-hour single-day versions of this loop.
We don't recommend them: 660 km of driving wrapped
around three sights leaves you photographing
everything through fatigue. The Saty guesthouse
night is half the charm — and it's what makes
sunrise-ish Kaindy possible, before the day-trip
vans arrive.

**Is the city walkable? Do we need a car?**
No car needed, ever: the centre walks beautifully
(it's a grid — uphill is always south, toward the
mountains, the local compass), Yandex covers the
rest for pocket change, and the mountain days run
on cable cars and tours. Driving yourself earns
nothing here except parking stories.

**What's Kazakhstan guide #2?**
Two contenders: **Astana** — the sci-fi capital of
mirrored spheres and Khan Shatyr's indoor beach —
or the **Shymbulak ski week** (Central Asia's best
lift system at a fifth of Alps prices). And beyond:
this is the site's first Central Asia guide —
**Uzbekistan's Samarkand and Bukhara** are the
natural sequel. Votes welcome.
