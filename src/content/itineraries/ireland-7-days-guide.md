Ireland runs on two renewable resources: rain and charm. The
rain keeps the island forty shades of green; the charm means
a request for directions ends fifteen minutes later with a
family history and a restaurant recommendation. Add a coast
that shatters into 214-metre cliffs, a capital that gave the
world half its great writers, and pubs where the music is
live, unamplified and started by whoever brought a fiddle —
and you have Europe's easiest country to love.

For this site's readers there's a headline the brochures
skip: **Ireland is not in Schengen, but a UK visit visa can
cover it.** Under the **British-Irish Visa Scheme (BIVS)**,
Indian nationals holding a UK short-stay visa (endorsed
BIVS — most are) can visit Ireland **without any Irish
visa**, provided they enter via the UK first. If you took our
[UK guide's](/itineraries/uk-10-days-guide/) trip, Ireland is already
half-unlocked.

The route: **Dublin (3 nights) → Galway (2 nights) + the
Cliffs of Moher → Dingle (1 night)** on the Slea Head drive,
then home. Figure **{{EUR 1350}} per person** including a standalone visa, excluding flights.
**Go May–September** for long light (October is moody-lovely;
every month is rain-possible — that's the deal).

## Before you go: the admin

### The BIVS trick, decoded

- **The BIVS trick, decoded:** UK short-stay visit
  visas issued to Indian nationals normally carry the
  **"BIVS" endorsement** — check the sticker. With it,
  you may visit **Ireland for up to 90 days with NO
  Irish visa**, on one condition: **you must enter the
  UK first** on that visa (even a London stopover
  counts), then continue to Ireland; you can fly home
  from either island. Mention BIVS in your UK cover
  letter to be safe. It works in reverse too (Irish
  visa endorsed BIVS → the UK).
- **The standalone route:** no UK visa? The **Irish
  short-stay 'C' visa** costs **€60** + VFS service
  fee at your nearest visa centre — the usual file
  (proof of residence, bank statements, employment letter,
  bookings, insurance) — and Irish processing runs
  slower than Schengen: **apply 6–8 weeks out.**

### Flights and money

- **Flights:** **Etihad flies Abu Dhabi–Dublin
  non-stop and Emirates flies Dubai–Dublin daily**
  (~8 h; commonly US$710–1,150 return).
- **Money:** euros (NOT in the North — that's
  sterling and a different visa question); cards tap
  everywhere; tipping is ~10% at table-service
  restaurants only, nothing at pubs.

### The car-hire honesty

- **The car-hire honesty (Ireland's famous
  gotcha):** basic rentals carry a **€2,000–3,000
  damage excess**, and — uniquely — **most credit-card
  rental insurance EXCLUDES Ireland**. Budget the
  super-damage-waiver (€15–30/day) or a standalone
  excess policy, book a SMALL car (the west's
  boreens are hedge-width), and remember: left side,
  like home-away-from-UAE.

## Day 1 — Land in Dublin, first stroll

- **Afternoon** — Land, AirCoach/bus into the centre
  (~€8), check in.
- **16:30** — Orientation loop: **Grafton Street's**
  buskers (Dublin's future stars audition here
  daily), **St Stephen's Green**, Molly Malone, and
  a first look at **Temple Bar** — with the honest
  note attached: it's the postcard, and it charges
  like one (€10 pints, tourist menus). Look,
  photograph, then eat one street over.
- **19:30** — Dinner around South William/Camden
  streets (modern Irish, €22–35 mains) — and Irish
  brown bread with everything, immediately a habit.

**Where to stay in Dublin** (three nights — the
honest warning: Dublin beds are among Europe's
priciest; book early, midweek beats weekends)

- *Luxury:* **The Merrion** — 4.8/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~5,300 reviews, 2026 Best of the Best), Georgian
  townhouses opposite government buildings. €450+.
- *Mid-range:* **The Alex** — 4.8/5 (~2,300
  reviews), sharp and central by Trinity. ~€190–260.
- *Value:* **Travelodge Plus City Centre** — 4.8/5
  (~1,600 reviews, the city's #1 Best Value) at
  ~€120–160. (Marlin Stephen's Green, 4.5/2,800, is
  the backup.)

## Day 2 — The Book of Kells, a castle, the emigrants

- **09:00** — **Trinity College**: the **Book of
  Kells Experience** (**~€25, timed — book online**):
  the 1,200-year-old illuminated gospels, then the
  **Long Room** — the barrel-vaulted library of
  200,000 ancient books that every fantasy film
  borrowed. Go at opening; by noon it's a procession.
- **11:30** — **Dublin Castle** grounds and the
  **Chester Beatty** (free — one of Europe's great
  small museums: Qur'ans, Mughal miniatures, Japanese
  scrolls; this site's readers will find half their
  heritage beautifully kept).
- **14:00** — Lunch, then the **EPIC Irish Emigration
  Museum** (~€21) in the docklands vaults — the story
  of the 70-million-strong diaspora, told brilliantly
  (and a resonant mirror for any NRI family). Ha'penny
  Bridge photo on the walk back.
- **19:00** — The evening institution, framed for
  everyone: a **trad session** — live jigs and reels
  around a pub table (The Cobblestone and
  O'Donoghue's are shrines). **Non-drinkers are
  fully welcome**: order a lemonade or a Guinness
  0.0, take a stool, stay hours. The music, not the
  pint, is the point.

## Day 3 — Howth's cliffs, then choose your Dublin icon

- **09:30** — **DART train to Howth** (25 min, ~€7
  return): the fishing-village peninsula — walk the
  **cliff path loop** (2 h, gorse, lighthouses,
  Dublin Bay glittering), watch seals mug the fish
  market, lunch on seafood chowder + brown bread
  (€9–12, the west's national starter arrives
  early).
- **15:30** — Back in town, one big ticket:
  - **Guinness Storehouse** (from ~€26 online,
    time-tiered): honestly framed — it's a
    seven-storey brand museum done superbly, ending
    at the 360° **Gravity Bar** city view; soft
    drinks pour as happily as stout, so
    non-drinkers lose only the sample; or
  - **Kilmainham Gaol** (~€8, **books out days
    ahead**): the prison where the 1916 leaders
    were executed — Ireland's most moving hour and
    the pick for history-first travellers.
- **19:30** — Last Dublin dinner; collect the
  rental car in the morning.

## Day 4 — West to Galway

- **09:00** — Collect the car and cross the island
  (2.5 h motorway; optional detour: **Clonmacnoise**,
  the monastic ruins on the Shannon, ~€8).
- **13:30** — **Galway**: lunch in the **Latin
  Quarter's** painted lanes, then the Spanish Arch,
  the Claddagh (yes, of the ring), and street
  musicians who'd headline elsewhere.
- **17:30** — **Salthill promenade** stroll —
  Galway Bay sunset with the Burren's grey hills
  across the water; locals kick the wall at the
  end for luck. Kick it.
- **20:00** — Dinner (Galway is Ireland's
  food-town: €20–35 mains, oysters if September's
  festival mood strikes), then the famous
  **Tig Cóilí** trad session — two a night, no
  cover, arrive early for a stool.

**Where to stay in Galway** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Glenlo Abbey** — 4.5/5 (~2,800
  reviews), the estate-with-golf outside town —
  dinner is served in two Orient Express
  carriages. ~€280+.
- *Mid-range:* **Park House Hotel** — 4.5/5
  (~3,700 reviews), old-school comfort off Eyre
  Square. ~€160–210.
- *Value:* **The Nest Boutique Hostel** — 4.6/5
  (~460 reviews), privates near Salthill from
  ~€90. (Leonardo Galway, 4.2/3,800, is the
  mid-budget chain answer.)

## Day 5 — The Burren and the Cliffs of Moher

- **09:00** — Loop south through **the Burren** —
  a grey limestone moonscape where Arctic and
  Mediterranean flowers somehow share cracks —
  stopping at **Poulnabrone dolmen** (a 5,800-
  year-old portal tomb, free, older than the
  pyramids and standing in a field).
- **12:00** — Lunch in **Doolin**, the
  trad-music village (chowder standards are set
  here).
- **13:30** — **The Cliffs of Moher** (**€15 at
  the gate — book online for tiered discounts;
  under-12s free**, parking + O'Brien's Tower
  included): 214 metres of vertical Atlantic
  theatre, 8 km of paths, puffins in early
  summer. **Stay behind the walls** — the edge
  wind is not a metaphor; people ignore this
  yearly and make the news. Off-peak PM slots
  (after 4) get golden light AND smaller
  crowds.
- **17:30** — The coastal R478 back via
  Kinvara's harbour castle; Galway evening,
  round two of everything.

## Day 6 — Dingle and the Slea Head Drive

- **09:00** — South to **Dingle** (3 h — take
  the Killimer–Tarbert **car ferry** shortcut
  across the Shannon, €28/car, and coffee in
  literary Listowel).
- **13:00** — Fish and chips or chowder on
  Dingle's harbour; say hello to the harbour
  town Nat Geo once called "the most beautiful
  place on earth."
- **14:30** — The **Slea Head Drive** (30 km
  loop, clockwise, 2–3 h with stops): beehive
  huts from the first millennium, **Coumeenoole
  beach** wedged in the cliffs, **Dunquin
  pier's** zigzag down to the Blasket Islands
  view, Gaelic road signs only — mainland
  Europe's westernmost edge, and this trip's
  scenery champion.
- **18:30** — Dingle evening: **Murphy's
  ice cream** (sea-salt flavour, made with
  local milk — mandatory), dinner, and pubs
  that double as hardware shops (Foxy John's
  sells pints and hammers; Dick Mack's,
  boots) with trad sessions in the back.

**Dingle night:** the B&B belt is the point
here — Pax House's harbour views or the
guesthouses along the Mall (~€110–160,
Irish breakfasts included and enormous) —
book by current reviews; it's a small town
that fills.

## Day 7 — Conor Pass, then home

- **08:30** — Weather permitting, leave over
  the **Conor Pass** — Ireland's highest road,
  Dingle shrinking behind, lakes in the
  corrie below (fog = take the N86 and lose
  nothing but the photo).
- **10:00** — The cross-country run back to
  Dublin (3.5 h; Adare's thatched village
  makes the fairest coffee stop), drop the
  car at the airport by mid-afternoon for an
  evening Emirates/Etihad departure — or add
  an airport-hotel night for a morning
  flight.

## What to pack

- **Rain shell always, umbrella never** (the
  wind eats them) — Irish rain is soft,
  sideways and brief; locals call a wet day
  "grand" and mean it.
- Layers: 12–19°C summer norms; one warm
  jumper (or buy the Aran wool one there —
  the west's best souvenir).
- Waterproof-ish walking shoes: Howth's
  path, cliff grass, cobbles.
- Sunglasses regardless — Atlantic light
  strobes between showers.
- **UK-style Type G plugs — UAE chargers
  work as-is** (the BIVS of electricity).
- Motion-comfort for the Slea Head and
  Conor Pass bends; small car, small bags.

## FAQ

**How exactly does the BIVS visa trick
work — can we fly UAE→Dublin directly on a
UK visa?**
No — that's the one rule: **the first entry
must be through the UK** on the UK visa;
after that, hop to Ireland (45-minute
flights from £30, or the ferry) visa-free
for up to 90 days, and fly home from
Dublin if you like. So the classic
combined trip is London first, Ireland
second. Flying UAE→Dublin nonstop needs
the Irish visa proper. Check your UK
sticker says "BIVS" — nearly all Indian
visit visas do.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Dublin is genuinely strong — a large
Pakistani/Bangladeshi/Malaysian scene with
certified restaurants across the centre
(the South Circular Road corridor has
history here), plus kebab reliability
everywhere. Galway has a handful; Dingle,
assume seafood/veg. Vegetarians do better
than the meat-and-potatoes myth: every menu
carries real options, the brown-bread-and-
soup lunch is accidentally veg-friendly
(check the chowder isn't the soup), and
Indian restaurants appear in every town
that has two streets.

**Is the pub culture awkward for
non-drinkers?**
Not remotely — the pub is Ireland's living
room, not its bar. Trad sessions, sport on
the telly, toasties and tea, kids until
early evening, and Guinness 0.0 poured
with the same ceremony. Nobody clocks
what's in your glass; they clock whether
you stayed for the third reel.

**Self-drive or bus tours for the west?**
Self-drive wins the west (Slea Head and
the Burren are stop-anywhere roads), IF
you accept the small-car-narrow-road
deal and price the excess cover honestly.
The no-car version works too: Citylink
buses Dublin–Galway (~€18), day tours to
the Cliffs (~€45) and Kerry — you lose
only the spontaneity, which is admittedly
half of Ireland.

**Cliffs of Moher or Slea Head — if
weather kills one day?**
The Cliffs are the postcard; **Slea Head
is the love affair** — most returners
vote Dingle. If the forecast gives you
one clear day, spend it on whichever
you'd regret more in fog, and know the
Cliffs' visitor centre half-saves a wet
visit while Slea Head in mist is just
wet.

**What's Ireland guide #2?**
**Belfast and the Giant's Causeway** —
Titanic, the Dark Hedges, the basalt
columns (with the UK-visa logistics that
make it a natural BIVS add-on) — or the
**full Wild Atlantic Way** for
returners. Votes welcome; both on the
list.
