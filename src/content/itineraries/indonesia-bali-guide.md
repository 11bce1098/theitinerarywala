Bali earns the hype and hides the fine print. The hype: a Hindu
island of 20,000 temples where mornings smell of incense and
frangipani, rice terraces stack like green amphitheatres, and a
cliff-top fire dance happens to own the world's best sunset. The
fine print: traffic that measures distance in minutes-per-
kilometre, a scooter culture that fills clinics, and an
Instagram economy with queues at the famous angles.

This guide runs the classic two-base shape — **jungle Ubud
first, beach-and-cliff south second** — with the honesty layer
built in. Bali still delivers magic on demand; it just rewards
travellers who plan like locals drive: patiently.

**Go April–October** (dry season; July–August is peak
everything). November–March is the rains — mornings often
clear, afternoons theatrical, prices kinder. **One absolute
date-check before booking: Nyepi**, the Hindu Day of Silence
(usually March) — the entire island stops for 24 hours,
**including the airport**; more below. All costs are in
Indonesian rupiah (IDR).

## Before you go: visa, levy, and the zeros again

- **e-VoA (recommended):** most visitors, India included, use
  Indonesia's Visa on Arrival — **IDR 500,000 (~US$32)**, 30 days,
  **extendable once** to 60. Do it online at the official
  **evisa.imigrasi.go.id** 48 hours–14 days before flying
  and walk past the airport VoA queue. Passport 6+ months +
  2 blank pages. (The airport counter sells the same VoA if
  you forget — after a queue.)
- **The Bali tourist levy: IDR 150,000 per person**, separate
  from the visa — pay at **lovebali.baliprov.go.id** before
  arrival and keep the QR; checkpoints at attractions
  increasingly scan it.
- **Zeros briefing, Indonesian edition:** IDR 100,000 ≈
  US$6. Menus drop the thousands ("50" = 50k). The 100k
  (red) and 10k (purple) notes don't look alike — but count
  changer cash twice anyway, and use **authorized money
  changers or bank-attached ATMs only** (skimming is Bali's
  quiet crime).
- The VoA follows your passport, not where you live — a
  residence permit elsewhere changes nothing either way.

## The safety page most Bali guides skip

- **Scooters are the #1 tourist injury.** Legally you need
  an IDP + home motorcycle licence; without them insurance
  pays nothing. First-ever scooter + Canggu shortcut +
  helmetless = the accident recipe. This itinerary uses
  **drivers and Gojek/Grab cars** instead (a full-day
  private car with driver and fuel is **IDR 700–900k** —
  split by two, it's the island's best deal).
- **The methanol warning, plainly:** bootleg arak (local
  spirit) cut with methanol kills tourists in Bali and
  Lombok most years. Drink only **sealed, branded** bottles
  from proper venues; skip free-pour spirits and
  suspiciously cheap cocktails at party bars. Beer and
  anything factory-sealed: fine.
- **Monkeys are professionals**: at Uluwatu and the Monkey
  Forest they snatch sunglasses, phones and dangling
  earrings for ransom (staff literally trade fruit to get
  items back). Nothing loose, zips closed, no toothy
  smiles (it reads as threat). **Don't pet strays** —
  rabies exists here; any bite or scratch = clinic the
  same day.
- **Bali belly:** bottled/refill-station water only
  (brushing teeth included, first-timers), ice at proper
  venues is factory-made and fine, and busy warungs beat
  quiet buffets. Pack the rehydration salts.
- Tap-water no, sunscreen reef-safe for snorkel day, and
  temple etiquette: sarong + sash (lent at big temples),
  shoulders covered, and respect the signs asking
  menstruating women not to enter — it's their house.

## Day 1 — Land, climb into the jungle

- **Afternoon** — Land DPS, e-VoA lane, levy QR ready.
  Driver to **Ubud** (34 km; **1.5–2 traffic-honest
  hours**, ~IDR 400–500k).
- **17:30** — First wander: **Ubud Palace** corner, the
  market lanes, frangipani offerings (canang sari)
  underfoot everywhere — step around, never on.
- **19:00** — Either the **Legong dance** at the palace
  (~IDR 100k, nightly, gorgeous costumes) or straight to a
  jungle-side dinner — smoked ribs at a riverside
  institution or nasi campur at a family warung
  (~IDR 60–150k pp).

**Where to stay in Ubud** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Komaneka at Bisma** — rice-field infinity
  pool, riverside spa, the serene-Ubud postcard made real.
  ~IDR 4,000,000+.
- *Mid-range:* **Adiwana Bisma** — 5.0/5 on TripAdvisor,
  jungle-view calm ten minutes' walk from the centre.
  ~IDR 1,800,000–2,600,000.
- *Value:* **Ubud Village Hotel** — 4.8/5 (~2,600
  reviews), TripAdvisor's #1 value here, dead-central.
  ~IDR 900,000–1,400,000.

## Day 2 — Terraces, holy water, volcano lunch

Driver day one (share the IDR 800k car; the stops are far).

- **07:00** — **Tegallalang rice terraces** before the
  crowds (entry IDR 25–50k): the stacked green
  amphitheatre at its dewy best. The famous **jungle
  swings charge separately** (IDR 200–500k) — fun,
  optional, queue-prone by 9.
- **09:30** — **Tirta Empul** (IDR 75k, sarong included):
  the thousand-year-old spring where Balinese Hindus
  queue through fountains of holy water. Visitors may
  join the purification respectfully (bring a change of
  clothes, follow the sequence, skip the fountains
  reserved for offerings).
- **12:30** — Lunch on the **Kintamani crater rim** —
  buffet views over Mount Batur and its lake
  (~IDR 100–150k pp).
- **15:00** — Coffee-plantation stop for luwak-and-
  tasting-flight theatre — **the ethical note: skip any
  place displaying caged civets**; plenty offer the
  tasting without the cruelty.
- **17:30** — Back for the **Campuhan Ridge walk** (free):
  the grassy spine at golden hour, Ubud's gentlest
  postcard.
- **19:30** — Dinner + the first proper **Balinese
  massage** (IDR 150–250k/hour — yes, really).

## Day 3 — Monkeys, waterfalls, lotus evenings

- **08:30** — **Sacred Monkey Forest** (IDR 130k adult /
  100k child) at opening: 1,200 macaques among mossy
  temples and banyan roots. Rules recap: nothing
  dangling, bags zipped, no food, no grin-flashing.
  Delightful and slightly lawless — exactly as advertised.
- **11:00** — Ubud café hour (the smoothie-bowl world
  capital; IDR 60–90k) and boutique lanes.
- **13:30** — Waterfall run: **Tibumana** (IDR 25k,
  gentle, swimmable) or **Tegenungan** (IDR 30k, big and
  busy) — dry-bag the phone.
- **16:30** — **Saraswati Temple's** lotus ponds (free
  courtyard) as the light softens.
- **19:00** — Ubud's modern-Indonesian dining scene for
  the trip's fancy dinner (~IDR 350–600k for two — a
  tasting-menu island bargain) or babi-free options for
  halal travellers: the excellent Padang and Javanese
  Muslim warungs on the main roads.

## Day 4 — Slow Ubud, or the north-Bali loop

Two good Day 4s:

- **Slow (recommended after Day 2's marathon):** sunrise
  yoga shala class (IDR 150–200k), a Balinese **cooking
  class** with market tour (~IDR 400–500k), pool hours,
  second massage. Ubud's actual luxury is unscheduled
  time.
- **The north loop (driver day):** **Ulun Danu Beratan**
  — the lake temple off the 50k note (IDR 100k) —
  **Handara Gate's** photo queue (small fee), **Jatiluwih's**
  UNESCO mega-terraces (IDR 75k, vast and quiet), and the
  Wanagiri swing-and-nest viewpoints. 9–10 hours; pack
  patience for the mountain road.

## Day 5 — South via the sea temple

- **10:00** — Checkout, driver south with the luggage.
- **12:30** — Beach-club or warung lunch en route through
  Canggu's chaos-cool (optional shortcut tour of why
  everyone complains about the traffic and comes anyway).
- **17:00** — **Tanah Lot** (IDR 75k): the sea temple on
  its wave-cut rock — the sunset silhouette that launched
  Bali tourism. Crowded, commercial, still genuinely
  spectacular at golden hour.
- **19:30** — Check in Seminyak; first **Eat Street**
  (Jalan Kayu Aya) dinner (~IDR 150–350k pp on the smart
  strip).

**Where to stay in Seminyak** (five nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Legian Seminyak** — 4.9/5 (~2,600
  reviews), the beachfront grande dame with butler-quiet
  service. ~IDR 6,500,000+.
- *Mid-range:* **Double-Six Luxury Hotel** — 4.8/5
  (~4,200 reviews), suites plus that rooftop sunset bar.
  ~IDR 2,800,000–3,800,000.
- *Value:* **The Haven Bali Seminyak** — 4.2/5 (~4,100
  reviews), central, pool-view balconies. ~IDR
  1,100,000–1,600,000.

## Day 6 — Nusa Penida: the dinosaur coastline

- **06:30** — Early pickup for the **Nusa Penida day**
  (**IDR 1,100,000–1,500,000 pp** including Sanur fast
  boat, island car + driver, lunch and usually a snorkel
  stop): 45 rough-and-ready minutes across the strait.
- **09:30** — **Kelingking Beach** — the T-Rex-shaped
  cliff that broke the internet; the viewpoint queue
  moves, the view repays it (the descent to the beach is
  a genuine scramble — skip with kids).
- **12:00** — **Broken Beach & Angel's Billabong**: the
  collapsed sea-arch bowl and the natural infinity pool
  (no swimming when swells run — obey the whistles).
- **14:30** — **Crystal Bay** snorkel or swim; manta-ray
  snorkel add-ons exist on two-stop boat versions.
- **The honesty:** Penida's roads are brutal, the day is
  long, and every stop has a queue by 10 — it is *still*
  the most jaw-dropping day in the Bali orbit. Early boat
  or don't bother.
- **19:30** — Back by ~18:00; recovery massage; easy
  dinner.

## Day 7 — Uluwatu: cliffs, fire, seafood in the sand

- **10:30** — South to the Bukit: morning at a white-sand
  cove — **Padang Padang** (small fee) or vast, calm
  **Melasti**; surfers detour to peek at **Suluban
  cave**.
- **16:30** — **Uluwatu Temple** (IDR 60k, sarong
  included): the 11th-century clifftop shrine 70 m above
  the breakers — and the monkey mafia's headquarters
  (glasses OFF before entering; they've unionised).
- **18:00** — The **Kecak fire dance** (IDR 150k; queue
  or pre-book seats an hour ahead in season): a hundred
  chanting men, the Ramayana at sunset, a monkey god in
  flames — Bali's single best evening.
- **20:00** — Down to **Jimbaran Bay**: grilled seafood
  on the sand, chosen by weight (snapper + clams +
  sambal for two ~IDR 350–600k), planes descending over
  the dark sea.

## Day 8 — Beach-club Bali (or waterpark Bali)

- **Morning** — Seminyak beach proper: flags respected,
  boards rentable, sun industrial-strength by 11.
- **13:00** — The **beach-club afternoon**: Potato
  Head/Ku De Ta-class daybeds run minimum spends
  (~IDR 350–500k pp — lunch and drinks absorb it); the
  budget version is a **La Plancha beanbag** at sunset
  for the price of a juice.
- **Family swap:** **Waterbom Bali** (Kuta; ~IDR 500k+
  adult) — consistently rated Asia's best waterpark, and
  it earns it.
- **19:30** — Seminyak dinner + night browse of the
  boutiques (Bali's homeware-and-linen shopping deserves
  suitcase space).

## Day 9 — Surf morning, slow goodbye

- **08:00** — **Surf lesson** (Seminyak/Kuta schools,
  IDR 350–500k for 2 hours, boards + coach included —
  Kuta's mushy beginner waves are famously kind), or a
  Sanur-side cycle for the flat-and-calm version of
  coastal Bali.
- **12:30** — Long lunch; final pool hours.
- **16:00** — Last-chance list: that unbought sarong, a
  silver ring from the Ubud class if you took it,
  reef-safe sunscreen restock for the flight-day beach
  hour.
- **19:00** — Farewell dinner: one more Jimbaran-style
  grill or Eat Street's best table, sunset from
  Double-Six rooftop if you kept it in reserve.

## Day 10 — Home from DPS

- **Morning** — Beach breakfast, then the airport (30–60
  traffic-minutes from Seminyak — pad it). Direct Gulf
  flight home, sarong in the hand luggage, zeros
  finally intuitive.

## What to pack

- Sarong + sash (or buy day one — every temple asks) and
  modest layers for holy sites.
- Reef-safe sunscreen (Penida's reefs), after-sun, rash
  vest, dry bag for boats and waterfalls.
- Grippy sandals for temple steps and waterfall rocks;
  proper trainers for Campuhan and Penida scrambles.
- Repellent (dawn/dusk), rehydration salts, basic meds —
  and the strays-are-not-pets resolve.
- A power bank for long driver days; Type C/F plugs
  (UAE-style two-pin works in most).
- Suitcase space: linen, silver, coffee and homewares
  have a way of committing to you.

## FAQ

**e-VoA or airport VoA?**
Same visa, same IDR 500k — the e-VoA just deletes a
queue after a 9-hour flight. Official portal only
(evisa.imigrasi.go.id); third-party sites charge
markups for form-filling. And don't skip the separate
**Love Bali levy QR** — spot checks are now routine at
major temples.

**What is Nyepi and can it really close the airport?**
Yes — completely. Nyepi (the Saka new year, usually
March) is a 24-hour island-wide silence: no traffic, no
lights, no leaving your hotel, **airport shut**. As a
guest inside a resort it's actually magical (stars like
you've never seen); as an arrival or departure date
it's a trip-wrecker. Check the date before booking
March.

**Driver or scooter?**
Driver. IDR 800k a day split two ways buys
air-conditioning, local knowledge and zero hospital
risk on roads that follow their own physics. Rent a
scooter only if you already ride at home, carry an IDP
+ motorcycle licence, and wear the helmet like you mean
it — insurers void claims without all three.

**Is Bali too crowded now?**
The famous 500 metres of it, yes — Kelingking's
viewpoint, Tegallalang's swing row, Canggu's shortcut.
The fix is timing (everything in this guide before 9
or after 4) and geography: Sidemen, Munduk, Amed and
West Bali are the empty-Bali sequels waiting for guide
#2. The magic hasn't left; it just sleeps in and
avoids traffic.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
A pleasant surprise both ways: Bali is Hindu, but
Indonesia is the world's largest Muslim country —
**Padang and Javanese Muslim warungs (halal by
default) sit on every main road**, Jimbaran's seafood
is unambiguous, and hotel breakfasts label pork
clearly. Vegetarians land in one of the world's
plant-based capitals — Ubud alone could feed a month
of it. The one watch-item: sambal and sauces
sometimes hide shrimp paste (terasi) — ask.

**What's Indonesia guide #2?**
Two contenders: **Java's icons** (Borobudur sunrise,
Prambanan, Mount Bromo's crater dawn) bolted to a
shorter Bali stay — or **slow-island Bali + the
Gilis** (Sidemen, Amed diving, car-free Gili air).
The volcano is currently winning.
