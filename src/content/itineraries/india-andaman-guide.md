A thousand kilometres east of the mainland, the Andamans are the
India nobody pictures: rainforest islands on the Bay of Bengal,
water in Maldives colours at a fraction of Maldives prices, and —
on Havelock — a beach TIME once ranked among the world's best. The
trip is three islands in one arc: Port Blair for arrival and its
haunting colonial jail, Havelock (officially Swaraj Dweep) for the
beaches and diving, little Neil (Shaheed Dweep) for the slow-bicycle
version of island life.

The catch — and the reason half the internet's Andaman plans go
wrong — is **logistics**: everything moves by ferry, December sells
out weeks ahead, and the last night must be spent in Port Blair
because ferries and flights don't forgive same-day gambles. This
guide is built around those rules.

**Go October–May.** December 20–January 5 is the surge (book
everything 6–8 weeks out); February–April has the calmest, clearest
water. Monsoon (roughly May–September) means rough seas and ferry
cancellations. **One more calendar trick: check the moon.** The
bioluminescence kayak — a genuine trip highlight — needs dark
skies, so aim your Havelock nights near the new moon.

All costs are in Indian rupees (INR), with conversions calculated
at today's exchange rate.

## Before you go: the short version of the admin

India visa/UPI/SIM basics live in our Golden Triangle guide.
Island specifics:

- **Foreigners: no special permit anymore.** The old Restricted
  Area Permit was abolished for the main tourist islands in 2018 —
  passports are simply registered on arrival at Port Blair.
  (Nicobar and tribal reserve areas remain strictly closed to
  everyone; nothing in this guide goes near them.)
- **Getting there:** fly to **Port Blair (IXZ)** — direct from
  Chennai (~2 h), Kolkata, Bengaluru, Delhi and Hyderabad. No
  international flights; Gulf readers connect via Chennai.
- **The ferries are the skeleton of the trip.** Private catamarans
  (Makruzz, Nautika, Green Ocean, ITT Majestic) run Port Blair ⇄
  Havelock ⇄ Neil: typically **₹1,100–1,800 per person per leg**
  (economy–premium; luxury cabins to ₹3,400), plus ~22% in GST and
  port fees at checkout. Morning sailings run cheaper; Green Ocean
  is the budget pick. **Book all three legs online as soon as
  dates are fixed** — the government ferries (₹400–700) are
  cheaper but near-impossible for tourists to secure.
- **Connectivity honesty:** the undersea cable improved things,
  but data is still patchy on Havelock and weak on Neil; ATMs are
  few and empty-able — **carry cash from Port Blair**. Download
  offline maps and tell people you'll be slow to reply. It's a
  feature.
- **The iron rule:** fly out only the day *after* your last ferry.
  This itinerary's final Port Blair night is not optional padding.

### Travelling with kids?

- Superb family destination: calm shallow water at Radhanagar and
  Bharatpur, glass-bottom boats for non-swimmers, sand for days.
- Ferry rides are short and air-conditioned; bring motion-sickness
  tablets for choppy days.
- Intro scuba is typically 10+; kids under that snorkel at
  Elephant Beach happily. Watch the sun — the equatorial UV is
  fiercer than anywhere on the mainland.

## Day 1 — Port Blair: arrival and the Cellular Jail

- **Midday** — Land at Port Blair, check in, seafood lunch.
- **15:00** — **Cellular Jail** (9:00–17:00, **closed Mondays**;
  foreigners ₹100) — the British "Kala Pani" prison where India's
  freedom fighters were held in solitary cells facing the sea.
  Sobering, essential context for everything the islands are.
- **18:00** — Stay for the **Light & Sound show** (evening slots
  ~18:00/19:15 by season; ~₹50; English shows on set days — have
  your hotel or an agent confirm and book, peak slots fill) — the
  jail's story told under floodlights.
- **20:00** — Dinner near Marina Park; early night — the ferry
  waits for no one.

**Where to stay in Port Blair** (nights 1 and 6)

- Comfortable, functional and near the jetty is the brief —
  Port Blair is a gateway, not the destination. Mid-range hotels
  run ₹3,500–6,000; pick by current reviews and jetty distance.

## Day 2 — Ferry to Havelock, sunset at Radhanagar

- **8:00** — Morning catamaran to **Havelock** (~90–120 min).
  Sit portside for island views; luggage limits are generous but
  real.
- **11:00** — Check in, rent a scooter (₹400–600/day; licence and
  helmet, as ever), long lunch.
- **15:30** — **Radhanagar Beach (Beach No. 7)** — the famous one:
  two kilometres of white sand and forest with not one shack on
  the sand. Swim between the flags (lifeguards on duty), then
  claim your spot for **sunset — the islands' best**.
- **19:30** — Dinner in the village — the café scene punches far
  above the island's size: grilled catch, wood-fired pizza,
  travellers' breakfasts all day.

**Where to stay on Havelock** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Taj Exotica Resort & Spa** — villas in the forest
  behind Radhanagar itself; the Andamans' one world-class
  address.
- *Mid-range:* **SeaShell Havelock** — superbly rated, private
  snorkelling reef, the reliable sweet spot.
- *Budget:* **Dolphin Resort** — sea-view rooms and sunsets at
  government-run prices; the beach-hut camps near the village
  are the backpacker tier.

## Day 3 — Elephant Beach: the reef day

- **8:30** — **Elephant Beach** by speedboat from the jetty
  (boat + snorkelling packages ~₹1,500–3,500 depending on
  inclusions — agree exactly what's in yours) — the island's
  liveliest shallow reef: coral heads, parrotfish, the occasional
  turtle metres from shore. Go early; by 11:00 the day-trip
  crowds land.
  - *The free alternative:* a ~40-minute jungle trek reaches the
    same beach at low tide — muddy, fun, bring shoes that agree.
- **12:30** — Back for lunch; afternoon nap is island law.
- **16:00** — **Kalapathar Beach** — the quiet black-rock beach at
  the island's far end; the scooter ride through the villages is
  half the pleasure.
- **19:00** — Book tomorrow's dive and confirm the night kayak;
  dinner and cards by candlelight when the power blips. It will.
  Charm, not crisis.

## Day 4 — First breath underwater, then the glowing sea

- **8:00** — **Intro scuba** — the Andamans are India's best
  first-dive water: warm, clear, instructor-held. Shore
  "discover" dives from ~₹4,500; proper boat intro dives from
  ~₹6,500 with the PADI/SSI schools (pick a school by reviews and
  small groups, not price). Certified divers: ask about
  **The Wall** and **Dixon's Pinnacle**.
- **13:00** — Long lunch, logbook bragging.
- **15:30** — Free hours: Radhanagar reprise, café crawl, or
  hammock audit.
- **19:30** — **Bioluminescent night kayaking** (~₹2,500/person,
  ~2 h, runs on dark-moon nights) — paddle the mangrove channels
  as every stroke ignites blue-green light. On a good night it's
  "your own private Milky Way" and the single most repeated
  sentence in Andaman reviews. This is why you checked the moon.

## Day 5 — Neil Island: the slow one

- **9:00** — Ferry **Havelock → Neil** (~45 min, ₹700–1,000).
  Neil is Havelock's little sibling: 8 km of flat lanes, paddy
  fields, three beaches and one bazaar.
- **10:30** — Check in; rent a **bicycle (₹100–200)** or scooter —
  Neil is the islands' best cycling.
- **11:30** — **Bharatpur Beach** by the jetty — glass-bottom
  boat over the coral (~₹500–800) for the non-swimmers' reef
  tour; shallow, calm swimming.
- **14:00** — Thali lunch in the bazaar.
- **16:00** — **The Natural Bridge** at Laxmanpur 2 — the coral
  rock arch, walkable **only at low tide** (check times locally;
  wear grippy sandals for the shelf).
- **17:30** — **Laxmanpur Beach 1** for sunset — wide white sand,
  pink sky, maybe ten other people.
- **20:00** — Early dinner; Neil sleeps by ten and so will you.

**Where to stay on Neil** (one night)

- Mid-range resorts (SeaShell and Summer Sands class,
  ₹4,000–8,000) and simple garden guesthouses (₹1,500–3,000)
  are the whole market — book ahead in season; walk-ins gamble.

## Day 6 — Sunrise, back to base, one last island

- **5:30** — **Sitapur Beach sunrise** — the east-facing beach
  does dawn the way Laxmanpur does dusk. Worth the alarm.
- **10:00** — Ferry **Neil → Port Blair** (~60–75 min); check in,
  lunch.
- **14:30** — Choose your finale: boat to **Ross Island (Netaji
  Subhash Chandra Bose Dweep)** — the ruined British capital
  being eaten by strangler figs, now patrolled by deer and
  peacocks (short boat + entry, ~₹500–800 all-in) — or drive to
  **Chidiya Tapu** for the sunset point and forest birdlife.
- **19:30** — Last seafood dinner; pack the sandy bag.

## Day 7 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Airport 15 minutes away; connect via Chennai or
  your mainland hub. You kept the buffer night, so today is
  boring — exactly as designed.

## What to pack

- Reef-safe sunscreen (the reefs are the product — protect them),
  hat, rash guard for snorkel days.
- Motion-sickness tablets for ferries and dive boats.
- **Cash from the mainland** — island ATMs are few and moody;
  UPI works where signal does.
- Water shoes or grippy sandals (coral shelves, the Natural
  Bridge, the Elephant Beach trek).
- Torch/headlamp (village lanes are dark), power bank, and a dry
  bag for boat days.
- Mosquito repellent for dusk; light long sleeves.
- A book — evenings are early, connectivity is thin, and that's
  the point.

## FAQ

**Andamans or Goa — which beach trip?**
Different products: Goa is scene, food and heritage with beaches
attached; the Andamans are the beaches themselves — clearer,
emptier, further — with logistics attached. Honeymoons and
divers: Andamans. First India beach trip with nightlife: Goa.

**Is one night on Neil enough?**
Yes for most — it's a one-road island and the
sunset-plus-sunrise pairing completes it. Slow travellers happily
double it; skipping Neil entirely to add a fourth Havelock night
is also a legitimate build.

**How real is the December sellout?**
Very. Ferries first, then Havelock's good rooms. If you're eyeing
Christmas–New Year, book at Diwali. February–April is the insider
window: same weather, calmer seas, saner prices.

**Can non-swimmers enjoy it?**
Completely: glass-bottom boats, shallow flag-zone wading at
Radhanagar and Bharatpur, and intro dives are literally
instructor-carried (many first-timers can't swim). The kayak
needs no skill beyond paddling.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Seafood-first islands make halal easy by default (confirm meat
specifics per kitchen); vegetarians do fine — thali houses in
every bazaar and the Havelock cafés cover it — though variety is
thinner than the mainland.

**What about Baratang and North Andaman?**
The limestone caves and mud volcanoes of Baratang involve long
convoy drives through tribal-reserve territory — ethically
murky (the road crosses Jarawa land) and a punishing day trip. We
leave it out deliberately; the three-island arc is the better
trip and the cleaner conscience.
