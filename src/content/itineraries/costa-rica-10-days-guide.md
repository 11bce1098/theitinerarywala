Costa Rica packs more into a small country than seems reasonable: an
active volcano, a cloud forest you walk through at canopy height, and
a Pacific coast where the monkeys come to the beach. It is also the
easiest wildlife trip in the Americas — no malaria on this route, tap
water you can drink, and a tourism industry that has been doing this
well for forty years.

The classic loop is **Arenal → Monteverde → the Pacific coast**, and
it works because each leg is different rather than more of the same.
Fly into **San José**, out of San José, and spend as little time
there as politeness allows — it is a transit city, not a destination.

Two things shape the trip more than anything in the brochures: **the
season**, which is binary here, and **the driving**, which is slower
than any map suggests.

## Before you go: seasons, driving and the parks

### Entry, and the season that decides everything

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for 90 days — the EU, UK,
  US, Canada, Australia and many others. Bring proof of onward
  travel; it is occasionally asked for and always satisfied by a
  return booking.
- **The season is not a preference, it is the trip.** **December to
  April is the dry season** — reliable sun, higher prices, busier
  parks. **May to November is the green season** — cheaper, emptier,
  spectacularly alive, and wet most afternoons. September and
  October are the wettest on the Pacific side.
- Green season is better value and better for wildlife. It is not
  better for lying on a beach.
- **There is an exit tax** on departure, usually bundled into the
  airfare. Check yours rather than assume.

### The driving, honestly

- **Distances are short and journeys are long.** Mountain roads,
  switchbacks, fog on the passes and one-lane bridges mean an
  80 km leg can take three hours. Plan by hours, never by
  kilometres, and do not drive the mountain roads after dark.
- **Rent a 4x4** if you are self-driving. Not for glamour — for
  ground clearance on the unpaved stretches around Monteverde.
- **The mandatory insurance** is the thing that surprises people:
  the headline rental rate excludes a compulsory liability policy
  that you cannot decline, and it often costs more than the car.
  Get the all-in quote in writing before you book.
- **The jeep-boat-jeep** between Arenal and Monteverde is the trip's
  best small decision: a minibus, a boat across Lake Arenal, another
  minibus up the other side. It takes about three hours against
  four-plus by road, and the boat is the nicest part of the day.
- Not driving? **Shared shuttles link every stop on this route** and
  cost far less than a car once insurance is counted.

### The parks, and when to be there

- **Wildlife is a morning activity.** Gates open early, animals are
  active early, and by midday the forest is quiet and hot. Every
  good day on this trip starts before seven.
- **Manuel Antonio caps daily entry and closes one day a week** —
  buy online in advance and check the closure day, which has moved
  before.
- **Hire a guide with a scope** at least once. The difference
  between walking past a sloth and being shown one is a guide, and
  it is the best money spent on the trip.
- Many private reserves — hanging bridges, night walks, the cloud
  forest reserves — are separately ticketed and worth it.

### Money and the small print

- **The colón (CRC)**, and prices are often quoted in **US dollars**
  as well. Both are accepted; you will usually get change in colones.
- Cards work widely. Carry cash for small sodas, roadside fruit and
  park guides.
- **Tap water is safe** on this route — a genuine rarity in the
  region.
- **"Pura vida" is not a slogan for tourists.** It is hello,
  goodbye, thank you and never mind, and using it correctly will get
  you further than Spanish.

## Day 1 — Land in San José

- **Afternoon** — Land, collect the car or meet the shuttle. Sleep
  near the airport or in **Alajuela** rather than the city centre;
  you are leaving early.

## Day 2 — North to Arenal

- **Morning** — Drive or shuttle to **La Fortuna**, about three
  hours.
- **Afternoon** — The volcano reveals itself when it feels like it.
  Settle in, and take the first clear view you get.
- **Evening** — **Hot springs**. There are resort versions with
  swim-up bars and a free river where locals go; both are the same
  water.

## Day 3 — The volcano, up close

- **Morning** — **Arenal Volcano National Park**: the lava-field
  trail and the lookout, early.
- **Afternoon** — **Hanging bridges** through the canopy, or the
  **La Fortuna waterfall** and its 500 steps down.
- **Evening** — A night walk, which is when the frogs and snakes
  come out.

## Day 4 — Jeep-boat-jeep to Monteverde

- **Morning** — The lake crossing: minibus, boat, minibus. Three
  hours door to door and the best transfer in the country.
- **Afternoon** — Arrive in **Santa Elena**. The temperature drops
  noticeably; this is cloud forest, not jungle.
- **Evening** — Book tomorrow's early reserve entry.

## Day 5 — Cloud forest

- **Early** — **Monteverde** or **Santa Elena** reserve at opening,
  with a guide. The quetzal, if you see one, is seen at dawn.
- **Afternoon** — Ziplines and canopy bridges, which were invented
  here and are done properly.
- **Evening** — A coffee or chocolate tour, better than it sounds
  and genuinely about the farming.

## Day 6 — Down to the Pacific

- **Morning** — The long leg: Monteverde down to the coast, roughly
  four hours, much of it unpaved at the start.
- **Afternoon** — Arrive at **Manuel Antonio** or **Quepos**. Swim.
- **Evening** — Sunset on the beach, which is the point of the
  second half of this trip.

## Day 7 — Manuel Antonio National Park

- **Early** — Be at the gate when it opens with a pre-booked ticket.
  Sloths, capuchins, howlers and coatis, on a park loop that ends at
  a beach inside the reserve.
- **Afternoon** — That beach. Guard your food from the monkeys, who
  are organised about it.
- **Evening** — Dinner overlooking the water.

## Day 8 — The coast your way

- Options: a **mangrove kayak**, a **catamaran** afternoon, surf
  lessons at **Playa Espadilla**, or a day doing none of it.

## Day 9 — Back toward San José

- **Morning** — The coastal road north, about three hours, or add
  **Carara** for the scarlet macaws near the river mouth.
- **Afternoon** — Sleep near the airport again.

## Day 10 — Fly home

- Early flights out of San José are the norm. Return the car with
  time to spare; the inspection is thorough.

## What to pack

- **Rain gear you will actually wear**, in either season.
- **Two pairs of shoes**: something that grips wet trails, and
  something you do not mind soaking.
- **Binoculars.** The single most useful object on this trip.
- **Type A/B plugs**, US-style, 120V.
- **Reef-safe sunscreen and strong insect repellent** for the coast.
- **A dry bag** for the boat crossing and the kayak day.

## FAQ

**Dry season or green season?**
Dry if you want guaranteed beach weather and will pay for it. Green
if you want the forest at its best, half-empty parks and lower
prices, and can accept rain most afternoons.

**Is ten days enough?**
For this loop, yes. Adding the Caribbean side or Corcovado means
adding four days, not two — they are a long way from this route.

**Do I need to drive?**
No. Shared shuttles connect every stop here and are cheaper for two
people. Drive if you want to stop at will, and only with a 4x4 and
daylight.

**Will we actually see wildlife?**
Yes, on this route, if you go early and hire a guide at least once.
Sloths and monkeys are near-certain at Manuel Antonio; quetzals are
luck plus an early start at Monteverde.
