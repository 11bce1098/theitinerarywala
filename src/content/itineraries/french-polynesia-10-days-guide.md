French Polynesia has a single image attached to it — an overwater
bungalow with a ladder into turquoise water — and that image costs
{{XPF 120000}} a night and keeps people from coming at all.

There is another version. **Pensions de famille** are
family-run guesthouses, usually with breakfast and dinner included,
at a fraction of the resort price, and they are how the islands
are actually travelled. With one of those and **Air Tahiti's
island-hopping pass**, ten days across four genuinely different
islands becomes an expensive trip rather than an impossible one.

And they are different. **Moorea** is a green cathedral of volcanic
spires thirty minutes by ferry from the capital. **Huahine** is
slow, agricultural and largely undeveloped. **Taha'a** smells of
vanilla. **Bora Bora**'s lagoon is the one thing here that is
exactly as advertised.

## Before you go: the pass, the pensions and the season

### Entry and money

- **French Polynesia is a French overseas collectivity but is
  outside the Schengen area** — it has its own entry rules. Many
  nationalities enter visa-free for up to 90 days; a Schengen visa
  does not automatically cover you here, and some nationalities
  need a separate visa. **Check your own case specifically** rather
  than assuming French rules apply.
- **The currency is the CFP franc (XPF)**, pegged to the euro at a
  fixed rate, so it does not swing.
- **Cards work** in Papeete, resorts and larger businesses. Pensions,
  roulottes, small boat operators and the outer islands often want
  cash. **Draw money in Papeete** — ATMs on the smaller islands are
  few and occasionally empty.
- Tipping is not expected.

### The Air Tahiti pass is the whole trip

- Inter-island flights are the only practical way between the
  Society Islands other than Tahiti–Moorea. Booked one by one they
  are punishing.
- **Air Tahiti sells multi-island passes** — the Bora Bora pass
  covers Moorea, Huahine, Raiatea, Taha'a and Bora Bora — at far
  less than the sum of the legs, with the condition that you travel
  in one direction around the loop and **book the whole thing before
  you start**.
- **Baggage allowance on inter-island flights is low.** Pack for it,
  or pay.
- **Tahiti to Moorea, take the ferry instead** — 30–45 minutes,
  several times a day, cheap, and you can bring a car.

### Pensions, and what they include

- **Pensions de famille** are family guesthouses, often with a
  half-board option (breakfast and dinner) which on the smaller
  islands is not optional in practice — there may be nowhere else
  to eat.
- **Book them well ahead.** Most have a handful of rooms.
- **Roulottes** are the food trucks that gather at Papeete's
  waterfront and in most towns each evening. They are excellent and
  they are what keeps the food budget survivable.

### Season

- **May to October** is the dry season: less humid, less rain, and
  the reason most people come.
- **July to October brings humpback whales**, and licensed
  operators run in-water encounters with them off Moorea and Rurutu.
  This is the single best reason to pick those months.
- **July is Heiva**, the month-long dance and sport festival in
  Papeete — extraordinary, and accommodation fills accordingly.
- **November to April** is hot, humid and the cyclone season. It is
  also cheaper and far quieter, and many days are simply fine.

## Day 1 — Land in Papeete

- **Afternoon** — Land at Faa'a. Most long-haul arrivals land in the
  morning, which gives you the day.
- **Evening** — The **roulottes at Place Vaiete** on the waterfront:
  grilled fish, chow mein, steak frites, from trucks, at tables on
  the quay.

## Day 2 — Tahiti's other side, then Moorea

- **Morning** — Drive the **Tahiti Iti** peninsula or the east coast
  to the **Arahoho blowhole** and the **Faarumai waterfalls**. Tahiti
  is treated as an airport and is worth a day.
- **Afternoon** — **Ferry to Moorea**, 30–45 minutes from Papeete.
- **Evening** — Cook's Bay or Opunohu Bay.

## Day 3 — Moorea's lagoon

- **Morning** — A **lagoon tour**: stingrays and blacktip sharks in
  waist-deep water, coral gardens, and a motu stop.
- **Afternoon** — **Whale encounter** if you are here between July
  and October, with a licensed operator.
- **Evening** — Sunset from the beach at Ta'ahiamanu.

## Day 4 — Moorea's interior

- **Morning** — The **Belvedere lookout** over both bays, the
  **Magic Mountain** track, and the pineapple fields.
- **Afternoon** — The **Three Coconuts** hike, or snorkelling.
- **Evening** — Last night on Moorea.

## Day 5 — To Huahine

- **Morning** — Fly to **Huahine**, 35 minutes.
- **Afternoon** — Settle in at **Fare**, the one small town.
- **Evening** — Sunset over the pass.

## Day 6 — Huahine, slow

- **Morning** — The **maraes at Maeva**, the largest concentration of
  pre-European stone temples in Polynesia, and the ancient stone
  fish traps still in the lagoon.
- **Afternoon** — Circle the island by scooter or car: vanilla,
  watermelon, the blue-eyed sacred eels at Faie, and almost no
  development.
- **Evening** — Dinner at the pension.

## Day 7 — Raiatea and Taha'a

- **Morning** — Fly to **Raiatea**, Polynesia's sacred island, and
  the **Taputapuatea marae** — a UNESCO site and the centre from
  which the great voyaging canoes departed.
- **Afternoon** — Boat across to **Taha'a**, the vanilla island.
- **Evening** — A pension on Taha'a, or a motu.

## Day 8 — The coral garden

- **Morning** — The **coral garden** between Taha'a and Motu Tautau:
  you enter at one end of a shallow channel and the current carries
  you over the coral to the other.
- **Afternoon** — A **vanilla plantation**, and a pearl farm.
- **Evening** — Quiet.

## Day 9 — Bora Bora

- **Morning** — Fly to **Bora Bora**. The approach over the lagoon
  is the point.
- **Afternoon** — A **lagoon tour**: manta rays, the coral gardens,
  and a motu lunch. Mount Otemanu above all of it.
- **Evening** — **Matira Beach**, the public beach, which is free and
  is the best beach on the island.

## Day 10 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Bora Bora back to Papeete, and the long flight out.
  Most international departures leave late at night, so the last day
  is usable.

## What to pack

- **Reef-safe sunscreen**, and more than you think. The sun here is
  severe.
- **Your own snorkel and mask.** You will use it every single day
  and rental gear is poor.
- **Reef shoes** for coral and stonefish.
- **Light long sleeves** for sun on boats.
- **Mosquito repellent** — dengue circulates.
- **European Type C/E plugs**, 220V.
- **Cash from Papeete** for the outer islands.
- **Pack light** — inter-island baggage limits are strict.

## FAQ

**Do I have to stay in an overwater bungalow?**
No, and most people who go for ten days do not. Family pensions
cost a fraction of the price, include meals, and put you with
people who live there. One resort night at the end is the usual
compromise if you want the photograph.

**Is Bora Bora worth it, or is it overhyped?**
The lagoon is genuinely the best of the group and the mountain
above it is extraordinary. The island itself is the most developed
and least interesting of the four here — which is exactly why it
belongs at the end of an island-hop rather than being the whole
trip.

**When should I go?**
**May to October** for dry weather. **July to October** if you want
to swim with humpback whales, which is the strongest single reason
to pick a month.

**How many islands in ten days?**
Four is right. Three is calmer. Five means you spend the trip in
airports — inter-island flights are short but the check-in and
transfer time is not.

**Is the Air Tahiti pass really necessary?**
If you are visiting more than two islands, yes. It costs roughly
half what the individual legs do, and the condition — travel in one
direction, book it all upfront — is easy to plan around.
