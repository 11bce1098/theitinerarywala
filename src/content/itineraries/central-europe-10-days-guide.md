Three capitals sit within four hours of each other by train, and the
railway between them is one of the reasons Europe is worth the
paperwork. **Prague** for the storybook version — a castle on a hill,
a bridge lined with saints, a square where the clock draws a crowd on
the hour. **Vienna** for the imperial one — Habsburg palaces, a
coffee-house culture that treats sitting down as a discipline, and
concert halls that still sound the way they were designed to.
**Budapest** for the one people remember longest — a parliament
building that looks staged, and a city that solved winter by building
swimming pools on top of its hot springs.

Ten days is enough for three nights in each, with two travel days
that are themselves worth having. Book it open-jaw — **into Prague,
home from Budapest** — and you never backtrack.

The catch is the small print: **three countries, three currencies**,
and only one of them uses the euro.

## Before you go: one visa, three currencies

### One Schengen visa covers all three

- **All three are in the Schengen area**, so one visa covers the
  trip. Apply to the country where you will spend the most nights —
  on this route that is a tie at three each, which means **you apply
  to the country you enter first**: the Czech Republic. The full
  process, the lead times and the documents that actually cause
  refusals are in our
  [Schengen visa guide](/guides/schengen-visa-how-it-works/).
- Visa-free nationalities need nothing beyond a passport, but the
  **EES biometric border** has been fully operational since April
  2026 — allow extra queue time on your first arrival.
- **Book flights open-jaw** (into Prague, out of Budapest). A return
  into one city costs you a wasted day going back for it.

### Three currencies, and the one trap

- **Czech Republic: the koruna (CZK)**, not the euro. Prices quoted
  in euros on a tourist-strip menu are a markup, not a convenience.
- **Austria: the euro.** The only one.
- **Hungary: the forint (HUF)**, not the euro either, whatever the
  menus near the river imply.
- **The exchange-office warning is real in Prague**, and the same
  trick appears in Budapest: storefronts advertising a great rate,
  with the real one applied after a "commission". Use bank-branded
  ATMs, decline the terminal's offer to charge you in your home
  currency, and walk past the blue Euronet machines in all three
  cities.
- Cards tap everywhere in all three capitals. Carry a small amount of
  local cash for markets, cloakrooms and the occasional bath locker.

### The trains are the trip

- **Prague → Vienna**, roughly **4 hours**, and **Vienna →
  Budapest**, roughly **2 hours 40**. Both run several times a day on
  comfortable intercity services.
- **Book the long legs in advance.** These operators release cheap
  advance fares two to three months out and raise them as seats sell
  — the same pattern as everywhere else in Europe, and a bigger
  saving than any pass delivers on a route this short. Our
  [rail pass guide](/guides/rail-passes-and-getting-around/) explains
  why a pass is the wrong instrument for three long legs.
- **No rail pass needed** for this route. Price the two journeys
  point-to-point; the arithmetic will not be close.
- Seat reservations are worth taking on the Prague–Vienna leg in
  summer, when it fills.

### The habits each city expects

- **Vienna's transport runs on trust** — no barriers on the U-Bahn,
  and inspectors who are not charmed by tourists. Validate your
  ticket.
- **Sundays close Austria.** Shops shut; museums, cafés and bakeries
  stay open. Plan Vienna's Sunday as a museum-and-coffee day and you
  will not notice.
- **The Viennese coffee house is a place to sit, not to queue.** You
  order, you stay, nobody hurries you, and asking for "a coffee" will
  get you a raised eyebrow — name one.
- **Budapest's baths have their own etiquette**: bring flip-flops and
  a towel, rent a cabin rather than a locker if you want somewhere to
  change in peace, and check which pools are swim-cap-only.

## Day 1 — Land in Prague, meet the Old Town

- **Afternoon** — Land, drop bags, and walk straight to the **Old
  Town Square**. The astronomical clock performs on the hour; it is
  brief, and the crowd is half the spectacle.
- **Late afternoon** — Cross **Charles Bridge** heading west, when
  the day-trip coaches have gone and the light is low.
- **Evening** — Dinner in the **Lesser Town** below the castle,
  where the streets empty early and the prices are calmer than on the
  square.

## Day 2 — Castle hill, done before the queues

- **08:00** — Be at **Prague Castle** when it opens. It is a walled
  district rather than a single building, and by eleven it is a
  procession.
- **Midday** — Walk down through the vineyard to the river rather
  than retracing the funicular.
- **Afternoon** — The **Jewish Quarter**: six synagogues and the old
  cemetery on one combined ticket, and the most affecting hour in the
  city.
- **Evening** — A beer hall. Czech beer is cheaper than water on the
  same menu, and the food is built for it.

## Day 3 — Prague at your own pace

- A free day, deliberately. Options that reward one: **Vyšehrad** for
  the view without the crowd, **Petřín** for the hill and the mirror
  maze, or the **Kutná Hora** day trip for the bone church, an hour
  each way by train.
- **Evening** — Pack. The train south is a morning departure and you
  want to be on it.

## Day 4 — The train to Vienna

- **Morning** — **Prague → Vienna**, about four hours. Sit on the
  right leaving Prague for the river. The train has a restaurant car
  and the countryside earns the window seat.
- **Afternoon** — Drop bags, then walk the **Ringstrasse** loop: the
  opera, the parliament, the city hall, each one built to be looked
  at.
- **Evening** — **Stephansdom** at dusk, then dinner in the lanes
  behind it.

## Day 5 — Habsburg Vienna

- **Morning** — **Schönbrunn**, early. The state rooms fill from
  mid-morning; the gardens behind are free and worth an hour.
- **Afternoon** — The **Hofburg** complex, or the **Kunsthistorisches
  Museum** if you would rather have the Bruegels than another palace.
- **Evening** — A concert. Vienna sells a lot of tourist-facing
  Mozart; the **Musikverein** and the **Staatsoper** sell the real
  thing, and standing tickets at the opera cost less than a pizza.

## Day 6 — Coffee, cake and the other Vienna

- **Morning** — A proper coffee house, unhurried. **Café Central**
  is the famous one and the queue reflects it; a dozen others are
  better and emptier.
- **Afternoon** — The **Naschmarkt** for lunch, then the
  **Belvedere** for Klimt's *The Kiss*, which is smaller and better
  than the posters suggest.
- **Evening** — Pack for the short hop east.

## Day 7 — The train to Budapest, and the river at night

- **Morning** — **Vienna → Budapest**, under three hours.
- **Afternoon** — Drop bags and walk the **Danube promenade** on the
  Pest side, past the shoes on the embankment.
- **Evening** — The single best hour of the trip: **a night cruise**
  or simply the walk across the Chain Bridge, with Parliament lit
  from the water.

## Day 8 — Buda hill, and the baths

- **Morning** — **Castle Hill**: the Fisherman's Bastion turrets are
  free before 9am, and the view across to Parliament is the shot
  everyone comes for.
- **Afternoon** — **Széchenyi** or **Gellért** baths. Széchenyi is
  the big outdoor one where people play chess in the steam; Gellért
  is the art-nouveau one. Three hours minimum.
- **Evening** — A ruin bar in the old Jewish quarter, which is more
  interesting than its reputation suggests.

## Day 9 — Budapest at your pace

- Options: **Parliament's interior tour** (book ahead, it sells out),
  the **House of Terror**, the **Great Market Hall**, or the train up
  the **Danube Bend** to Szentendre.
- **Evening** — Last dinner. Hungarian food is heavier than the other
  two capitals and better than either at half the price.

## Day 10 — Fly home from Budapest

- A slow morning, then out. Budapest's airport is small and calm by
  European standards.

## What to pack

- **European Type C/F plugs** in all three — one adapter covers the
  whole trip.
- **Shoes for cobbles.** All three old towns are paved in them, and
  Prague's castle hill is a climb.
- **Swimwear, flip-flops and a quick-dry towel** for Budapest. Most
  baths rent towels, none of them cheaply.
- **A layer more than you think** between October and April. These
  are continental cities: colder than the coast, and drier.

## FAQ

**Is ten days enough for three capitals?**
For three nights each, yes, and that is the right amount. Two nights
per city means you spend the trip packing; four means you run out of
things you actually wanted to see.

**Which direction — Prague first or Budapest first?**
Prague first, as written. It is the gentlest introduction, the
trains run downhill in cost as you go east, and ending in Budapest
means ending on the baths.

**Could I add a fourth city?**
Bratislava sits an hour from Vienna and is a common add. It is a
pleasant half-day, not a stop worth a night on a trip this length.

**Do I need three sets of cash?**
Cards cover almost everything. Take a small amount of koruna and
forint from bank ATMs on arrival in each, and do not change money at
a storefront in either city.
