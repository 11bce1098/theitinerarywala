Montenegro is smaller than Wales and holds more landscape than
countries ten times its size. The **Bay of Kotor** is not a fjord —
it is a submerged river canyon — but it looks like one, with
mountains dropping straight into water and Venetian towns along the
shore. Behind it, an hour inland, the **Durmitor** massif has
Europe's deepest canyon and a glacial lake ringed by pine.

The catch is timing. **Kotor takes cruise ships**, and on a day with
two in port the old town is a slow-moving crowd. Before nine and
after six it is one of the loveliest towns on the Adriatic.

Seven days covers the bay, the mountains and the coast, and you will
drive most of it — which is where the other honest brief comes in.

## Before you go: driving, cruise ships and the euro question

### Entry, and the currency oddity

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for 90 days — the EU, UK,
  US, Canada, Australia and many others.
- **Montenegro is not in the EU or Schengen**, so your time here
  does not count against a Schengen allowance. Useful if you are
  combining with Croatia.
- **It uses the euro anyway**, unilaterally and without being in the
  eurozone. Prices are in euros and cards work widely.

### The driving, honestly

- **Hire a car.** The bay, Durmitor and the coast are not usefully
  connected by public transport, and the roads are the point.
- **The Kotor–Lovćen serpentine** climbs the mountain behind Kotor
  in twenty-five numbered hairpins, with the bay unfolding below.
  It is narrow, it has passing places rather than two lanes in
  sections, and it is spectacular. **Go up it, not down**, and go
  early.
- **The P-1 to Durmitor** crosses the **Đurđevića Tara bridge** over
  a 150 m gorge, and is a good road.
- **The Sozina tunnel** is the fast way between the coast and
  Podgorica and costs a small toll.
- **Parking in Kotor and Budva in summer is genuinely difficult.**
  Stay somewhere with parking and walk in.

### The cruise-ship rule

- **Check the Kotor cruise schedule** for your dates — it is
  published online — and plan your old-town time around it.
- **Two ships in port** means several thousand extra people in a
  town you can walk across in ten minutes.
- **Climb the fortress walls at 7am.** It is 1,350 steps to San
  Giovanni, it is hot by nine, and the light and the emptiness are
  both better early.

### Season

- **June and September are the answer** — warm sea, open mountain
  roads, and manageable crowds.
- **July and August** on the coast are busy and considerably more
  expensive; Budva in August is a different country.
- **Durmitor's high roads can hold snow into May** and close again
  from November. Check if you are going at the edges.

## Day 1 — Land, and into the bay

- **Afternoon** — Fly to **Tivat** or **Podgorica**, collect the
  car, drive to **Kotor** — 20 minutes from Tivat.
- **Evening** — The old town after the ships have gone: Venetian
  squares, a cathedral from 1166, and cats everywhere, which is a
  local institution.

## Day 2 — The walls, and the bay towns

- **07:00** — The **fortress walls** to **San Giovanni**, 1,350
  steps, before the heat and the crowds.
- **Late morning** — **Perast**, a single street of baroque palaces,
  and the boat to **Our Lady of the Rocks**, an artificial island
  built by sailors dropping stones for four centuries.
- **Afternoon** — Swim off the bay, which is calm and cold and clear.
- **Evening** — Dinner in Perast or Dobrota, away from Kotor's
  main square.

## Day 3 — The serpentine and Lovćen

- **Morning** — Up the **twenty-five hairpins** to the **Kotor
  serpentine viewpoint**, then on to **Lovćen National Park** and the
  **Njegoš mausoleum** at 1,657 m, reached by 461 steps through the
  mountain.
- **Afternoon** — **Cetinje**, the old royal capital, small and
  faded and interesting.
- **Evening** — Back down to the bay, or on toward the north.

## Day 4 — North to Durmitor

- **Morning** — The drive north, about three hours, over the
  **Đurđevića Tara bridge** with the **Tara Canyon** — Europe's
  deepest, second only to the Grand Canyon worldwide — 150 m below.
- **Afternoon** — **Žabljak**, the highest town in the Balkans, and
  **Black Lake** at the foot of the massif.
- **Evening** — Mountain food, which is meat and cheese and
  unapologetic.

## Day 5 — Durmitor

- **Option A** — The **Black Lake circuit** and up toward
  **Ćurevac** for the canyon view.
- **Option B** — **Rafting the Tara**, which runs roughly May to
  October and is the classic Montenegrin activity.
- **Option C** — The **Ring Road** loop through the park, which is
  an afternoon of driving through alpine meadow.

## Day 6 — South to the coast

- **Morning** — The long drive south, three to four hours.
- **Afternoon** — **Sveti Stefan**, the fortified islet turned
  hotel, photographed from the road above since you cannot walk onto
  it, then **Budva**'s old town.
- **Evening** — A beach near **Petrovac**, which is calmer than
  Budva.

## Day 7 — Fly home

- Tivat and Podgorica are both within about ninety minutes of the
  southern coast. Return the car with time for the inspection.

## What to pack

- **Proper shoes for the walls** — 1,350 uneven steps, and people
  do it in flip-flops and regret it.
- **Layers for Durmitor**, which is 1,450 m and cold at night even
  in July.
- **Swimwear and water shoes** — the beaches are pebble.
- **European Type C/F plugs.**
- **A small amount of cash**; cards are widely taken but not
  universally.

## FAQ

**Is Montenegro just cheaper Croatia?**
It is cheaper, and the bay is genuinely comparable to anything in
Dalmatia. What it does not have is Croatia's island network. What it
does have is Durmitor an hour and a half inland, which Croatia has
no answer to.

**Do I need a car?**
For this itinerary, yes. Buses link the coastal towns but not
Durmitor or Lovćen, and the serpentine is the best road in the
country.

**How bad are the cruise crowds?**
Bad enough to plan around, and easy to plan around. Check the
schedule, climb the walls at seven, and see the old town before nine
or after six.

**When should I go?**
**June or September.** Warm sea, open mountain roads, and none of
August's crush on the coast.
