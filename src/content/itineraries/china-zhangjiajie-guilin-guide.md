Our [first China guide](/itineraries/china-10-days-guide/) was civilisation:
palaces, warriors, skylines. This one is what the same country
looks like when geology does the building. In northwest Hunan,
three thousand quartzite towers rise out of subtropical mist —
the mountains that became Pandora in *Avatar*. Six hundred
kilometres south, the Li River winds through karst cones that
Chinese painters have been trying to get down on silk for a
thousand years, and the ¥20 note carries the proof.

This is a **nature-first, legs-required** trip — cable cars and
eco-buses do the heavy lifting, but 15,000-step days are
standard — and it runs deeper into "real China" than the golden
route: less English, fewer foreigners, more pointing at menus.
Which is exactly the appeal.

**Everything in our first guide's setup section applies here,
harder:** the visa (CVASC, ~AED 270–320 single-entry for Indian
passports from the UAE), **Alipay configured before you fly**,
and the **foreign eSIM** that keeps WhatsApp and Google alive.
Read [that section](/itineraries/china-10-days-guide/) first; this guide
assumes it's done.

**Go mid-September to mid-November** — the acknowledged sweet
spot: clear air, golden terraces, mild sun. April–June is green
and misty (the pillars *floating* in cloud is a feature, not a
failure); July–August is hot, wet and crowded; **never Golden
Week (Oct 1–7)**, when the glass bridge queue becomes the
attraction. All costs are in Chinese yuan (CNY).

## Getting in and out (read once, relax after)

- **Fly into Zhangjiajie (DYG), out of Guilin (KWL)** — both
  connect through Chinese hubs (Guangzhou, Kunming, Shanghai,
  Chongqing) on one ticket from the Gulf; there are no direct
  international long-hauls. Book the whole journey as one
  itinerary so the domestic legs check your bags through.
- Between the two: **one direct bullet train a day** — D3967,
  Zhangjiajie West 07:12 → Guilin 14:13, **~7 h, ¥342 second
  class**. Miss it and you connect via Changsha South (21
  trains daily to Changsha, then onward — figure 8+ hours and
  ~¥370+). This single-train fact shapes the itinerary below;
  book it the day the ~2-week window opens (Trip.com queues
  it).
- **Real-name everything**: park tickets, cable cars and trains
  all book against your passport — carry it daily.

## Day 1 — Arrive Wulingyuan, the pillars at dusk

Stay in **Wulingyuan town** — the park's east gate is walkable,
restaurants line the stream, and dawn entries become possible.

- **Afternoon** — Land DYG, taxi/DiDi to Wulingyuan (~40 min,
  ¥100–130). Check in, buy tomorrow's park tickets on the
  official WeChat/Trip.com channel tonight.
- **17:30** — Walk the stream to the park gate for a first
  look at the sandstone wall glowing orange, then the town's
  night market: Sangzhi salted fish, sour-and-spicy Hunan
  noodles (~¥25–40) — Hunan chilli is real; "wei la" (mildly
  spicy) is a useful lie to tell yourself.

**Where to stay in Wulingyuan** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Pullman Zhangjiajie** — 4.7/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~2,200 reviews), resort grounds five minutes from the
  gate, pool and mountain views. ~¥600–900.
- *Boutique:* **Lee's Boutique Resort** — 4.9/5, spotless
  rooms and a breakfast guests rave about, park-gate
  district. ~¥350–500.
- *Value:* **Neodalle Zhangjiajie Wulingyuan** — 4.2/5,
  walk-to-everything location. ~¥250–350.

## Day 2 — Avatar day: Yuanjiajie and Tianzi Mountain

**The ticket maths, once:** park entry is a flat **¥236**
(4-day validity, eco-buses included, under-14s/1.3 m free-ish
tiers apply) — but **every cable car and lift costs extra**.
The **¥298 unlimited combo** (all cable cars + the Bailong
Elevator, all four days) beats paying per ride (¥65–76 each)
the moment you ride three. Buy both together.

- **07:00** — In the gate at opening; eco-bus to the
  **Bailong Elevator** — a glass lift bolted onto a cliff,
  326 m in 88 seconds (the world's tallest outdoor elevator).
- **08:00** — **Yuanjiajie** before the tour groups: the
  **Avatar Hallelujah Mountain** (the floating-peak
  prototype), the **First Bridge Under Heaven** — a natural
  stone span between towers. The platforms are busiest
  09:30–15:00; you'll have beaten them.
- **11:00** — Bus onward and up to **Tianzi Mountain**: the
  emperor's-view balconies over a thousand pinnacles, McDonald's
  at 1,200 m (China commits), and the He Long Park lookouts.
- **14:00** — Noodle lunch at a summit stall (~¥30), then
  cable car down (covered by the combo).
- **16:00** — Decompress in **Ten-Mile Gallery** by open tram
  (¥38/76 return — or walk it, flat and pretty) under peaks
  named for what they resemble; the "Three Sisters" require
  the least imagination.
- **19:00** — Hotpot back in town — Hunan's dry-pot version,
  order rice early and often (~¥60–100 a head).

## Day 3 — Golden Whip Stream and the quiet side

- **08:00** — Walk the **Golden Whip Stream**: 7.5 flat
  kilometres along a creek at the *bottom* of the pillars —
  the inverse of yesterday, towers vertical overhead, wild
  **macaques** working the crowd (zip your snacks; they've
  unzipped better tourists than you).
- **11:30** — Cable car or hike up **Yangjiajie** for the
  wilder, rawer platforms — Natural Great Wall, One Step to
  Heaven — most tours skip it entirely, which is the
  recommendation.
- **15:00** — Optional third helping (Huangshizhai's 360°
  mesa, cable car included in the combo) or early return for
  a foot massage (~¥100/hr) — day three's legs will vote.
- **19:30** — Optional: the **Charming Xiangxi show**
  (~¥230–280) — Tujia and Miao folk theatre, fire-and-drums
  spectacle; touristy, warm-hearted, kids love it.

## Day 4 — The Grand Canyon glass bridge, then into the city

- **08:00** — Bus/DiDi (~30 min) to **Zhangjiajie Grand
  Canyon**: the **glass-bottom bridge** — 430 m long, 300 m
  above the gorge floor, the span that launched a thousand
  knee-wobble videos. Shoe covers on, phones strapped.
  **Canyon + bridge combos from ¥175** (bridge-only and
  canyon-hike variants exist; the combo with the gorge walk —
  waterfalls, cliff stairs, a boat exit — is the full
  experience). Book a morning slot; it caps daily entries.
- **12:30** — Lunch, then transfer to **Zhangjiajie city**
  (~1 h), checking in near the Tianmen cableway base.
- **16:00** — Ride the escalator-era wonder that is downtown
  Zhangjiajie's food street; watch **Tianmen Mountain's**
  cliff-face light up at dusk — tomorrow you're up there.
- **19:30** — Sour-fish-soup dinner (~¥50–80 a head), early
  night: the cableway queue rewards the early.

**Where to stay in Zhangjiajie city** (two nights)

- *The picks:* **Hilton Garden Inn Tianmen Mountain** —
  4.8/5, newly built at the mountain's foot ("great location,
  mountain views"), ~¥350–500 — and **Hampton by Hilton
  Tianmen Mountain** — 4.3/5, some rooms watch the cable
  cars sail past, ~¥280–400. Both are new-generation
  foreigner-easy hotels; review counts are still small
  because they're new, not because they're unloved.

## Day 5 — Heaven's Gate

- **07:00** — Queue at the **Tianmen Mountain** cableway for
  the first cars: the **~¥285 A-route package** covers entry
  plus the mountain's transport spine — including the
  **7.5-km cable car**, one of the world's longest, rising
  straight out of the city over 99 switchbacks.
- **08:30** — On top: the **cliff-hanging glass skywalks**
  (¥5 shoe covers, courage BYO), the coiled plank roads, and
  the forest boardwalk loop with the drop always at your
  elbow.
- **11:00** — Descend to **Heaven's Gate itself** — the
  131-m-high natural arch punched through the mountain, cloud
  pouring through it on the right mornings. The 999 steps
  below it are for photographs (escalators inside the
  mountain do the actual work).
- **14:30** — Down by cableway, late lunch, pack.
- **Evening** — Free for the food street, a massage, or an
  early night — **the 07:12 train waits for no one.**

## Day 6 — The one train south, Guilin by afternoon

- **07:12** — **D3967 to Guilin** (~7 h, ¥342): Hunan's
  gorges give way to Guangxi's cones — a scenery transfer as
  much as a transport one. Snacks from the station shop;
  dining car exists.
- **14:13** — Arrive Guilin, check in by the lakes.
- **16:00** — Gentle re-entry: **Elephant Trunk Hill** from
  the free riverside viewpoints, then the **Two Rivers and
  Four Lakes** waterfront as the pagodas light up (the Sun
  and Moon Pagodas double on the water).
- **19:30** — Guilin rice noodles for dinner the way locals
  eat them (~¥15–25) plus beer fish reconnaissance for
  Yangshuo.

**Where to stay in Guilin** (two nights, plus your Yangshuo
bags can loop back here)

- *Luxury:* **Shangri-La Guilin** — 4.8/5 (~5,300 reviews),
  Li River and karst views from the pool lawn; the classic.
  ~¥700–1,000.
- *Boutique:* **Aroma Tea House** — 4.6/5, antique-wood
  calm by the lakes. ~¥300–450.
- *Central value:* **Lijiang Waterfall Hotel** — 4.0/5
  (~1,200 reviews), tired but grand, unbeatable position
  (and yes, the building performs a waterfall at 20:30).
  ~¥350–500.

## Day 7 — The Longji rice terraces

- **07:30** — Day trip north (~2.5 h each way; tours
  ~¥250–350 pp with transfer + entry, or DIY bus): the
  **Longji "Dragon's Backbone" terraces** — 700 years of
  hand-carved contour lines stacked 800 m up the hillsides.
- **10:30** — Walk village to viewpoint from **Ping'an**
  (classic, easier) or **Dazhai** (bigger amphitheatres,
  cable car option): rice-terrace panoramas, Yao women's
  floor-length hair, bamboo-tube rice lunch (~¥30–50).
  **Entry ~¥80** where not bundled.
- **Season honesty:** May–June the terraces are mirrors
  (flooded), July–August electric green, **late September–
  October gold** — and post-harvest winter is brown geometry.
  Match your month to your expectations.
- **18:30** — Back to Guilin; pack a small overnight bag —
  big luggage can stay with your Guilin hotel through the
  Yangshuo leg (every hotel here is used to it).

## Day 8 — The Li River cruise: riding the ¥20 note

- **08:30** — Transfer to the pier for the **Li River cruise
  to Yangshuo**: 4–5 hours, 60+ km of karst theatre. Boat
  classes: **3-star ~¥215** (+ simple lunch), **4-star
  ¥360–480 with buffet and an open top deck** — worth it for
  the deck alone. Right side seats going south get slightly
  more of the famous bank, but everyone ends up on the roof
  anyway.
- **11:00** — The reaches roll past on cue: Nine-Horse
  Fresco cliff (count your horses; nine means genius),
  **Yellow Cloth Shoal — the exact scene on the ¥20 note**
  (hold a note up; everyone does; do it anyway), cormorant
  fishermen posing for their pension.
- **13:30** — Dock at Yangshuo; check in, ice cream on
  **West Street** while it's still calm.
- **16:00** — Cycle or e-bike (~¥30–60/day) into the
  countryside — the ten-minute rule: ten minutes from West
  Street, rural Guangxi has you to itself.
- **19:00** — **Beer fish** — Yangshuo's dish, river fish
  braised with Liquan beer, tomatoes and chilli (~¥80–120
  for the pan) — then West Street's neon circus, once,
  for science.

**Where to stay in Yangshuo** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Banyan Tree Yangshuo** — 5.0/5 (~28,600
  reviews), pavilion suites in the karst amphitheatre.
  ~¥1,500+.
- *The riverside classic:* **Yangshuo Mountain Retreat** —
  4.7/5 (~1,500 reviews), on the Yulong bank with the peaks
  in the breakfast window; long the foreigners' favourite,
  deservedly. ~¥450–650.
- *Boutique value:* **Yangshuo Sugar House** — 4.8/5, Li
  River views away from the noise. ~¥350–500.

## Day 9 — Rafts, Moon Hill and the river on fire

- **08:30** — **Yulong River bamboo raft**: two per raft,
  a poler, and 90 minutes of drifting under peaks and over
  little weirs (splash guaranteed; ~¥200–260 per raft).
  Morning light, empty river.
- **11:00** — Pedal the **Yulong valley loop**: Moon Hill's
  arch (20-min climb for the view through it), farmhouse
  lunch (~¥40), the villages doing their thousand-year
  thing.
- **15:00** — Bus/boat to **Xingping old town** for the
  ¥20-note viewpoint at river level and cormorant-fisherman
  photo hour at golden light; back by dark.
- **20:00** — **Impression Liu Sanjie** (from **~¥238**,
  better seats ¥300+): Zhang Yimou's night show performed
  ON the Li River — 600 locals, twelve lit karst peaks as
  the set, fishermen's torches on black water. Touristy and
  transcendent are not opposites here. Book a day or two
  ahead in season.

## Day 10 — Home from Guilin

- **Morning** — Bus or DiDi back to Guilin (~1.5 h),
  collect the big bags, KWL and home via your Chinese hub.
  You'll spend the first flight scrolling your own photos
  in disbelief, which is the correct souvenir.

## What to pack

- Grippy, broken-in walking shoes — this trip is stairs with
  scenery attached.
- A packable rain shell and a dry bag: mist is the region's
  business model, and the bamboo raft splashes.
- Layers — summit cable stations run 5–8°C cooler than town;
  autumn evenings bite.
- Power bank (apps run the day), passport pouch (it *is*
  your ticket), motion-sickness tabs for the Longji
  switchbacks.
- Snack discipline equipment: zips the macaques can't work.
- A crisp **¥20 note** for the Yellow Cloth Shoal photo —
  the cheapest prop in world tourism.

## FAQ

**Is this doable without a tour company?**
Yes — that's how this guide is built: real-name tickets
online, eco-buses inside the park, one direct train, tours
only where they beat DIY (Longji, and arguably Chichén-style
convenience for the cruise transfer). The catch vs the
golden route: much less English. Alipay, a translation app
and hotel-name screenshots carry you.

**Glass bridge or Tianmen skywalk — and are they scary?**
Different fears: the bridge is 300 m of air *under* your
feet; Tianmen's skywalk is a cliff at your *shoulder*. Do
both; the bridge queues harder (book the morning slot).
Genuine vertigo sufferers can skip the glass entirely and
lose little — the ordinary boardwalks are the real show.

**How much walking, really — and with kids or older
parents?**
Plan on 12,000–18,000 steps on park days, mostly flat but
relentless. The system is merciful: elevators, cable cars
and eco-buses bypass every brutal climb (that's what the
¥298 combo buys). Kids do brilliantly (macaques, glass,
rafts); take the escalators inside Tianmen rather than the
999 steps, and give older knees the Huangshizhai skip.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Thinner than Xi'an, workable with the standard kit: Hui-run
**Lanzhou lamian** shops (halal by definition) exist in
Zhangjiajie city, Guilin and Yangshuo's West Street; Guilin
rice noodles come veg-able ("su de"); beer fish is the
region's halal-friendly signature if fish qualifies for
you. Hunan cooking loves pork fat — say "bu yao zhurou,
bu yao zhuyou" (no pork, no lard) and deploy the
translation app. Self-catering fruit from markets fills
gaps.

**When exactly for the floating-mountain mist vs golden
terraces?**
You can't max both: **April–June** gives Zhangjiajie its
floating-in-cloud look (and rain roulette); **late
September–October** gives dry trails, blue skies and
Longji's gold. This guide's default is autumn; romantics
with rain shells choose spring.
