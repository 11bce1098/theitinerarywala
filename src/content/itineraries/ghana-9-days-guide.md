Ghana is where most people start in West Africa, and the reasons
are practical: **English is the official language**, it is one of
the more stable democracies on the continent, and the distances
between the things worth seeing are manageable.

It is also where the Atlantic slave trade is least abstract.
**Cape Coast Castle** and **Elmina Castle** stand on the shore
within twenty minutes of each other — whitewashed, UNESCO-listed,
and holding dungeons where people were kept for months before
being walked through a doorway onto a ship. You go into those
rooms. The guides are Ghanaian and they do not soften it.

The rest of the country is lighter: Ashanti craft villages still
working in kente and brass, a rainforest canopy walkway at Kakum,
and the Volta region's waterfalls and monkey sanctuaries.

## Before you go: visas, roads and the harmattan

### Entry and health

- **Most nationalities need a visa in advance**, applied for
  online or at a mission before travel. Start this early — it is
  the single biggest piece of admin on this trip.
- **Yellow fever vaccination is mandatory** and the certificate is
  checked on arrival. Without it you will not be let in.
- **Malaria is present year-round and Ghana is a high-transmission
  country.** Take prophylaxis properly, from before you arrive to
  after you leave.

### Getting around

- **Hire a car with a driver.** It is the normal arrangement, it
  costs far less than you would expect, and it removes the two
  real difficulties: Accra's traffic and finding anywhere without
  local knowledge. Budget roughly {{GHS 900}} a day including fuel.
- **Roads between the main towns are tarred and fine.** The
  Accra–Cape Coast road is good; the road north to Kumasi is
  mostly good and sometimes not.
- ***Tro-tros*** — shared minibuses — are how Ghanaians travel,
  cost almost nothing, and leave when full rather than on a
  timetable. Worth doing once.
- **Accra traffic is severe.** Allow double what the distance
  suggests for anything crossing the city at rush hour.

### When to go

- **November to March is the dry season** and the best time, with
  **December and January** the most comfortable.
- **The harmattan** blows Saharan dust south roughly December to
  February: hazy skies, cooler nights, and poor long-distance
  visibility. It makes photographs flat and the heat bearable.
- **April to July is the main wet season** — heavy afternoon rain,
  green landscape, fewer visitors.
- **Note on timing:** the **Year of Return** and its successors
  have made **December** extremely busy in Accra, with high
  prices and full flights.

### Money and courtesies

- **Ghanaian cedi (GHS).** Cash dominates outside hotels; **mobile
  money** is near-universal and cards are not. Draw cash in Accra.
- **Greet before you transact.** Walking up and asking a question
  without a *good morning* and a handshake reads as rude. The
  handshake often ends with a finger snap, which is worth learning.
- **Use your right hand** for giving, receiving and eating.
- **Ask before photographing people**, and always at the castles,
  the markets and in villages.

## Day 1 — Land in Accra

- **14:00** — Land at **Kotoka (ACC)**, which is inside the city
  — 20 minutes to Osu in light traffic and an hour in real
  traffic.
- **15:30** — Drop bags. **Osu** and **Airport Residential** are
  the convenient areas; **Jamestown** is where the city started
  and is better visited than slept in.
- **17:00** — **Independence Square** and **Black Star Gate** —
  Ghana was **the first sub-Saharan African country to gain
  independence**, in 1957, and the square was built to say so.
- **18:00** — The **Kwame Nkrumah Memorial Park**, with the
  first president's mausoleum and the statue the British-era
  authorities once toppled, reassembled.
- **19:30** — Dinner in Osu. ***Jollof rice*** — and Ghanaians
  will tell you unprompted that Nigeria's is inferior, which is a
  genuine and good-humoured national argument — plus *waakye*,
  *banku* with tilapia, and grilled kelewele.
- **21:00** — Confirm the driver and the route for the week.

## Day 2 — Accra, and Jamestown

- **08:30** — **Jamestown** with a local guide, and go with one:
  this is a dense working fishing quarter below the colonial
  lighthouse, and a guided walk is both more informative and more
  welcome than wandering with a camera.
- **09:30** — Climb the **lighthouse** for the view over the
  fishing harbour, then down into it — hundreds of painted canoes,
  the catch being landed and sorted, and a crowd that is entirely
  indifferent to you.
- **11:30** — **Makola Market**, which is enormous and
  overwhelming and the commercial centre of the city.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then out of the heat.
- **15:00** — The **W.E.B. Du Bois Centre**, where the American
  scholar lived out his last years and is buried, having taken
  Ghanaian citizenship at 95.
- **17:00** — **Labadi Beach** late afternoon, when half of Accra
  arrives and there is drumming, horses, and a great deal going on.
- **19:30** — Dinner, and live **highlife** if it is a weekend.

## Day 3 — West to Cape Coast

- **08:00** — Drive west along the coast, about three hours.
- **11:00** — Stop at **Elmina**, and start here rather than at
  Cape Coast. **São Jorge da Mina** was built by the **Portuguese
  in 1482** — **the oldest European building in sub-Saharan
  Africa** — taken by the Dutch, then the British, and used
  throughout for the same trade.
- **11:30** — The guide takes you through the **female dungeon**,
  the punishment cell with no ventilation, the church built
  directly above the dungeon, and the **Door of No Return**. The
  governor's quarters are airy and have a sea view. That
  juxtaposition is the entire point and the guides make sure it
  lands.
- **13:30** — The **Elmina fishing harbour** below the castle,
  which is one of the most photographed and most alive places in
  Ghana.
- **15:00** — Check in at **Cape Coast**, 20 minutes on.
- **17:00** — Walk the town — the old post office, the
  colonial-era streets.
- **19:30** — Dinner, early. Tomorrow is heavy.

## Day 4 — Cape Coast Castle, and Kakum

- **08:00** — **Cape Coast Castle** at opening, when it is quiet.
  Built by the Swedes in 1653, enlarged by the British, and the
  headquarters of the Gold Coast administration.
- **08:30** — The tour goes down into the **male dungeon**: a
  stone chamber that held **up to a thousand people at a time**,
  for six to twelve weeks, with a single narrow light shaft. The
  floor you stand on is compacted waste and remains that
  archaeologists have left in place. There is a wreath there.
  Most groups go silent and stay that way.
- **09:30** — Then the **Door of No Return**, and on the far side
  of it, facing the sea, a second sign added later: **Door of
  Return**, for the descendants who have come back.
- **11:00** — The museum upstairs, which is excellent and
  unflinching on the numbers and on African complicity.
- **12:30** — Out, and lunch. Take the time; people need it.
- **14:30** — **Kakum National Park**, 45 minutes inland — and the
  deliberate change of register. The **canopy walkway** is **seven
  rope bridges, 350 m long and 40 m up**, strung between emergent
  trees above rainforest. It moves underfoot and it is safe.
- **16:30** — The shorter forest trail below, with a guide, for
  the medicinal plants and the butterflies.
- **18:30** — Back to Cape Coast.

## Day 5 — North to Kumasi

- **08:00** — Drive north to **Kumasi**, about four hours through
  cocoa country — Ghana is the world's second largest producer and
  you will pass the beans drying on mats at the roadside.
- **12:30** — Arrive in the **Ashanti** capital, a city of two
  million and the heart of a kingdom that fought the British to a
  standstill four times in the 19th century.
- **14:00** — The **Manhyia Palace Museum**, the former seat of
  the **Asantehene** — the Ashanti king, who is still a
  constitutionally recognised figure with real authority.
- **16:00** — **Kejetia Market**, among the largest open-air
  markets in West Africa — tens of thousands of stalls under a sea
  of rusted roofs. Go with your guide and keep your bag in front
  of you.
- **18:30** — Back out, which will take a while.
- **19:30** — Dinner.

## Day 6 — The Ashanti craft villages

- **08:30** — **Bonwire**, 30 minutes out, where ***kente*** is
  woven. The cloth is made in narrow strips on a treadle loom and
  sewn together; **the patterns have names and meanings**, and
  cloth was historically restricted to royalty. Sit at a loom and
  try — you will be very bad at it, and that is the point.
- **10:30** — **Ntonso** for ***adinkra***: symbols stamped with
  carved calabash dies in bark dye, each with a proverb attached.
  You print your own cloth and the printing takes five minutes
  and the explanation takes an hour, which is the right ratio.
- **12:30** — Lunch.
- **14:00** — **Ahwiaa** for woodcarving — stools especially. The
  **Golden Stool** is the Ashanti nation's soul, said to have
  descended from the sky, and the 1900 war began when a British
  governor demanded to sit on it.
- **16:00** — **Lake Bosumtwi**, an hour south-east: a meteorite
  crater lake, sacred to the Ashanti, where fishermen traditionally
  paddle on wooden planks rather than boats. Swim if you want.
- **19:00** — Back in Kumasi.

## Day 7 — East to the Volta

- **08:00** — The long drive east to the **Volta Region**, five to
  six hours, crossing the **Volta River** at Adomi Bridge.
- **13:00** — Lunch on the way.
- **15:00** — **Tafi Atome Monkey Sanctuary**: a village where the
  **mona monkeys are considered sacred** and have been protected
  by local taboo for generations. They come down for bananas held
  in your hand and take them with both of theirs.
- **17:00** — Check in near **Hohoe** or **Wli**.
- **18:30** — The Volta is green, hilly and quiet — a completely
  different Ghana from the coast.
- **19:30** — Dinner at the guesthouse.

## Day 8 — Wli Falls, and back west

- **07:30** — **Wli Falls**, **the highest waterfall in West
  Africa**. The **lower falls** are a flat 45-minute walk through
  forest with nine stream crossings on footbridges, ending at a
  pool you swim in under the spray, with **thousands of fruit bats
  on the cliff face** above.
- **10:00** — The **upper falls** if you are fit: three hours
  return, steep, muddy and genuinely hard, with a view back over
  the Togo border.
- **12:30** — Lunch, then start west.
- **14:00** — **Mount Afadja (Afadjato)**, Ghana's highest point at
  885 m, is a 45-minute climb if the legs are willing.
- **16:00** — The drive back toward Accra, about four hours.
- **20:00** — Last dinner in the city.

## Day 9 — Fly home

- **Morning** — **Accra Arts Centre** or the **Wild Gecko**
  showroom for kente, carvings and adinkra — and bargain, politely
  and at length, because it is expected and enjoyed.
- **Afternoon** — **Allow serious time for traffic to Kotoka.**
  The airport is in the city and the roads to it are the worst in
  the country at the wrong hour.
- **Note** — Spend the cedis. They are not convertible outside
  Ghana.

## What to pack

- **Light, modest cotton.** Shoulders and knees covered is normal
  dress away from the beach, and it is respected.
- **Long sleeves and trousers for dusk**, plus **DEET repellent**
  — this is a serious malaria area.
- **A torch** — power cuts (*dumsor*) still happen.
- **Shoes you can get wet and muddy** for Wli and Kakum.
- **Cash in small cedi notes.** Change for a large note is often
  genuinely unavailable.
- **UK-style Type G plugs**, 230V.
- **Something smarter** for an evening in Accra, which dresses up.

## FAQ

**How hard are the castles, emotionally?**
Harder than people expect. You stand in a dungeon where a thousand
people were held in the dark for weeks, and the guide tells you
what happened in that room without softening it. Visitors cry
regularly, including on group tours. Allow the afternoon
afterwards, do not stack anything demanding behind it, and go —
it is the single most important thing in the country.

**Both Cape Coast and Elmina, or just one?**
Both, and in that order of significance but the opposite order of
travel. Elmina is older and the building is more striking; Cape
Coast has the better museum and the heavier tour. They are twenty
minutes apart and doing both in one day is a great deal to absorb,
which is why this itinerary splits them.

**Is Ghana safe?**
Yes, for this itinerary, with ordinary urban caution in Accra and
Kumasi markets. It is among the more stable countries in the
region, and the standard advisories concern the far northern
border areas, which are nowhere near this route.

**Do I need a driver?**
Not strictly, but almost everyone uses one and it is the single
best decision here. Self-driving Accra is unpleasant, and a driver
who knows the Volta roads turns a difficult day into a simple one.

**Is December the time to go?**
It is the most comfortable weather and the busiest month by a
distance — the diaspora return season fills flights and hotels and
raises prices sharply. **January and February** are nearly as
pleasant and much calmer.
