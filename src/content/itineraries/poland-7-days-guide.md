Kraków is the European old town the twentieth century somehow
spared — a vast medieval square where a trumpeter has played
the hour from the basilica tower for 600 years, stopping
mid-note where legend says an arrow caught his predecessor.
Warsaw is the opposite story: erased in 1944, then rebuilt so
faithfully from paintings and photographs that UNESCO listed
the reconstruction itself. Together they're Europe's best
history-per-dirham trip — and **flydubai flies non-stop to
BOTH cities**, so the route runs open-jaw with zero
backtracking.

Poland also keeps its own currency, and the **złoty sits
almost exactly 1:1 with the dirham** — which makes menus feel
like a misprint: a 30-złoty pierogi feast costs about US$8
because it is.

The shape: **Kraków (4 nights)** — the square, Wawel,
Kazimierz, the Wieliczka Salt Mine, and the Auschwitz day
done properly — then the fast train to **Warsaw (2 nights)**.
Figure **{{PLN 3650}} per person**
including the visa, excluding flights. **Go April–June or
September–October**; December adds one of Europe's loveliest
Christmas markets to the square, July–August adds the crowds.

### The one booking that rules the trip

- **The one booking that rules the trip:** the
  **Auschwitz Memorial**. From 2026 entry passes are
  **online-only at visit.auschwitz.org** (on-site
  sales permanently ended) — free individual passes
  exist for afternoons, but the **guided
  "educator" tour (PLN 150, 3.5 h)** is the visit to
  book, and slots open 90 days out and **sell out
  2–3+ weeks ahead** April–October. Set the reminder
  now.

### Money and getting around

- **Money:** Poland uses the **złoty, not the euro** —
  and the same two traps as our Prague and Budapest
  guides apply: "0% commission" kantor exchange
  booths bury the rate (use bank ATMs, decline
  conversion), and card terminals offering to charge
  in your home currency skim 5–10% (**always choose PLN**). Cards
  tap everywhere otherwise.
- **Getting around:** both old towns are walkable;
  Bolt/Uber are cheap and honest; the
  **Kraków–Warsaw EIP express** does the hop in
  **2 h 20 (PLN 60–170** — book ~30 days out at
  intercity.pl for the cheap end, seat reservation
  included).

### The Sunday note

- **Sunday note:** most SHOPS close on Sundays
  (trading ban); museums, restaurants and
  attractions run normally — plan shopping for
  Saturday.

## Day 1 — Land in Kraków, meet the square

- **Afternoon** — flydubai lands mid-afternoon;
  train/Bolt into the Old Town (20 min), check in.
- **17:00** — **Rynek Główny**, Europe's largest
  medieval square: the arcaded **Cloth Hall** down
  the middle, flower stalls, pigeons with
  seniority — and on the hour, the **hejnał**: a
  live trumpeter in St Mary's tower playing to each
  compass point, ending abruptly mid-phrase, as
  tradition insists. People applaud a man in a
  window. You will too.
- **19:30** — First Polish table: pierogi (ruskie,
  or duck-and-cranberry for the ambitious —
  PLN 25–40 a plate), żurek soup in a bread bowl,
  and the discovery that "cheap and superb" is
  Poland's national cuisine setting.

**Where to stay in Kraków** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Hotel Copernicus** — 4.5/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~730 reviews), frescoed 16th-century
  rooms in Wawel's shadow; popes have slept here.
  ~PLN 1,300+.
- *Mid-range:* **PURO Kraków Kazimierz** — 4.8/5
  (~2,200 reviews), design-fresh base where the
  evenings happen. ~PLN 420–560.
- *Value:* **ibis budget Stare Miasto** — 4.2/5
  (~2,300 reviews), five minutes from the square at
  ~PLN 220–300. (Aparthotel play: Venetian House,
  4.7/1,400.)

## Day 2 — Wawel, the Old Town's layers, Kazimierz nights

- **09:00** — **Wawel Hill**: the castle courtyard
  and cathedral exterior are free — buy the
  **State Rooms** ticket (~PLN 40) for the
  tapestried interiors and the **cathedral +
  Sigismund Bell** climb (~PLN 25) for the
  coronation church of Polish kings. Fire-breathing
  dragon statue at the riverside cave: mandatory
  child (and adult) stop.
- **12:30** — Lunch the local-institution way: a
  **bar mleczny (milk bar)** — Poland's subsidised
  canteens where two courses cost PLN 25–35 and
  grandmothers judge your plate approvingly.
- **14:00** — Old Town layers: **St Mary's
  Basilica** inside (~PLN 20 — Veit Stoss's
  50-foot carved altarpiece opens at 11:50 daily),
  then the **Rynek Underground** museum beneath
  the square (~PLN 32, timed — medieval Kraków
  excavated in situ) or the Czartoryski Museum's
  **Leonardo — Lady with an Ermine** (~PLN 35;
  yes, a real Leonardo, one of four portraits in
  existence).
- **18:30** — **Kazimierz**, the old Jewish
  quarter: synagogue lanes, candle-lit courtyards,
  and dinner from the **Plac Nowy rotunda** —
  zapiekanka (the half-baguette national street
  food, PLN 15–20) — or Jewish-Polish dining at
  the klezmer restaurants (PLN 70–110 pp).

## Day 3 — Auschwitz-Birkenau, done with respect

- **08:00** — The day this trip owes history.
  Buses/organised transfers run from Kraków
  (~1 h 30; DIY bus ~PLN 20 each way, or
  tour-with-transport packages PLN 200–260 pp).
  Your **educator-guided slot (PLN 150)** covers
  ~2 hours at **Auschwitz I** (the barracks
  exhibitions — the corridor of photographs, the
  rooms of belongings) and, after the 3 km
  shuttle, ~1 hour at **Birkenau**, where the
  scale — the gate, the ramp, the endlessness —
  does what no book can.
- **The house guidance, as on our Phnom Penh
  history day:** this is a memorial,
  not a sight — the museum recommends **14+**, and
  we agree; dress and speak accordingly; no posed
  photos at the gate or on the ramp; small bags
  only (lockers exist); eat before, not inside.
  Give the afternoon after it to quiet — the
  Vistula boulevards back in Kraków were made for
  exactly that walk.
- **19:30** — A gentle dinner; Kazimierz's calmer
  corners suit the evening.

## Day 4 — The salt cathedral, then Schindler's factory

- **09:00** — **Wieliczka Salt Mine** (25 min by
  train/bus, ~PLN 20 return): the **Tourist
  Route** (foreign-language guided, **~PLN
  100–150** by season) descends ~800 wooden steps
  into a 700-year-old mine where miners carved
  chapels from rock salt — culminating in the
  **Chapel of St Kinga**, a full underground
  cathedral with salt chandeliers and salt
  altarpieces, 100 metres down. 3 hours;
  a lift returns you to daylight. Book the
  English slots ahead — they're few and full.
- **14:30** — Back to the city: **Schindler's
  Factory museum** (~PLN 36, timed — book
  online): the factory from the film, now a
  superb museum of Kraków under occupation —
  it pairs with yesterday as context rather
  than repetition.
- **18:00** — Golden hour on the **Vistula
  boulevards** below Wawel; farewell-Kraków
  dinner in the Old Town (the splurge tier —
  modern Polish tasting rooms — runs PLN
  150–250 pp and outclasses its price tag).

## Day 5 — The fast train north, Warsaw's phoenix act

- **09:30** — **EIP express Kraków → Warsaw**
  (2 h 20; the quiet car is bliss; PLN 60–170
  booked ahead).
- **12:30** — Check in, lunch, then the **Old
  Town** with its secret in mind: everything you
  see — the Market Square, the Barbakan, the
  Royal Castle — was **rubble in 1945** and
  rebuilt from Canaletto paintings and salvaged
  fragments. Knowing it changes every
  photograph. Climb St Anne's tower (~PLN 25)
  for the rooftops.
- **17:00** — The **Royal Route** stroll: Castle
  Square's column, Nowy Świat's cafés,
  presidential palace, university gates.
- **20:00** — Dinner on Nowy Świat or Foksal
  (Warsaw eats more modern and slightly
  pricier than Kraków — PLN 80–140 pp for the
  good stuff).

**Where to stay in Warsaw** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Hotel Bristol** — 4.6/5 (~2,800
  reviews), the 1901 grande dame beside the
  presidential palace. ~PLN 900+. (Raffles
  Europejski, 4.8/450, is next door and newer
  money.)
- *Mid-range:* **Polonia Palace** — 4.6/5
  (~3,600 reviews), belle-époque comfort
  opposite the Palace of Culture. ~PLN 450–600.
- *Value:* **Safestay Warsaw Old Town** — 4.6/5
  (~480 reviews), privates from ~PLN 250 steps
  from the square.

## Day 6 — Uprising, POLIN, and Chopin's park

- **09:30** — The **Warsaw Uprising Museum**
  (~PLN 40): the 63-day 1944 rising told at
  full immersive volume — sewers, insurgent
  radio, the B-24 you walk under. It explains
  the rebuilt city outside. 2–3 hours;
  teenagers rate it the trip's best museum.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then **POLIN — Museum of
  the History of Polish Jews** (~PLN 50, on
  the former ghetto's ground): a thousand
  years told forward, not only toward
  destruction — the painted synagogue-roof
  reconstruction alone justifies the ticket.
  *(Lighter swap: the Palace of Culture's
  30th-floor terrace, ~PLN 35, Stalin's
  "gift" with the city at its feet.)*
- **17:00** — **Łazienki Park** (free): royal
  gardens with peacocks patrolling, the
  palace-on-the-water, red squirrels with no
  fear — and **free open-air Chopin concerts
  under the monument every summer Sunday**
  (mid-May–September, 12:00 & 16:00) — time
  Day 6 to a Sunday if you possibly can.
- **20:00** — Farewell feast: duck with apples,
  or the full milk-bar-to-fine-dining arc
  completed at a modern Polish tasting menu.

## FAQ

**Is Poland safe?**
Among Europe's safest — both cities are
relaxed, walkable at night in the centres, and
family-friendly to a fault. The complete
watch-list is financial, not physical: kantor
exchange booths, DCC card prompts, and
unmetered taxis outside stations (Bolt ends
that). That's the whole list.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Very workable. Halal: both cities carry a
solid Turkish/Middle Eastern/South Asian
bench (Kraków's kebab scene is a student-city
institution; Warsaw adds proper Indian and
Bangladeshi rooms — look for displayed
certification, as classic Polish kitchens are
pork-forward). Vegetarian: accidentally
excellent — **pierogi ruskie, placki
(potato pancakes), żurek without sausage,
zapiekanki**, and milk bars where half the
menu is meat-free by tradition; both cities
also rank highly on Europe's vegan-friendly
indexes. "Bez mięsa" gets warm nods.

**Is Auschwitz appropriate for our kids?**
The museum itself recommends **14 and up**,
and this guide holds that line: younger
children gain little and absorb the
distress. Workable family split: one parent
takes teens to the memorial while the other
does Wieliczka's salt mine with the young
ones — then swap stories gently. For those
who go, the educator tour (vs wandering
alone) is the difference between witnessing
and merely walking.

**Kraków–Warsaw or Kraków–Zakopane?**
History vs mountains. This guide's pairing
tells Poland's full story — medieval
survivor + phoenix capital — and rides the
open-jaw flights. Zakopane (2 h south:
Tatra cable cars, highlander grill-huts)
suits returners and winter trips, and heads
the guide-#2 ballot below.

**Is Warsaw worth two nights, honestly?**
Yes — for the Uprising Museum and POLIN
alone, which are among Europe's best
museums full stop, plus the strangeness of
an Old Town younger than your grandparents
that feels six centuries old. It's a
different pleasure from Kraków's, which is
exactly why the pairing works.

**What's Poland guide #2?**
**Gdańsk and the Baltic** — amber lanes,
the WWII museum, Sopot's pier — or
**Zakopane and the Tatras** for the
mountain version. Votes welcome; both on
the list.
