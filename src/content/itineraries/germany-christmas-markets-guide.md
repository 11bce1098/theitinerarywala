For eleven months Germany is an efficient country with good
trains. Then Advent arrives, every town square sprouts wooden
stalls and strings of lights, the air fills with cinnamon,
roasted almonds and hot spiced wine — and the country that
invented the Christmas market (and the Christmas tree, and half
your ornaments) runs the greatest winter show on earth for
exactly four weeks.

This guide joins our winter-specials shelf (alongside
[Siberia](/itineraries/russia-siberia-winter-guide/) and
[Hokkaido](/itineraries/japan-hokkaido-winter-guide/)) and runs the
classic Bavarian-Franconian trio: **Nuremberg**, the most famous market
of them all; **Rothenburg ob der Tauber**, the walled medieval town that
looks like the inside of a snow globe; and **Munich**, which doesn't
have a Christmas market so much as a Christmas *district*. One airport,
three trains, seven days.

**The window is strict: roughly 20 November – 24 December.**
Markets open the week before the first Advent Sunday and — the
mistake this paragraph prevents — **nearly everything closes on
December 23–24**. There is no Christmas market on the 26th.
Plan inside the window; early December (weekdays) is the
crowd-to-magic sweet spot. All costs are in euros (EUR).

## Before you go: the admin and the mug economics

- - **Visa:** everything from our [Schengen visa
  guide](/guides/schengen-visa-how-it-works/) applies — VFS Global, €90,
  the usual Schengen file — with one December-specific warning:
  **appointment demand spikes for the market season, so apply by late
  September/October**.
- **The markets themselves are free.** You pay for what you
  hold: and what you hold works on the **Pfand system** — a
  glühwein (~**€4.50–5.50**) comes with a **€3–5 mug
  deposit**; return the mug for your coins, or keep it as
  the year-stamped souvenir (everyone keeps the first one;
  each market and year has its own design — a collecting
  habit begins).
- **Cash rules the stalls** (Germany's cash habit doubles at
  a wooden hut): €50–100/day in notes and coins keeps the
  queue moving. Bigger stalls now tap cards; don't rely
  on it.
- **The cold is the price of the glow:** late-afternoon
  temperatures run 0–5°C, evenings dip below freezing. This
  trip is spent *standing outdoors at night* — thermal base
  layers, real gloves (mug-holding doubles as hand-warming),
  wool hat and thick-soled boots for cobbles. Kids need one
  layer more than you think.
- **Crowd calendar:** weekday mornings = dreamy; Friday and
  Saturday evenings = shoulder-to-shoulder (and pickpocket
  prime — zips forward in the crush). Every market closes
  by 20:00–21:00 — this trip runs on afternoons and early
  evenings, then dinner.

## Day 1 — Land in Munich, train to Nuremberg, first mug

- **Morning** — Land MUC (non-stop from most of Europe, the
  Gulf and North America);
  **ICE to Nuremberg** (~1 h, from ~€18–35 booked ahead).
- **14:00** — Check in inside the old town walls; orient
  via the **Kaiserburg** ramparts — red roofs, church
  spires, market stripes below.
- **16:30** — Descend into the **Christkindlesmarkt**
  (**27 November – 24 December 2026**; 10:00–21:00): 180
  red-and-white stalls around the Frauenkirche —
  "the little town of wood and cloth" running since the
  1600s. First glühwein, first gasp.
- **19:00** — Dinner the Nuremberg way: **Drei im Weggla**
  — three finger-sized Nuremberg bratwursts in a bun
  (~€4.50–5.50) from the market grills, or the 1419-vintage
  Bratwursthäusle for the sit-down version (~€15–20).

**Where to stay in Nuremberg** (two nights)

- *Old-town classic:* **Hotel Drei Raben** — storytelling
  boutique rooms five minutes from the market. ~€180–240.
- *Mid-range:* **Sorat Hotel Saxx** — directly on the
  Hauptmarkt; market-view rooms exist and go early.
  ~€150–200.
- *Value:* **Motel One Nürnberg-Hauptbahnhof** — the
  reliable German budget-design chain at the station.
  ~€100–140.

## Day 2 — Nuremberg: the Christkind's own market

- **09:30** — Choose your morning:
  - **The Kaiserburg** (~€9) — the imperial castle above
    the town, Holy Roman Empire HQ; or
  - **The Documentation Centre at the Nazi Party Rally
    Grounds** (~€7.50) — Nuremberg owns its whole history,
    and this is the sobering, excellently-done other half
    (12+ advisable; the market feels different, and
    better-earned, afterwards).
- **12:30** — Market lunch crawl: **3 im Weggla** round
  two, feuerzangenbowle (glühwein's dramatic cousin — a
  rum-soaked sugarloaf *set on fire* over the mug, ~€6–8),
  and the city's UNESCO-listed **Elisenlebkuchen** —
  proper gingerbread, ~25% nuts, from Wicklein or Schmidt
  (tins from ~€8–15; the ones in decorative chests are
  the gifts that survive the flight).
- **15:00** — **Kinderweihnacht** on Hans-Sachs-Platz —
  the children's market: nostalgic carousel, little
  Ferris wheel, steam train, craft tents where kids dip
  candles and bake lebkuchen. Then the **Handwerkerhof**
  craft courtyard by the Königstor.
- **17:00** — Souvenir with a story: the
  **Zwetschgenmännle** — Nuremberg's prune-and-walnut
  people, made here for 200 years (~€8–15).
- **18:30** — The market at full glow from the
  Frauenkirche steps — if your dates catch the **opening
  Friday, the Christkind herself delivers the prologue
  from this balcony** at 17:30 (beautiful chaos; arrive
  an hour early or watch from a side street).
- **20:00** — Dinner in a vaulted Altstadt cellar
  (schäufele, dumplings, ~€18–28).

## Day 3 — Rothenburg: the snow-globe town

- **10:00** — Regional trains to **Rothenburg ob der
  Tauber** (~1h15–1h30 via Ansbach + the little Steinach
  shuttle — a **Bayern-Ticket (~€40–45 for two)** covers
  the whole day). Drop bags at the hotel *inside the
  walls* — staying overnight is the entire trick: the
  town empties of coaches by 17:00 and becomes yours.
- **12:00** — The greatest-hits walk while light lasts:
  the **Plönlein** corner (the most photographed
  half-timber junction in Germany), a stretch of the
  **covered medieval wall walk** (free), Burggarten
  views over the Tauber valley.
- **14:00** — **Käthe Wohlfahrt's Christmas Village** —
  the flagship of the world's most famous ornament
  company: 1,500 m² of year-round Christmas, plus the
  **German Christmas Museum** upstairs (**€4 adult /
  €7 family**) explaining how this country invented the
  whole aesthetic. Wallets: brace.
- **15:30** — **Schneeball verdict**, delivered honestly:
  Rothenburg's baseball-sized pastry snowballs (~€3.50–5)
  are 500 years old, photogenic, and taste like sweet
  shortcrust rubble. Buy one, share it, no regrets
  either way.
- **16:30** — The **Reiterlesmarkt** (**20 November –
  23 December 2026**; to 19:00/20:00) as the lanterns
  win: a compact, 500-year-old market where the mulled
  wine is white as often as red (a Franconian wine-country
  quirk — try it).
- **20:00** — **The Night Watchman's Tour** (English,
  nightly at 8 from the Marktplatz, ~€9–10): an hour of
  deadpan medieval storytelling that every traveller
  rates among their favourite hours in Germany. Then
  empty lamplit lanes back to the hotel — the moment the
  whole trip is built around.

**Where to stay in Rothenburg** (one night, inside the
walls — non-negotiable)

- *Historic splurge:* **Hotel Eisenhut** — the grand old
  patrician-house hotel on Herrngasse. ~€180–250.
- *Half-timbered charm:* **Hotel Reichsküchenmeister** —
  by St Jakob's, hearty Franconian restaurant attached.
  ~€140–190.
- *Value:* **Gasthof Goldener Greifen** — a 700-year-old
  guesthouse two doors from the Plönlein. ~€100–140.

## Day 4 — Morning-empty Rothenburg, then Munich's main square

- **08:30** — The photographs everyone else can't get:
  Plönlein, Markusturm and the walls **before the
  day-trip coaches arrive (~10:30)**. Breakfast at a
  bakery window-seat.
- **11:30** — Trains south to **Munich** (~3 h regional
  on the same Bayern-Ticket logic, or backtrack via the
  Nuremberg ICE to save an hour). Check in near
  Marienplatz.
- **16:00** — The **Münchner Christkindlmarkt on
  Marienplatz** (typically ~23/24 November – 24 December
  — confirm exact 2026 opening): the giant tree and
  stalls under the neo-Gothic Rathaus, the Glockenspiel
  doing its 17:00 turn, daily live Advent music from the
  town-hall balcony at 17:30.
- **18:00** — First Munich mug, then two minutes away the
  **Kripperlmarkt** — Germany's largest nativity-figure
  market, a craft unto itself.
- **19:30** — Dinner warm and Bavarian: Haxnbauer's
  crackling pork knuckle or Ratskeller vaults (~€20–35);
  the sausage-averse pivot to käsespätzle without regret.

**Where to stay in Munich** (three nights) — the verified
picks from our [Germany guide](/itineraries/germany-10-days-guide/)
apply: **Platzl Hotel** (4.5/5, ~€280+ in season, two
minutes from the market), **Hotel Torbräu** (4.6/5,
~€230–300), **Hotel Mirabell** (4.4/5, ~€150–200) — and
December is Munich's second-highest season: **book by
October**.

## Day 5 — The Munich market crawl (five in one day)

Munich's genius is variety — each market a different
flavour, all walkable or one U-Bahn apart:

- **11:00** — **Residenz Weihnachtsdorf**: the "Christmas
  village" in the palace's Kaiserhof courtyard — arguably
  the prettiest setting in the city.
- **12:30** — **Wittelsbacherplatz Medieval Market**
  (Mittelaltermarkt): staff in tunics, fire-pits, meat
  roasting on swords, and **Feuerzangenbowle served in
  clay mugs** — the market your kids (and you) will call
  the best.
- **14:30** — U-Bahn to the **Chinese Tower market** in
  the snow-dusted Englischer Garten — the locals'
  market, carousel included; or swap in **Schwabing's**
  artsy Münchner Freiheit market.
- **17:00** — Ice-skating hour at **Karlsplatz
  (Stachus)** (~€8–12 + skate hire) under the lights.
- **19:00** — **Tollwood Winter Festival** on the
  Theresienwiese (free entry): the alternative
  wonderland — world-food halls (this is where halal,
  vegan and everything-else eating is easiest), craft
  tents, live music until late. Munich's answer to
  "aren't all these markets the same?"

## Day 6 — Day trip: Salzburg's silent night (or the snow castle)

Two great endings — the Bayern-Ticket covers both:

- **The recommended: Salzburg** (~1h50 by regional
  train — yes, Austria; your Schengen visa doesn't
  blink): the **Christkindlmarkt on Domplatz** beneath
  the fortress (opens ~19 November), chestnut smoke in
  baroque squares, Mozart's birthplace in fairy lights —
  and this is *Silent Night* country: the carol was first
  sung in 1818 in Oberndorf, 20 minutes away, and the
  city sings it accordingly. Funicular up
  **Hohensalzburg** (~€14) for the white-Alps panorama.
- **The alternative: Neuschwanstein in winter** — the
  castle dusted white above a frozen valley (tours as per
  our [Germany guide](/itineraries/germany-10-days-guide/); the
  Marienbrücke bridge closes in ice conditions — check
  before committing).
- **20:30** — Back in Munich: last mug at Marienplatz
  (final mug-keep decision), pretzels the size of
  steering wheels, pack around the lebkuchen tins.

## Day 7 — Home from MUC

- **Morning** — S-Bahn to the airport (~45 min). Suitcase
  status: two mugs, three ornament boxes, one
  Zwetschgenmännle, 2 kg of lebkuchen, zero regrets.
  Fly home from MUC.

## What to pack

- **Thermal base layers** (the trip is spent standing
  still outdoors at night), wool hat, scarf, real gloves.
- Boots with grippy soles — wet cobbles + occasional ice;
  thin sneakers end trips.
- Layers over one warm coat; markets alternate with
  overheated trains and cellars.
- A tote bag for mug-and-ornament accumulation; bubble
  wrap earns its space on the return.
- Cash wallet with coins (Pfand economy) and hand cream —
  cold plus glühwein-steam chaps knuckles.
- Kids: one more layer than yours, and a thermos the
  kinderpunsch stalls will happily refill.

## FAQ

**If we can only do ONE market?**
Fame says Nuremberg; this guide's heart says **an
overnight in Rothenburg** — the market is smaller, but a
walled medieval town by lantern-light after the coaches
leave is the single most Christmassy experience in
Germany. Do both; they're 90 minutes apart.

**What are the exact dates — and the closing trap?**
2026: Rothenburg **20 Nov–23 Dec**, Nuremberg **27
Nov–24 Dec** (Christmas Eve until 14:00 only), Munich
Marienplatz ~**23/24 Nov–24 Dec**, Salzburg from ~19 Nov.
**After December 24 the lights come down** — Christmas
week travellers arriving on the 26th find dismantled
stalls (Munich's Tollwood and ice rinks run on; the
markets don't). Early December weekdays are perfection.

**Is it good with kids?**
It might be the best kids' trip in Europe: carousels,
steam trains, candle-dipping, ice rinks, kinderpunsch
(alcohol-free punch at every stall), and a nightly 8pm
bedtime that the market hours actively support. Cold
management is the whole job — see the packing list.

**Halal and vegetarian at the markets?**
Better than the bratwurst image suggests: the default
sausage is pork (assume so unless labelled), but every
market carries **kartoffelpuffer** (potato pancakes
with apple sauce), **käsespätzle**, roasted chestnuts
and almonds, flammkuchen variants, fish stalls
(fischsemmel), crêpes and the whole sweet universe —
and **kinderpunsch/alcohol-free glühwein** stands at
every corner make the drinking ritual fully halal.
Munich's **Tollwood** is the jackpot: world-food halls
with labelled halal, vegan and everything between.

**Will it snow?**
Maybe — and the markets don't need it. Early-December
snow odds in Bavaria run roughly one year in three;
Salzburg and any Alpine day trip raise them. The lights,
steam and lebkuchen air do the atmospheric work either
way; treat snowfall as a bonus scene, not the plot.

**Extensions?**
The market corridor is endless: **Salzburg** is built
into Day 6; **Vienna** (2h40 from Salzburg) and
**Strasbourg/Colmar** (France's "capital of Christmas" —
Schengen visa already covers it) are the famous
next-doors; **Prague** joins from Munich in ~4h. Any of
them turns this into a 10-day markets grand tour — say
the word and we'll write it.
