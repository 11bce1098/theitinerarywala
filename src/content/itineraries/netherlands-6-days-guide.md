Amsterdam is a seventeenth-century plan that still works: 165
canals looping through gabled townhouses that lean like
gossiping neighbours, crossed by a million bicycles and lined
with some of the greatest art on Earth. It's compact enough
to master in a day and layered enough to reward five — and
the Dutch flatlands beyond hand you windmills, cheese towns
and (in spring) the world's most famous garden, each under an
hour from Centraal station.

The practicals: **Emirates and KLM fly Dubai–Amsterdam
non-stop (~7 h)**, one **Schengen visa** covers it, and this
guide's one non-negotiable is calendar discipline — the
**Anne Frank House sells out in minutes, six weeks ahead**,
and tulip-season Keukenhof dates go before the season opens.

The shape: **one base, five nights**, museums and canals in
the middle, two day trips at the edges. Figure
**{{EUR 1200}} per person**
including the visa, excluding flights — with the honest note
that Amsterdam hotels are the budget's heavy item, and the
Sloterdijk/Zaandam trick below is how locals' visiting
relatives beat it.

## Before you go: the admin

- **Schengen visa, Netherlands flavour:** the standard
  VFS drill from our other Schengen guides — **€90** +
  service fee at your nearest VFS centre, biometrics,
  proof of residence, bank statements, insurance, itinerary;
  apply **4–6 weeks out**. Existing multi-entry
  Schengen stickers work.
- **The Anne Frank House ticket war, decoded:** tickets
  (**€16.50**) exist ONLY online, released **in batches
  about six weeks ahead** — and "a release can be gone
  inside ten minutes on a summer weekend." Set an alarm
  for the release moment six weeks before your dates,
  have accounts and card details pre-saved, book the
  moment slots appear. No walk-ups, no resellers —
  anyone selling "skip-the-line Anne Frank" tickets is
  selling something else.
- **Timed-museum calendar:** **Van Gogh (€25)** books
  out 1–2 weeks ahead (weekend mornings 3–4);
  **Rijksmuseum (€25)** needs only a few days.
- **The I amsterdam Card verdict** (€67–140): it
  **hasn't covered the Van Gogh Museum since 2022 and
  has never covered Anne Frank** — for this itinerary,
  skip it and buy tickets individually.
- **Money & transit:** cards/contactless everywhere
  (many places are card-ONLY — the reverse problem);
  tap in/out on trams with your bank card, or buy GVB
  day passes (~€9.50/24 h, cheaper tiers for 48–72 h).
  Trains to day trips use the same tap-in system.
- **Hotel honesty:** Amsterdam rooms are Europe's
  priciest per star, plus **12.5% tourist tax**. The
  saver: stay at **Sloterdijk or Zaandam** (8–12 min
  by train, half the price) and commute like a local.

## Day 1 — Land, walk the ring, float it at dusk

- **Afternoon** — Land, train to Centraal (15 min,
  ~€4.60), check in.
- **16:30** — First walk: Dam Square → the **Begijnhof's**
  hidden courtyard → the **9 Streets** (De 9 Straatjes)
  where the canal ring is at its postcard best.
  Stroopwafel warm off the iron (~€5) en route.
- **19:30** — **Evening canal cruise** (€18–30; the
  small open-boat versions beat the glass buses —
  book a sunset slot): bridges lit, gables doubled in
  the water — the city explains itself best from its
  own canals.
- **21:00** — Dinner in the Jordaan (mains €18–28) —
  and the naming note every family needs once:
  a **"coffeeshop" sells cannabis; a "café" or
  "koffiehuis" sells coffee.** Now you know.

**Where to stay in Amsterdam** (five nights)

- *Luxury:* **De L'Europe** — 4.6/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~2,200 reviews), riverfront grande dame with
  Rijksmuseum views. €600+.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Estheréa** — 4.6/5 (~5,200
  reviews), velvet-and-chandeliers boutique across
  eight Singel canal houses. ~€230–300.
- *Value:* **Monet Garden Hotel** — 4.7/5 (~1,800
  reviews), canal views at sane money. ~€150–190.
  (True-budget play: **MEININGER at Sloterdijk**,
  3.9/~2,700 reviews, ~€90–120 — 8 minutes by
  train, dinner savings pay the fare.)

## Day 2 — Museumplein: Rembrandt to Van Gogh

- **09:00** — **Rijksmuseum** at opening (€25,
  booked days ahead): make straight for the Gallery
  of Honour — Vermeer's Milkmaid, then **The Night
  Watch** under its glass research chamber. Two to
  three hours; the library atrium is the sleeper
  photo.
- **12:30** — Lunch on the Museumplein grass or at
  the Cobra café; fries with oorlog sauce (€5.50)
  count as culture.
- **14:00** — **Van Gogh Museum** (€25, timed —
  booked 1–2 weeks back): Sunflowers, the
  bedroom, and the self-portraits in
  chronological heartbreak. 90 lean minutes beats
  three flagging hours.
- **16:30** — Decompress in **Vondelpark** — join
  the rollerbladers-and-herons economy — then
  browse the **Albert Cuyp market** (herring
  stand: €4.50, once, bravely).
- **19:30** — Dinner in **De Pijp**: Amsterdam's
  most international eating quarter — Surinamese
  roti, Indonesian rijsttafel (the Dutch-colonial
  feast, ~€30–40 pp, book ahead), or the
  Foodhallen's stalls back west.

## Day 3 — Anne Frank, the Jordaan, the north

*(Order this day around your Anne Frank slot.)*

- **09:30** — **Anne Frank House** (€16.50, the
  ticket you booked six weeks ago): the swinging
  bookcase, the pencil lines marking the girls'
  heights, the emptied rooms left empty at Otto
  Frank's wish. Ninety minutes of held breath —
  the most important interior in the city.
  **With kids:** the museum suggests 10+; the
  audio handles it gently.
- **11:30** — Climb the **Westerkerk** tower
  beside it (seasonal) or sit with the canal a
  while — most people need a minute.
- **12:30** — **Jordaan** afternoon: the courtyard
  hofjes, brown-café lunch (split-pea soup, tostis,
  €8–14), Noordermarkt's Saturday farmers' stalls.
- **15:30** — Free ferry from Centraal to
  **NDSM/Amsterdam-Noord** (the backdoor city:
  street-art shipyards, waterfront cafés) — or the
  **A'DAM Lookout** swing over the harbour edge
  (~€17.50).
- **19:00** — Dinner back over the water; the
  canal ring at blue hour is the free show.

## Day 4 — Windmills: Zaanse Schans

- **08:30** — Train Centraal → **Zaandijk Zaanse
  Schans** (**17 minutes**, ~€3.90 each way) —
  beat the 10:30 tour-bus surge.
- **09:00** — The Zaan riverbank: working
  windmills grinding pigment, oil and spices
  (village free; **going inside a mill ~€7** —
  De Kat, the colour mill, is the pick), the
  clog-carving demo, the cheese farm's free
  tasting counter, all against green-timber
  houses. Two to three unhurried hours.
- **13:00** — Lunch back in town — or detour: the
  Zaans Museum (~€14) if the Dutch-industry story
  hooked you.
- **15:00** — Amsterdam afternoon at your pace:
  **rent bikes (€15/day)** and do the locals'
  loop — Vondelpark → Amstel river dike →
  Magere Brug (the Skinny Bridge) — with the
  survival rules below observed religiously.
- **19:30** — Dinner in the Plantage quarter;
  pre-book tomorrow's garden/boat.

## Day 5 — Tulips (in season) — or the fairy-tale village

- **Spring option (19 March – 10 May 2026):**
  **Keukenhof** — seven million bulbs in
  showpiece gardens (**€20.50 online / €38.50
  with return coach from Amsterdam**; book
  dates early, **peak bloom ~10–30 April**,
  arrive before 10:30). Add the DIY upgrade:
  rent a bike at the gate (~€15) and ride the
  **flower fields** around Lisse — the striped
  horizons the postcards crop.
- **Rest of the year:** pick one —
  **Giethoorn** (the roadless canal village;
  DIY by train+bus ~€25 each or tour ~€90 —
  hire a whisper-boat, €20/h, and captain your
  own canals) or **Utrecht** (25 min, ~€10
  return: canal wharves lower than the street,
  the Dom tower climb, Miffy's museum for the
  under-6s).
- **19:00** — Farewell dinner: modern Dutch
  tasting rooms (~€45–70 pp) or one last
  rijsttafel; end at a canal-side bench with
  poffertjes. Correct.

## Day 6 — Fly home

- Morning: last market pass for cheese-shop
  vacuum packs and stroopwafel tins (airport
  prices are +40%), train to Schiphol, and the
  ~7-hour hop home.

## What to pack

- Layers + a compact umbrella-proof rain shell:
  Dutch weather does four seasons before lunch,
  all of them windy.
- Comfortable flat shoes — cobbles, bridges, and
  15,000-step days.
- **Bike-lane survival, the packing-list rule
  that isn't an object:** the smooth reddish
  strip is a BIKE ROAD, not a pavement. Never
  walk in it, never stand in it for photos, look
  BOTH ways twice — the bell you hear is a
  warning, not a greeting. Amsterdam's cyclists
  take no prisoners and apologise to none.
- A zipped bag for tram crowds (Centraal and
  the 2/12 lines are the pickpocket map).
- **European Type C/F adapter.**
- Spring visitors: tulip-field mud says trainers
  you don't love.

## FAQ

**Is Amsterdam family-friendly? What about the
red-light district and coffeeshops?**
Very — canals, pancakes, bikes, NEMO's science
museum and the zoo make it a superb kids' city.
The Red Light District is one compact old-town
pocket (De Wallen) that's easily and naturally
skipped; the guide's routes don't pass through
it. Coffeeshops are just storefronts to walk
past — the naming note on Day 1 is all a family
needs.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
One of Europe's best cities for both. Halal:
De Pijp, Amsterdam-West and Nieuw-West run deep
with Turkish, Moroccan, Surinamese and Pakistani
kitchens (Surinamese **roti** is the local halal
comfort-food institution), plus certified
doner/grill rooms everywhere. Vegetarian: the
Dutch-Indonesian table loves you (gado-gado,
tempeh rijsttafel), cheese is a food group, and
dedicated veg restaurants are mainstream. Zero
planning stress.

**Do we really need to book everything ahead?**
Three things, in order of ruthlessness: Anne
Frank (six-week alarm, minutes to sell out),
Keukenhof spring dates, Van Gogh weekends.
Everything else on this itinerary — Rijksmuseum
included — forgives a few days' notice, and the
canals, markets, parks and ferries need none.

**Keukenhof or the "free" tulip fields?**
Both, ideally: Keukenhof is a curated
masterpiece (and worth its €20.50 once); the
surrounding **Lisse–Hillegom fields** are the
endless striped horizons — free, best by bike
from Keukenhof's own rental racks. Outside
19 March–10 May, neither exists: that's when
Giethoorn takes the slot, and nobody files a
complaint.

**Is the city walkable or do we need the
trams?**
The canal belt is entirely walkable (Centraal
to Museumplein is 35 minutes of postcard);
trams earn their pass on museum-day feet and
rainy evenings. Bikes are the soul of the city
— rent them for the parks and rivers, not for
your first rush-hour crossing of the centre.

**What's Netherlands guide #2?**
**Rotterdam + Delft + The Hague** — cube
houses, Vermeer's Girl, and Europe's best
modern skyline — or the **tulip-season
special** built entirely around the 2026 bloom.
Votes welcome; both on the list.
