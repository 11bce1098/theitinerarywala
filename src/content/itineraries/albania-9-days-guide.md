Albania spent forty-five years as the most isolated country in
Europe, sealed off by a regime that built 170,000 concrete bunkers
against an invasion that never came. It opened in the 1990s with
almost no tourism infrastructure, and for two decades hardly anyone
went.

That has changed fast, and the window is closing. What you get right
now is a Mediterranean coast with water as clear as Croatia's at a
third of the price, UNESCO Ottoman towns that are lived in rather
than curated, and mountains in the north that feel genuinely remote.

**Berat** is the "city of a thousand windows", tiers of white
Ottoman houses stacked up a hillside. **Theth** and **Valbonë** sit
in the **Accursed Mountains**, reached by a ferry up a reservoir that
looks like a Norwegian fjord. The **Riviera** in the south has the
coves.

Nine days covers all three, and the driving between them is the
thing to plan around.

## Before you go: driving, cash and the season

### Entry

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** — the EU, UK, US, Canada,
  Australia and many others, typically for 90 days. Albania has
  also run seasonal visa-free schemes for additional nationalities;
  check your own case.
- **Albania is not in the EU or Schengen**, so time here does not
  count against a Schengen allowance — useful if you are combining
  with Greece or Montenegro.
- **The lek (ALL)**, not the euro, despite what coastal menus imply.

### The driving, honestly

- **Hire a car.** Public transport is furgon minibuses that leave
  when full and do not serve the mountain routes.
- **The main roads are good now** — the Tirana–Durrës and
  Tirana–south corridors are modern. **Mountain and village roads
  are not**, and some are genuinely rough.
- **Albanian driving is assertive.** Expect overtaking on blind
  bends, and drive defensively rather than quickly.
- **Do not drive to Theth expecting a normal road.** It is now
  paved most of the way but steep and narrow; many people leave the
  car in Shkodër and take a transfer.
- **Fuel stations are frequent on main roads and scarce in the
  mountains.** Fill up before going north.

### Cash, and the coast

- **Albania runs on cash** far more than western Europe. Cards work
  in Tirana, larger hotels and some coastal restaurants; assume cash
  everywhere else.
- **ATMs are plentiful in towns** and absent in villages. Draw
  before heading to Theth.
- **Prices are genuinely low** — a good meal costs a few hundred lek,
  a guesthouse bed a fraction of Croatia. The Riviera in August is
  the exception and is no longer cheap.

### The season is the whole question

- **June and September are the answer.** Warm sea, open mountain
  guesthouses, and none of August's crush.
- **July and August** on the Riviera are busy, expensive and full of
  visitors from Kosovo and the diaspora; book far ahead or avoid.
- **The Theth–Valbonë trail and the mountain guesthouses run roughly
  June to September** and close outside it, snowbound.
- **Winter** is fine for Tirana and Berat and closed for everything
  north.

## Day 1 — Land in Tirana

- **Afternoon** — Land, collect the car. Tirana is a small capital
  of painted apartment blocks — an actual mayoral policy in the
  2000s.
- **Evening** — **Skanderbeg Square**, then **Blloku**, once the
  sealed quarter of the party elite and now where everyone drinks.

## Day 2 — Tirana, then north

- **Morning** — **Bunk'Art 1** or **2**, nuclear bunkers turned into
  museums of the communist period, and the best explanation of what
  Albania was.
- **Afternoon** — Drive north to **Shkodër**, about two hours.
- **Evening** — The pedestrian old street, and **Rozafa Castle**
  above the confluence at sunset.

## Day 3 — The lake ferry to the mountains

- **Early** — The **Koman Lake ferry**: a three-hour boat up a
  flooded river gorge with walls hundreds of metres high, which is
  one of the great boat journeys in Europe and costs almost nothing.
- **Afternoon** — Onward to **Valbonë**, deep in the Accursed
  Mountains.
- **Evening** — A guesthouse, with dinner of whatever the family
  cooked.

## Day 4 — Valbonë to Theth

- **All day** — The **Valbonë–Theth pass**: about 17 km and six to
  eight hours over a 1,800 m col, with a mule option for bags. The
  classic Albanian walk, well marked and busy in season.
- **Evening** — **Theth**: a stone village with a church, a
  lock-in tower and no phone signal to speak of.

## Day 5 — Theth, and out

- **Morning** — The **Blue Eye of Theth** or the **Grunas
  waterfall**, both short walks from the village.
- **Afternoon** — Transfer out to Shkodër by road, about three
  hours.
- **Evening** — Back in a town with wifi.

## Day 6 — South to Berat

- **Morning** — The drive south, about three and a half hours,
  around Tirana.
- **Afternoon** — **Berat**: the Mangalem and Gorica quarters facing
  each other across the river, white Ottoman houses stacked so the
  windows look like a single façade.
- **Evening** — Up to the **castle**, which is still inhabited —
  people live inside the walls.

## Day 7 — Gjirokastër or the coast

- **Morning** — Drive south. **Gjirokastër**, the other UNESCO
  Ottoman town, is stone rather than white and worth the detour.
- **Afternoon** — Over the pass to the **Riviera** at **Himarë** or
  **Dhërmi**.
- **Evening** — First swim.

## Day 8 — The Riviera

- **Morning** — **Gjipe** beach, in a canyon mouth and reachable
  only on foot or by boat, which keeps it quiet.
- **Afternoon** — **Ksamil**'s islets, or the Roman and Byzantine
  ruins of **Butrint**, which are excellent and shaded.
- **Evening** — Seafood on the water at a fraction of Greek prices
  across the strait.

## Day 9 — Back to Tirana

- **Morning** — The coast road north, then inland. Around four to
  five hours to Tirana, more in August traffic.
- **Afternoon** — Fly home.

## What to pack

- **Walking boots** for the Valbonë–Theth pass, which is a proper
  mountain crossing rather than a stroll.
- **Cash in lek**, drawn in towns before heading north.
- **A swimsuit and reef shoes** — Riviera beaches are pebble, not
  sand.
- **European Type C/F plugs.**
- **A power bank** for the mountain days.
- **Sun protection.** The coast is hot and shade is scarce.

## FAQ

**Is Albania actually cheap?**
Yes, outside the Riviera in August. Guesthouses, food and fuel are
all well below western European levels and below Croatia's.

**Do I need a car?**
For this itinerary, effectively yes. Furgon minibuses connect the
main towns but not the mountains, and the coast road is where the
best of it is.

**Is the Theth–Valbonë walk hard?**
It is a full day over a 1,800 m pass — six to eight hours, well
marked, and busy in July and August. Mules carry bags for a modest
fee, which most people use.

**When should I go?**
**June or September.** Warm sea, mountain guesthouses open, and
none of August's crowds. The mountain section is closed outside
roughly June to September.
