France invented the tourist itinerary, and the first-timer version
still beats its imitators: five days in Paris — museums, the tower,
one royal palace — then a single high-speed train to a different
country that happens to share the flag: the Riviera, where Nice's
old town smells of socca and the day trips are Èze's eagle-nest
village and an actual principality. Two Frances, one rail ticket,
no internal flights.

What's changed for 2026 — and what this guide gets right that
older ones don't: the EU's new **Entry/Exit System is fully live
(biometrics at the border)**, the **Louvre now charges non-EEA
visitors €32** under its new dual pricing, and the closed-day
chessboard (Louvre shut Tuesdays, Versailles Mondays, Orsay
Mondays) is built into the day order below so you never stand
before locked doors.

**Go April–June or September–October.** July–August is crowded and
hot (Nice is at its best, Paris at its most queued); winter Paris
is cheap and moody with short days.

All costs are in euros (EUR).

## Before you go: the admin, 2026 edition

### Visas and the new EU border tech

- **Visa-free passports** (US, UK, GCC, Japan and ~60 others): no
  visa for 90 days — but two new systems apply:
  - **EES (live now, full rollout 10 April 2026):** first entry to
    the Schengen area registers your face and fingerprints instead
    of a passport stamp. Nothing to do in advance — just allow
    extra queue time on your first arrival.
  - **ETIAS (expected Q4 2026):** a €20 online travel
    authorisation for visa-exempt visitors, valid 3 years. Not yet
    operational at writing — **any site selling ETIAS today is a
    scam**; check the official EU page near your travel date.
- **Schengen visa passports (India included):** apply to the
  French consulate or its visa partner where you live — **€90**
  consular fee plus a service fee, biometrics in person. France
  is among the faster deciders at **5–10 working days** (allow
  2–3 weeks in peak). The classic rejection reason is thin
  **proof of funds** — France expects ~€65/day per person with
  hotels prepaid; bank statements matter more than cover letters.
  Apply 4–6 weeks out, and good travel history often earns
  multi-entry. Full process in our
  [Schengen visa guide](/guides/schengen-visa-how-it-works/).
- Travel insurance with €30,000 medical coverage is mandatory with
  the visa — and sensible for everyone.

### Money and the honesty list

- Cards everywhere, contactless is life; carry €50–100 cash for
  markets and small bakeries.
- **Tipping is not American:** service is included by law ("service
  compris") — rounding up or leaving €1–2 for good service is
  plenty.
- **Pickpockets are Paris's one real crime problem**: metro line 1,
  Montmartre's fabric of bracelet-sellers, Trocadéro and museum
  queues. Phones off café tables, bags zipped and forward. That
  paragraph, taken seriously, is your whole security plan.
- Sunday/Monday closures are real in France — markets thrive
  Sunday morning, much else rests.

### SIM and getting around

- EU roaming eSIMs are cheap (~€10–20 for the trip); French
  operators' tourist SIMs (Orange Holiday) work too.
- **Paris:** the Métro is the fastest way everywhere — buy a
  **Navigo Easy card** and load a carnet of t+ tickets; a single
  flat fare (~€2.50) now covers the network. Airport: RER B or the
  **flat-rate taxis** (CDG → right bank €56).
- **Paris → Nice:** the **TGV from Gare de Lyon** — 5h40,
  city-centre to seafront. Fares from **€19 (OUIGO low-cost)** and
  **€29–45 on TGV inOui booked early**; sales open ~4 months out
  on SNCF Connect. Sit on the **right side** after Marseille — the
  final hour runs along the Mediterranean. (Budget romantic
  alternative: the Paris–Nice **night train** with couchettes.)
- **The Riviera runs on trains:** Nice ⇄ Monaco/Villefranche/
  Antibes every 20–30 minutes for a few euros. No car needed
  anywhere in this guide.
- **Fly open-jaw:** into Paris CDG, home from Nice (NCE) — both
  have direct Gulf links — and skip the return leg entirely.

### Travelling with kids?

- Paris with children is better than its reputation: carousels
  everywhere, Luxembourg Gardens' sailboats, the Eiffel's
  glass-floor first level, and crêpes as negotiation currency.
- Métro stairs punish strollers — carrier for the city, stroller
  for the parks; museums free for under-18s almost everywhere.
- The famous family swap: trade Day 5 for **Disneyland Paris**
  (RER A, 40 min; book dated tickets online) — this guide's
  structure absorbs it cleanly.

## Day 1 — Land in Paris, the river and the sparkle

Stay central: Marais, Saint-Germain, Opéra or the 7th — anywhere
inside the first ring of arrondissements repays its price in walked
minutes.

- **Afternoon** — Land at CDG; RER B or flat-fare taxi in. Check
  in, shake off the flight with coffee and a people-watch.
- **17:30** — First walk: the **Seine banks** from Pont Neuf
  toward the Louvre's courtyards (free, open, glowing).
- **20:00** — Dinner in your quarter — a classic bistro plat du
  jour (€18–26) beats a "tourist menu" every time.
- **21:00 sharp** — Be at **Trocadéro or the Champ de Mars** when
  the **Eiffel Tower sparkles** (five minutes every hour after
  dark). The correct first-night ending.

**Where to stay in Paris** (five nights)

- *Luxury:* **Park Hyatt Paris-Vendôme** — the modern palace
  standard, steps from Opéra.
- *Mid-range:* **Hôtel Maison Mère** (9th) — superbly rated
  boutique, metro on the doorstep.
- *Budget:* **Hotel Astoria – Astotel** — the reliable
  well-located pick; the Astotel group generally is Paris
  budget-mid done right.

## Day 2 — Louvre morning, island afternoon

The Louvre is **closed Tuesdays** — this itinerary parks it on
Day 2; shuffle accordingly if your Day 2 lands on one.

- **8:45** — Be early for a **9:00 timed slot at the Louvre**
  (book at ticket.louvre.fr weeks ahead in season; **€32 for
  non-EEA visitors** under 2026's new dual pricing, €22 EEA,
  under-18s free). Go straight to the Mona Lisa first, then
  wander back through the Grande Galerie in peace. Three hours;
  you cannot "finish" it, so don't try.
- **12:30** — Lunch in the **Palais-Royal** gardens or the
  Tuileries kiosks.
- **14:30** — **Île de la Cité**: **Sainte-Chapelle** (~€13,
  timed entry — book; the upper chapel's walls of 13th-century
  glass are Paris's single most gasped-at interior) and
  **Notre-Dame**, gloriously reopened — free, with a
  reservation app for the queue-averse.
- **17:30** — Cross to the **Latin Quarter**: Shakespeare &
  Company, the winding streets, an early-evening crêpe.
- **20:00** — Dinner in Saint-Germain or the Latin Quarter.

## Day 3 — The tower, the Orsay, the river by night

- **9:00** — **Eiffel Tower** — pre-booked (tickets open ~60 days
  ahead and summit slots vanish): summit by lift **€36.70**,
  second floor €23.50, and the stairs option (€14.80, now
  reservation-required) is genuinely fun with kids. Morning slots
  mean thinner crowds and Alps-clear air.
- **12:00** — Picnic assembly on **Rue Cler** (fromagerie,
  boulangerie, rotisserie) eaten on the **Champ de Mars** — the
  best-value great meal in Paris.
- **14:30** — **Musée d'Orsay** (closed Mondays; ~€16, book) — the
  Impressionists in a Beaux-Arts railway station; the clock-window
  photo is upstairs.
- **17:30** — Rest, or Saint-Germain shopping.
- **20:30** — **Seine cruise by night** (~€16–20, one hour,
  Vedettes/Bateaux from the islands or Eiffel) — monuments lit,
  bridges overhead; unironically worth it.

## Day 4 — Versailles, then Montmartre at dusk

Versailles is **closed Mondays** — hence Day 4.

- **8:00** — RER C to **Versailles Château Rive Gauche**
  (~35–40 min, ~€7.30 return; 5-min walk to the gates).
- **9:00** — First entry slot at the **Palace** (book the
  **Passport ticket** — €35 April–Oct including the Musical
  Fountains days, €25 winter; palace-only €21): the Hall of
  Mirrors is nearly empty at 9:10 and a scrum by 11. Then the
  gardens, the Grand Canal (rowboats, kids), and the **Trianon
  estate** — Marie-Antoinette's hamlet is the sleeper hit.
- **15:30** — Train back; recovery coffee.
- **18:00** — **Montmartre**: funicular or the stairs to
  **Sacré-Cœur** for sunset over all of Paris, then the Place du
  Tertre lanes when the day-trippers thin. Watch for the
  bracelet-and-petition hustlers on the steps; a firm non suffices.
- **20:30** — Dinner on the hill's back streets (rue des Abbesses
  end, not the square-side tourist menus).

## Day 5 — Your Paris day

The margin day — four proven shapes:

- **The flâneur:** Le Marais morning (falafel on rue des Rosiers,
  Place des Vosges), **Luxembourg Gardens** afternoon, Galeries
  Lafayette **rooftop** (free) for the golden-hour skyline.
- **The art continuation:** Rodin's garden museum + the Orangerie's
  Water Lilies (both bookable same-day off-season).
- **The families' swap:** **Disneyland Paris** (RER A, 40 min;
  dated tickets online ahead).
- **The pastry pilgrimage:** a morning bakery crawl — croissant
  rankings are a legitimate itinerary item in this city.
- **Evening** — Pack; early TGV tomorrow. Farewell-to-Paris dinner
  accordingly.

## Day 6 — The TGV south, first Riviera evening

- **9:00** — **Gare de Lyon → Nice** (5h40; buy the moment your
  dates fix — €29–45 early inOui, €19+ OUIGO). Right-side seats
  after Marseille: the last hour skims the Mediterranean's edge.
- **15:00** — Arrive **Nice Ville**, tram or walk to your hotel;
  first swim or promenade shakedown.
- **18:00** — **Promenade des Anglais** at golden hour — the bay
  earns the name Baie des Anges nightly.
- **20:00** — Old Town (Vieux Nice) dinner: **socca** (chickpea
  pancake) from a counter as the opener, then niçoise cooking —
  the real salade niçoise has no cooked vegetables and locals will
  tell you so.

**Where to stay in Nice** (three nights)

- *Icon:* **Hôtel Negresco** — the pink-domed grande dame of the
  promenade; part hotel, part art museum.
- *Mid-range:* **Hôtel du Centre (Ammi)** — superbly rated,
  walkable to Old Town and beach.
- *Budget:* **Hôtel Byakko** — rooftop terrace, tram at the door,
  honest prices.

## Day 7 — Nice itself: market, old town, castle hill

- **9:00** — **Cours Saleya market** (mornings, closed Mondays for
  produce — antiques instead): flowers, fruit, olive stalls;
  breakfast standing up.
- **10:30** — Vieux Nice's lanes properly: Sainte-Réparate
  cathedral, gelato at the famous fenocchio counters (the
  lavender flavour is the local dare).
- **12:30** — Lunch in the old town or beach-club style on the
  pebbles (yes, pebbles — water shoes make Nice beaches lovely).
- **15:00** — **Castle Hill (Colline du Château)** — free lift or
  stairs to the panorama over the bay and port; the waterfall and
  playgrounds make it the family hour too.
- **18:00** — Port side aperitif, or the promenade by bike
  (city-bike stations everywhere).
- **20:30** — Dinner reservation somewhere you loved at lunch —
  Nice rewards repeat visits within the same day.

## Day 8 — Èze and Monaco: the eagle's nest and the principality

The Riviera's greatest-hits day, entirely by public transport.

- **9:00** — Bus or train+shuttle to **Èze village** — the
  medieval eagle's-nest 400 m above the sea. Wander the stone
  lanes early, then the **Jardin Exotique** (~€7–8) crowning the
  ruin: cacti, sculptures, and the coast's best viewpoint.
- **12:00** — Lunch in Èze or push on and eat in Monaco.
- **13:30** — Train or bus onward to **Monaco** (Nice–Monaco
  trains run every ~20–30 min, ~€5): the palace square's 11:55
  guard change if you time it, the **old town (Le Rocher)** and
  cathedral, the port's absurd yachts, and **Casino Square,
  Monte-Carlo** — where the cars outside are the free museum
  (casino atrium visitable; gaming rooms ticketed, dress up).
- **17:30** — Optional add-on stop on the way home:
  **Villefranche-sur-Mer**'s perfect crescent for a golden-hour
  drink (one train stop from Nice).
- **20:30** — Farewell Riviera dinner in Vieux Nice.

## Day 9 — Fly home from Nice

- **Morning** — One last promenade coffee; tram T2 runs straight
  to **Nice airport (NCE)** — direct Gulf and European departures,
  no Paris backtrack. Au revoir being the operative phrase.

## What to pack

- Real walking shoes — this guide is 15,000 steps a day on
  cobbles and museum marble.
- Smart-casual layers: Paris dresses up slightly and so will you
  by day three; a scarf is the universal French adapter.
- Water shoes or thick soles for Nice's pebble beaches; swimwear
  May–October.
- A packable tote for market picnics; corkscrew in checked
  luggage, obviously.
- Rain layer for Paris any season; sunscreen for the Riviera.
- Patience for queues and none for bracelet-sellers.

## FAQ

**Is 5 days too many for Paris?**
No — it's the minimum for this list without sprinting. The
closed-day chess (Louvre Tue, Versailles Mon, Orsay Mon) is why
winging it fails; this order exists so you never lose a morning
to a shut door.

**TGV or fly to Nice?**
Train, almost always: city centre to seafront in 5h40 beats
airport theatre both ends, luggage rides free, and the last hour
is a sea view. Fly only if a €25 promo fare meets a schedule the
train can't.

**Is Paris safe? What about the pickpockets?**
Violent crime against tourists is rare; theft-by-distraction is
industrial. The metro-and-hotspots paragraph above is the whole
game — travellers who zip bags and keep phones off tables almost
never report problems.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Both easier than reputation suggests: Paris has one of Europe's
largest halal dining scenes (from Le Marais falafel to
south-Asian Faubourg Saint-Denis), and vegetarians eat well
everywhere the menu says légumes — though classic bistros remain
butter-and-beef country; the picnic strategy solves all diets
deliciously.

**Can we add a third stop?**
The graceful add-ons: **Avignon or Aix-en-Provence** as a TGV
break between Paris and Nice (+1–2 days), or **Antibes/Cannes**
as a second Riviera day. Resist adding a third *region* — this
route's calm is the feature.

**When do the new EU border rules bite?**
EES is already live — build in slower first-entry queues
(biometrics once per passport, faster after). ETIAS's €20
authorisation for visa-exempt travellers is expected from late
2026: check the official europa.eu page before travel and buy it
nowhere else.
