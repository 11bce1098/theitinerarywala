Nothing prepares you for the moment the Siq — a kilometre of
winding, cathedral-high crack in the mountain — suddenly
frames the **Treasury**, glowing rose-gold at the end of the
dark. Petra is the rare wonder that beats its own hype. And
Jordan stacks the rest of the week around it: Roman **Jerash**
with its chariot-rutted streets, the **Dead Sea** where you
bob like a cork at the lowest point on Earth, and a night
under desert stars in **Wadi Rum**, where every Mars movie
you've seen was filmed.

From the Gulf it is absurdly convenient: **a three-hour
flight** (flydubai, Emirates, Royal Jordanian, Air Arabia to
Amman), food that tastes like the region's home cooking
because it is, and the **Jordan Pass** — the rare government
scheme that genuinely saves money by bundling your **visa,
Petra and forty other sites into one QR code**.

The route is the classic self-drive loop: **Amman (2 nights)
→ Jerash + Dead Sea → Petra (2 nights) → Wadi Rum camp (1
night) → back**. Figure **{{JOD 675}} per person** excluding flights. **Go October–November
or March–May**; summer at Petra is a 40°C stone oven, and
December desert nights touch zero (pack accordingly — the
camps are magical in the cold).

## Before you go: the admin

### The Jordan Pass IS the visa strategy

- **The Jordan Pass IS the visa strategy:** Indians are
  eligible for **visa on arrival (JOD 40)** at Queen
  Alia airport — but buy the **Jordan Pass first at the
  official jordanpass.jo** and the visa fee is **waived
  entirely** (condition: 3+ nights in the country —
  this trip qualifies twice over). Tiers: **Wanderer
  JOD 70** (1-day Petra), **Explorer JOD 75** (2-day —
  this guide's pick), Expert JOD 80 (3-day). It also
  swallows Jerash, the Citadel, Wadi Rum's entry and
  ~40 more tickets — bought separately that's JOD 100+.
  **Buy only on the official .jo site** — lookalike
  resellers charge US$229 for the same QR code.
- Carry the pass PDF plus hotel bookings and return
  ticket; immigration scans it and stamps you through.
  (No pass? Plain VoA JOD 40 or the MOI eVisa, 3–5
  days, same price.)

### Money and tipping

- **Money:** the dinar is strong and STABLE (pegged) —
  **JOD 1 ≈ US$1.41**, which surprises people mid-haggle.
  Cards work in cities and hotels; carry JOD 100–150
  cash for camps, tips and falafel. ATMs everywhere in
  Amman, thin after Wadi Musa.
- **Tipping:** small and constant — JOD 1–2 rounds at
  restaurants, JOD 5–10 for camp staff, a dinar for
  helpful everything. Careem works in Amman (yes,
  really) and ends taxi-meter debates.

### Driving

- **Driving:** Jordan is an easy, rewarding self-drive —
  one main highway south, light traffic, signage in
  English (rentals from ~JOD 25–30/day; UAE/Indian
  licences accepted for visits). Prefer being driven?
  Private car + driver runs JOD 75–95/day, and JETT
  buses link Amman–Petra (JOD 11) for the car-free
  version.

## Day 1 — Amman: citadel sunset

- **Morning flight** — Land by early afternoon (3 h
  from Dubai), collect the car or Careem into town.
- **15:30** — The **Roman Theatre** (Jordan Pass): 6,000
  seats cut into the hillside, still hosting concerts
  twenty centuries on.
- **17:00** — Up to the **Citadel** (pass) for golden
  hour: Hercules' temple columns, the Umayyad palace
  dome, and the whole white-stacked city turning amber
  as the call to prayer rolls across seven hills — the
  single best introduction to Jordan.
- **19:30** — Down to **Rainbow Street**: dinner at a
  local institution (the famous downtown falafel-and-
  hummus houses feed two for JOD 6–10 — join the queue,
  thank us later), knafeh from Habibah (JOD 1.5) for
  the walk.

**Where to stay in Amman** (nights 1, 2 and 6)

- *Luxury:* **Fairmont Amman** — 4.9/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~2,900 reviews), the city's polished flagship.
  ~JOD 160+.
- *Mid-range:* **The Amman Landmark** — 4.7/5 (~3,800
  reviews), big pool-garden comfort mid-city.
  ~JOD 60–85.
- *Value:* **Toledo Hotel** — 4.0/5 (~1,200 reviews),
  clean and central at ~JOD 35–50.

## Day 2 — Roman morning, Dead Sea afternoon

- **08:30** — Drive north (50 min) to **Jerash**
  (Jordan Pass): the best-preserved Roman provincial
  city on Earth — the Oval Plaza's colonnade, chariot
  ruts in the Cardo's stones, a hilltop temple of
  Artemis, and a hippodrome. Two to three hours;
  hat and water, there's no shade in the empire.
- **13:00** — Lunch en route, then swing southwest,
  descending 1,400 vertical metres of switchbacks —
  ears popping — to **the lowest point on Earth
  (−430 m): the Dead Sea**.
- **15:00** — Float. A day-pass beach (Amman Beach-
  style public resorts ~JOD 20–25, or hotel day
  passes JOD 30–50 with pools and showers) gets you
  the full ritual: walk in, lean back, physically
  cannot sink, laugh, get photographed reading a
  newspaper, coat yourself in black mineral mud, bake,
  rinse. **Rules that matter: no shaving that
  morning, do NOT get water in your eyes, 20-minute
  soaks max, and shower after** — this water is 34%
  salt and takes itself seriously.
- **17:30** — Sunset over the water (the far shore is
  another country), drive back up to Amman for the
  night.

## Day 3 — South to Petra, and Petra by candlelight

- **09:00** — The **Desert Highway** south (3 h to
  Wadi Musa) — or the slow, spectacular **King's
  Highway** (5 h) with a stop at **Kerak's** Crusader
  castle (JOD 2, a maze of vaulted halls) if castles
  outrank pool time.
- **14:30** — Check in at Wadi Musa, then warm up at
  **Little Petra** (free, 15 min away): a miniature
  Siq and carved halls with 2,000-year-old painted
  ceilings, nearly tourist-free.
- **17:00** — Early dinner, early night — with one
  exception:
- **20:30** — **Petra by Night** (Mon/Wed/Thu,
  **JOD 17**, not covered by the pass): the Siq and
  Treasury lit by 1,500 candles, Bedouin flute
  echoing off the rock. Honest review: it's slow,
  crowded at the start and utterly worth it once the
  candles open out in front of the Treasury. If your
  dates include a running night — go.

**Where to stay in Wadi Musa** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Mövenpick Resort Petra** — 4.2/5
  (~5,500 reviews), literally across the road from
  the gate — the 6 am head-start money buys.
  ~JOD 140+.
- *Mid-range:* **Petra Moon Hotel** — 4.4/5 (~2,300
  reviews), rooftop pool, five minutes' walk to the
  entrance. ~JOD 70–95.
- *Value:* **Petra Guest House** — 4.3/5 (~1,900
  reviews), at the gate with a bar inside an actual
  Nabataean cave. ~JOD 55–75. (The uphill guesthouse
  belt runs JOD 25–45 with free gate shuttles.)

## Day 4 — Petra: the full day

- **06:00** — Be at the gate at opening (Jordan Pass
  scanned; day-visitors without an overnight pay
  JOD 90 — your Explorer pass laughs at this). Walk
  the **Siq** in near-solitude, and take the
  **Treasury** with a few dozen people instead of a
  few thousand — the trip's goosebump moment.
- **07:30** — Keep moving while it's cool: the
  **Street of Facades**, the theatre, then up to the
  **Royal Tombs** (the Urn Tomb's blue-veined
  ceiling) for breakfast-with-a-view from your
  daypack.
- **11:00** — The Colonnaded Street to the museum
  and lunch at the basin restaurants (JOD 10–15
  buffet, or packed sandwiches for half).
- **14:00** — **The Monastery (Ad Deir): 850
  rock-cut steps**, 45–60 minutes up — and the
  honest brief: it's a real climb in real heat, the
  Monastery is BIGGER than the Treasury and shares
  none of its crowds, and the "best view in Jordan"
  café opposite serves the best-earned lemon-mint
  in the Middle East. Late afternoon light sets the
  facade glowing.
- **On the animals:** you'll be offered donkeys up
  the Monastery steps constantly. **Animal-welfare
  groups have documented real problems here — walk
  if you can, and if you can't, the horse carriage
  on the flat main trail is the kinder option.**
  Firm, friendly "la, shukran" works on repeat.
- **17:30** — The long, happy 4 km walk out
  (20,000+ steps today — this is Petra's true
  ticket price). Shower, mansaf (Jordan's national
  yoghurt-lamb feast, JOD 10–14), collapse.

## Day 5 — Petra's high place, then into the red desert

- **07:00** — Explorer-pass day two, pick one
  masterpiece: the **Al-Khubtha trail** (steps
  behind the Royal Tombs to the famous
  **Treasury-from-above ledge** — the legal,
  free version of the view the Instagram "guides"
  sell) or the **High Place of Sacrifice** loop
  past obelisks and god-blocks. Two–three hours;
  out by noon.
- **13:30** — Drive south (1 h 45) to **Wadi Rum**
  village (entry on the Jordan Pass); meet your
  camp's pickup.
- **15:00** — **Jeep safari** (2–3 h, ~JOD 55–65
  per jeep — split it): red dunes you sprint down,
  the Lawrence of Arabia spring, natural rock
  bridges, canyon walls scratched with 2,000-year-
  old Thamudic graffiti — ending on a ridge for a
  sunset that redefines the colour orange.
- **19:30** — At camp: **zarb** dinner — lamb and
  vegetables buried in an underground sand oven,
  dug up steaming — then tea by the fire and a
  sky with no light pollution for 50 km. **Camp
  tiers:** classic Bedouin tents (JOD 35–60 pp
  half-board), or the famous **bubble domes**
  (JOD 120–200) if the stargazing should come
  with air-conditioning. Book by current reviews;
  there are hundreds.

## Day 6 — Desert sunrise, back to the capital

- **06:00** — Sunrise from the dune behind camp
  (they'll point you at it) — the desert does its
  best work at both ends of the day. Optional
  camel hour (~JOD 15–20).
- **09:00** — The drive north (4 h on the Desert
  Highway, coffee at the truck-stop bakeries).
- **14:30** — Amman round two: the **Jordan
  Museum** (JOD 5 — home of Dead Sea Scrolls and
  the eerie 9,000-year-old ʿAin Ghazal statues),
  then gold-souk browsing downtown.
- **19:30** — Farewell feast: a proper mansaf
  house or Rainbow Street's grills; knafeh
  encore mandatory.

## Day 7 — Fly home

- Queen Alia is 35 minutes south of town; morning
  flights land you in Dubai by mid-afternoon.
  Dead Sea mud kits and za'atar at the airport
  cost double the downtown souk — you shopped
  yesterday, obviously.

## What to pack

- Broken-in walking shoes with grip — Petra is
  20,000+ steps of sand-polished stone, and the
  Monastery's 850 steps take no prisoners.
- Sun kit: hat, SPF 50, refillable bottles (fill
  stations at Petra's gate), electrolyte sachets
  in warm months.
- **Layers for the desert flip:** Rum nights drop
  near 0°C in winter and 10–14°C even in spring —
  fleece + warm sleep layer for camp; the tents
  supply heavy blankets.
- Swimwear + flip-flops + an old towel for the
  Dead Sea (mud stains; hotel towels forbidden),
  waterproof phone pouch, zero fresh shaves.
- Modest daywear (shoulders/knees) for city and
  villages; scarf for mosque visits (King
  Abdullah Mosque welcomes visitors).
- Torch/headlamp for camp paths, power bank for
  jeep days, **UK-style Type G plugs mostly (with
  some C/F)** — UAE chargers largely just work.

## FAQ

**Is Jordan safe right now?**
Jordan has spent decades as the region's calm
room, and the tourist trail — Amman, the Dead
Sea, Petra, Rum, Aqaba — sits far from any
border tension, guarded by a tourism police
force that treats visitors as a national
project. Standard advice: check current
guidance when you book, skip border-adjacent
detours, and then relax — solo women travellers
consistently rate Jordan among the region's
easiest.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
The site's easiest answer yet: **everything is
halal by default**, and vegetarians eat like
royalty at the mezze table — hummus, falafel,
moutabal, fattet hummus, galayet bandora,
stuffed vine leaves — before a single main
arrives. The only note runs the other way:
alcohol is limited to bigger hotels and a few
Amman districts, and Rum camps are dry — plan
your sundowners as tea, like the Bedouin do.

**Is one full day in Petra enough?**
One day covers the icons at a march; the
**two-day Explorer pass costs JOD 5 more** and
buys you the Monastery AND a high trail without
a death march — this guide's structure. Skip
day two only if knees or dates demand it, and
then do: dawn Treasury → Royal Tombs →
Monastery, and be honest about the 25,000
steps.

**Bubble camp or Bedouin camp in Wadi Rum?**
Bedouin for the experience (fire circle, zarb,
star-blanket silence — and JOD 45 feels like
theft); bubbles for the occasion (panoramic
star-ceilings, heating/AC, JOD 150ish). The
desert is identical outside both. Families:
classic camps with private bathrooms are the
sweet spot.

**Can we add the Dead Sea as an overnight
instead of a stop?**
Yes — resort-strip hotels (Marriott, Mövenpick,
Hilton) run JOD 90–150 B&B and turn the float
into a spa day; it suits honeymooners and
anyone allergic to day-pass changing rooms.
This guide keeps it as a stop to buy Petra two
nights — swap if soaking outranks stone.

**What's Jordan guide #2?**
**Aqaba and the Red Sea** — Jordan's
snorkel-and-dive corner (military wrecks,
coral, winter sun) that pairs with Rum for a
south-loop week — or the **King's Highway
castles-and-canyons** slow route. Votes
welcome; both on the list.
