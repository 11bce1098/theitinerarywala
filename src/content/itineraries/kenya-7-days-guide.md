"Safari" is a Swahili word, and the Masai Mara is where it
still means what it promised: horizon-wide savannah, flat-top
acacias, and more big animals per square kilometre than
anywhere on Earth. Between July and October the **Great
Migration** pours a million wildebeest across the Mara river
with crocodiles waiting; the rest of the year the resident
cast — lion prides, cheetah on termite mounds, elephant
families in the light — carries the show without them.

It is also among the most reachable of the world's great
safaris: **Nairobi is a 5-hour non-stop from the Gulf** and
well connected from Europe, the **eTA is an online form open
to nearly every nationality**, and Kenya's enormous Indian
community means the food is a good deal more interesting than
lodge buffets suggest. The shape: **Nairobi (2 nights)** for the
gentlest wildlife encounters anywhere, the **Masai Mara (3
nights)** for the main event, and **Lake Naivasha (1 night)**
to walk — actually walk — among giraffe and zebra on the way
back.

Safari economics run in dollars, so this guide does too:
figure **{{USD 1450}} per person**
excluding international flights — with the honest note that
the Mara's new fee regime (below) is what moves this number.

## Before you go: the admin

- **eTA for everyone:** Kenya scrapped visas and requires
  an **Electronic Travel Authorisation** instead — apply
  at the official **etakenya.go.ke** (~**US$34**,
  processed **within 3 working days**; every traveller
  including infants needs one). Passport 6 months valid;
  upload the hotel booking and return ticket. Beware
  paid lookalike sites, as ever.
- **The park-fee truth (read before booking anything):**
  Masai Mara National Reserve fees are now
  **US$200 per adult in high season (July–December)**
  and **US$100 in low season (January–June)** — children
  9–17 $50, under-9s free — and tickets are
  **12-hour-validity**, so nights INSIDE the reserve can
  mean paying twice per day. The workarounds this guide
  uses: **stay at camps just outside the Talek/Sekenani
  gates** (one entry per game-drive day) or base in the
  **Mara Triangle side (~$70/24 h)**. Always confirm
  whether a safari quote **includes park fees** — it's
  the single biggest hidden-cost trap in Kenya.
- **Health, honestly:** the Mara is a **malaria area** —
  see a travel clinic about prophylaxis (Nairobi's
  altitude keeps it low-risk there). **Yellow fever
  vaccination is not required if you fly in direct from a
  non-endemic country**, but IS if you transit an endemic
  one —
  carry the certificate if you have one anyway.
- **Money:** Kenya is startlingly cashless — cards and
  **M-Pesa** everywhere, park gates card-only. Carry
  modest USD cash for tips (crisp notes), KES small
  change from an ATM for markets.
- **Safari vehicle truth:** quotes come in two species —
  **minivan (pop-up roof, fine, cheaper)** and **4x4
  Land Cruiser (better views, better off-road reach,
  ~$100–150 more pp)**. In green-season mud or for
  photographers, the Cruiser earns it.

## Day 1 — Land in Nairobi, meet the giraffes

- **Morning** — The overnight flight lands early.
  Check in (Karen/Westlands side), shake off the
  altitude — Nairobi sits at 1,795 m, which is why
  the air feels alpine.
- **14:00** — **Giraffe Centre** (~KSh 1,500 / $12):
  eye-level with endangered Rothschild giraffes on the
  raised platform; a pellet on the palm earns you the
  famous 45-cm-tongue handshake.
- **16:00** — **Karen Blixen Museum** (~$12) — the *Out
  of Africa* farmhouse under the Ngong Hills — or
  souvenir recon at the Kazuri bead workshop
  (women's cooperative, lovely story, fair prices).
- **19:00** — Dinner: Nairobi's Indian food is
  genuinely among the world's best outside India
  (a century-old community) — book Open House or
  similar (~$15–25 pp). Early night; elephants at
  eleven tomorrow.

**Where to stay in Nairobi** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Villa Rosa Kempinski** — 4.7/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~2,400 reviews), the city's polished
  flagship. ~$250+. (The bucket-list outlier:
  **Giraffe Manor**, 4.9/2,260 — giraffes at the
  breakfast window, ~$1,000+ pp and booked out a
  year ahead. Mentioned so you know it's real.)
- *Mid-range:* **Tamarind Tree Hotel** — 4.8/5
  (~2,400 reviews), smart rooms + rooftop pool.
  ~$110–150.
- *Value:* **ibis Styles Westlands** — 4.6/5
  (~2,400 reviews), bright and safe at ~$60–85.

## Day 2 — Elephants at eleven, then the park with a skyline

- **09:30** — Leave early for Karen: the **Sheldrick
  Wildlife Trust elephant orphanage** admits the
  public for **one hour only, 11:00–12:00 — ~$45–50
  conservation levy, book online weeks ahead**.
  Orphaned elephant calves thunder in for milk
  bottles, keepers tell each rescue story, and
  cynics leave sniffling. Adopting a calf ($50/yr)
  is the souvenir that matters.
- **13:00** — Lunch in Karen (~$12–18).
- **15:00** — Choose the afternoon:
  **Nairobi National Park** (~$50–80 via eCitizen —
  lions and rhino against a skyline of cranes; the
  only capital on Earth with a national park inside
  it; 3-hour game drive ~$60–90 extra for the
  vehicle) — or the free-range version: Karen's
  cafés and the giraffe-view terraces.
- **19:00** — Nyama choma (grilled-meat institution)
  or Swahili coast cooking; brief your safari
  pickup time. Pack duffel-soft.

## Days 3–5 — The Masai Mara

Book as a **4-day/3-night package** — the standard,
and this budget's engine: **shared 4x4 with window
seat guaranteed, full board at a tented camp outside
Talek/Sekenani gate, park fees included — from
~$800–950 pp high season** (minivan versions ~$550;
fly-in luxury from $2,000). Confirm the fee-inclusion
line in writing.

- **Day 3** — The Great Rift drive down (5–6 h,
  escarpment viewpoint stop), camp lunch, and the
  **first game drive at 16:00**: within an hour
  you'll have stopped counting zebra. Sundowner
  light on the grass is what camera companies dream
  about.
- **Day 4** — **Full Mara day** (dawn start, picnic
  breakfast by the hippo pools): lion prides before
  the heat, cheetah scanning from mounds, elephant
  crossings — and in **July–October, the migration's
  river-crossing drama** (your guide reads the herds;
  patience at the bank is the price of the
  spectacle). Optional dawn add-on tomorrow:
  the **hot-air balloon safari (~$430–480 pp,
  champagne breakfast included)** — the site's
  priciest hour and, by every account, worth
  saving for.
- **Day 5** — Short dawn drive, then the choice:
  **Maasai village visit (~$25–30 pp — go with your
  camp's partner village; ask where the money
  goes** — the honest version funds the school and
  feels like a welcome, the roadside version feels
  like a transaction), and transfer out to **Lake
  Naivasha** (4–5 h) for the soft landing:
  hippos grunting offshore at sunset.

**Where to stay in the Mara** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Governors' Camp** — 4.9/5 (~1,400
  reviews), the river-bend legend inside the
  reserve (its rates absorb the fee maths).
  $800+ pp/night. (Angama Mara, 4.9, owns the
  escarpment view.)
- *Mid-range:* **Ashnil Mara Camp** — 4.6/5
  (~1,200 reviews), riverside tents with hippo
  soundtrack. ~$250–400 pp full board.
- *Value:* **PrideInn Mara Camp, Sekenani** —
  4.8/5 (~1,100 reviews, the reserve's #1 Best
  Value) — comfortable tents **outside the gate**,
  which is exactly how the fee trick works.
  ~$120–180 pp full board.

## Day 6 — Naivasha: the walking safari

- **07:30** — **Boat ride on Lake Naivasha**
  (~$35–45 per boat/hour): hippo pods, fish
  eagles diving on cue, papyrus shorelines.
- **09:00** — Land on **Crescent Island** (~$35
  entry): no predators, so you **walk** — among
  giraffe, zebra, wildebeest and impala, at their
  pace, no vehicle between you. After three days
  of engines, walking with giraffes is the trip's
  quiet perfect ending. *(Energetic swap:
  **Hell's Gate NP** (~$26) — cycle between the
  cliffs that inspired the Lion King's gorge.)*
- **13:00** — Lunch at a lakeshore lodge, then the
  scenic Rift-wall drive back to Nairobi (2.5–3 h).
- **18:00** — Day room / dinner near the airport
  (Crowne Plaza Airport 4.8/2,300 does this job
  well) for tonight's or tomorrow's flight home.

## Day 7 — Fly home

- Morning flight home (or the late-night
  departure gives you a bonus Nairobi day —
  the railway museum, Maasai Market by day of
  week, or one more mall of Kenyan coffee
  shopping).

## What to pack

- **Neutral colours** (khaki, olive, stone) — and
  the science one: **avoid dark blue/black, which
  attract tsetse flies**. No camouflage (illegal
  for civilians in Kenya).
- Layers: dawn drives at 10°C, noon at 28°C —
  fleece + windproof over T-shirts. A beanie for
  the balloon.
- Binoculars (the single biggest upgrade), zoom
  lens, double the memory cards.
- Soft duffel (hard shells don't fit safari
  vehicles), power bank (camp charging is
  solar-hours).
- DEET repellent + prophylaxis per your clinic;
  SPF 50 (equatorial altitude sun).
- Dust: a buff/scarf for the roof-open hours and
  a ziplock for the camera.

## FAQ

**Is Nairobi safe? And the Mara?**
The Mara and lakes circuit is as safe as tourism
gets — camps are staffed, escorted and remote.
Nairobi needs city rules: use Uber/Bolt (cheap,
everywhere), don't walk after dark, keep phones
off the street edge — the nickname "Nairobbery"
is dated but earned once; hotel areas (Karen,
Westlands, Upper Hill) are relaxed by day. Follow
the two-city-rules and the week is easy.

**When exactly should we go — is the migration
overrated?**
**July–October** is the migration window (river
crossings peak Aug–Sep) — extraordinary, and
priced accordingly ($200 fee, peak lodges,
vehicle scrums at crossings). The site's honest
alternative: **January–February** — half the
fee, dry golden light, resident cats hunting,
calving season on the plains, half the vehicles.
If it's your one safari ever: migration. If
you want the better-value superb safari:
Jan–Feb.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Among Africa's easiest. Kenya's coast-rooted
Muslim heritage and huge Indian community mean
**halal butchers and restaurants are common**
(Nairobi's Swahili and Somali kitchens
especially), and safari camps handle
halal/vegetarian with advance notice as
routine — many run permanent veg menus for
Indian groups. Nairobi's pure-veg Indian
restaurants are destination-grade in their own
right.

**How much do we really tip?**
The etiquette line, plainly: **$10–15 per
traveller per day for your driver-guide**,
$5–10/day into the camp staff box, a couple of
dollars for porters. Hand it over on the last
morning, in an envelope, with the thanks said
out loud — guides make these trips.

**Group van, private 4x4, or fly-in?**
Shared 4x4 (max 6, window seats) is this
guide's sweet spot. Private 4x4 (~+$400–600
total) buys route freedom — worth it for
photographers and families of 4+. Fly-in
(45 min vs 5.5 h drive) buys time and the
airstrip-to-lions dissolve — worth it on
short trips with deep pockets.

**What's Kenya guide #2?**
**Amboseli** — elephant herds under
Kilimanjaro's snow — or the **bush-and-beach
week** (Mara + Diani's white sand). Votes
welcome; both on the list.
