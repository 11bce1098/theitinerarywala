Armenia is the oldest Christian country on earth, and it wears its
age lightly: 4,000-year-old sites sit an hour from a capital that
runs on espresso, wine bars and pink volcanic stone. The country is
compact enough that you unpack once — everything in this guide is a
day trip from Yerevan, except one deliberate overnight in the
forests of Dilijan — and cheap enough that a private driver for a
day costs what two museum tickets cost elsewhere.

It also pairs perfectly with Georgia: Tbilisi and Yerevan are six
hours apart by road, and many travellers run our Georgia loop and
this guide as one two-week Caucasus trip.

All costs are in Armenian dram (AMD), with conversions calculated at
today's exchange rate. Opening hours are approximate — treat
anything marked ~ as a guide, not a contract.

## Before you go: the 15-minute admin

### Visas — read the fine print this year

- **Visa-free (180 days/year):** EU, UK, USA, Japan, Korea,
  Australia, Russia, Georgia, and — since July 2025 — **GCC
  citizens** including the UAE. No forms, no fees.
- **The residence-permit exemption — verify before booking:**
  Armenia ran a temporary scheme (1 January–1 July 2026) letting
  citizens of 113 countries — including India — enter **visa-free
  for up to 180 days** with a valid residence permit from the US,
  EU/Schengen, UK or a GCC state (physical card, 6+ months
  validity). Its extension beyond July 2026 was unconfirmed at the
  time of writing — **check the official
  [evisa.mfa.am](https://evisa.mfa.am) before you book.**
- **The fallback is painless anyway:** Armenia's e-visa is fully
  online, cheap (from ~USD 6 for 21 days, ~USD 31 for 120 days),
  and issued in a few working days.
- Flights: ~3 hours from the Gulf (Air Arabia from Abu Dhabi and
  Sharjah, flydubai, Wizz Air), with Yerevan's Zvartnots (EVN)
  20 minutes from the centre.

### Money

- Currency: the dram (AMD), ~387 to the US dollar.
- Cards work widely in Yerevan — restaurants, supermarkets,
  hotels — but monasteries' candle stalls, village lunches,
  markets and drivers are **cash**. Withdraw from bank ATMs in the
  city; exchange offices on Tigran Mets and around Republic Square
  give fair rates for USD, EUR and AED.
- The scale of things: an excellent dinner for two with wine runs
  15,000–25,000 AMD, a cross-town taxi 800–1,500, a monastery —
  usually free.

### Taxis and getting around

- **GG and Yandex Go** are the taxi apps — install both before you
  fly, pay cash. Rides are startlingly cheap; nobody haggles with
  street taxis anymore.
- **For the day trips**, two honest options: organised group tours
  (comfortable coaches, English guides, ~8,000–15,000 AMD per
  person per route) or a **private driver for ~25,000–35,000 AMD
  per car per day** — the better deal for two-plus people, and it
  makes every stop yours. Hotels arrange drivers routinely, or use
  the established local operators.

### SIM and data

- Tourist SIMs from **Ucom, Viva-MTS or Team** cost a few thousand
  dram for generous data — airport counters or any city branch,
  passport needed. eSIMs available. Coverage holds on every road
  in this guide.

- An eSIM set up before you fly means data the moment you land, with
  the local SIM as a cheap top-up if you stay longer.

### Travelling with kids?

- Armenia is warmly child-friendly, drives are short (the longest
  in this guide is ~2 h), and monastery grounds are run-around
  space.
- The Cascade's escalators, Republic Square's evening fountains
  and Sevan's beach edges are the child-pleasers; Khor Virap's
  pit-dungeon ladder and Sevanavank's long stairway are the two
  spots to manage.
- Strollers work in central Yerevan; bring the carrier for
  monastery paths.

## Day 1 — Land in Yerevan, Republic Square by night

Base yourself within walking distance of Republic Square — the
whole city works from there.

- **15:00** — Check in, SIM, dram, first Armenian coffee.
- **17:00** — First walk: **Republic Square**'s pink-and-cream
  tuff-stone ensemble, up **Northern Avenue** to Opera.
- **19:00** — Dinner — Yerevan's modern-Armenian restaurants
  (the famous ones around the centre book out on weekends;
  reserve) — dolma, khorovats barbecue, and your first proper
  lavash, ~15,000–22,000 AMD for two.
- **21:00** — Back to Republic Square for the **dancing fountains**
  (evenings roughly May–October) — the city's nightly free show.

**Where to stay in Yerevan** (five nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Alexander (Luxury Collection)** — the city's
  five-star benchmark, steps from Republic Square.
- *Mid-range:* **Holiday Inn Yerevan – Republic Square** —
  superbly rated and exactly where you want to be.
- *Budget:* **Olympia Garden Hotel** — spacious rooms and a pool
  at gentle prices, a short walk out.

## Day 2 — Yerevan: the Cascade, manuscripts and memory

- **9:00** — **The Cascade** — the giant limestone stairway-museum:
  ride the internal escalators up through the **Cafesjian Center's
  sculpture terraces** (gardens free; galleries small fee), and at
  the top — on clear mornings — **Mount Ararat** floating over the
  city. Mornings are the mountain's honest hours.
- **11:30** — **Matenadaran** (closed Sun–Mon; ~9:00–17:00, ~2,500
  AMD) — the fortress-library of illuminated manuscripts,
  Armenia's single most treasured institution.
- **13:30** — Lunch off Saryan Street — Yerevan's wine street; the
  natural-wine bars pour Areni by the glass all afternoon.
- **15:00** — **Tsitsernakaberd Genocide Memorial and Museum**
  (museum closed Mon; free) — sober, essential, beautifully done.
  Allow ninety quiet minutes.
- **17:30** — **GUM Market**'s dried-fruit pyramids and sujukh
  (walnut-grape sweets) — buy the gifts now, they travel well.
- **20:00** — Dinner and a Saryan Street nightcap.

## Day 3 — Garni and Geghard: pagans, monks and basalt organ pipes

The classic half-day-plus east of the city (~30–40 min each way);
private driver ~25,000 AMD, or every operator runs it.

- **9:00** — Depart Yerevan.
- **9:45** — **Garni Temple** (~9:00 to late, ~1,500 AMD) — the
  only standing Greco-Roman temple in the former USSR, on a
  gorge-edge platform. Below it, the **Symphony of Stones** —
  a canyon wall of hexagonal basalt columns like a pipe organ
  (walk down ~40 min, or 4x4 shuttles from the village).
- **12:00** — Village lunch in Garni — the tonir-baked **lavash
  demonstration with fresh cheese and herbs** is the meal to
  choose.
- **13:30** — **Geghard Monastery** (daylight hours, free; UNESCO)
  — chapels carved whole out of the mountain, a spring in the
  rock, and acoustics that turn any chant into architecture. If a
  choir is singing when you're inside, cancel your afternoon.
- **15:30** — Back to Yerevan; ice cream on Northern Avenue,
  evening free.

## Day 4 — South to Ararat: Khor Virap, Areni wine and Noravank

The big southern day (~9 hours; private driver ~30,000–35,000 AMD).

- **8:30** — Depart early — Ararat is clearest before noon.
- **9:30** — **Khor Virap** (daylight, free) — the monastery in
  the vineyard plain **directly under Mount Ararat**: the closest,
  greatest view of the mountain anywhere. Climb down the ladder
  into the pit where St Gregory was held for 13 years — the hole
  Christianity's first state religion crawled out of.
- **11:30** — **Areni** — the village that gave its name to
  Armenia's flagship grape: taste at a family winery
  (2,000–5,000 AMD tastings) and visit **Areni-1 cave**, where
  archaeologists found the world's **oldest known winery — 6,100
  years old**. Wine lunch here.
- **14:30** — **Noravank** (daylight, free) — the trip's most
  dramatic setting: blood-red canyon walls around a 13th-century
  masterpiece; climb the narrow external stairs of the Astvatsatsin
  church if your nerve allows. Golden hour here is superb —
  linger.
- **18:30** — Back in Yerevan; late khorovats dinner.

## Day 5 — Etchmiadzin, ruins and brandy

- **9:00** — **Etchmiadzin** (~30 min west) — the Vatican of the
  Armenian Church, founded 301 AD (cathedral restoration has been
  ongoing — the complex, treasury and churches still merit the
  trip). Modest dress.
- **11:30** — **Zvartnots** (~9:00–18:00, closed Mon in low
  season; ~1,500 AMD) — the ring of a collapsed 7th-century round
  cathedral with Ararat behind the columns: one of Armenia's great
  photographs.
- **13:30** — Lunch back in Yerevan.
- **15:00** — Choose your Yerevan finale: the **History Museum of
  Armenia** on Republic Square (closed Mon; ~2,500 AMD — the
  world's oldest leather shoe lives here, from that same Areni
  cave), or the **Ararat Brandy Factory tour** (~4,500–7,000 AMD
  with tasting) — Churchill's favourite, and the tour is genuine
  fun. Doing both is legal.
- **19:00** — Reserve the dinner you liked best; last fountains.

## Day 6 — Lake Sevan and into the forests of Dilijan

Pack an overnight bag; the driver day (~2 h total driving,
~30,000–35,000 AMD including stops) ends in the mountains.

- **9:30** — Depart Yerevan north.
- **10:45** — **Lake Sevan + Sevanavank** (free) — the blue inland
  sea at 1,900 m; climb the stairway to the twin 9th-century
  churches on the peninsula for the postcard over turquoise water.
  Early = light and no coaches.
- **12:30** — Lakeside lunch: **Sevan trout and crayfish** are the
  order — as fresh as the window view.
- **14:00** — Over the Sevan pass and down into **Dilijan** —
  suddenly alpine, forested, cool: locals call it Armenia's
  Switzerland, which oversells the chalets and undersells the
  monasteries.
- **15:00** — **Haghartsin Monastery** (daylight, free) — towers
  and khachkars deep in maple forest, 20 minutes up a wooded
  valley.
- **17:00** — Check in, then wander **Sharambeyan Street** — the
  restored old lane of timber balconies, craft workshops and
  slow cafés.
- **19:30** — Dinner at your hotel or a family restaurant in
  town — Dilijan's mountain-village cooking is its own genre.

**Where to stay in Dilijan** (one night)

- *The pick:* **Tufenkian Old Dilijan Complex** — 18 heritage
  rooms on Sharambeyan Street itself; staying here is the reason
  to overnight.
- *Mid-range:* **Dilijazz Hotel** — pool, spa, and live jazz; the
  comfortable modern alternative.
- *Budget:* Dilijan's family guesthouses — garden breakfasts and
  hosts who feed you like relatives.

## Day 7 — Forest morning, fly home

- **9:00** — One more Dilijan hour: **Goshavank monastery**
  (~30 min east, free) above its village, or simply the forest
  paths and a slow breakfast.
- **11:00** — Drive back to Yerevan (~1.5–2 h).
- **13:30** — Last errands — Vernissage flea market if it's a
  weekend (carpets, silver, Soviet oddities; haggle kindly) — then
  out to Zvartnots for an evening flight, home overnight for Gulf
  travellers.

## What to pack

- Layers year-round: Yerevan can be 30°C while Sevan (1,900 m) and
  Dilijan run 10°C cooler the same afternoon.
- Modest cover for monasteries; a scarf for women at active
  churches — offered at doors, nicer to bring your own.
- Real shoes: Sevanavank's stairs, Noravank's ledges, Dilijan's
  forest paths.
- Sun kit — the Ararat plain is high-altitude bright.
- Space for dried fruit, sujukh, Areni bottles and a brandy — the
  edible souvenirs here are the best in the region.

## FAQ

**Is Armenia safe?**
Yes — among the safest countries in the region: low crime, calm
streets at night, and famously hospitable to visitors. The
Azerbaijan border areas are the one no-go zone; nothing in this
guide goes near them.

**When is the best time to go?**
May–June and September–October: warm, clear, Ararat visible, and
Areni's harvest in October. July–August cooks in Yerevan (Dilijan
and Sevan become the escape); winter is cheap and moody with snow
on the monasteries.

**Will I actually see Mount Ararat?**
It hides in haze more than photos admit — clearest early morning,
autumn through spring. Khor Virap at 9:30 am is your best single
shot; the Cascade at 8 am is the backup. Build both in, and treat
a clear Ararat day as the win it is.

**Can I combine this with Georgia?**
Perfectly — it's the classic pairing. Tbilisi–Yerevan is ~6 h by
comfortable daily minivans/private transfer (border formalities
are quick for most passports — check yours for both countries).
Run our 7-day Georgia loop plus this guide for the full Caucasus
fortnight.

**What about vegetarians and halal food?**
Vegetarians thrive — the mezze end of the table (dolma variants,
grilled vegetables, herb lavash wraps, lentil everything) is half
the cuisine. Halal-observant travellers: Armenia is not a halal
destination by default — meat is generally not certified — so
seafood (Sevan trout), the rich vegetarian spread, and the handful
of halal-marked restaurants in Yerevan are the practical path.

**Is one night in Dilijan worth the pack-up?**
Yes — it's a different Armenia (forest, cool air, timber lanes),
Haghartsin alone justifies the drive, and returning via the same
road as a day trip makes for a 10-hour slog. The overnight is the
kinder shape of the same sights.
