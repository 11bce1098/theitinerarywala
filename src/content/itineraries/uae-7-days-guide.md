The UAE is two very different cities pretending to be one
destination. Dubai is the show: the world's tallest building, malls
with ski slopes, dune bashing at sunset. Abu Dhabi, ninety minutes
down the road, is the depth: the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque —
arguably the most beautiful modern building in the Islamic world —
and Saadiyat Island, which in the last year quietly became one of
the planet's great museum districts. Most visitors do Dubai and
day-trip Abu Dhabi; this guide gives the capital the two nights it
now deserves.

**Go November–March** — 25°C days, everything outdoors alive. April
and October are hot but workable. June–September is 45°C: the trip
becomes indoor-only, though hotel prices halve and the malls are
Antarctic.

All costs are in UAE dirhams (AED), with conversions calculated at
today's exchange rate. Opening hours are approximate — treat
anything marked ~ as a guide, not a contract.

## Before you go: the 15-minute admin

### Visas

- **Visa-free / visa on arrival (30–90 days):** ~80 passports —
  EU, UK, USA, GCC, Japan, Singapore, most of the Americas — get
  stamped in free on arrival. No forms.
- **Indian passports:** visa on arrival (14 days, extendable) only
  with a valid **US visit visa, US Green Card, or EU visa/residence
  permit** — note the UK-visa route was withdrawn in 2026.
  Everyone else pre-arranges the standard tourist e-visa through
  airlines (Emirates, Etihad, flydubai, Air Arabia all process
  them with your booking) or licensed agents — apply 5+ working
  days ahead.
- Rules shift; check [ICP](https://icp.gov.ae) or your airline's
  visa page close to travel.

### Money and costs — honest framing

- Cards everywhere; cash is nearly optional (keep ~AED 200 for
  abras, souks and tips).
- The UAE is as cheap or expensive as you order it: a metro ride
  is AED 3–7, a legendary curry at a Satwa institution AED 30, a
  Downtown cocktail AED 90. This guide runs mid-range with the
  cheap-and-brilliant options named.
- **The tourist-trap tax is real**: gold souk "deals", unmetered
  taxis at attractions, and AED 450 Burj "Sky" tickets for
  essentially the same view as the AED 179 deck. This guide flags
  each.

### Getting around

- **Dubai:** the Metro is clean, driverless and cheap — buy a
  **Nol card** (AED 25 incl. credit) at any station; it also pays
  buses, trams and the abra boats. **Careem/Uber** for everything
  the metro misses.
- **Dubai ⇄ Abu Dhabi:** the **E100/E101 intercity buses** run
  every ~20 minutes (~AED 25, 2 hours) — or a taxi/ride-hail at
  ~AED 250–300. In Abu Dhabi itself, taxis are the default
  (cheap, metered).
- Airports: DXB is metro-connected; if you can, book **open-jaw**
  (into Dubai, out of Abu Dhabi's AUH) and skip the backtrack.

### SIM and data

- Tourist eSIMs/SIMs from **du, Etisalat (e&) or Virgin Mobile** at
  the airport or any mall — plans from ~AED 50–100. International
  eSIMs work fine too. Note internet calling (WhatsApp/FaceTime
  voice) is restricted — locals use BOTIM or regular calls; plan
  accordingly.

### Etiquette in one paragraph

Dress is relaxed in tourist areas — but shoulders/knees covered in
malls and government buildings is the respectful default, swimwear
stays at the pool/beach, public displays of affection stay minimal,
and photographing strangers (especially local women) without asking
is genuinely offensive. During Ramadan, eating in public daytime is
restricted outside screened areas; evenings become wonderful.
Alcohol is served in licensed venues (hotels, restaurants); public
drunkenness is a legal problem, not a faux pas.

### Travelling with kids?

- Possibly the easiest destination on this site: strollers glide
  everywhere, malls have nurseries, and half the attractions are
  built for children (Aquaventure, aquariums, Ferrari World).
- Two cautions: **dune bashing is rough** — for under-5s book a
  "family/gentle" desert safari or a camp-only package; and
  distances are car-scale — cluster each day's plan by area.
- The abra boat crossing (AED 1!) is the best value child-delight
  in the country.

## Day 1 — Land in Dubai, start in the old city

The right first evening is the Dubai that existed before the
towers.

- **15:00** — Check in, Nol card sorted. Metro or Careem to the
  creek.
- **16:00** — **Al Fahidi Historical District** (open access) —
  wind-tower lanes, courtyard cafés, tiny free museums. Coffee at
  the **Arabian Tea House** — the courtyard classic.
- **17:30** — **Cross the Creek by abra** (AED 1, pay cash on the
  wooden boat) — the best dirham you'll spend — into the **Spice
  and Gold Souks** (evening = full theatre; haggle at 50–60% of
  first quotes, buy saffron by weight, and treat "designer" offers
  as street theatre).
- **19:30** — Dinner: **Ravi** in Satwa — the legendary Pakistani
  canteen (~AED 30–50/person, cash-friendly) — or **Al Seef**'s
  creekside restaurants for the prettier setting.

**Where to stay in Dubai** (four nights)

- *Splurge:* **Waldorf Astoria DIFC** — the current
  best-of-the-best rated luxury pick in town.
- *Mid-range:* **Rove Downtown** — the smart-money choice:
  superbly rated, walkable to Dubai Mall, Burj views from the
  pool deck.
- *Budget:* **Premier Inn / Rove / Ibis** metro-adjacent branches —
  Dubai's chain budget tier is clean and reliable; pick by metro
  station, not by brand.

## Day 2 — Downtown: the future, the tower, the fountains

Book **Museum of the Future weeks ahead** and the **Burj sunset
slot 1–2 weeks ahead** — both are timed-entry sellouts.

- **10:00** — **Museum of the Future** (~9:00–19:00, AED ~159,
  timed) — inside the torus-shaped calligraphy landmark; a
  2-hour immersive tour of 2071. Kids and adults rate it equally.
- **12:30** — Lunch around DIFC or Downtown.
- **14:00** — **Dubai Mall** — not shopping, spectacle: the
  aquarium's free viewing wall, the waterfall, the souk section.
  (Full Aquarium & Underwater Zoo entry, AED ~175, is skippable
  unless kids insist.)
- **16:30** — **Burj Khalifa "At the Top"** (AED ~179 pre-booked
  for levels 124/125) — book the slot ~1 h before sunset: daylight,
  golden hour and city lights in one visit. **Skip the AED ~450
  level 148** — same view, triple price.
- **18:30** — **Dubai Fountain** shows (every 30 min through the
  evening, free) — best from the Souk Al Bahar bridge.
- **20:00** — Dinner Downtown — Time Out Market for variety, or
  book a fountain-view terrace and pay the view premium knowingly.

## Day 3 — Beaches, the Palm and a night in the desert

- **9:30** — Morning on the sand: **Kite Beach** (free, Burj Al
  Arab views, kiosks) or JBR's The Beach.
- **11:30** — **Palm Jumeirah**: monorail out over the fronds, the
  **The View at The Palm** observation deck (AED ~100) or just the
  Atlantis photo stop and the Pointe fountains.
- **13:00** — Lunch at the Marina; walk the yacht-lined loop.
- **15:00** — Pickup for the **evening desert safari** (~AED 250
  per person for the standard: dune bashing, camel ride, sandboard,
  sunset photos, BBQ dinner and shows at a camp; back ~21:30).
  Two honest notes: it's touristy and it's still great — pick a
  higher-rated operator over the cheapest, and for small kids or
  queasy stomachs book the gentle/family version or a
  conservation-reserve safari (pricier, wilder, calmer).

## Day 4 — Your Dubai, then Global Village

Modular day — pick the lane that fits your crew:

- **Families:** **Atlantis Aquaventure** (AED ~320 online) — a
  full day, and the best waterpark in the region; or IMG Worlds
  indoors in summer.
- **Culture:** the **Jumeirah Mosque** guided visit (the one
  mosque in Dubai open to non-Muslims, morning sessions), then the
  **Etihad Museum** (the UAE's founding story, sharply told).
- **Icons and snow:** Mall of the Emirates + **Ski Dubai** for the
  novelty, Dubai Frame (AED ~60) for the old-vs-new panorama.
- **Evening (Oct–Apr):** **Global Village** (~AED 30 entry;
  evenings until late) — 90 country pavilions of street food and
  kitsch; unironically one of the best cheap nights in Dubai.
  Pack for tomorrow's transfer.

## Day 5 — To Abu Dhabi, and the Grand Mosque at sunset

- **10:00** — E100/E101 bus (~AED 25, ~2 h) or taxi to Abu Dhabi;
  check in, easy lunch.
- **16:00** — **Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque** (Sat–Thu 9:00–22:00,
  **Fri from 16:30**; free, online registration recommended; last
  entry 21:30). Dress code enforced: long sleeves and trousers for
  everyone, head covering for women (provided free). Arrive
  ~90 min before sunset: white marble in gold light, then the
  courtyard reflecting pools as the illumination system comes on —
  the single best hour in the UAE.
- **20:00** — Dinner on the **Corniche** or in Al Mina's fish
  market restaurants — pick your fish, they grill it.

**Where to stay in Abu Dhabi** (two nights)

- *Splurge:* **Emirates Palace Mandarin Oriental** — the gold-leaf
  landmark; even non-guests come for the (gold-dusted) cappuccino.
- *Mid-range:* **Corniche Hotel Abu Dhabi** — beachfront, central,
  solid.
- *Budget:* the **Premier Inn / Centro** chains — reliable and
  cheap; check the map first, some sit near the airport rather
  than the Corniche.

## Day 6 — Saadiyat Island: the new museum mile

The day that separates this guide from the pre-2026 ones: Saadiyat's
Cultural District is now fully open — three world-class museums
within walking distance, plus teamLab.

- **10:00** — **Louvre Abu Dhabi** (AED 49; ~10:00–18:30, later
  weekends) — Jean Nouvel's floating dome and its "rain of light";
  the collection walks all civilisations in one line. 2–3 hours.
- **13:00** — Lunch under the dome or at Saadiyat's beach clubs.
- **14:30** — **Zayed National Museum** (AED 49; daily 10:00–20:00)
  — the UAE's new national museum in Foster's falcon-wing towers —
  or the **Natural History Museum** (AED 49) with its meteorites
  and T. rex. **The 3-museum pass (AED 120, valid 3 days)** makes
  doing all three across your stay the obvious play.
- **17:00** — **teamLab Phenomena** (timed entry, roughly AED
  100–150 by date) — Abu Dhabi's own immersive-art flagship, and a
  different show from the Tokyo ones.
- **19:30** — Sunset on **Saadiyat Beach** (protected turtles,
  white sand), dinner on the island or back in town.

## Day 7 — Palace morning, fly home

- **9:30** — **Qasr Al Watan** (~10:00–17:00 visitor hours; AED
  ~65) — the working Presidential Palace's staggering great hall
  and library. 90 minutes well spent.
- **11:30** — Coffee at **Emirates Palace** (the gold cappuccino,
  ~AED 60+ of pure kitsch) or the free-and-breezy Corniche walk.
- **13:00** — Families with a later flight: **Yas Island** is 30
  min toward the airport — Ferrari World, Warner Bros or the
  SeaWorld, each a half-day (AED ~300+ each; only if energy and
  hours truly allow).
- **Evening** — Fly from AUH — or the E100 back to DXB if your
  ticket demands it (allow 3+ hours of buffer).

## What to pack

- Light layers + one warm one: malls, museums and buses are
  aggressively air-conditioned — the indoor UAE is 19°C.
- Modest-cover options for the mosque day (or use the provided
  abayas) and for malls: shoulders/knees covered travels well
  here.
- Real sunscreen, sunglasses, and a refillable bottle — the sun is
  sincere even in January.
- Swimwear for beaches and pools; sandals that survive hot sand.
- A scarf for women (mosque, sun, AC — triple duty).
- Power: UAE uses UK-style type G plugs.

## FAQ

**Is a week too long for Dubai?**
For Dubai alone, arguably. For Dubai *plus* Abu Dhabi — with
Saadiyat's museum district now fully open — seven days is finally
the honest length. If you must cut, cut a Dubai day, not an Abu
Dhabi one; that's new advice as of this year, and we mean it.

**Is the UAE expensive?**
It's optional. The icons are ticketed and add up (budget AED
800–1,000/person for the attractions in this guide), but transport
is cheap, the best cheap food in the Gulf is here (Satwa, Deira,
Al Mina), and beaches, souks, fountains and the Grand Mosque —
half this itinerary — are free.

**What should women travellers know?**
The UAE is one of the safest destinations anywhere for women,
solo included. Dress norms in tourist zones are relaxed;
the mosque and government buildings are the covered-dress
occasions. Metro trains have women-and-children carriages if
preferred.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Halal is the national default — everything is easy. Vegetarians
also thrive: the Indian, Pakistani and Levantine kitchens that
feed the UAE are full of meat-free classics.

**Can I do Abu Dhabi as a day trip instead?**
You can — mosque + Louvre in a long day is the classic — but
you'll skip the sunset mosque hour, the new museums, and Qasr Al
Watan, which are now collectively the best reason to visit the
country. Two nights is the better trade.

**When is Ramadan and does it matter?**
Ramadan shifts ~11 days earlier each year (check dates for your
travel window). Visiting during it is fine and often beautiful —
iftar tents, night markets — but daytime dining is restricted and
hours shift; first-timers wanting the standard trip should book
around it.
