The **Pamir Highway** — the M41 — is the second highest road in
the world, built by Soviet engineers in the 1930s to supply a
border that mattered. It crosses the **Ak-Baital pass at
4,655 m**, runs for days above 3,500 m, and for one long stretch
the **Panj river is the only thing between you and Afghanistan**,
close enough to see children walking to school on the far bank.

This is not a comfortable trip. The road surface ranges from
tarmac to riverbed, the altitude is genuinely high, and the
infrastructure is homestays and shared toilets. It is also one of
the great overland journeys left, and the **Pamiri** hospitality
along it is unlike anything else in Central Asia.

**Read the permit and the security sections before you book
anything.**

## Before you go: permits, altitude and the honest caveats

### The GBAO permit, and the security position

- The Pamirs lie in **Gorno-Badakhshan (GBAO)**, an autonomous
  region, and you need a **separate GBAO permit** on top of your
  visa. It is applied for with the e-visa or added in Dushanbe,
  and it is checked at posts along the road.
- **Be realistic about this:** GBAO has seen periods of unrest and
  the permit has been suspended or restricted at times, with roads
  closed at no notice. **Check your government's current travel
  advice before booking**, and check again before you fly.
- Carry **several photocopies of passport, visa and permit** —
  checkpoints keep them.
- Work with a Dushanbe agency or a Pamiri driver who knows the
  current position. They will know what has changed this month,
  which no guidebook can.

### Altitude is the real constraint

- You sleep at **3,600 m in Murghab** and cross **4,655 m** at
  Ak-Baital. This itinerary goes **west to east**, gaining
  altitude gradually over days, which is the correct direction for
  acclimatisation.
- **Do not reverse it** to save a flight unless you have time to
  acclimatise at the eastern end.
- Expect headaches and broken sleep at Murghab and Karakul. Drink
  constantly, eat lightly, and **go down if symptoms get worse
  rather than better**.

### The vehicle and the season

- **A 4x4 with a driver is the only sensible way.** Shared jeeps
  exist and are cheap; a private vehicle costs more and buys you
  the ability to stop.
- **June to September** is the season. **July and August** are the
  most reliable, with the passes clear.
- **October to May the high passes close** with snow, and much of
  the Wakhan becomes impassable. Late May and early October are a
  gamble.
- Expect **punctures, breakdowns and river crossings**. The driver
  will fix them; a day's delay is normal and should be in your
  plan.

### Money, fuel and food

- **Somoni (TJS).** **Draw everything in Dushanbe** — there is an
  ATM in Khorog that sometimes works and effectively nothing after
  it. Carry USD as backup.
- **No card payment anywhere** east of Khorog.
- **Homestays** are the accommodation: a room, a shared squat
  toilet, often no shower, and dinner with the family. They cost
  very little and they are the best part of the trip.
- Vegetarians should carry supplements — the diet east of Khorog
  is bread, rice, potato and mutton.

## Day 1 — Land in Dushanbe

- **05:00** — Most flights land in Dushanbe before dawn, which is
  the one unhelpful thing about arriving.
- **10:00** — Dushanbe is low, green and startlingly orderly —
  wide Soviet avenues, fountains, and one of the **tallest
  flagpoles in the world** on Rudaki.
- **11:00** — **Confirm the GBAO permit** if you are collecting it
  here, and meet your driver.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then the **National Museum of Antiquities**
  for the **13-metre reclining Buddha** from Ajina-Tepe — the
  largest in Central Asia, and a reminder that this was Buddhist
  long before it was Muslim.
- **16:00** — **Rudaki Park** and the Ismoil Somoni monument.
- **17:30** — **Mehrgon** or **Green Bazaar** for the week's
  supplies: dried fruit, nuts, bottled water and anything you will
  want that a village shop will not have.
- **19:30** — Dinner — *qurutob*, torn bread with yoghurt sauce
  and onion, eaten communally by hand from a shared platter.
- **21:00** — Sleep early. Tomorrow is a long one.

## Day 2 — South to the Panj

- **06:00** — Leave early. **Dushanbe to Kalaikhum is 10 to 14
  hours** depending on the route and the road.
- **09:00** — The **Shurobod pass** route goes over 3,200 m; the
  northern Tavildara route is longer and sometimes closed. Your
  driver will choose on the morning.
- **13:00** — Lunch at a roadside *chaikhana*.
- **15:00** — **And then the Panj.** The road drops to the river
  and **Afghanistan begins on the far bank**, fifty metres away —
  villages, terraced fields, donkeys on a footpath cut into the
  cliff, and people who look across at you. It stays that way, on
  and off, for the next three days, and it is the thing nobody
  forgets.
- **18:00** — **Kalaikhum (Darvoz)**, a strip of houses along the
  river.
- **19:30** — First homestay dinner, and the first of a great many
  cups of tea.

## Day 3 — Up the Panj to Khorog

- **07:00** — The river road to **Khorog**, seven to nine hours
  and spectacular the whole way.
- **09:00** — The road is cut into the gorge wall with the river
  below and Afghanistan opposite. In places the Afghan side has
  only a footpath; in others a surprisingly good Chinese-built
  road.
- **12:30** — Lunch wherever there is somewhere.
- **16:00** — **Khorog**, the GBAO capital at 2,200 m, sitting in
  a bowl where the Gunt meets the Panj. It is small, educated and
  has the feel of a frontier university town — the **University of
  Central Asia** is here.
- **17:30** — The **botanical garden** above the town, the
  **second highest in the world** at 2,320 m.
- **19:00** — Dinner, and **this is where you draw cash and buy
  anything you still need**. After Khorog there is very little.

## Day 4 — Into the Wakhan

- **08:00** — Turn south into the **Wakhan corridor**, the finger
  of Afghan territory that the British and Russians left between
  their empires in 1895 specifically so they would not share a
  border.
- **10:00** — **Garam Chashma** hot springs if you detour, or
  straight on along the valley as it opens out and the Panj slows.
- **12:00** — **Yamchun Fortress**, a 3rd-century BC Silk Road
  fort on a spur 500 m above the valley, with walls and towers
  still standing and a view down both ways.
- **13:30** — **Bibi Fatima** hot springs just above it — hot
  mineral water in a cave pool, and the local belief is that it
  helps women conceive. Men and women bathe separately.
- **16:00** — **Langar**, at the eastern end of the Wakhan, with
  **thousands of petroglyphs** on the rocks above the village —
  ibex, horsemen and hunting scenes going back several thousand
  years.
- **18:00** — Homestay. **Look south:** the **Hindu Kush** stands
  across the valley in Afghanistan, 6,000 m and snow-covered.

## Day 5 — The Wakhan, and up onto the plateau

- **07:30** — **Vrang** and its Buddhist stupa, cut into the
  hillside and dating to the 4th century.
- **09:30** — The valley narrows and the road begins to climb away
  from the river. **You are leaving Afghanistan behind** and
  gaining a thousand metres.
- **12:00** — **Khargush pass** at 4,344 m, and a landscape that
  changes completely: no trees, no villages, just high desert,
  wind and enormous distance.
- **14:00** — Join the main **M41**, and the salt lakes.
- **16:00** — **Bulunkul** and **Yashilkul** — the first a village
  of about forty families at 3,700 m **recorded as the coldest
  inhabited place in Tajikistan**, the second a long turquoise lake
  below it.
- **18:00** — Homestay at Bulunkul. It will be cold, the toilet
  will be outside, and dinner will be fish from the lake.
- **21:00** — **The stars.** 3,700 m, no moisture, no light for a
  hundred kilometres.

## Day 6 — Murghab

- **08:30** — East on the M41 across the plateau to **Murghab**,
  the highest town in Tajikistan at **3,618 m**.
- **11:00** — The landscape is Tibetan rather than Central Asian —
  ochre, treeless, with yak and Kyrgyz yurts, because **the
  population out here is ethnically Kyrgyz**, not Tajik.
- **12:30** — Arrive. **Take it very easy today.** This is the
  altitude night most people feel.
- **14:00** — The **container bazaar** — a market made of shipping
  containers, which is exactly as bleak and functional as it
  sounds and is the town's centre of gravity.
- **16:00** — A short walk, slowly, and no more.
- **18:30** — Dinner at the homestay.
- **Note** — Headache and poor sleep here are normal. Water, no
  alcohol, and tell your driver if you feel worse rather than
  better.

## Day 7 — Ak-Baital and Karakul

- **08:00** — North, and up. The **Ak-Baital pass at 4,655 m** is
  **the highest point on the Pamir Highway** and the second
  highest road pass in the world. The air at the top is thin
  enough that walking twenty metres leaves you breathless.
- **10:00** — Over, and down onto the Karakul basin.
- **11:30** — **Lake Karakul**, 3,900 m, 25 km across, intensely
  blue and ringed by white peaks. **It sits inside a 45 km
  meteorite impact crater**, about 25 million years old, and the
  lake is saline and frozen for much of the year.
- **13:00** — The **village of Karakul** on the shore: a few dozen
  houses, yaks, and the Chinese border fence visible to the east.
- **15:00** — Walk the shore. Nothing grows and nothing moves
  except the wind.
- **18:00** — Homestay, and this will be the coldest night.

## Day 8 — Back toward Murghab, or on to Kyrgyzstan

- **Option A — continue north.** The **Kyzyl-Art pass at 4,280 m**
  and the **Kyrgyz border**, then down to **Osh** — which is how
  most people finish the highway, and it pairs directly with our
  [Kyrgyzstan guide](/itineraries/kyrgyzstan-9-days-guide/). If
  you are doing this, today is a border day and the trip ends in
  Osh.
- **Option B — turn back, 08:00.** Return south to Murghab and
  then west, which is this itinerary — you see the plateau in the
  other light and it is a genuinely different drive.
- **12:00** — **Pshart** or **Madiyan** valley for the yurt camps,
  and the Marco Polo sheep if you are lucky with binoculars.
- **16:00** — Murghab.
- **19:00** — Dinner, and a shower if one is available, which it
  may not be.

## Day 9 — Back to Khorog

- **07:30** — West on the M41, the direct route this time rather
  than the Wakhan, over the **Koi-Tezek pass** at 4,271 m.
- **11:00** — **Jelondy** hot springs on the way down, which is a
  Soviet-era sanatorium and a very welcome bath.
- **14:00** — The descent into the Gunt valley, losing 2,000 m,
  and trees reappear.
- **16:30** — **Khorog**. After a week above 3,500 m, 2,200 m
  feels like sea level and you will sleep properly for the first
  time in days.
- **18:00** — The **Khorog park**, a shower, and a beer.
- **19:30** — Dinner, and settle up with the driver.

## Day 10 — Fly or drive to Dushanbe

- **Option A — fly, 07:00.** The **Khorog–Dushanbe flight** is 45
  minutes in a small aircraft that flies *between* the peaks
  rather than over them, and it is one of the most spectacular
  short flights anywhere. **It only goes in good weather and is
  cancelled often** — treat it as a bonus, not a plan.
- **Option B — drive, 06:00.** Back down the Panj, 14 to 16 hours,
  and a long day.
- **19:00** — Dushanbe. A hotel with a proper bathroom, which
  after ten days will be memorable.
- **20:30** — Dinner somewhere with a menu.

## Day 11 — Fly home

- **Morning** — The **Hissar Fortress**, 30 km west, if your
  flight is late — a restored gateway and madrasas on a hill.
- **Afternoon** — Dushanbe airport is close to the centre.
- **Note** — Keep the GBAO permit until you have left the country;
  it is occasionally asked for again.

## What to pack

- **A sleeping bag liner**, and a sleeping bag if you feel
  strongly about it. Homestay bedding is clean and heavy and
  shared.
- **Serious warm layers.** Nights at Karakul drop below freezing
  in July.
- **Altitude medication** if your doctor advises it, and a plan
  for what you do if symptoms worsen.
- **All your cash, in somoni and USD**, drawn in Dushanbe.
- **Water purification** — tablets or a filter bottle. Bottled
  water runs out east of Khorog.
- **Toilet paper, wet wipes and hand sanitiser.** There are no
  shops.
- **A power bank.** Electricity is intermittent and often only
  in the evening.
- **Lip balm and sun cream.** At 4,000 m the sun is brutal and the
  air has no moisture in it at all.

## FAQ

**Is it safe?**
The road itself is safe in the ordinary sense — the risks are
altitude, driving and remoteness rather than people, and Pamiri
hospitality is remarkable. **But GBAO has had periods of unrest,
permits have been suspended, and roads have closed at no notice.**
Check your government's current advice before booking and again
before flying, and use an operator who knows this month's
position. This is the one guide on this site where that caveat is
not boilerplate.

**How bad is the altitude?**
You sleep at 3,600 m and cross 4,655 m. Most people get headaches
and sleep badly for two or three nights. Going west to east, as
this itinerary does, gains the height gradually and is much
kinder than the reverse.

**Can I do it without a private vehicle?**
Shared jeeps run the route and cost a fraction. You give up every
stop that is not where the driver is already going — which means
most of the Wakhan, Bulunkul and the fortresses. On a once-in-a-
lifetime drive, hire the vehicle.

**Should I finish in Kyrgyzstan?**
Many people do, crossing at Kyzyl-Art to Osh, and it is the
natural end of the highway. It also pairs neatly with our
[Kyrgyzstan guide](/itineraries/kyrgyzstan-9-days-guide/) for a
three-week Central Asia trip.

**When is the season?**
**June to September**, with July and August the most reliable.
Outside it the high passes are closed by snow and the Wakhan road
is impassable.
