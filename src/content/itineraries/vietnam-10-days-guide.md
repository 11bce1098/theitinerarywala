Vietnam runs on a different exchange rate for wonder. A
million-dong note is US$38; a bowl of the world's best noodle
soup is two dollars; a night on a boat inside a UNESCO bay costs
less than a European dinner. And the sights themselves refuse to
be background: Ha Long's two thousand karst towers, Hoi An's
mustard-yellow lantern streets, Hanoi's Old Quarter where every
pavement is a kitchen and every kitchen is a legend.

This first Vietnam guide does the **north and centre properly**
instead of sprinting the whole country: Hanoi deep, one night
afloat, the Ninh Binh river day, then four slow days around Hoi
An. Saigon, the Mekong and Phu Quoc get their own guide — Vietnam
is two trips wearing one visa.

**Go February–April** — the overlap month-window when the north
is dry-ish and warming and the centre is at its sunny best.
October–April suits the north overall (Dec–Jan is cool and
misty — atmospheric Ha Long, pack a jacket); the centre's
danger months are **September–November: typhoon-and-flood
season in Hoi An** (the town floods photogenically and
inconveniently — don't book those months blind). All costs are
in Vietnamese dong (VND).

## Before you go: the five-minute visa (and the zeros)

- **E-visa, done at home:** most nationalities, India included,
  apply on the official **evisa.gov.vn** — **US$25 single / US$50
  multiple entry, 90 days**, approved in **3–5 working days**.
  No embassy, no agent needed.
- The rejections are all self-inflicted: photo spec (4×6,
  white background, no glasses), **name order matching the
  passport exactly**, and — the classic — **selecting the
  wrong entry port** (choose the airport you actually land
  at: Hanoi's Noi Bai for this trip). Apply two weeks out;
  print two copies.
- Passport 6+ months. The e-visa follows your passport, not
  where you live.
- **The zeros briefing:** prices come in thousands. ₫25,000
  is a dollar coffee; ₫250,000 is a nice dinner for two;
  ₫2,500,000 is a boutique hotel night. The 20,000 and
  500,000 notes are both blue — **the most expensive mistake
  in Vietnam is a 25× tip in dim light**. Sort notes by
  denomination on day one.

## Money, roads and the food rules

- **Cash-leaning but modernising:** hotels, cruises and
  bigger restaurants take cards; street food, markets and
  boat ladies are cash. ATMs everywhere (₫3M typical limit
  per pull); carry ₫500k–1M in small notes daily.
- **Grab is the answer** to every taxi question — cars and
  bikes, fixed prices, no meter theatre. Street taxis: only
  Mai Linh or Vinasun brands, meter on.
- **Crossing the road is a skill, and here it is:** wait for
  a gap-ish, step off, walk *slow and steady, never stop,
  never step back* — the scooter river reads your vector and
  parts around you. Locals cross reading their phones;
  you'll manage.
- **Scooter honesty:** technically you need a Vietnamese-
  recognised licence + IDP; most tourist renters check
  nothing, most travel insurance then covers nothing. In
  cities, Grab bikes (₫15–30k a hop) give you the wind
  without the liability.
- Tipping: not traditional; ₫20–50k for guides/spa is warm.
  Cruise crews expect US$3–5/day (budgeted below).
- **Kids:** Vietnam adores them — lantern-making, basket
  boats, beach sand, kid-mild pho. The road-crossing hand-
  hold rule is absolute.

## Day 1 — Land in Hanoi, Old Quarter immersion

Stay in the **Old Quarter/Hoan Kiem** — the point of Hanoi is
to sleep inside the noise.

- **Afternoon** — Land HAN (one-stop from most long-haul hubs, or
  direct where seasonal). Grab into town (~₫350–450k,
  45 min).
- **17:00** — First walk: **Hoan Kiem Lake** loop — Turtle
  Tower, the red **Huc Bridge** and Ngoc Son Temple (₫30k) —
  then dissolve into the **Old Quarter's** 36 guild streets:
  Silk Street, Tin Street, the one that now sells shoes.
- **19:30** — Dinner the Hanoi way: low plastic stools,
  **bún chả** (grilled-pork noodles — the Obama-Bourdain
  dish, ~₫50–70k) — then the trip's first **egg coffee**
  (cà phê trứng, ~₫35–50k): custard-topped genius invented
  when milk was scarce, at Café Giảng where it happened.

**Where to stay in Hanoi** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Sofitel Legend Metropole** — the 1901 colonial
  legend (Chaplin honeymooned; ~9,000 TripAdvisor reviews),
  wartime bunker tours under the bar. ~₫8,000,000+.
- *Mid-range:* **The Oriental Jade Hotel** — 5.0/5 (~6,100
  reviews), TripAdvisor's #1 value in the city: rooftop
  pool over the rooftops, two minutes from the lake.
  ~₫1,800,000–2,600,000.
- *Old Quarter boutique:* **La Siesta Classic Ma May** —
  the long-running Old Quarter favourite with a view-bar on
  top. ~₫1,500,000–2,200,000.

## Day 2 — Hanoi's history, and a train through a coffee shop

- **08:00** — The **Ho Chi Minh complex**: the mausoleum's
  changing guard (free; mornings only, closed Mon/Fri and
  autumn maintenance weeks; dress modestly), the stilt
  house, the **One Pillar Pagoda**.
- **10:30** — The **Temple of Literature** (₫70k) —
  Vietnam's 1070 AD first university, turtle-borne stelae
  of doctoral names; then the **Hoa Lo Prison** museum
  (₫50k) — the "Hanoi Hilton", sobering and worth an hour.
- **13:00** — Lunch: **phở bò** at a famous house (Phở Gia
  Truyền's queue moves fast; ~₫60–90k) — northern pho,
  clear and pure.
- **15:00** — **Train Street**: the café-lined lane where
  the mainline squeezes between kitchens (timetables shift
  and access gets policed — your hotel knows today's rules
  and times; obey the whistle absolutely).
- **17:00** — Water-puppet theatre by the lake (~₫100–150k;
  charming, 50 minutes, kids adore it) or massage hour
  (~₫250–350k).
- **20:00** — **Beer-corner Hanoi** at Tạ Hiện: bia hơi —
  the world's cheapest fresh beer (~₫10–15k) — and grilled
  everything; lime-tea stools for the abstaining.

## Day 3 — Ninh Binh: Ha Long on the rice fields

- **07:30** — Day trip south (~2 h; small-group tours
  **~₫900k–1,100k pp** with lunch beat DIY logistics):
  limestone karsts rising out of paddies instead of sea.
- **10:00** — **Trang An boat ride** (**₫250k**, included
  in most tours): a sampan rowed — often by foot-rowing
  grandmothers — through river caves and between the
  towers. Three unhurried hours of the most peaceful
  scenery in Vietnam.
- **14:00** — **Mua Cave viewpoint**: ~500 steps up the
  dragon-spine ridge for the postcard over Tam Coc's
  bends (bring water; earn it).
- **16:00** — Optional Bai Dinh pagoda or Hoa Lu ancient
  capital, then back to Hanoi by ~19:00.
- **20:00** — Easy Old Quarter dinner; pack a small
  overnight bag for the boat — big luggage can stay with
  your Hanoi hotel or ride under the cruise bus.

## Day 4 — Ha Long Bay: sleep among the towers

- **08:30** — Cruise-shuttle pickup (2.5 h motorway).
  **The price map, honestly:** budget boats **US$105–160
  pp**, solid 4★ mid-range **US$145–250** (this guide's
  lane — Bhaya/Indochina Sails class), 5★ **US$250–450**;
  all include transfers, meals, a cave or island stop and
  kayaking. **Add 15–30% reality-tax** for drinks, tips
  (US$3–5/day crew) and extras. **Lan Ha Bay** (same
  karsts, far fewer boats, via Cat Ba) runs US$20–50 less
  and is the connoisseur's swap.
- **12:30** — Board, cabin, seafood lunch as the karst
  towers close around the boat — two thousand limestone
  islands the legend says a dragon spat as jade.
- **15:30** — Kayak a lagoon or visit **Sung Sot (Surprise)
  Cave**; swim stop in season.
- **18:00** — Sunset on the top deck — the reason overnight
  beats any day trip: the day boats leave, the bay goes
  quiet, and the towers turn to ink.
- **20:00** — Dinner aboard; squid-fishing off the stern
  for the optimists.

## Day 5 — Bay sunrise, fly to the lantern town

- **06:30** — Tai chi or just coffee on deck at sunrise —
  the bay's best hour.
- **09:00** — Cruise brunch while looping back; dock
  ~11:00, bus to Hanoi ~15:00.
- **17:30** — Fly **Hanoi → Da Nang** (~1h20; Vietnam
  Airlines/Vietjet **~₫1,200k–1,800k with a bag** — book
  ahead, Vietjet charges for everything).
- **20:00** — Grab to **Hoi An** (45 min, ~₫400–450k);
  first sight of the old town glowing — check in and walk
  fifteen lantern-lit minutes before bed.

**Where to stay in Hoi An** (five nights)

- *Luxury:* **La Siesta Hoi An Resort & Spa** — 5.0/5
  (~8,700 reviews), pools and gardens a shuttle-hop from
  the old town. ~₫3,000,000–4,500,000.
- *Mid-range:* **Allegro Hoi An — A Little Luxury Hotel** —
  5.0/5 (~6,600 reviews), walking distance to the lanterns.
  ~₫2,000,000–2,800,000.
- *Value:* **Cozy An Boutique Hotel** — TripAdvisor's #1
  value here, riverside near the market. ~₫900,000–
  1,400,000. (Hoi An's homestays, ₫500–800k, are also the
  best budget beds in Vietnam.)

## Day 6 — Hoi An old town, slowly

- **07:30** — The old town **before the day-trippers**
  (best hours: 7–9 am): the **Japanese Covered Bridge**,
  mustard shophouse lanes, market ladies stacking herbs.
  The **old-town ticket (₫120k**, valid days, funds
  restoration) admits five heritage houses — Tan Ky house
  and the Fujian assembly hall first.
- **10:30** — **Tailor time, round one:** Hoi An's 400+
  tailors cut suits and dresses in 24–48 h (~US$80–150 a
  suit at reputable houses — pick by recent reviews, insist
  on two fittings; you'll wear it for years).
- **13:00** — Lunch on the local trinity: **cao lầu**
  (noodles made with lye-well water, only real here),
  white-rose dumplings, bánh mì at Phượng (Bourdain's,
  ~₫35k).
- **15:30** — **Basket-boat hour** in the Bay Mau coconut
  palms (~₫100k pp; touristy, spinning, giggly) or a
  riverside nap — this town rewards idleness.
- **18:30** — **Lantern hour**: the Hoai River at dusk,
  ₫20k paper lanterns set afloat, the Japanese Bridge lit.
  Full-moon nights switch off the electricity entirely.
- **20:00** — Riverside dinner (~₫300–500k for two);
  fitting number two on the walk home.

## Day 7 — Ba Na Hills: the bridge held by giant hands

- **07:30** — Day trip into the mountains (~45 min):
  **Ba Na Hills** (**₫1,000,000 adult** — steep by Vietnam
  standards and includes the record-breaking cable-car
  system, the French-village theme park and the alpine
  coaster).
- **09:00** — The reason you came: the **Golden Bridge** —
  the ribbon of gold held up by two colossal moss-stone
  hands over the clouds. Photograph it before 10:00 or
  after 15:00; midday is a queue with a view.
- **11:00** — Fantasy Park's free rides for the kids, the
  garden terraces, lunch up top (buffet add-on ~₫300k) —
  or descend for a seafood lunch back at sea level.
- **16:00** — **Marble Mountains** on the return (₫40k +
  lift ₫15k): cave pagodas inside the marble crags; or
  straight to **An Bang Beach** for the golden hour.
- **19:30** — Dinner + the **Hoi An Memories Show** option
  (~₫350–500k by tier — 500 performers on a river island;
  spectacle-lovers say yes).

## Day 8 — Beach, kitchen, or ruins: the choose-day

- **Morning menu, pick one:**
  - **An Bang Beach** (free, loungers ~₫40k with a drink):
    the easy, excellent default.
  - **Cooking class** (~₫700–900k pp): market shopping by
    basket-boat, then spring rolls and cao lầu you'll
    actually recreate at home. Hoi An's classes are
    Vietnam's best.
  - **My Son Sanctuary** (₫150k + ~₫700k car/tour): the
    4th–13th-century Cham temple ruins in their jungle
    bowl — go at 07:00 before heat and coaches.
- **15:00** — Final fittings; tea at Reaching Out Teahouse
  (run by deaf artisans; the silence is the point).
- **18:00** — **An Bang sunset dinner** on the sand
  (~₫400–600k for two) or the night market's chè desserts.

## Day 9 — Da Nang day, farewell lanterns

- **10:00** — Into **Da Nang**: My Khe beach hour, the
  **Cham Museum** (₫60k) for the My Son backstory, lunch
  of mì quảng (the turmeric noodle the city runs on,
  ~₫40–60k).
- **15:00** — **Son Tra Peninsula** run: the 67-m Lady
  Buddha at Linh Ung pagoda, monkeys in the banyans, the
  coast road views.
- **19:00** — Back in Hoi An for the last lantern walk —
  or, if it's a **weekend, stay for Da Nang's Dragon
  Bridge breathing fire at 21:00** (free, Sat–Sun).
- **21:00** — Farewell dinner; collect the tailoring;
  attempt suitcase Tetris.

## Day 10 — Home from Da Nang

- **Morning** — DAD airport (20 min): fly home **via a
  one-ticket connection** (Hanoi, Saigon or a Southeast
  Asian hub — DAD's long-haul non-stops are seasonal at best).
  Last Vietnamese iced coffee at the gate, lanterns in
  the hand luggage.

- Layers for the north (Dec–Feb Hanoi is 12–18°C and
  misty) + summer kit for the centre; a light rain shell

- Deck-friendly sandals and one warm layer for the bay's
  evening breeze.
- Mua-viewpoint trainers (500 steps), mosquito repellent,

- Modest cover for pagodas and the mausoleum (knees/

  coffee beans, Vietnam packs itself home with you.

  denomination — see the zeros briefing.

## FAQ

**Is the e-visa really that easy?**
Yes — five minutes and $25 if you respect the details:
official site only (evisa.gov.vn; copycat sites charge
triple), photo to spec, surname/given-name order exactly
as the passport, **entry port matching your actual
airport**, applied ~2 weeks out. Rejections are almost
always one of those four.

**Which cruise tier is actually worth it?**
The 4★ mid-range is the sweet spot: real cabins with
balconies, good food, small-group kayaking — for half a
5★ price. Budget boats are fine for the young and easy;
what you're really buying up-tier is cabin comfort and
crowd size. And **Lan Ha Bay** is the same geology with
a fraction of the traffic — the quiet-lover's correct
answer.

**September–November in Hoi An — how bad, really?**
It's typhoon-and-flood season: some years pass gently,
some years the old town canoes down its own streets for
a week. If those are your only dates, buy refundable
rooms, watch forecasts, and have a Da Nang-city plan B.
February–April is the no-drama window for this exact
route.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
The honest ranking: harder than Malaysia/Singapore,
very manageable with strategy. Hanoi and Da Nang/Hoi An
each keep a small cluster of halal kitchens (Indian and
Malay-run; your hotel will map them), seafood is
everywhere and unambiguous, and Buddhist **cơm chay**
(vegetarian) eateries appear in every town — but fish
sauce is Vietnam's bloodstream: "không nước mắm" (no
fish sauce) and "ăn chay" (I eat veg) are the two
phrases to screenshot.

**Can we do this with kids?**
Wonderfully: lantern-making, basket-boat spinning,
water puppets, beach afternoons, cyclo rides — and
Vietnamese food has a kid-mild lane (pho, fresh rolls,
bánh mì). The two absolutes: hands held at every road,
and the cruise's child-railings briefing taken
seriously.

**What about Saigon, the Mekong and Phu Quoc?**
That's the other Vietnam — faster, hotter, delta-green,
with an island finale — and it's guide #2, sized for
the same 90-day e-visa (buy multiple-entry if you'll
combine both trips with a Cambodia hop between).
