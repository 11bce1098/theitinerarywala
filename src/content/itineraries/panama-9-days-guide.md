Panama is 50 km wide at its narrowest, which is why the canal is
here and why you can stand on the Pacific in the morning and reach
the Caribbean by lunchtime. It is also the easiest country in
Central America to travel: the currency is the US dollar, the
capital has a real skyline and a metro, and English is common.

Three parts make the trip. **Panama City** has the canal locks, a
restored colonial quarter and the causeway built from the spoil.
**Guna Yala**, usually called **San Blas**, is an archipelago of 365
islands governed autonomously by the Guna people — palm, sand, reef,
and almost nothing else. **Bocas del Toro** is the Caribbean island
chain near Costa Rica, busier and more developed, with good
snorkelling and a strong social scene.

The two island groups are very different trips, and it is worth
being clear which one you actually want.

## Before you go: boats, the Guna, and the two coasts

### Entry

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for 90 to 180 days — the
  EU, UK, US, Canada, Australia and most of Latin America.
- **Proof of onward travel and funds** can be requested at
  immigration, and occasionally is.
- **Panama uses the US dollar** as legal tender, alongside its own
  balboa coins which are pegged one to one. Cards work widely in
  the city; **the islands are cash**.

### San Blas is not a resort, and that is the point

- **Guna Yala is autonomous territory.** You enter with permission,
  pay a community fee, and the rules are theirs.
- **Getting there is a 4x4 transfer from Panama City** — roughly two
  and a half hours over a mountain road that is steep and winding —
  followed by a small boat. Departures are before dawn.
- **Accommodation is basic by design**: sand-floor cabins, bucket or
  limited showers, generator power for a few hours, and no wifi
  worth the name. That is the deal, and the islands are
  extraordinary.
- **Drones are banned** and photographing people requires
  permission, which is often declined. Respect it.
- **Boats between islands are small and open.** In swell it is wet;
  in wind it can be cancelled.

### Bocas del Toro is the other thing

- **Bocas is reachable by a short domestic flight** from Panama City
  (about an hour) or by a long bus and ferry.
- It is **developed, sociable and easy** — hostels, restaurants,
  water taxis between islands, and reliable wifi.
- The snorkelling and surf are good; the beaches are not San Blas.
- **Do not try to do both in a week.** This itinerary does, in nine
  days, and it is tight.

### Seasons and the canal

- **December to April is the dry season** and the better time,
  especially for the islands.
- **May to November** is wet — usually afternoon rain rather than
  all-day, and the Caribbean side is wetter and less seasonal.
- **The canal visitor centres** at **Miraflores** and
  **Agua Clara** have viewing platforms. Ships transit on a
  schedule; check the day's timetable so you are there when
  something is actually in the lock.

## Day 1 — Land in Panama City

- **Afternoon** — Land, drop bags in **Casco Viejo**, the restored
  colonial quarter.
- **Evening** — The seafront promenade with the skyline across the
  bay, and dinner in the old town.

## Day 2 — The canal, and the old city

- **Morning** — **Miraflores Locks**, timed to a transit. The scale
  of a Panamax ship rising in a chamber is the thing to see.
- **Afternoon** — **Casco Viejo** properly: the ruined Jesuit
  convent, the plazas, and the Biomuseo on the causeway if you want
  the Gehry building.
- **Evening** — Pack light for San Blas; you are leaving before
  dawn.

## Day 3 — To San Blas

- **05:00** — 4x4 transfer over the mountains, then a boat out to
  the islands.
- **Midday** — Arrive. There is sand, palms, reef and a horizon.
- **Afternoon** — Snorkel, swim, do nothing, which is the whole
  programme.
- **Evening** — Fish and coconut rice, and stars with no light
  anywhere.

## Day 4 — The islands

- **All day** — Boat trips between islands, a sandbar that is
  nothing but sand, and a shipwreck reef. Your host decides the
  route by the weather.
- **Evening** — A second night, which is the minimum that makes the
  journey worth it.

## Day 5 — Back to the city, and on to Bocas

- **Morning** — Boat and 4x4 back, arriving around midday.
- **Afternoon** — Fly to **Bocas del Toro**, about an hour.
- **Evening** — **Bocas Town** on Isla Colón: wooden buildings over
  the water, and a very different energy.

## Day 6 — Reefs and islands

- **Morning** — A boat day: **Dolphin Bay**, the coral gardens at
  **Cayo Coral**, and **Red Frog Beach** on Bastimentos.
- **Afternoon** — Snorkelling, which is the best reason to be here.
- **Evening** — Back to town.

## Day 7 — Bastimentos, or surf

- **Option A** — **Bastimentos** island: Wizard Beach, the jungle
  path, and the Ngäbe communities inland.
- **Option B** — Surf lessons at **Playa Bluff** or Isla Carenero,
  depending on season.
- **Evening** — Last island night.

## Day 8 — Back to Panama City

- **Morning** — Fly back, an hour.
- **Afternoon** — **Panamá Viejo**, the ruins of the original city
  sacked in 1671, or the **Amador Causeway** by bike.
- **Evening** — A rooftop, since the skyline is the city's own
  favourite view.

## Day 9 — Fly home

- Tocumen is Latin America's main hub, and it is busy. Allow time.

## What to pack

- **Cash in US dollars, in small notes**, for San Blas — there is no
  card machine on an island of forty people.
- **A dry bag**, which is not optional for either archipelago.
- **Reef-safe sunscreen and a rash vest.** You will be in the water
  for days and the sun is equatorial.
- **A head torch** for generator-free hours on San Blas.
- **Insect repellent**, particularly for Bocas.
- **Type A/B plugs**, 120V, as in the US.

## FAQ

**San Blas or Bocas — which if I only do one?**
San Blas for the islands themselves: emptier, more beautiful,
culturally distinct, and basic. Bocas for comfort, snorkelling
variety and company. They are not substitutes.

**Is San Blas really that basic?**
Yes. Sand floors, limited showers, generator power for a few hours
and no useful internet. Two nights is the sweet spot for most
people.

**How long does the canal take to see?**
Two hours at Miraflores if you time it to a transit, and the
timetable is published daily. A full transit cruise takes most of a
day and is a different, much more expensive thing.

**When should I go?**
**December to April**, the dry season, especially if the islands
matter. The Caribbean side is less seasonal and rains a bit
year-round.
