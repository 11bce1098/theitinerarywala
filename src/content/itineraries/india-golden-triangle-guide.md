India rewards preparation like nowhere else. The same week that
overwhelms an unprepared traveller — the noise, the touts, the
sheer voltage of it — becomes one of the great trips when you know
which train to book, which gate to stand at before sunrise, and
which "helpful" stranger to walk past. This is the Golden Triangle
— Delhi, Agra, Jaipur — planned to that standard.

**Go October–March.** April–June is 40°C+; monsoon (July–
September) is atmospheric but sticky. One honest winter caveat:
December–January brings Delhi fog and smog — flights delay, and
the Taj can hide in mist until mid-morning; late October–November
and February are the sweet spots.

All costs are in Indian rupees (INR), with conversions calculated
at today's exchange rate. Foreigner ticket prices are quoted;
**Indian passport and OCI holders pay domestic rates — the ticket
lines in this guide shrink by ~80% for you.**

## Before you go: the 15-minute admin

### Visas

- **e-Visa:** 171 nationalities qualify — apply only at the
  official [indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa](https://indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa)
  (copycat sites charge triple). Tourist options: 30-day
  (~USD 10–25), 1-year, and 5-year multiple-entry. Standard
  processing ~5 working days; apply 2+ weeks out. Print it.
- **GCC-based readers:** eligibility follows your passport, not
  residence; most nationalities on the list apply normally.
- Six months' passport validity and two blank pages, as ever.

### Money — the UPI revolution, now for visitors

- India runs on **UPI QR payments** — chai stalls to five-stars —
  and since the **UPI One World** wallet, foreign tourists (G20
  nationalities) can join: pick it up at major airports/authorised
  counters with passport + visa, load it in INR, and pay by QR
  everywhere. Daily limits are generous (₹1 lakh).
- Foreign cards work at hotels, malls and bigger restaurants;
  small India is QR-or-cash.
- Carry **₹3,000–5,000 in small notes** — monuments' shoe minders,
  rickshaws, tips. ATMs everywhere in cities (use bank-attached
  ones).

### SIM and data

- **Airtel or Jio tourist SIMs** at airport counters (passport +
  visa + a photo; ~₹500–1,000 for generous data) — or an
  international eSIM to skip the paperwork. Data is among the
  cheapest on earth; get it — Uber/Ola, maps and UPI all need it.

### Getting around

- **In cities:** **Uber and Ola** are the sanity-savers — cheap,
  metered, no negotiation. Delhi's **metro** is excellent (women's
  carriage at the front); Jaipur autos by app or agreed fare.
- **Delhi → Agra:** the **Gatimaan Express** — Hazrat Nizamuddin
  8:10 → Agra Cantt 9:50 (1h40; Chair Car ₹860, Executive ₹1,695,
  breakfast served). India's fastest train and the painless way in.
  Book via the IRCTC site/app (foreigner registration takes a
  day — do it from home) or simply through 12Go/an agent for a
  small markup.
- **Agra → Jaipur:** hire a private car (~₹7,000–8,500, ~5 h) —
  it turns the transfer into sightseeing via **Fatehpur Sikri**,
  which no train does.
- **Leaving:** fly out of Jaipur or connect domestically — don't
  drive back to Delhi just to fly.

### The scam list — read once, relax all week

India's tourist scams are famous, predictable and entirely
avoidable:

- **"That office/hotel is closed"** — it isn't. Anyone at a
  station or gate redirecting you to "official tourist offices" is
  a commission agent. Walk on.
- **Only book trains and tickets on official sites** or through
  your hotel — never the "government office" a driver suggests.
- **Agra and Jaipur gem/carpet "export deals"** — the classic.
  Shops your driver insists on pay him commission; polite refusal
  is expected and painless.
- **Monument "guides"** — use licensed guides at official counters
  (worth it at the Taj and Amber) and agree the price first.
- Use app taxis or prepaid counters from airports. That's 95% of
  the trouble gone.

### Health and food, honestly

Drink bottled/filtered water only (check seals), eat at busy
places where turnover is high — street food included; the empty
stall is the risky one, not the crowded one. Ease in for two days
before going full chaat. Carry oral rehydration salts and your
usual stomach basics; most travellers are fine, and the food is a
top-three reason to come.

### Travelling with kids?

- India adores children and hotels accommodate them easily; the
  challenges are heat, crowds and traffic noise — plan monument
  mornings, pool/AC afternoons.
- A carrier beats a stroller everywhere except Lutyens' Delhi and
  hotel grounds.
- Autorickshaw rides, monkey-spotting at Amber, and elephant-free
  jeep rides up the fort are the child highlights. (Skip the
  elephant rides — welfare concerns are real and jeeps are
  faster.)

## Day 1 — Land in Delhi, ease in with the Mughals

- **13:00** — Land, SIM + UPI wallet + cash at the airport, Uber
  to the hotel (Aerocity or central Delhi).
- **16:00** — **Humayun's Tomb** (sunrise–sunset; ~₹600
  foreigners) — the Taj's architectural grandfather in golden
  afternoon light, with the restored Sunder Nursery gardens next
  door for a first gentle walk.
- **18:30** — **Bangla Sahib Gurudwara** (free; head covered,
  provided) — the great white-and-gold Sikh temple: calm, kind,
  and the community kitchen feeds thousands daily.
- **20:00** — Dinner in Connaught Place — start the food trip
  properly: butter chicken and dal makhani where they were
  arguably invented.

**Where to stay in Delhi** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Imperial** — colonial-era grande dame in the
  centre; the art collection alone is a museum.
- *Mid-range:* **ibis New Delhi Aerocity** — clean, reliable,
  airport-handy; central alternatives around Connaught Place at
  similar rates.
- *Budget:* **Hotel Hari Piorko** (Paharganj) — the backpacker
  district's best-run option, metro at the door.

## Day 2 — Old Delhi's voltage, New Delhi's sweep

- **8:30** — Metro to Chandni Chowk. **Red Fort** (~9:30–16:30,
  **closed Mondays**; ~₹600–950 by ticket type) — the Mughal
  seat of power; 90 minutes covers it.
- **11:00** — **Jama Masjid** (small camera fee; modest dress,
  robes lent) — climb the minaret for the old-city rooftops.
- **12:00** — **Chandni Chowk by cycle-rickshaw** (agree ~₹200–300
  for the loop): the spice market's sneeze-inducing lanes, the
  silver street, wedding-brocade alleys.
- **13:00** — Lunch in the lanes — **Paranthe Wali Gali**'s
  stuffed parathas or Karim's by the mosque (both legendary,
  both ~₹200–400/person).
- **15:00** — **Qutub Minar** (sunrise–sunset; ~₹600) — the
  12th-century victory tower and its ruined mosque complex — or
  swap for the National Museum if the day is hot.
- **17:30** — Drive past **India Gate and the Rajpath sweep** at
  dusk.
- **19:30** — Dinner + early night: tomorrow is an 8:10 train.
  Pack a small bag; big luggage can travel with you (racks are
  fine).

## Day 3 — Gatimaan to Agra: the fort and the river sunset

**Check the calendar now: the Taj is closed Fridays** — this
itinerary puts you at its gates tomorrow morning, so shift the
whole trip a day if that lands on a Friday.

- **7:30** — Hazrat Nizamuddin station; **Gatimaan Express** 8:10
  → Agra Cantt 9:50, breakfast on board.
- **10:30** — Drop bags near the Taj **East Gate** (staying here
  makes sunrise trivial).
- **11:30** — **Agra Fort** (sunrise–sunset; ~₹650) — the red
  sandstone palace-fortress where Shah Jahan spent his last years
  gazing at the tomb he built; the river-window view of the Taj is
  the day's teaser.
- **14:00** — Lunch, then rest through the heat — Agra afternoons
  are for pools and naps.
- **16:30** — **Mehtab Bagh** (~₹300) — the garden directly across
  the Yamuna: the Taj at sunset, front-on, with a fraction of the
  crowd. Photographers' hour.
- **19:30** — Dinner near the hotel; sleep early. Set two alarms.

**Where to stay in Agra** (one night)

- *Splurge:* **The Oberoi Amarvilas** — every room faces the Taj,
  600 m away; the single most justified luxury spend in India.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Taj Resorts** — clean, rooftop Taj view,
  two minutes from the East Gate ticket office.
- *Budget:* the East Gate cluster of guesthouses — pick by current
  reviews; location beats luxury tonight.

## Day 4 — The Taj at first light, then the royal road to Jaipur

- **5:45** — Be at the **East Gate** queue before the
  sunrise opening (gates open ~30 min pre-sunrise; buy tickets
  online the night before at the official ASI portal — **₹1,300 +
  ₹200 mausoleum add-on**, and pay the ₹200: standing in the
  marble chamber is the point). Walk through the great gateway
  and let the first view happen — no photo prepares you.
- **6:15–8:30** — The Taj with soft light and thin crowds: the
  bench photo early, then the mausoleum platform (free shoe
  covers), the mosque side, the river edge. Licensed guides at
  the counter are worth it here (~₹1,200–1,500).
- **9:00** — Breakfast back at the hotel; check out.
- **10:00** — Private car toward Jaipur (~5 h with the stop;
  ~₹7,000–8,500).
- **11:00** — **Fatehpur Sikri** (sunrise–sunset; ~₹610) —
  Akbar's abandoned red-sandstone capital, intact and eerie;
  90 minutes with the licensed guide, ignore the freelance ones
  at the parking.
- **13:00** — Lunch on the highway (the big clean midway
  restaurants exist for exactly this).
- **17:00** — Arrive **Jaipur**, check in.
- **19:30** — Dinner: **LMB** in Johri Bazaar for the classic
  Rajasthani thali, or your haveli's rooftop.

**Where to stay in Jaipur** (three nights)

- *Palace:* **Rambagh Palace** — an actual maharaja's palace;
  peacocks on the lawns, one of Asia's great hotels.
- *Heritage mid-range:* **Samode Haveli** — courtyards, frescoes
  and a pool in a 175-year-old mansion; the smart-money pick.
- *Budget:* **ibis Jaipur City Centre** — modern, central,
  rooftop pool.

## Day 5 — Amber Fort and the Pink City

Buy the **composite ticket** (₹1,000 foreigners, valid 2 days:
Amber, Hawa Mahal, Jantar Mantar, Nahargarh, Albert Hall,
Isarlat) — it pays for itself by mid-afternoon.

- **8:00** — **Amber Fort** at opening (8:00–17:30; composite or
  ₹500) — the honey-coloured fort-palace over Maota lake: the
  Sheesh Mahal mirror hall, the ramparts, the courtyards before
  the coaches. Go up by jeep or on foot — **skip the elephant
  ride**.
- **10:30** — **Panna Meena ka Kund** stepwell (free) below the
  fort — the geometric-staircase photo — then the **Jal Mahal**
  water palace photo stop on the drive back.
- **13:00** — Lunch in the old city.
- **14:30** — **City Palace** (9:30–17:00; ~₹700, separate from
  the composite) — the royal family still lives upstairs; the
  four seasonal gates of Pritam Niwas courtyard are Jaipur's
  most photographed doors.
- **16:30** — **Jantar Mantar** (composite) — the giant
  18th-century astronomical instruments; a guide makes it ten
  times better.
- **18:00** — **Bazaar hour**: Johri (jewellery), Bapu (textiles,
  mojari shoes) — haggle cheerfully from half.
- **20:00** — Dinner, then the famous **Lassiwala** on MI Road
  tomorrow morning if the queue defeats you tonight.

## Day 6 — Hawa Mahal light, block prints and a sunset fort

- **8:45** — **Hawa Mahal** (9:00–16:30; composite) — the
  953-window facade glows pink in morning light; best full-facade
  view with chai from the cafés opposite, then the surprisingly
  good interior climb.
- **10:30** — Choose your Jaipur: a **block-printing workshop**
  (Sanganer/Bagru or in-town studios, ~₹1,500–2,500 — the
  souvenir you made yourself), the **Albert Hall Museum**
  (composite), or the monkey-filled **Galtaji temple** gorge.
- **13:00** — Lazy thali lunch; afternoon pool or bazaar round
  two.
- **17:00** — **Nahargarh Fort** (composite) on the ridge for
  sunset over the whole pink city — drinks at the terrace café as
  the lights come on.
- **20:00** — Farewell option A: a serious Rajasthani dinner in
  town. Option B: **Chokhi Dhani** (~₹1,200–1,500/person) — the
  village-fair resort of folk dances, camel rides and unlimited
  thali; touristy, and children think it's the best night of the
  trip.

## Day 7 — Fly home from Jaipur

- **Morning** — Last lassi, last bazaar errand; Jaipur airport is
  20 minutes out. Fly home direct or via Delhi/Mumbai — no need to
  retrace the road.

## What to pack

- Breathable modest layers — shoulders/knees covered eases every
  temple, mosque and gurudwara visit; a scarf earns its space
  daily.
- Slip-on shoes (removed constantly) + socks for hot marble
  forecourts.
- Sun kit, refillable bottle with filter or reliance on sealed
  bottles, and hand sanitiser.
- Stomach kit: rehydration salts, your usual remedies,
  probiotics if you believe in them.
- Winter mornings (Dec–Jan) need a real jacket — Delhi at dawn
  is 7°C; afternoons are 22°C. Layers, always.
- Earplugs for light sleepers. India is gloriously loud.

## FAQ

**Is the Golden Triangle too touristy?**
It's touristy the way Rome is touristy — because it's the best
stuff. The counter-move isn't avoiding it, it's timing: Taj at
sunrise, Amber at 8:00, Old Delhi by rickshaw at midday. This
itinerary is built entirely around those windows.

**Is it safe, and what about women travellers?**
The triangle is India's most practiced tourist circuit. Standard
precautions apply — app taxis over street hails, no unlit walks
in unfamiliar areas at night, firmness with touts. Women
travellers, including solo, do this route in numbers; dressing
modestly reduces attention, the metro's women's carriage and
women-driver cab options (Delhi) help, and hotel pickups for
late arrivals are worth the small cost.

**Will I get sick?**
Most visitors don't, and caution costs little: sealed water, busy
kitchens, fruit you peel. The two-day ease-in before full street
food is the tested strategy.

**Can I do it in 5 days?**
Yes — cut a Delhi day and a Jaipur day. But the Taj sunrise and
the Amber morning are non-negotiable anchors; protect those and
compress around them.

**What's the natural extension?**
South from Jaipur into blue **Jodhpur** and lakeside **Udaipur**
(our Rajasthan guide, coming) — or fly to **Kerala** for the
opposite India: quiet backwaters after the north's intensity. Ten
days total handles the Triangle plus one.

**Tipping?**
₹50–100 for bellhops and drivers per service, ~10% in restaurants
without a service charge, ₹100–200/day for a hired car's driver at
the end. Small, frequent, appreciated.
