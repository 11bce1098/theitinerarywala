The Seychelles are what the Maldives' marketing department
wishes it had: not just water in seventeen blues, but
**granite** — billion-and-a-half-year-old boulders sculpted
into curves no camera has ever quite captured, forests full of
a palm found nowhere else on Earth, and giant tortoises
strolling public roads like retired landlords.

Whatever passport you hold, this is the **easiest
long-haul-feeling trip that exists**: Seychelles lets in every
nationality without a visa, a €10.90 online form is the entire
paperwork, non-stops reach Mahé from the Gulf, East Africa and
Europe, and the time zone is the
same as home. The catch is the price tag — the Seychelles is
honest-to-goodness expensive — so this guide is built around
the local tricks (guesthouses, takeaway boxes, the SCR 12 bus)
that cut the bill without cutting the postcard.

The route is the classic three-island ladder: **Mahé (3
nights) → Praslin (2 nights) → La Digue (2 nights)**, hopping
by fast ferry. Costs are in Seychellois rupees (**SCR ~16 to
the euro; hotels quote EUR, everything local runs in SCR**).
Figure **{{SCR 23000}} per person (≈ €1,480 / AED 6,200 /
₹1.4 lakh)** excluding international flights.

## Before you go: the admin (there barely is any)

- **No visa — for anyone.** Seychelles is visa-free for all
  nationalities, Indian passports included: you receive a
  free **Visitor's Permit on arrival** (30 days). What you
  must do is submit the **digital Travel Authorisation at
  seychelles.govtas.com** (or the mobile app) before
  boarding: **€10.90 standard**, apply **up to 10 days
  before your flight** — most approvals land within a day.
  That's it. That's the whole section.
- Have printouts/PDFs ready at immigration: **return
  ticket, accommodation bookings for every night**, and
  proof of funds (the official guideline is ~US$150/day —
  a card statement suffices; families are rarely asked).
- Passport valid 6 months. No mandatory vaccinations, **no
  malaria anywhere in Seychelles** — a genuine tropical
  rarity.
- **Getting there:** Emirates flies **Dubai–Mahé non-stop
  daily (~4h15)**; returns commonly AED 2,400–4,000 —
  book 2–3 months out. Air Seychelles also connects
  Mumbai for India-side family joining the trip.
- **When to go:** the magic windows are the trade-wind
  changeovers — **April–May and October–November** — when
  the sea goes glassy. November–March is hotter and
  stiller with brief downpours; **May–September is
  breezier with seaweed washing onto east-facing beaches**
  (it moves around; west coasts stay clean). An October
  trip hits the sweet spot.

## The money truth (read before booking anything)

Seychelles has no budget-airline, hostel-circuit economy —
it's a small island nation importing almost everything. The
honest numbers: restaurant mains run **SCR 250–450**, a beer
SCR 90–120, resort dinners €50+ a head. The system that
keeps this trip at {{SCR 23000}} pp instead of double:

- **Sleep in guesthouses and self-catering apartments** —
  Seychelles' family-run guesthouses are licensed,
  spotless, and half the price of hotels. We list real
  ones below.
- **Lunch is a takeaway box**: every island has Creole
  takeaways selling fish curry, chicken curry or octopus
  with rice for **SCR 75–125**. Locals eat this daily;
  so should you.
- **The SCR 12 bus**: Mahé and Praslin's SPTC buses cost
  twelve rupees flat, go almost everywhere, and are an
  attraction in themselves (sit left on coastal routes).
  Taxis, by contrast, are SCR 500+ per hop — the single
  fastest way to blow the budget.
- **Cards work everywhere** that matters; keep ~SCR 1,000
  cash for takeaways, buses and beach vendors. Prices
  quoted in EUR at hotels are payable by card without
  drama.

## Day 1 — Land on Mahé, find your feet

- **Morning** — The Emirates red-eye lands you at Mahé
  by early morning. Pick up the eSIM/local SIM at the
  airport (Cable & Wireless / Airtel ~SCR 400), grab the
  hire car if self-driving (from ~€45/day, right-hand
  drive like home) or a taxi to Beau Vallon (~SCR 500).
- **11:00** — **Victoria**, one of the world's smallest
  capitals, walkable in an hour: the silver **clock
  tower** (a mini Big Ben, 1903), the riotous
  **Sir Selwyn Selwyn-Clarke Market** (fruit bats and
  red snapper on ice), and the technicolour **Arul Mihu
  Navasakthi Vinayagar Temple** — Seychelles' Hindu
  temple, a little piece of Tamil Nadu in the Indian
  Ocean.
- **14:00** — Check in at Beau Vallon; swim at the big,
  safe, lifeguard-adjacent crescent — the best easy
  swimming on Mahé.
- **18:15** — Sunset walk the full arc of the bay;
  dinner at a beach shack (grilled catch, ~SCR 350) —
  or, if it's Wednesday, the **Bazar Labrin** night
  market: grills, gato pima, music, the whole island
  out.

**Where to stay on Mahé** (three nights, Beau Vallon side)

- *Luxury:* **Constance Ephelia**, Port Glaud — 4.6/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~9,000 reviews — the country's most
  reviewed resort), two beaches and a zipline. €550+.
  (**STORY Seychelles**, 4.6/~2,800, is the Beau Vallon
  glamour pick.)
- *Mid-range:* **Savoy Resort & Spa**, Beau Vallon —
  4.3/5 (~2,500 reviews), big pool, two minutes from the
  sand. ~€250–330.
- *Value:* **Blue Lagoon Chalets** — 4.6/5 (~140
  reviews), self-catering chalets by the water for
  ~€110–150 — the guesthouse economy at its best.

## Day 2 — Mahé's wild south

- **08:30** — Over the spine of the island via the
  **Sans Soucis road**: pull over at the **Mission
  Lodge** viewpoint (Queen Elizabeth had tea here; the
  view explains why) and the small **tea factory**
  (~SCR 100) on the misty flank of Morne Blanc.
- **11:00** — Down to the south coast's heavyweights:
  **Anse Intendance** — thundering, board-short surf,
  turtle nests in season — then **Anse Takamaka** and
  lunch at a south-coast Creole spot (~SCR 300 pp).
  **Swim rule for the wild south: these beaches have
  real currents and no lifeguards** — waist-deep is the
  game, and if locals aren't swimming, you don't.
- **15:00** — **Anse Royale** for the calm, reef-guarded
  swimming the south does allow, coconut ice cream in
  hand.
- **18:00** — Back via the east coast in time for a
  Victoria supermarket run if self-catering; dinner at
  the guesthouse.

## Day 3 — The Anse Major trail and a lazy afternoon

- **07:30** — Beat the heat on the **Anse Major coastal
  trail** (from Bel Ombre, ~1 hr each way): a carved
  granite path above the sea ending at a beach with
  nobody on it. Carry water and reef shoes; swim in
  the marked cove.
- **12:30** — Takeaway-box lunch (~SCR 100), then
  earn your afternoon nap.
- **15:00** — Snorkel hire at Beau Vallon (or a
  glass-bottom trip to Baie Ternay marine park,
  ~SCR 900 pp) — turtles are a coin-flip, sergeant
  majors guaranteed.
- **19:30** — Dinner splurge night: **La Plage** or a
  beachfront table on the bay (~SCR 600–800 pp). Pack
  for the morning ferry.

## Day 4 — Ferry to Praslin, then the world's best beach

- **09:00** — **Cat Cocos fast ferry Mahé → Praslin**
  (**€56 economy, 1 hour**; the upper open deck is €6
  more and worth it — flying-fish escort included).
  Book online days ahead; take the sea-sickness tablet
  if swells are up.
- **11:00** — Check in on the **Côte d'Or (Anse
  Volbert)** side: the long, shallow, family-perfect
  lagoon.
- **13:00** — Bus or taxi across to **Anse Lazio** —
  the granite-framed sweep that tops "world's best
  beach" lists with monotonous regularity. Go
  afternoon-into-golden-hour when the tour groups thin;
  snorkel the left-hand boulders; keep valuables zipped
  and with you (beach bags are the islands' one crime
  scene).
- **19:00** — Dinner on Côte d'Or (~SCR 400 pp);
  takeaway boxes here run SCR 100 and eat just as well
  at a picnic table on the sand.

**Where to stay on Praslin** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Constance Lémuria** — 4.8/5 (~4,700
  reviews), its paths ending at near-private **Anse
  Georgette**. €700+.
- *Mid-range:* **Le Duc de Praslin**, Côte d'Or — 4.6/5
  (~2,200 reviews), gardens and a serious kitchen.
  ~€220–300.
- *Value:* **Les Lauriers Eco Hotel** — 4.5/5 (~600
  reviews), steps from Anse Volbert with a famous
  Creole dinner buffet. ~€130–170.

## Day 5 — The prehistoric forest and a pirate island

- **08:30** — **Vallée de Mai** at opening (entry
  **SCR 450**, under-12s free-ish — confirm at gate;
  open 8:30–16:30, last entry 15:30): a UNESCO forest
  of **coco de mer** palms — six-metre fronds, the
  famous double coconut, and the endemic **black
  parrot** whistling overhead. The 1.5–2 h middle loop
  with a guide (~SCR 300 extra, shared) turns botany
  into storytelling. This is the "other Eden" —
  General Gordon genuinely believed it.
- **12:00** — Takeaway lunch at Baie Ste Anne
  (~SCR 100).
- **13:30** — **Half-day boat to Curieuse & St
  Pierre** (~€75–90 pp incl. BBQ or snacks): giant
  Aldabra tortoises wandering free on Curieuse —
  hundreds of them, ancient and opinionated — then
  snorkelling the boulder islet of St Pierre,
  the postcard with fish in it.
  *(Swap: walk into **Anse Georgette** instead —
  register free at the Lémuria gate — if boats and
  budgets don't align.)*
- **19:00** — Grilled jobfish on Côte d'Or; early-ish
  night. Tomorrow, the island where time gave up.

## Day 6 — La Digue: bicycles and THAT beach

- **09:30** — Ferry **Praslin → La Digue** (**€15,
  15 minutes**). No real cars to speak of — **hire
  bikes at the jetty (SCR 150–200/day)**; your
  guesthouse is five pedalled minutes away. Ox-carts
  retired; the vibe didn't.
- **11:00** — Pedal south into **L'Union Estate**
  (entry **~SCR 150–200**, covers the whole estate):
  the old coconut mill, vanilla vines, the colonial
  planter's house, a corral of giant tortoises — and
  then, at the end of the track:
- **12:30** — **Anse Source d'Argent.** The most
  photographed beach on Earth, and the cameras aren't
  lying: pink-tinged sand, sculpted granite, water so
  shallow and warm it feels poured. Low tide = wading
  pools; high tide = swimming between boulders. Stay
  for hours; the crowds fade after 15:30 and the late
  light on the granite is the trip's screensaver.
- **18:30** — Dinner in the village (Creole buffet
  night at many guesthouses, ~SCR 350) — then
  star-count on the beach; there's nothing to do on La
  Digue at night, which is the entire point.

**Where to stay on La Digue** (two nights): **Le Domaine
de L'Orangeraie** (4.4/5, ~3,300 reviews, the island's
one resort-resort, ~€350+), **Le Repaire Boutique
Hotel** (4.6/5, ~1,100 reviews, seafront Italian-run
charm, ~€200–260), or **Pension Hibiscus / Cabanes des
Anges** (4.8/~320 and 4.7/~280 reviews, the guesthouse
sweet spot at ~€100–140).

## Day 7 — The wild east and a sunrise you'll keep

- **06:15** — Sunrise mission: back into L'Union
  (ticket from yesterday if same-day stamped — buy the
  two-day pass if offered) for **Anse Source d'Argent
  empty**. An hour alone with the granite before
  breakfast is worth the alarm.
- **09:30** — Cycle cross-island to **Grand Anse →
  Petite Anse → Anse Cocos** (20 min ride + 30 min
  coastal walk): three wild, roaring, gorgeous bays.
  **Hard rule: these are looking-and-paddling beaches —
  the rip currents are the islands' most dangerous
  thing.** Anse Cocos' far-corner natural pools are
  the safe splash.
- **13:00** — Creole takeaway under a takamaka tree
  (~SCR 100), lazy afternoon: snorkel trip to **Coco
  Island** (~€50–60 pp, an hour of turtle-rich reef)
  or nothing whatsoever, executed perfectly.
- **18:00** — Farewell dinner; settle the guesthouse
  bill in EUR-by-card or SCR; pre-book the morning
  ferry seats.

## Day 8 — Ferry home

- **Morning** — Direct ferry **La Digue → Mahé (€68,
  ~1h45)** across open water (breakfast light, tablet
  taken), landing in Victoria by late morning —
  comfortable for afternoon/evening departures. On an
  early flight? Spend night 7 on Mahé instead and give
  Day 7's beaches to Praslin.
- Airport rule of thumb: be there 2½ h early —
  it's small, but so are its queues' patience.

## What to pack

- Reef-safe sunscreen (the equatorial sun is a
  different instrument — SPF 50, reapply) and a rash
  vest for snorkelling.
- **Reef shoes**: granite + coral shallows eat bare
  feet.
- Snorkel set from home if you have one — hire runs
  SCR 150+/day and adds up.
- Mosquito repellent (no malaria, but dusk ankles are
  a buffet), light rain shell for two-minute
  downpours.
- A dry bag for ferry decks and bike baskets; padlock
  for beach-bag peace of mind.
- UK-style **Type G plugs** — the UAE traveller's rare
  luxury: your chargers just work.
- Modest wear only for Victoria's temple and church
  visits; everywhere else, the dress code is salt.

## FAQ

**Seychelles or Maldives — which one?**
Different animals. **Maldives** is one resort on one
ring of coral: the water is (slightly) more surreal,
everything happens at your hotel, and it's the better
pure-luxury cocoon — our [Maldives guide](/itineraries/maldives-7-days-guide/)
covers it. **Seychelles** is a real country you explore:
three islands, mountains, markets, buses, hikes,
guesthouses — twice the trip for travellers who get
bored on day three of a sunlounger. Honeymoon: Maldives
by a nose. Everything else: Seychelles.

**Is it safe?**
One of Africa's safest countries — solo women cycle La
Digue at night. The two real risks are **unattended
beach bags** (take turns swimming, or bring nothing) and
**rip currents on the wild south/east beaches** — respect
the swim rules above and the trip's biggest danger is
sunburn.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Seafood makes halal-conscious eating natural — grilled
fish, octopus curry and veg creole dishes dominate
menus — but **formal halal certification is rare**
outside a few Victoria eateries and resorts on request;
observant travellers default to seafood/veg, which here
is no hardship. Vegetarians do well: lentil dal,
breadfruit, pumpkin chatini and veg curries are Creole
staples (the Indian-origin community runs deep), and
self-catering guesthouses make it trivial.

**Can we do it without a hire car?**
Yes — that's the SCR 12 bus's whole job on Mahé and
Praslin (last buses ~19:00–20:30; Sundays sparse), plus
taxis for the awkward hops. La Digue needs nothing but
a bicycle. A car earns its €45 only on the Mahé
south-loop day.

**Is the sea calm year-round?**
No — and it matters. **October–November and April–May
are the glass months.** May–September's southeast
trades bring chop on the crossings (tablets!) and
seaweed to east-facing beaches like Grand Anse; Anse
Lazio and the west coasts stay lovely. December–March
is calm, hot, and briefly torrential.

**What's Seychelles guide #2?**
**The honeymoon edition** — one week, two resorts (a
Praslin hideaway + a Mahé finale, or the private-island
splurge), zero ferries at dawn — plus the Bird Island
day-trip nobody talks about. On the list.
