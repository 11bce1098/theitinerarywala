Tanzania is the safari superlative country: the **Serengeti**
is where the word "endless plain" was coined (that's what
*Siringet* means in Maasai), the **Ngorongoro Crater** packs
25,000 large animals into a collapsed volcano's grass floor,
and when the dust settles you can be on **Zanzibar** — a
Swahili spice island of carved doors, dhows and
bath-warm turquoise — by lunchtime.

That pairing is this guide: **five safari days on the
northern circuit** (Tarangire → Serengeti → the Crater),
then **three nights on Zanzibar**, flying home on
**flydubai's Zanzibar–Dubai non-stop**. It's the priciest
kind of trip this site covers — Tanzania's park fees run
higher than [Kenya's](/itineraries/kenya-7-days-guide/) — so the fee
maths is laid out plainly, along with the migration's
month-by-month location so you book the right corner of an
ecosystem the size of a small country.

Safari economics run in dollars: figure **{{USD 2650}} per
person** excluding international
flights. **June–October is prime** (dry season, river
crossings in the north); **December–March** is calving
season in the south and superb; April–May is the green,
cheap, wet gamble.

## Before you go: the admin

- **One eVisa covers mainland + Zanzibar:** **US$50**
  at the official **visa.immigration.go.tz** —
  passport scan + photo, allow **up to 10 days**
  (approvals can cut it fine; apply 3+ weeks out).
  Visa-on-arrival ($50, cash) exists at Kilimanjaro,
  Dar and Zanzibar airports as the backup queue.
- **Zanzibar's mandatory insurance:** since late 2024
  every visitor to Zanzibar must buy the island's own
  policy — **US$44, valid up to three months**, bought
  online at **inbound.visitzanzibar.go.tz** before you
  land (your own travel insurance doesn't replace it).
  Screenshot the QR; it's checked with immigration.
- **The park-fee reality (why quotes look high):**
  per-day, per-person conservation fees for
  non-residents run **Serengeti ~$83, Ngorongoro ~$83,
  Tarangire ~$60**, plus vehicle fees — and the
  Ngorongoro Crater adds a famous **~$295 per-vehicle
  "crater service fee"** for the descent. With 18% VAT
  folded in, two people's five park days carry ~$1,000
  of fees before a single bed or guide — which is why
  packages are priced the way they are, and why
  **any suspiciously cheap quote is leaving something
  out. Get fees itemised in writing.**
- **Flights:** open-jaw is the move — **into
  Kilimanjaro (JRO)** one-stop (Qatar, Ethiopian,
  Kenya Airways combos, ~US$600–950), **home from
  Zanzibar on flydubai's direct** (~5½ h). Book the
  Arusha→Zanzibar hop (~$150–220) with the safari
  operator so delays stay their problem.
- **Health:** the whole route is a **malaria area** —
  travel-clinic prophylaxis, DEET at dusk. Yellow
  fever certificate only if you transit an endemic
  country (a Nairobi/Addis connection can count —
  carry it if you have it).
- **Money & tips:** USD cash in crisp post-2009 notes
  is safari currency; cards work at lodges with fees.
  Budget **$15–20/day per couple for the
  driver-guide**, $10/day for camp staff boxes —
  handed over on the last morning.

## Day 1 — Land at Kilimanjaro, breathe in Arusha

- **Afternoon** — Land at JRO with Kilimanjaro
  (weather willing) filling the left windows;
  transfer to **Arusha** (45 min), meet the safari
  operator, gear check, early dinner.
- Sleep: a garden lodge in Arusha's coffee-farm belt
  (~$90–120 — usually bundled in the package).

## Day 2 — Tarangire: the elephant river

- **08:00** — Into **Tarangire** (2 h): baobabs like
  upturned trees, and **elephants by the hundred**
  digging the dry river for water — the park's
  signature scene — plus lion prides that climb
  trees when the mood takes them.
- Full afternoon game drive; sundowner light through
  baobab silhouettes is the day's postcard.
- Overnight: tented camp near the park (full board;
  the canvas-walls-and-hyena-whoops initiation).

## Days 3–4 — The Serengeti

- **Morning** — The long, spectacular drive up the
  Rift wall and across Ngorongoro's highlands
  (Maasai bomas, crater-rim viewpoint pause) then
  down into the **endless plain** — game-driving your
  way to camp by sunset.
- **The migration map, honestly:** the herds move in
  a giant annual circle, so *where* in the Serengeti
  matters — **Dec–Mar:** south (Ndutu; **February =
  calving**, cheetah-and-newborn drama); **Jun–Jul:**
  west/central; **Jul–Sep:** far north at the Mara
  river (**the crossings**); **Oct–Nov:** heading
  back south. **Central Seronera works year-round**
  (resident lions, leopards along the river) — tell
  your operator which spectacle you're buying and
  make camp location match the month.
- **Day 4** — Dawn-to-dusk Serengeti: kopjes
  (Lion King rocks, occupied by actual lions),
  hippo pools, cheetah scanning the shimmer.
  Optional splurge at first light: the **balloon
  safari (~$599 pp here — pricier than Kenya, and
  the Serengeti's version of it)**.
- Overnights: tented camps positioned by season
  (full board).

## Day 5 — Ngorongoro Crater, then the rim

- **06:30** — Descend 600 m into the **crater** —
  a 260 km² grass bowl where the Big Five commute:
  the highest predator density in Africa, the last
  easily-seen **black rhinos** in the north, flamingo
  smudge on the soda lake, all ringed by
  2,300-metre walls. Five hours is the permit
  rhythm; picnic by the hippo pool (kites
  dive-bomb — eat inside the vehicle, guides will
  insist).
- **Afternoon** — Up and out to the rim or back to
  Karatu's coffee farms for the night; farewell
  bush dinner with the crew (tips morning is
  tomorrow).

**Safari camps by tier** (packages assign the exact
camp — these are the verified benchmarks to ask for):

- *Luxury:* **Meliá Serengeti Lodge** — 4.9/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~700 reviews), infinity-edge views
  over the Mbalageti valley. (Four Seasons Safari
  Lodge is the brand-name splurge.)
- *Mid-range:* **Kubu Kubu Tented Lodge** — 4.9/5
  (~1,250 reviews), the stylish permanent-tent
  standard.
- *Value:* the mobile/seasonal tented camps your
  operator runs (Kati Kati-style, ~$180–220 pp full
  board) — bucket showers, lantern paths, and the
  lions sound exactly the same.

## Day 6 — Fly to the spice island: Stone Town

- **Morning** — Arusha → **Zanzibar** (1 h 20;
  ~$150–220). Land, buy nothing at the airport
  except the taxi ($15 fixed-ish into town).
- **14:00** — **Stone Town** on foot: carved
  brass-studded doors, the House of Wonders facade,
  the old fort, Freddie Mercury's birthplace
  (small museum, ~$10 — yes, really, Zanzibar's
  most famous son), and the sobering **slave-market
  memorial at the Anglican cathedral** (~$8 with
  guide) — the island's history has both spice and
  shadow, and an hour here honours it.
- **18:00** — Sunset from a rooftop tea house, then
  **Forodhani Gardens' night grill market** —
  Zanzibar pizza, sugarcane juice, seafood skewers
  (haggle gently; $8–12 feeds two well).

**Stone Town night:** **Zanzibar Palace Hotel** —
4.7/5 (~890 reviews), antique-carved boutique
(~$140–190); or seafront **Tembo House** — 4.1/5
(~2,100 reviews), ~$90–120.

## Days 7–8 — The beach: Nungwi

- **Morning** — Transfer north to **Nungwi**
  (1 h 15, ~$40): the island's swim-all-day coast —
  minimal tide swing, dhows moored on gin-clear
  water. *(Trade-off note: the east coast —
  Paje/Jambiani — is prettier-wilder and
  kitesurf-central, but the lagoon walks out
  hundreds of metres at low tide and seaweed
  seasons visit; Nungwi/Kendwa is the
  reliable-swimming answer.)*
- **Day 7** — Beach, swim, sunset dhow cruise
  (~$25–35 pp) — and the one local friction,
  named: **"papasi" beach touts** work the sand;
  a firm, smiled "hapana asante" (no thanks) on
  repeat is the entire technique.
- **Day 8** — Pick the classic day: **Safari Blue**
  (full-day dhow, sandbank, snorkelling, seafood
  feast — ~$70–90 pp from the southwest) or the
  **spice-farm tour** (~$30–35 with transfer:
  vanilla vines, clove trees, fruit-tasting
  overload — Zanzibar was the world's clove
  kingdom) plus Prison Island's giant tortoises
  (~$45 by boat). Last sundowner: Kendwa's beach
  bars do the island's best.

**Beach nights:** **The Z Hotel, Nungwi** — 4.7/5
(~2,600 reviews), adultish boutique on the best
sand (~$220–300); **Hotel Riu Palace** — 4.7/5
(~5,600 reviews) for the all-inclusive route;
**Makofi Guest House** — 4.9/5 (~700 reviews, the
island's #1 Best Value) at ~$60–90.

## Day 9 — Fly home direct

- Slow breakfast, one last swim, airport by noon
  (allow padding — island time is a real
  timezone), and **flydubai's non-stop to Dubai**
  (~5½ h). Vanilla pods and clove packets in the
  bag; the insurance QR never asked for again.

## What to pack

- Safari neutrals (khaki/olive; **no dark
  blue/black — tsetse bait — and no camouflage,
  illegal for civilians**), fleece for 6 am
  crater rim cold, dust buff.
- Binoculars + zoom lens + double memory cards;
  soft duffel only (4x4s and small planes).
- DEET, prophylaxis, SPF 50, basic meds kit
  (pharmacies vanish after Arusha).
- **Crisp USD notes** (2009+ series) split
  between bags; the $44 insurance QR and eVisa
  PDFs offline.
- Zanzibar leg: reef shoes, modest wear for
  Stone Town (it's a conservative Muslim old
  town — shoulders/knees off the beach),
  swimwear for the sand.
- Power: Tanzania uses **UK-style Type G** —
  UK and Gulf plugs just work.

## FAQ

**Tanzania or Kenya for the safari — the site
now has both?**
Honest split: **Kenya** ([guide](/itineraries/kenya-7-days-guide/))
is cheaper, shorter-drive, and unbeatable
July–October when the herds are in the Mara.
**Tanzania** owns the migration the other nine
months, adds the crater — the single most
reliable Big Five morning in Africa — and feels
emptier per vehicle. First safari on a budget:
Kenya. The full epic, or any trip
December–June: Tanzania.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
The trip's easy half is Zanzibar: a ~99% Muslim
island where **halal is simply the default** —
eat anywhere, Forodhani included. Safari camps
cater halal/veg smoothly **with advance
notice** (say it at booking; the kitchens are
used to it), and Indian-veg requests are
routine thanks to Tanzania's old Gujarati
community — Arusha even does a proper thali.

**Is Zanzibar just a beach, or worth real
time?**
Worth it as a place, not just a pool: Stone
Town is a UNESCO Swahili-Arab-Indian palimpsest,
the spice farms explain three centuries of
world trade, and the slave-market memorial
grounds the paradise in its history. Three
nights is the right minimum; a week is a
different (lovely) trip.

**When exactly for the river crossings /
calving?**
Crossings: **late July–September** at the
northern Kogatende sector (book those camps
6+ months out). Calving: **late January–
February** around Ndutu — 8,000 wildebeest
born a DAY at peak, with cheetahs working the
nursery. Both are nature's scheduling, not a
show: build two-plus Serengeti nights so
patience can do its work.

**Can we skip the crater to save the $295?**
You can — and shouldn't, first time. The fee is
per VEHICLE (split it), the five permitted
hours deliver more guaranteed sightings than
any other morning in Africa, and it's the only
realistic black-rhino card in the north. Skip
Tarangire before you skip the crater.

**What's Tanzania guide #2?**
**Zanzibar solo, done properly** — a full week
of Stone Town, Paje kites and Pemba-side
diving on the flydubai direct — or the
**Kilimanjaro trek** for the summit-minded.
Votes welcome; both on the list.
