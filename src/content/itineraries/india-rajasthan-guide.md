The Golden Triangle shows you Rajasthan's front gate; this trip
goes inside the palace. South and west of Jaipur the crowds thin
and the state gets better: Pushkar's holy lake ringed by ghats,
Jodhpur's blue old town crouched under the most dramatic fort in
India, a Jain temple with 1,444 individually carved pillars, and
finally Udaipur — white palaces standing in a lake, the closest
India comes to Venice. It runs as one southbound arc with a single
car and driver, ending at Udaipur's airport.

**Done our Golden Triangle guide already?** Fly into Jaipur, skip
Day 2 below (you've seen it), and spend the spare day on the
Ranthambore tiger option in the FAQ or a fourth Udaipur night.

**Go October–March.** Summer is 42°C+ in Jodhpur; the
November **Pushkar Camel Fair** week is spectacular and triples
Pushkar prices — a feature or a bug depending on your taste.

All costs are in Indian rupees (INR), with conversions calculated
at today's exchange rate. Foreigner ticket prices quoted; **Indian
passport and OCI holders pay domestic rates — roughly 80% less at
the gates.**

## Before you go: the short version of the admin

Visas, UPI One World, SIMs, health notes and the full scam list
live in our Golden Triangle guide — they apply unchanged. Circuit
specifics:

- **One car + driver for the whole arc** (~₹3,500–4,000/day
  including fuel and the driver's expenses; any Rajasthan operator
  or hotel arranges it). Distances are 2.5–5 h per hop and the
  roads are good; the driver waits, carries, and knows where the
  clean lunch stops are. This is how Rajasthan is done.
- **Sequencing rule that matters:** on the Jodhpur→Udaipur day,
  **Ranakpur admits non-Jain visitors only from 12:00 noon** —
  so mornings belong to Kumbhalgarh or the road, never the
  temple. This guide is ordered accordingly.
- **Book Udaipur's lakeside rooms first** — the lake-view havelis
  are small and December–February weekends vanish.
- Tipping the driver: ~₹150–250/day, given at the end.

### Travelling with kids?

- Forts are giant playgrounds (Mehrangarh has an elevator;
  Kumbhalgarh's walls are stroller-hostile, carrier territory).
- Camel rides at Pushkar and the puppet segment of Udaipur's
  evening show are the child headliners.
- Drive segments are 2.5–5 h — the standard snacks-and-naps
  playbook applies; cars have AC, always confirm seatbelts in the
  back row when booking.

## Day 1 — Arrive Jaipur

- **Afternoon** — Land at Jaipur (direct from the Gulf and every
  Indian hub), meet the driver who'll be yours for the week, check
  in.
- **17:00** — Warm-up circuit: the **Jal Mahal** photo stop, then
  **Nahargarh Fort** for sunset over the pink city.
- **20:00** — Dinner: a Rajasthani thali at **LMB** in Johri
  Bazaar, or your hotel rooftop.

**Where to stay in Jaipur** (two nights)

- *Palace:* **Rambagh Palace** — the maharaja's own, peacocks
  included.
- *Heritage mid-range:* **Samode Haveli** — courtyards and
  frescoes, the smart-money pick.
- *Budget:* **ibis Jaipur City Centre** — modern, central,
  rooftop pool.

## Day 2 — Jaipur's essentials (Triangle alumni: skip ahead)

The compressed version — the full Jaipur playbook is in our Golden
Triangle guide. Buy the **composite ticket** (₹1,000 foreigners,
2 days, six sites).

- **8:00** — **Amber Fort** at opening (composite) — Sheesh Mahal
  before the coaches; up by jeep, not elephant.
- **11:00** — **Panna Meena stepwell** + drive-by sights back into
  town; lunch in the old city.
- **14:00** — **City Palace** (~₹700, separate ticket) and
  **Jantar Mantar** (composite).
- **17:00** — **Hawa Mahal** facade chai from the cafés opposite;
  bazaar hour in Johri and Bapu.
- **20:00** — Dinner; pack for the desert road.

## Day 3 — Pushkar: the holy lake and the dunes

- **9:00** — Drive to **Pushkar** (~2.5 h) — the road crosses the
  Aravalli hills past Ajmer.
- **11:30** — Check in; lunch (Pushkar is a holy town: fully
  vegetarian, alcohol-free, and better for it — the café scene is
  excellent).
- **13:00** — **Pushkar Lake and the 52 ghats**: shoes off near
  the water, no photos of bathers, and know the one local hustle —
  the "free" flower pressed into your hand becomes a demanded
  ₹500 blessing; a polite no thank you, or agree a small sum
  (₹50–100) for the genuine ritual if you want it.
- **14:30** — **Brahma Temple** (free; bags/cameras stored) — one
  of the very few Brahma temples anywhere.
- **16:30** — **Camel ride into the dunes for sunset**
  (~₹500–800/person, hotel-arranged) — kitschy, lovely, the
  photos are keepers.
- **19:00** — Evening **aarti** at the ghats — bells, lamps and
  chanting over the water; sit quietly at the rim and take it in.
- **20:00** — Rooftop dinner overlooking the lake.

**Where to stay in Pushkar** (one night)

- *The charmer:* **Inn Seventh Heaven** — the vine-draped haveli
  around a courtyard that every guest seems to leave in love with;
  book well ahead.
- *Lakeside heritage:* **Pushkar Palace** — the old royal
  guesthouse right on the water.
- *Budget:* the ghat-side guesthouses — pick by current reviews;
  ₹1,500–3,000 gets a rooftop view.

## Day 4 — West to the blue city

- **8:00** — Optional: the **Savitri temple ropeway** for a
  morning view over lake and desert.
- **9:30** — Drive to **Jodhpur** (~4 h) — proper desert now,
  mesas and scrub.
- **14:00** — Check into the old city; late lunch.
- **16:00** — **Ghanta Ghar (clock tower) and Sardar Market** —
  spice pyramids, bangle lanes, and the mandatory
  **makhaniya lassi at Shri Mishrilal** (~₹60; in business since
  1927, the queue is the menu).
- **18:00** — First sight of **Mehrangarh** lit up from a rooftop
  restaurant — the fort hangs over the city like a ship's prow;
  dinner under it.

**Where to stay in Jodhpur** (two nights)

- *Palace:* **Umaid Bhawan Palace** — half royal residence, half
  hotel, wholly absurd in the best way.
- *Heritage mid-range:* **The Rani Mahal** — a 450-year-old
  haveli in the blue lanes, fort view from the roof.
- *Budget:* **Khamma Heritage** — superbly rated, rooftop fort
  view, blue-city location.

## Day 5 — Mehrangarh and the blue lanes

- **9:00** — **Mehrangarh Fort** at opening (9:00–17:00, last
  entry 16:30; foreigners ₹600 — and take the **₹250 audio
  guide**, widely called the best in India; elevator available).
  The palace museum, the cannonball-scarred gates, the ramparts
  over an ocean of blue cubes below. 3 hours.
- **12:30** — Adrenaline option: the **Flying Fox zipline**
  (₹1,500–2,500, ~2 h, six lines over the fort's lakes and
  bastions; book ahead) — or straight to lunch.
- **14:00** — **Jaswant Thada** (small fee) — the white marble
  cenotaph on the ridge, a quiet counterpoint.
- **15:30** — Walk down into **Navchokiya**, the bluest of the
  blue lanes — indigo walls, temple squares, chai stops; then the
  restored **Toorji ka Jhalra stepwell** and its café ring for
  the golden-hour photo.
- **19:30** — Dinner in the old city — laal maas (fiery mutton) is
  Jodhpur's dish; mirchi bada its street snack.

## Day 6 — The mountain road to Udaipur: wall and pillars

The best transfer day in Rajasthan — ordered around Ranakpur's
noon gate.

- **8:00** — Leave Jodhpur into the Aravallis.
- **10:30** — **Kumbhalgarh Fort** (daylight hours; ~₹600
  foreigners) — the second-longest continuous wall on earth
  (36 km) snaking over the hills, and the birthplace of Maharana
  Pratap. Ninety minutes on the ramparts.
- **13:00** — **Ranakpur Jain Temple** (non-Jain entry **from
  12:00** to 17:00; ~₹200 with audio guide + camera fee;
  shoulders/knees covered, **no leather items inside**) — 1,444
  marble pillars, no two alike, in a forest clearing. One of
  India's great interiors; an hour of slow wandering.
- **14:30** — Late lunch at the temple-road restaurants.
- **17:00** — Arrive **Udaipur**; check in lakeside.
- **19:30** — First rooftop dinner over **Lake Pichola** — the
  Lake Palace glowing mid-water is the view you came for.

**Where to stay in Udaipur** (four nights)

- *The icon:* **Taj Lake Palace** — the white marble palace *in*
  the lake; arrival is by boat. If one great splurge ends the
  trip, it's this.
- *Lakeside heritage:* **Amet Haveli** — 350-year-old rooms with
  scalloped windows on the water, at a tenth of the icon's price.
- *Budget:* **Moustache Udaipur** — rooftop lake views at hostel
  prices.

## Day 7 — Udaipur: palace, lake, golden hour

- **9:30** — **City Palace** (9:30–17:30; ~₹700–800 museum entry)
  — Rajasthan's largest palace complex: peacock mosaics, mirrored
  chambers, balconies over the lake. 2–3 hours.
- **12:30** — **Jagdish Temple** (free) next door, then lunch in
  the old city lanes.
- **15:00** — Wander the ghats: **Gangaur Ghat**, the vintage-car
  museum for enthusiasts, or a miniature-painting studio visit —
  Udaipur is the living school of the art.
- **17:00** — **Lake Pichola boat cruise** (from the City Palace
  jetty; sunset slots premium, ~₹700–900 with the **Jag Mandir**
  island stop) — the city from the water at golden hour is the
  trip's closing argument.
- **20:00** — Dinner at **Ambrai Ghat**'s waterline restaurant or
  your rooftop — book the good tables ahead.

## Day 8 — Gardens, the monsoon palace and the dance show

- **9:30** — **Saheliyon ki Bari** (small fee) — the maidens'
  garden of fountains and lotus pools.
- **11:00** — Old-city shopping done properly: leather journals,
  miniature paintings, block prints — or a **cooking class**
  (Udaipur's home classes are among India's best rated).
- **16:30** — **Sajjangarh, the Monsoon Palace** (small fee +
  vehicle) on its mountain — sunset over the whole lake system
  from the ramparts.
- **19:00** — **Bagore ki Haveli's Dharohar show** (nightly
  19:00, ~1 h; foreigners ~₹150–200 + camera fee; **arrive by
  18:30** — seating is first-come) — Ghoomar and Kalbeliya
  dances, puppetry, and the ten-pot balancing finale in a lit
  haveli courtyard. Touristy and terrific.
- **20:30** — Late dinner in the lanes.

## Day 9 — The flex day — three ways

- **Option A — Chittorgarh** (2 h east; ~₹600): the vast fort of
  Rajput last stands — towers, tanks and tragedy on a mesa; a
  half-day with your driver.
- **Option B — Kumbhalgarh revisited or Eklingji/Nagda temples**
  if Day 6 felt rushed.
- **Option C — The correct answer for many: nothing.** A lake-view
  breakfast that becomes lunch, a last boat ride, the spa. Udaipur
  rewards idleness.
- **Evening** — Farewell dinner somewhere with the palace lights
  on the water.

## Day 10 — Fly home from Udaipur

- **Morning** — Udaipur airport is 30–40 minutes out; fly home via
  Delhi/Mumbai, or direct where routes allow. No backtracking —
  the arc is complete.

## FAQ

**How does this connect with the Golden Triangle?**
Seamlessly — Jaipur is the shared node. The combined 14–15 day
version (Delhi → Agra → Jaipur → this circuit) is the classic
"grand Rajasthan" and needs no internal flights at all.

**Where are the tigers?**
**Ranthambore**, 3.5 h from Jaipur — the add-on this route's
alumni day buys: 2 nights, two safaris (book the official zones
60+ days ahead, October–April; sightings are luck weighted by
zone). It slots between Days 2 and 3 at the cost of two extra
days.

**Why no Jaisalmer?**
Distance: the golden city and its dunes are 5+ hours beyond
Jodhpur each way — magnificent, but it turns a comfortable arc
into a forced march. It's the anchor of a future desert-deep
route (with overnight dune camps) rather than a squeeze here.

**Is Pushkar worth a night vs a lunch stop?**
A night. The town at dusk — aarti bells, lamp-lit ghats, no
day-trippers — is a different and better place than at noon, and
the dune sunset needs the evening anyway.

**Safety, women travellers, general hassle?**
Same picture as the Triangle guide, a notch calmer: this circuit
is practiced tourist country. Pushkar's flower-blessing hustle
and Jodhpur market touts are the local flavours of the same
game — the scam list in the Triangle guide covers it all.

**Vegetarian and halal food?**
Vegetarian Rajasthan is glorious (dal baati churma, gatte ki
sabzi, the entire thali tradition — and Pushkar is 100% veg).
Halal-observant travellers: major-city restaurants and Muslim-run
kitchens exist in Jaipur, Jodhpur and Udaipur but aren't the
default — the vegetarian route is the practical daily answer, and
it's no sacrifice here.
