Bulgaria gets skipped, which is why it is still cheap and still
quiet. It should not be: **Plovdiv** has been continuously inhabited
for around eight thousand years and has a Roman theatre still used
for concerts; **Rila Monastery** sits in a mountain valley under
black-and-white striped arcades covered in frescoes; and **Veliko
Tarnovo** stacks itself up a gorge around the fortress of the
Second Bulgarian Empire.

Sofia itself is an unhurried capital where a Roman street runs under
the shopping centre, a mosque, a synagogue and an Orthodox cathedral
stand within a few hundred metres, and the mountain behind the city
is a ski resort in winter and a hike in summer.

Eight days, mostly by road, in the cheapest country in the European
Union.

## Before you go: Schengen, the alphabet and the head-shake

### Entry, and where Bulgaria now sits

- **Bulgaria joined the Schengen area** — air and sea borders first,
  land borders following — so the same visa rules apply as the rest
  of the area. The process is in our
  [Schengen visa guide](/guides/schengen-visa-how-it-works/).
- **It is not yet in the euro.** Bulgaria uses the **lev (BGN)**,
  pegged to the euro at a fixed rate, with euro adoption repeatedly
  discussed and repeatedly moved.
- Visa-free nationalities need only a passport.

### Two things that catch visitors out

- **The head-shake is inverted.** A nod means no; a shake means yes.
  Bulgarians dealing with tourists often switch to the Western
  version, which makes it worse rather than better. When it matters,
  ask for a verbal answer.
- **Cyrillic was created here**, in the First Bulgarian Empire, and
  road signs outside the main routes are often only in Cyrillic.
  Learn to sound out the alphabet on the plane — it takes twenty
  minutes and transforms the driving.

### Getting around

- **Hire a car** for Rila and Veliko Tarnovo. It is the difference
  between seeing the country and seeing three cities.
- **Sofia to Plovdiv is a good motorway**, about ninety minutes.
  **Sofia to Veliko Tarnovo** is around three hours.
- **Rila Monastery is two hours from Sofia** on winding mountain
  road, and worth a night nearby rather than a rushed day trip.
- **Trains are slow**; buses are better and cheap if you would rather
  not drive.
- **Parking in Plovdiv's old town** is restricted; stay below it and
  walk up.

### Money and the cost

- **The lev**, and **Bulgaria is the cheapest country in the EU** —
  noticeably below Romania, and a fraction of western Europe.
- Cards work in cities; carry cash for monasteries, village
  restaurants and small guesthouses.
- **Tipping** around 10% is normal.
- **Mehanas** — traditional taverns — are where to eat: grilled
  meat, shopska salad, and rakia, which is the national spirit and
  taken seriously.

### When to go

- **May to June and September to October** are best: warm, green or
  golden, and quiet.
- **July and August** are hot inland and busy on the Black Sea coast.
- **Winter** is for skiing at Bansko, which is a different trip and a
  genuinely cheap one.

## Day 1 — Land in Sofia

- **Afternoon** — Land, collect the car or leave it until you head
  out. The metro reaches the airport.
- **Evening** — **Vitosha Boulevard** with the mountain at the end of
  it, and dinner in a mehana.

## Day 2 — Sofia

- **Morning** — **Alexander Nevsky Cathedral**, then the
  **Serdica** Roman ruins exposed under the city centre and
  visible from the metro station.
- **Afternoon** — The **Boyana Church** on the edge of town —
  UNESCO-listed for frescoes from 1259 that anticipate the
  Renaissance by a century — and the **National History Museum**
  next to it.
- **Evening** — The mineral baths square, and the mosque, synagogue
  and cathedral within sight of each other.

## Day 3 — Rila Monastery

- **Morning** — Two hours south into the **Rila mountains**. The
  monastery's courtyard — black-and-white striped arches, a painted
  porch, mountains above — is the image of Bulgaria.
- **Afternoon** — The **Hrelyo Tower**, the museum, and the walk to
  **St Ivan's cave** up the valley.
- **Evening** — Stay nearby rather than driving back, and eat trout.

## Day 4 — East to Plovdiv

- **Morning** — Drive across to **Plovdiv**, about three hours.
- **Afternoon** — The **Roman theatre**, still in use, cut into the
  hillside with a view over the city.
- **Evening** — **Kapana**, the craft quarter, which is where
  Plovdiv's European Capital of Culture money went and went well.

## Day 5 — Plovdiv, and the old town

- **Morning** — The **Old Town**'s National Revival houses —
  timber-framed, overhanging, painted — and the Roman stadium under
  the main shopping street.
- **Afternoon** — **Bachkovo Monastery**, 30 minutes south and the
  country's second largest, or the **Asen's Fortress** on its crag.
- **Evening** — Back in Kapana.

## Day 6 — North to Veliko Tarnovo

- **Morning** — The drive north over the Balkan range, about three
  hours, through the **Shipka Pass** with its gold-domed Russian
  church.
- **Afternoon** — **Veliko Tarnovo**: the **Tsarevets** fortress on
  its own hill above a meander of the Yantra, and houses stacked up
  the gorge behind.
- **Evening** — The fortress from **Samovodska Charshia**, the
  craft street.

## Day 7 — Arbanasi and the villages

- **Morning** — **Arbanasi**, five minutes up the hill: stone
  merchants' houses like small fortresses, and the **Nativity
  Church**, plain outside and covered in frescoes within.
- **Afternoon** — **Tryavna** or **Etar** for the revival-era
  crafts, or back toward Sofia.
- **Evening** — Last night.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- Veliko Tarnovo to Sofia airport is about three hours. Allow for
  it, or fly from Varna if your route runs to the coast.

## What to pack

- **Layers.** Rila and the Balkan range are cool even in summer, and
  Plovdiv can be 35°C the same week.
- **Shoes for cobbles and monastery hills.**
- **Modest cover for monasteries** — shoulders and knees, and some
  provide wraps.
- **European Type C/F plugs.**
- **Cash in lev** for villages and monastery entries.

## FAQ

**Is Bulgaria really the cheapest in the EU?**
Yes, on most measures — food, accommodation and fuel all sit below
Romania's, which is itself well below western Europe.

**Do I need a car?**
For Rila and the villages, effectively yes. Sofia to Plovdiv is easy
by bus, but the monasteries and the Balkan range are not usefully
served.

**Is the head-shake thing real?**
Yes. A nod means no and a shake means yes, and people who deal with
tourists sometimes invert it back, which is worse. Ask for words
when it matters.

**How does it compare with Romania?**
Bulgaria is cheaper and flatter to travel — better roads, shorter
distances. Romania has the Saxon villages and the higher mountains.
See our [Romania guide](/itineraries/romania-8-days-guide/); the two
combine well over two weeks.
