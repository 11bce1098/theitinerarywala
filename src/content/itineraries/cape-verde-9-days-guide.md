Most visitors to Cape Verde land on **Sal** or **Boa Vista**,
check into an all-inclusive on a flat sandy island, and never
leave it. Those two islands are the desert ones. The archipelago
has ten, and the others are mountainous, green in places, and
considerably more interesting.

**Mindelo** on São Vicente is the cultural capital — a harbour
town of faded Portuguese buildings and live music every night,
where ***morna*** was born and **Cesária Évora** sang barefoot in
bars long before the world heard her.

An hour away by ferry, **Santo Antão** has **no working airport**,
a hand-built cobbled road over a crater rim, and ridge walks that
drop two thousand metres into valleys of sugar cane and mango.
It is among the best hiking in the Atlantic and almost nobody
outside Cape Verde knows it.

And on **Santiago**, **Cidade Velha** was the **first European
colonial settlement in the tropics** — with the pillory still
standing in the square it was used in.

## Before you go: the inter-island problem, and the season

### Entry and money

- **Many nationalities enter visa-free**, including the EU and
  UK. Others pay an **airport security fee (TSA)** registered
  online in advance — check your own case and register before you
  fly if required.
- **Cape Verdean escudo (CVE)**, **pegged to the euro** at a fixed
  rate, so there is no currency guesswork. **Euros are widely
  accepted** at roughly that rate.
- Cards work in Mindelo, Praia and the resort islands. **Santo
  Antão is cash** — draw before the ferry.
- **Portuguese is official; Kriolu is what everyone speaks.**
  French gets you further than English outside the tourist
  islands.

### The inter-island flights are the weak point

- **Domestic flights are unreliable.** Cancellations and
  retimings are routine, the fleet is small, and a cancelled leg
  can cost you a day.
- **Never connect a domestic flight to an international one on the
  same day.** Build a night's buffer in whichever island you fly
  home from.
- **Ferries** link São Vicente–Santo Antão (about an hour, several
  daily, reliable) and some southern islands (slower, rougher,
  and prone to cancellation in swell).
- **Book domestic legs early**, and keep the itinerary loose.

### Santo Antão has no airport

- The island's airstrip **has been closed for years**. The only
  way in is the **ferry from Mindelo to Porto Novo** — about an
  hour, two or three sailings daily, and it can be a rough
  crossing in winter swell.
- Check the day's timetable locally. It changes.

### When to go

- **Year-round warm**, 24–30°C, with the Atlantic keeping it
  moderate.
- **November to June is dry** and the hiking season on Santo
  Antão.
- **August to October** brings the brief rains, which is when
  Santo Antão is greenest and also when trails are slippery and
  ferries lumpier.
- **The harmattan** can blow Saharan dust across in December to
  February, hazing the views for a few days at a time.
- **Loggerhead turtles nest** on Boa Vista and Sal from **June to
  October**, if that shapes your dates.

## Day 1 — Land, and into Mindelo

- **14:00** — Land at **São Vicente (VXE)**, which is 15 minutes
  from town — or at **Sal (SID)** and connect, which is the more
  common routing.
- **15:30** — **Mindelo**: a natural harbour under a bare volcanic
  ridge, with a grid of pastel Portuguese colonial buildings in
  varying states of repair.
- **17:00** — Walk **Rua de Lisboa** and the **Praça Nova**, where
  the town sits out in the evening, and the pink **Palácio do
  Povo**.
- **18:00** — The **Torre de Belém** replica on the waterfront —
  a scaled copy of the Lisbon original, which tells you something
  about how this place saw itself.
- **19:30** — Dinner. ***Cachupa*** — the slow-cooked corn and
  bean stew that is the national dish, eaten at dinner and fried
  up with an egg for breakfast the next morning as *cachupa
  refogada*.
- **21:30** — **Live music.** Mindelo has it nightly — the bars on
  and around Rua de Lisboa — and this is where **morna** and
  **coladeira** come from. The musicians are often extremely good
  and the entry is usually free.

## Day 2 — Mindelo, and Cesária

- **09:00** — The **Mercado Municipal** and the **fish market** on
  the front, where the morning catch is sold and argued over.
- **10:30** — The **Casa da Morna** or the **Cesária Évora
  museum** — the barefoot diva was born here, sang in these bars
  for decades, and died in 2011; **morna is now on UNESCO's
  intangible heritage list**. The airport carries her name.
- **12:30** — Lunch, then across to **Laginha beach**, which is in
  the town itself.
- **15:00** — Drive out to **Monte Verde**, the island's high
  point at 750 m, for the view over the harbour and across to
  Santo Antão sitting on the horizon — which is where you are
  going tomorrow and it looks impossible from here.
- **17:30** — **Baía das Gatas** on the north coast, or **Calhau**
  with its volcanic cone and black beach.
- **19:30** — Dinner, and more music. **Draw cash tonight.**

## Day 3 — Ferry to Santo Antão, and the Cova crater

- **08:00** — The **ferry to Porto Novo**, about an hour. Sit
  outside; the crossing is open water and the island grows in
  front of you.
- **09:30** — Land, and take the ***estrada da corda*** — the
  **cobbled road**, laid by hand across the spine of the island
  and one of the genuine feats of the archipelago. It climbs from
  dry desert on the south coast to cloud forest on the ridge.
- **10:30** — **Cova de Paúl**: a collapsed volcanic crater with
  fields farmed on its floor, which you look down into from the
  rim.
- **11:00** — **The walk down into the Paúl valley.** From the Cova
  rim you descend through the crater, over the lip and down the
  valley — **about three to four hours and 1,100 m of descent** —
  past sugar cane, mango, breadfruit and houses stacked on
  terraces, finishing at the sea at **Vila das Pombas**. It is
  steep, cobbled in places, and the best single walk in Cape
  Verde.
- **15:30** — A ***grogue*** distillery in the valley — sugarcane
  spirit, distilled in small *trapiches*, often with an ox turning
  the press. Try the *ponche*, which is grogue with honey and
  lime.
- **18:00** — Check in at **Paúl** or **Ponta do Sol**.
- **19:30** — Dinner.

## Day 4 — The coast road and Ponta do Sol

- **08:30** — **Ponta do Sol**, the island's northern tip, a small
  town of coloured houses with the Atlantic hitting the rocks
  below it.
- **09:30** — **The coastal path to Fontainhas**, and this is the
  one people come for: a trail cut into the cliff face, dropping
  to sea level and climbing again, with **Fontainhas** itself — a
  village of perhaps two hundred people perched on a spur above
  the ocean, which from a distance looks like it has been glued
  on. About two hours to there.
- **12:00** — Continue to **Corvo** and **Formiguinhas** if you
  are walking on, or turn back.
- **13:30** — Lunch at Ponta do Sol.
- **15:30** — **Ribeira Grande (Povoação)**, the island's main
  town, and the valley behind it.
- **17:30** — **Sunset at Ponta do Sol**, from the old airstrip
  that is now a promenade.
- **19:30** — Dinner — *lagosta* if it is on, or tuna, which is
  everywhere and excellent.

## Day 5 — Santo Antão, the big walk

- **07:30** — Choose your day. These are the two best:
- **Option A — Xoxo to Cruzinha.** From the **Ribeira Grande**
  valley over to the north coast, about six hours, with the
  **Xoxo** needle rock and villages reached by no road at all.
  It ends at the sea and you arrange a pickup.
- **Option B — Ribeira da Torre.** Up the valley behind Ribeira
  Grande, three to four hours, into a gorge with walls that close
  over and terraced cultivation to the top of them.
- **13:00** — Lunch wherever you finish.
- **15:30** — **Tarrafal de Monte Trigo** if you have a 4x4 and a
  spare day — a black-sand village on the west coast reached by a
  track with a thousand-metre drop, and genuinely remote.
- **18:00** — Back. **Your legs will know about it.**
- **19:30** — Dinner, and grogue.

## Day 6 — Back across, and fly to Santiago

- **08:00** — The ferry back to Mindelo, an hour.
- **10:00** — Time in Mindelo, then the airport.
- **13:00** — **Fly to Santiago (Praia)**, about 50 minutes — and
  this is the leg most likely to move, so keep the afternoon
  flexible.
- **15:00** — **Praia** is the capital and the largest city in the
  country, built on a plateau — the **Plateau** — above the
  harbour. It is louder, poorer and more African than Mindelo, and
  that contrast is worth seeing.
- **17:00** — The **Plateau**: the Praça Alexandre Albuquerque,
  the **Mercado de Sucupira** — a vast informal market — and the
  **Museu Etnográfico**.
- **19:30** — Dinner, and *funaná* rather than morna here — faster,
  accordion-driven, and banned by the Portuguese for decades.

## Day 7 — Cidade Velha

- **09:00** — **Cidade Velha**, 15 km west, and give it the
  morning. Founded in **1462** as Ribeira Grande, it was the
  **first European colonial settlement in the tropics** and the
  archipelago's capital for three centuries.
- **09:30** — Its reason for existing was the **Atlantic slave
  trade**: Cape Verde was uninhabited before the Portuguese
  arrived, and this town was the entrepôt where enslaved people
  from the West African coast were held, processed and shipped
  onward. **The *pelourinho* — the marble pillory — still stands
  in the main square**, where it was used.
- **11:00** — The **Fortaleza Real de São Filipe** above the town,
  built in 1590 after Francis Drake sacked the place twice, with
  the whole valley and coast below it.
- **12:00** — The ruins of the **Sé Catedral**, begun 1556, and
  the **Igreja de Nossa Senhora do Rosário** — **the oldest
  colonial church still standing anywhere in the world**.
- **13:30** — Lunch by the beach at the bottom of the Ribeira,
  under the coconut palms.
- **15:30** — Back to Praia, or on into the interior.
- **17:00** — **Quebra Canela** beach for the late afternoon.
- **19:30** — Dinner.

## Day 8 — The interior of Santiago

- **08:30** — North into the mountains, which most visitors to
  this island never see.
- **10:00** — **Assomada** and its **Sucupira market** — the
  biggest on the island, best on a Wednesday or Saturday, and
  entirely a working market rather than a craft one.
- **11:30** — The **Tabanka museum** at Assomada, on the
  syncretic religious brotherhoods that survived here.
- **13:00** — **Serra Malagueta Natural Park**, a ridge at 1,000 m
  with walking trails and views down both coasts.
- **15:00** — **Tarrafal** on the north coast: a crescent of
  white sand under palms, and the best beach on Santiago — with
  the **former concentration camp** above it, where the Portuguese
  Estado Novo imprisoned political dissidents from Portugal and
  the colonies. The museum is small and unsparing.
- **18:00** — Drive back, about 90 minutes.
- **19:30** — Last dinner.

## Day 9 — Fly home

- **Morning** — **Praia (RAI)** for international departures, or
  connect via Sal.
- **Build in the buffer.** If your international flight leaves
  from a different island, **do not take the domestic leg on the
  same day** — this is the single most common way to miss a flight
  out of Cape Verde.
- **Last buy** — **grogue** from Santo Antão, Cesária Évora on
  CD, and coffee from Fogo, which is grown in volcanic ash and is
  very good.

## What to pack

- **Proper walking boots with grip.** The Santo Antão trails are
  cobbled, loose and steep, and trainers are not enough on the
  descents.
- **Walking poles** if your knees have opinions — the Paúl descent
  is 1,100 m in one go.
- **A windproof layer.** The Cova rim and the ridges are cold and
  the trade wind never stops.
- **Cash in escudos**, drawn before the ferry.
- **Sun protection.** The Atlantic light is strong and the dry
  islands have no shade.
- **European Type C/F plugs**, 220V.
- **Something for the ferry** if you are prone — it can be rough.

## FAQ

**Which islands should I pick?**
**Santo Antão for walking, São Vicente for music, Santiago for
history, Fogo for the volcano, Sal and Boa Vista for beaches and
kitesurfing.** They are genuinely different places and choosing
the wrong one for what you want is the commonest mistake. This
route takes the first three.

**Is the hiking really that good?**
Yes, and it is the island's secret. **Santo Antão has ridge and
valley walking comparable to Madeira or La Gomera**, with
hand-built cobbled paths linking villages that have no road, and
a fraction of the visitors. If you only have four days in Cape
Verde, spend them there.

**What about Fogo?**
**Pico do Fogo is 2,829 m and you can climb it**, and people live
inside the caldera at **Chã das Caldeiras**, growing vines in
volcanic ash. The **2014 eruption buried the village** and it was
rebuilt on top. It is a superb addition and it needs two or three
days and another flight — which is why it is not in this nine.

**How bad are the domestic flights?**
Bad enough to plan around. Cancellations and retimings are
normal. **Keep a night's buffer before any international
departure**, and do not book tight connections between islands.

**Do I need Portuguese?**
It helps a great deal, and French helps too. English is limited
outside Sal, Boa Vista and the Mindelo tourist places.
