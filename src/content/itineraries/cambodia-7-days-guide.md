Angkor is not a temple; it's a **vanished megacity** — at its
height around 1200 AD, when London held 20,000 people, close
to a million lived here among reservoirs and boulevards whose
stone survivors now rise out of jungle. You will have seen a
thousand photos of Angkor Wat's towers against a pink dawn.
The photos undersell it. Nothing prepares you for Ta Prohm's
strangler figs digesting whole galleries, or the Bayon's 200
stone faces smiling in every direction at once.

This 7-day shape pairs the wonder with the country: **Siem
Reap (4 nights)** for the temples, the lake and the best
circus you'll ever see, then **Phnom Penh (2 nights)** for
royal gold, riverside ease — and the Khmer Rouge history that
explains modern Cambodia, visited the respectful way.

Cambodia runs on **US dollars** (riel for small change —
details below), so the budget does too: figure
**{{USD 700}} per person** excluding
international flights. **Go November–February**: dry,
"cool" (30°C), green from the monsoon just gone. March–May
is a furnace; June–October is wet-green with afternoon
downpours and the best light.

## Before you go: the admin

- **e-Visa, the easy kind:** **US$30 + US$6 processing
  (≈ $36 all-in)** at the official **evisa.gov.kh** —
  passport scan + photo, **3–5 working days**, 30-day
  single-entry stay. Visa-on-arrival ($30 cash, two
  photos) also works at Siem Reap and Phnom Penh
  airports, but the e-visa skips the queue after a long
  connection. Use only the official site; lookalikes
  charge double.
- **Plus the free e-Arrival Card** at **arrival.gov.kh**
  within 7 days of flying — ten minutes, QR code,
  airlines increasingly ask.
- **Getting there:** no non-stops from the Gulf — one-stop via
  Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur, Singapore or Ho Chi Minh City,
  ~US$490–820 return, 9–12 h door to door. Note
  both airports are NEW: **Siem Reap–Angkor (SAI)** sits
  40 km east of town (~$30–35 taxi, 50 min), and Phnom
  Penh's **Techo International** (opened 2025) is ~30 km
  south.
- **The dollar–riel dance:** prices quote in USD; change
  under a dollar comes in riel (~4,100 = $1). Both are
  real money — spend the riel on tuk-tuks and water.
  **Bring crisp, untorn USD notes** (worn bills get
  refused), and small denominations: breaking a $50 at
  a noodle stall is a diplomatic incident.
- **Apps:** **Grab and PassApp** price every tuk-tuk
  ride fairly ($1–3 around town) and end all haggling
  drama. Download before landing.

## Day 1 — Land in Siem Reap, buy tomorrow's dawn

- **Evening arrival** via your connection; taxi into
  town, check in.
- **19:30** — First amok (coconut fish curry steamed
  in banana leaf, $4–7) in the lanes off **Pub
  Street** — touristy, twinkly and fun for exactly one
  evening — and a **Night Market** wander.
- **21:00** — Buy the **Angkor Pass online tonight**
  at ticket.angkorenterprise.gov.kh (**3-day pass
  $62** — any 3 days within 10; 1-day $37, 7-day $72;
  **under-12s free** with passport). Or use the old
  trick: the ticket office after 5 pm sells passes
  valid from tomorrow, skipping the 4:45 am queue.

**Where to stay in Siem Reap** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Jaya House River Park** — 5.0/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~5,400 reviews) and routinely in
  world-best lists: riverside calm, plastic-free
  pioneer, staff who remember your coffee order.
  ~$260+.
- *Mid-range:* **Viroth's Hotel** — 5.0/5 (~3,100
  reviews), 60s-modernist pool courtyard, endlessly
  photographed. ~$90–130.
- *Value:* **Golden Temple Residence** — 5.0/5
  (~4,800 reviews, the town's #1 Best Value):
  near-boutique rooms, airport pickup and legendary
  hospitality at ~$45–65.

## Day 2 — Sunrise at Angkor Wat, Ta Prohm's jungle

- **04:45** — Tuk-tuk into the dark (temple-day
  tuk-tuks: ~$20–25 small circuit, agree the day
  price up front or book via the hotel). Stake out
  the **north reflecting pond** at Angkor Wat;
  sunrise cracks over the five towers around 6:00 to
  a soundtrack of two thousand shutters. Then do
  what the crowd doesn't: **go inside immediately** —
  the galleries' 800 m of bas-reliefs (churning of
  the ocean of milk!) and the steep top tier are
  half-empty while everyone else eats breakfast.
- **10:30** — Back to town or the hotel pool through
  the midday furnace — this is the pacing secret of
  Angkor: **temples 5–10 am and 3–6 pm, water in
  between.**
- **15:00** — **Ta Prohm** — the "Tomb Raider
  temple", left half-swallowed: silk-cotton roots
  pour over doorways like slow-motion waterfalls.
  Late light makes the stones glow and thins the
  tour groups. Finish at **Banteay Kdei** across
  the road, blissfully ignored.
- **19:00** — Khmer barbecue dinner ($8–12) and
  early to bed; tomorrow has 200 faces.

## Day 3 — Angkor Thom, then the circus steals the day

- **07:00** — **Angkor Thom**, the walled capital:
  enter by the **South Gate's** causeway of gods and
  demons, then the **Bayon** — 54 towers, 200
  serene colossal faces, corridors of everyday-life
  carvings. This is the morning to **hire a licensed
  guide (~$35–45/day, shared)**: the difference
  between piles of stones and a functioning city
  reassembling in your imagination.
- **10:00** — Baphuon's flying causeway, the royal
  terraces of the **Elephants** and the **Leper
  King**, exit via Victory Gate.
- **12:30** — Pool. Non-negotiable.
- **17:00** — Gentle sunset option: **Pre Rup's**
  brick towers (fewer buses than the famous Phnom
  Bakheng scrum, which we'd honestly skip).
- **20:00** — **Phare, the Cambodian Circus**
  ($18–38 by seat tier — book a day ahead): young
  performers from at-risk backgrounds telling Khmer
  stories through acrobatics and fire. Ask anyone
  who's been: it out-emotions the temples. The
  site's strongest evening recommendation in
  Southeast Asia.

## Day 4 — Pink sandstone and the grand circuit

- **07:30** — Drive out (37 km, tuk-tuk ~$35–45 or
  share a car) to **Banteay Srei**, the 10th-century
  "citadel of women": miniature, rose-pink, and
  carved so finely it looks 3D-printed by devas.
  En route back, the **Landmine Museum** ($5) tells
  one de-miner's astonishing story — small, sobering,
  worthwhile.
- **13:00** — Lunch + pool.
- **15:30** — Finish the pass on the **grand
  circuit**: **Preah Khan's** long corridors going
  golden, or **Neak Pean's** island shrine on the
  old reservoir — the quiet, crowd-free Angkor most
  visitors never meet.
- **19:30** — Best-dinner night: Siem Reap's new
  Khmer kitchens ($15–25 pp) do tasting menus that
  would cost 4× in Bangkok.

## Day 5 — The lake, then fly to the capital

- **08:00** — **Tonlé Sap** — Southeast Asia's
  largest lake, which quadruples in size each
  monsoon and feeds half of Cambodia. Do the
  **ethics-checked version: Kampong Phluk** (or
  Kompong Khleang) — genuine stilt villages ~$25–30
  pp with boat — and **skip Chong Khneas**, the
  near-shore "floating village" infamous for
  rice-buying and crocodile-farm shakedowns. Wet
  season floats the forests; dry season turns
  stilts into 8-metre ladders — both are
  extraordinary.
- **13:00** — Lunch, pack, tuk-tuk goodbyes.
- **16:00** — Fly **Siem Reap → Phnom Penh**
  (45 min, ~$70–100 — or the honest budget swap:
  **Giant Ibis bus, $15, 6 h** of countryside).
- **18:30** — Check in, then the **riverside**: the
  Tonlé Sap and Mekong meet at Sisowath Quay —
  evening aerobics classes, monks with phones,
  dinner on a breezy terrace ($6–12).

**Where to stay in Phnom Penh** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Raffles Hotel Le Royal** — 4.5/5
  (~2,700 reviews), 1929 grande dame; Jackie
  Kennedy's cocktail is still on the bar menu.
  ~$180+. (Sofitel Phokeethra, 4.7, is the modern
  five-star.)
- *Mid-range:* **Palace Gate Hotel** — 4.9/5
  (~2,900 reviews), beside the Royal Palace with
  breakfast in. ~$70–100.
- *Value:* **Pavilion** — 4.6/5 (~3,400 reviews),
  colonial villas, garden pools, adults-calm.
  ~$50–70.

## Day 6 — The kingdom's gold and its darkest chapter

- **08:00** — **Tuol Sleng (S-21)** — the school the
  Khmer Rouge turned into a prison, now the genocide
  museum (**~$10 with the essential audio guide**).
  Go early, go quiet, give it two hours. Some
  mornings, the last survivors sign books in the
  courtyard — shake their hands.
- **10:30** — **Choeung Ek killing fields** (15 km
  out, tuk-tuk ~$15 return; **$6 with audio**): the
  memorial stupa of skulls, the orchard paths, the
  quiet. **Our honest guidance:** this pairing is
  emotionally heavy — take it in the morning,
  together; think hard before bringing under-12s
  (the audio guide is unflinching); and let the
  afternoon be gentle. Cambodia asks visitors to
  witness this, and rewards those who do with
  understanding the country's miracle: everything
  you've enjoyed all week was rebuilt from zero
  after 1979.
- **14:30** — The gentle afternoon: the **Royal
  Palace & Silver Pagoda** ($10 — gold spires,
  emerald Buddha, a floor of 5,000 silver tiles;
  opens 14:00, shoulders/knees covered), then the
  Art-Deco dome of **Central Market** or a
  **sunset Mekong cruise** ($10–15, drinks aboard).
- **19:30** — Dinner in Bassac Lane's shophouse
  bars-and-bites alley — Phnom Penh's cheerful
  modern face, which after today feels like the
  point.

## Day 7 — Russian Market, fly home

- **08:30** — **Toul Tom Poung (Russian) Market**:
  the best souvenir hunting in the country — kramas
  (checked scarves), silver, coffee, $3 "brand"
  T-shirts of negotiable authenticity — plus a
  legendary local breakfast corner (noodle soup,
  $2).
- **11:30** — Pack, checkout, and out to Techo
  International (~45 min–1 h with traffic — pad it)
  for the flight home via your connection.

## What to pack

- **Temple uniform:** shoulders AND knees covered
  (strictly enforced on Angkor Wat's top tier) —
  light linen, not leggings-as-pants; a scarf
  upgrades any outfit at a doorway.
- Real walking sandals or trainers — temple stairs
  are ship-ladders polished by nine centuries.
- Sun kit: hat, SPF 50, refillable bottle (hotels
  refill; the heat is a pickpocket).
- Mosquito repellent (dusk, and the lake trip),
  small torch for sunrise footing.
- **Crisp small-denomination US dollars** — the
  packing item unique to Cambodia.
- Modest-lightweight for the memorial morning;
  cameras stay down inside S-21's rooms as marked.

## FAQ

**Is the e-visa really all we need? Any airport
surprises?**
That plus the free e-Arrival QR — Cambodian
immigration is quick and friendly by regional
standards. The only classic surprise is money:
worn or torn dollar bills refused at the visa
desk (VoA payers) or shops. Bank-fresh notes
solve it.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Manageable with light planning. Cambodia's **Cham
Muslim community** runs halal Khmer restaurants in
both cities (clusters near Siem Reap's Old Market
and Phnom Penh's mosques), and Malaysian/Indian
halal spots back them up. Vegetarians: veg amok,
stir-fried morning glory, fresh spring rolls and
a solid Indian-restaurant bench in both towns —
say "no fish sauce/prahok" clearly (it hides in
everything).

**Is it safe? Anything to watch?**
Tourist Cambodia is friendly and low-threat; the
real items are **bag-snatching from moving motos
in Phnom Penh** (phones and totes on the
road-side arm — wear cross-body, riverside
especially), standard scam-radar at Chong Khneas
(we route you elsewhere) and around child-begging
(give to vetted NGOs, never at temples — it keeps
kids out of school), and tuk-tuk pricing solved
by Grab/PassApp. Roads are the actual hazard:
cross like Vietnam taught you — steady pace, no
darting.

**Can our kids handle the Phnom Penh history
day?**
Teens: yes, and many call it the most important
thing they've seen. Under-12s: we'd skip S-21 and
Choeung Ek entirely — one parent can visit while
the other does the Royal Palace and a riverboat.
The audio guides pull no punches, and that's what
makes them worth it for those who go.

**One-day, three-day or seven-day Angkor pass?**
Three-day, almost always: $62 vs $37 buys you
sunrise day, Angkor Thom day and the Banteay
Srei/grand-circuit day WITHOUT temple-fatigue
death-marches. Archaeology obsessives with slow
itineraries take the $72 seven-day. The one-day
pass is for layovers only — and it shows.

**What's Cambodia guide #2?**
**The south coast**: Kampot's pepper farms and
river kayaks, Kep's crab market, and Koh Rong
Samloem's blissed-out beaches — the
after-the-temples decompression week. On the
list.
