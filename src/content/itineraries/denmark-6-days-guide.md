Copenhagen is the rare capital that has been designed rather than
merely grown, and the design decision that matters is the bicycle.
More than half the city commutes by bike on separated lanes with
their own traffic lights, and renting one for a week is not a
gimmick — it is how you see the place.

The city gives you a painted harbour at **Nyhavn**, a canal-side
palace quarter, and — genuinely — **harbour baths you can swim in**
in the middle of a capital city, because the water was cleaned up
enough to allow it.

Two short train rides fill out the week: **Helsingør**, where
Hamlet's castle guards the narrowest point between Denmark and
Sweden, and **Roskilde**, where five Viking ships were raised from
the fjord and put in a museum built around them.

Six days is right. Denmark is small, and Copenhagen rewards slowness.

## Before you go: bikes, kroner and the cost

### Entry and season

- **Denmark is in Schengen** — one visa covers it. The full process
  is in our
  [Schengen visa guide](/guides/schengen-visa-how-it-works/).
- **Not the euro.** Denmark uses the **krone (DKK)**, pegged closely
  to the euro but a separate currency.
- **May to September** is the season, and **June and July** give the
  long evenings that make the harbour work. **Winter is dark and
  genuinely cold**, but December has the best version of Tivoli and
  the hygge is not marketing at that time of year.

### The bicycle is the transport plan

- **Rent a bike for the whole stay.** It is faster than the metro
  for most journeys in the centre, and the infrastructure is
  genuinely separated from cars.
- **The rules are enforced**: hand signals before stopping and
  turning, lights after dark (a fineable offence without them), and
  never ride in the pedestrian zones.
- **Stay right, overtake left**, and expect Danes to be travelling
  considerably faster than you.
- Not cycling? The **metro runs driverless and 24 hours**, and the
  city is small enough to walk most of.

### Money, and the honest cost

- **Denmark is expensive**, and Copenhagen more so than the rest.
  Cards work everywhere including on buses; cash is rarely needed.
- **Lunch is where the value is** — the smørrebrød open sandwiches
  that are a Danish institution are a lunchtime thing, and cost a
  fraction of dinner.
- **Street food halls** — Reffen and the covered markets — are how
  locals eat out without spending a fortune.
- **Tap water is excellent** and free everywhere. Refill.
- **The Copenhagen Card** pays off only if you are doing several
  paid attractions a day. Price your actual list first; for a slow
  trip it usually does not.

### Small things that help

- **Hygge is real but mundane** — it means candles, low light and
  staying in with people, not a purchasable experience.
- **Everyone speaks excellent English**, and will switch instantly.
- **Tipping is not expected.** Service is included and rounding up
  is plenty.

## Day 1 — Land, and Nyhavn

- **Afternoon** — Land, drop bags, collect the bike.
- **Evening** — **Nyhavn**'s painted houses, then a walk along the
  harbour to the opera house. In summer this is a late-evening city.

## Day 2 — The city by bike

- **Morning** — **Rosenborg Castle** for the crown jewels and the
  gardens, then **Amalienborg** for the palace square.
- **Afternoon** — The **Little Mermaid** briefly, since you will be
  asked, then **Kastellet**'s star fort, which is better.
- **Evening** — **Reffen** or another street-food hall.

## Day 3 — Design, and a swim

- **Morning** — **Designmuseum Danmark**, which explains why
  everything in the city looks the way it does.
- **Afternoon** — **Islands Brygge harbour bath** if it is warm
  enough, which is the most Copenhagen thing you can do.
- **Evening** — **Tivoli** after dark — older than almost every
  other amusement park in the world, and more garden than rides.

## Day 4 — Helsingør and Hamlet's castle

- **Morning** — Train north, about 45 minutes, to **Kronborg**, the
  castle Shakespeare called Elsinore. Sweden is visible across the
  water.
- **Afternoon** — The **Maritime Museum** next door, built into a
  dry dock, or **Louisiana** modern art museum on the way back —
  one of the best-sited galleries anywhere.
- **Evening** — Back to Copenhagen.

## Day 5 — Roskilde and the Viking ships

- **Morning** — Train west, about 25 minutes. **Five Viking ships**,
  deliberately sunk to block the fjord a thousand years ago, raised
  and reassembled.
- **Afternoon** — **Roskilde Cathedral**, where Danish monarchs are
  buried, then back.
- **Evening** — Last dinner. Book if it is somewhere good;
  Copenhagen's kitchens fill.

## Day 6 — Fly home

- The metro runs to the airport from the city centre in about
  fifteen minutes, driverless, every few minutes.

## What to pack

- **A waterproof layer.** It rains often and briefly, and you will
  be on a bike.
- **Lights for the bike** if your rental lacks them — it is a fine,
  not a suggestion.
- **European Type C/F plugs.**
- **Swimwear in summer** for the harbour baths.
- **Warm layers outside July and August.** The wind comes off the
  sea and the city is flat and open.

## FAQ

**Is six days too long for Copenhagen?**
No, because two of them are day trips. The city alone would be three
full days; Kronborg and Roskilde justify the rest.

**Should I get the Copenhagen Card?**
Only if you are visiting several paid attractions daily. Price your
actual list — for a slower trip it usually does not repay.

**Is it really that expensive?**
Beds and dinners, yes. Lunch, transport and street food, much less
so. Eating the Danish way — the big meal at midday — makes a
noticeable difference.

**Can I combine it with Sweden?**
Easily. Malmö is about 35 minutes across the bridge from Copenhagen,
and Stockholm is a short flight or a five-hour train.
