The Dominican Republic receives more visitors than any other
Caribbean country, and the overwhelming majority of them see one
walled resort in Punta Cana. That is a shame, because the country
behind it is the most varied in the region.

**Santo Domingo's Zona Colonial** is the **oldest continuously
inhabited European settlement in the Americas** — founded 1498,
with the first cathedral, the first university and the first
paved street in the New World, all still standing and all still
in use.

The island also has **the Caribbean's highest mountains** — Pico
Duarte is 3,098 m, and the valleys below it are cool enough to
grow strawberries. And between **January and March**, several
thousand **humpback whales** arrive in **Samaná bay** to calve,
which is among the most reliable whale encounters anywhere.

## Before you go: seasons, driving and the two coasts

### Entry and money

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free.** A **tourist card** is
  bundled into the air fare for most arrivals — check, because
  occasionally it is not.
- You must complete the **free online E-Ticket form** before
  arrival and departure. Do it the day before; the QR code is
  checked.
- **Dominican peso (DOP).** US dollars are accepted in resorts at
  a poor rate. **Pay in pesos.** Cards work in cities and resorts;
  colmados, guaguas and street food are cash.

### When to go — and the whales

- **December to April is the dry season** and the peak.
- **Mid-January to late March is whale season in Samaná**, and it
  is the single best reason to time a trip. Thousands of humpbacks
  come to the Silver Bank and the bay to breed and calve.
  **Outside those weeks the boats do not run**, so if the whales
  matter, that is your window.
- **June to November is hurricane season**, with **August and
  September** the peak. Rain comes in bursts and prices drop.
- **The north coast (Samaná, Puerto Plata) is wetter** year-round
  than the south and east.

### Driving, honestly

- **Hiring a car gives you this itinerary**, and Dominican driving
  demands attention: *motoconchos* — motorbike taxis — appear from
  any direction, lanes are advisory, and rural roads are unlit
  with livestock on them.
- **Do not drive at night outside cities.**
- **Full insurance is effectively mandatory** and sold on top of
  every quoted rate.
- The alternative is **guaguas** (shared minibuses) and long-
  distance coaches, which are cheap and good between cities but
  will not get you to the Jarabacoa waterfalls.

## Day 1 — Land in Santo Domingo

- **14:00** — Land at **Las Américas (SDQ)**, 30 minutes east of
  the city.
- **15:30** — **Stay in the Zona Colonial.** This is not a
  preserved quarter — people live and work in it — and staying
  inside means the evenings are yours.
- **17:00** — **Calle El Conde**, the pedestrian spine, down to
  **Parque Colón** with the cathedral on it.
- **18:00** — The **Catedral Primada de América**, begun 1512 and
  **the first cathedral built in the Americas**, in a Gothic shell
  with a Renaissance front.
- **19:30** — **Plaza España** at dusk, with the **Alcázar de
  Colón** — Diego Columbus's palace, 1510 — lit above the row of
  restaurants in the old arsenal below it.
- **20:30** — Dinner. *Mofongo* — mashed fried plantain with
  garlic and pork crackling — and a Presidente, served
  *vestida de novia*, so cold the bottle frosts.

## Day 2 — The Zona Colonial

- **09:00** — **Calle Las Damas**, the **first paved street in the
  Americas**, named for the ladies of the viceroy's court who
  walked it. The **Museo de las Casas Reales** on it covers the
  colonial administration properly.
- **10:30** — The **Alcázar de Colón** inside — furnished to
  period, and the family that built it ran the Spanish colonial
  enterprise from these rooms.
- **12:00** — The **Monasterio de San Francisco** ruins, roofless
  since a 1673 earthquake and a hurricane finished it, now used
  for concerts, and the **Hospital San Nicolás de Bari** — **the
  first hospital in the Americas**, also a ruin.
- **13:30** — Lunch, then out of the heat.
- **16:00** — The **Fortaleza Ozama** on the river, the oldest
  military structure in the Americas, with its 1505 tower.
- **18:00** — **Calle Hostos** and the back streets, which is
  where the quarter stops being a monument.
- **21:00** — ***Merengue*** or ***bachata***. Both were born
  here, bachata out of the rural south and dismissed for decades
  as music for the poor before it conquered Latin America.
  **Sunday evening at Parque Duarte** is where people actually
  dance.

## Day 3 — North-east to Samaná

- **08:30** — Drive north-east on the toll road, about three
  hours, and it is a good road.
- **12:00** — **Samaná town** on its bay, a small port that looks
  across at hills.
- **13:00** — Lunch on the waterfront.
- **14:30** — **Cayo Levantado**, twenty minutes offshore by boat
  — a small island with a public beach, busy at midday and empty
  by four.
- **16:30** — Or drive out to **Playa Rincón**, which is
  consistently rated one of the best beaches in the Caribbean: 3
  km of sand backed by coconut palms with a cold river at one end,
  and a rough track in that keeps the numbers down.
- **19:00** — Dinner in Samaná or **Las Galeras**.
- **Note** — Book tomorrow's whale trip tonight if you are here in
  season; the licensed operators fill.

## Day 4 — Whales, or the waterfall

- **In season (mid-January to late March), 08:30** — The **whale
  watching** boat into **Samaná bay**. Humpbacks migrate from the
  North Atlantic to breed here — **the males sing, and the boats
  cut their engines so you hear it through the hull**. Breaching
  and tail-slapping are routine. Regulation is strict: licensed
  boats only, limited numbers, set distances.
- **12:30** — Back, and lunch.
- **Out of season, 09:00** — **El Limón waterfall**: 40 m, reached
  on horseback or on foot through muddy forest for about 40
  minutes, with a pool you swim in at the bottom.
- **15:00** — **Las Terrenas**, the north-coast town — French and
  Italian-influenced, with the best restaurants in the region and
  a long beach.
- **18:00** — Sunset at **Playa Bonita**.
- **20:00** — Dinner in Las Terrenas.

## Day 5 — Los Haitises, and inland

- **08:00** — **Los Haitises National Park** by boat from Sabana
  de la Mar: **mogotes** — limestone karst humps — rising straight
  out of the water in a mangrove bay, with caves containing
  **Taíno pictographs** several hundred years old, and frigatebird
  and pelican colonies on the rocks.
- **12:30** — Back, and lunch.
- **14:00** — Drive inland and south-west toward the **Cordillera
  Central**, about three and a half hours.
- **17:30** — **Jarabacoa**, at 530 m, and the temperature drops
  noticeably — this is pine forest, river gorges and strawberry
  farms, which is not the Caribbean anybody advertises.
- **19:30** — Dinner, and you may want a jumper.

## Day 6 — The mountains

- **08:30** — **Salto de Jimenoa Uno**, a 60 m fall reached across
  a swinging footbridge, and **Salto de Baiguate**, lower and
  swimmable.
- **11:00** — **White-water rafting on the Yaque del Norte**, the
  Caribbean's longest river — grade II–III, half a day, and the
  only commercial rafting in the region.
- **14:30** — Lunch, then the **Pico Duarte** trailhead at La
  Ciénaga if you want to see where it starts. **The summit is
  3,098 m, the highest point in the Caribbean**, and a two to
  three day trek with guides and mules — a different trip, but
  worth knowing exists.
- **17:00** — **Constanza** if you have the afternoon: higher
  still, growing vegetables, and genuinely cold at night.
- **19:30** — Dinner.

## Day 7 — Across to the east coast

- **08:30** — The drive east, four to five hours via Santo
  Domingo and the toll road.
- **11:00** — Break at **Boca Chica** or push on.
- **14:00** — Arrive **Punta Cana** or **Bávaro**, and this is
  where the country's reputation comes from: **50 km of continuous
  white sand and coconut palm** on the eastern tip, with the
  resorts strung along it.
- **15:30** — **Playa Bávaro** or **Macao**, the latter public,
  wilder and with surf.
- **18:00** — Sunset.
- **19:30** — Dinner — and if you are not in an all-inclusive,
  **El Cortecito** and **Los Corales** have the independent
  restaurants.

## Day 8 — The east, your way

- **Option A — the water, 09:00.** **Isla Saona** inside Parque
  Nacional del Este: a catamaran and speedboat day to a sandbar
  and a shallow natural pool with starfish. It is the classic
  excursion and it is busy; the early boats are better.
- **Option B — the inland east, 09:00.** **Hoyo Azul**, a cenote
  of improbable blue at the foot of a cliff, and the **Indigenous
  Eyes** lagoons — twelve freshwater pools in a private reserve,
  five of which you can swim in.
- **Option C — Montaña Redonda, 07:00.** A hill an hour north with
  swings and hammocks on the summit facing the lagoon, which is
  entirely an Instagram construction and is also a genuinely good
  sunrise.
- **16:00** — The beach, properly, for the last afternoon.
- **19:30** — Last dinner.

## Day 9 — Fly home

- **Morning** — **Punta Cana (PUJ)** is the country's busiest
  airport and 20 minutes from the hotels. If you are flying from
  **Santo Domingo (SDQ)** instead, that is a three-hour drive —
  check which your ticket says before you book the car's drop-off.
- **Return the car** with a full tank and allow for the
  inspection.
- **Note** — Complete the **departure E-Ticket** before you get to
  the airport.

## What to pack

- **Reef-safe sunscreen** and more than you think.
- **A warm layer for Jarabacoa and Constanza** — the mountains get
  genuinely cold at night and nobody packs for it.
- **Water shoes** for the waterfalls and the river.
- **Repellent** for the mangroves and the mountain evenings.
- **Pesos in small notes** for colmados, motoconchos and tips.
- **US-style Type A/B plugs**, 110V.
- **A dry bag** for the whale boat and Los Haitises, both of which
  are wet.

## FAQ

**When exactly are the whales?**
**Mid-January to late March**, with February the peak. Several
thousand humpbacks come to Samaná bay and the Silver Bank to breed
and calve, and the encounters are regulated and reliable. Outside
that window the boats simply do not run — so if this is why you
are going, book the dates around it.

**Is it worth leaving Punta Cana?**
That is the whole argument of this itinerary. Punta Cana has the
best beaches and nothing else; **Santo Domingo has 500 years of
Americas history and the mountains have pine forest and
white water**. Doing both is why this is nine days and not seven.

**Is driving really that demanding?**
It needs attention rather than nerve. Motoconchos, informal
overtaking and unlit rural roads are the issues. Stick to daylight
outside the cities and take the full insurance. If that does not
appeal, a driver for the week is common and not expensive.

**Haiti shares the island — does that affect travel?**
The border is closed to ordinary tourism and the situation in
Haiti is severe. **Nothing on this itinerary goes near it** — the
whole route is in the east and centre of the country.

**Santo Domingo or Puerto Plata for the north?**
This route uses **Samaná**, which has the whales and the better
beaches. Puerto Plata has the cable car and more resorts, and is
the alternative if you are flying into POP rather than SDQ.
