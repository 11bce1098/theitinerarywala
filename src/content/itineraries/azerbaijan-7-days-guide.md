Azerbaijan is the Caucasus with the contrast turned up: a capital
where a UNESCO-walled medieval city sits under flame-shaped glass
towers, a desert coast that leaks fire and mud from the earth
itself, and — five hours inland — Sheki, a Silk Road town whose
Khan's Palace might be the most beautiful room between Istanbul and
Samarkand. It's three hours from the Gulf on budget airlines,
visa-easy for almost everyone, and cheaper than its skyline
suggests.

This guide completes our Caucasus trio — Georgia and Armenia are
its siblings — and ends with the overland trick that links them.

**Go April–June or September–October** for the sweet spot. July–
August cooks on the Absheron peninsula (35°C+); November–March is
cool, moody and fine for the city but grey in the mountains. Sheki
runs ~8°C cooler than Baku year-round.

All costs are in Azerbaijani manat (AZN), with conversions
calculated at today's exchange rate — the manat sits near 1.7 to
the US dollar, so prices are gentler than they look.

## Before you go: the 15-minute admin

### Visas — easier than you think

- **Visa-free (90 days):** UAE, Qatar, Turkey, and most CIS
  passports (Russia, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and more).
- **The residence-permit shortcut — big for Gulf readers:** holders
  of a valid **UAE residence visa or GCC permanent residence** get
  a **30-day visa on arrival** at Baku airport, whatever their
  passport — an Indian passport with an Emirates ID walks up to
  the counter.
- **Everyone else — the ASAN e-visa:** most nationalities
  (including India, EU, UK, USA) apply at
  [evisa.gov.az](https://evisa.gov.az) — **USD 25, issued within 3
  working days** (urgent: USD 60, ~3 hours), single entry, 30-day
  stay. One of the world's least painful e-visas.
- **The regional fine print, stated plainly:** Azerbaijan's border
  with Armenia is closed and Armenian citizens cannot enter;
  ordinary Armenian tourist stamps in other passports are not an
  issue. Building a full Caucasus trip? Route it
  Azerbaijan → Georgia → Armenia (or reverse) — Georgia is the
  hinge, and this guide ends conveniently near its border.

### Money

- The **manat (AZN)** is stable and strong (~1.7/USD). Cards work
  widely in Baku — including Bolt taxis and most restaurants —
  but **Sheki and the road stops are cash country**: withdraw in
  Baku before Day 5.
- Costs run friendly: metro rides 0.50 AZN, a good dinner for two
  35–60 AZN, museum entries 5–15 AZN.
- Tipping: 5–10% appreciated, occasionally added — check the bill.

### SIM and data

- Tourist SIM/eSIMs from **Azercell** (best coverage) or Bakcell at
  the airport or city stores — passport needed, ~20–35 AZN for
  generous data. International eSIMs work fine for a week.

### Getting around

- **Baku:** the metro (BakiKart, pennies per ride) covers the
  spread-out sights, and **Bolt is everywhere and absurdly cheap**
  (most rides 3–8 AZN). Install before you fly.
- **The day trips** (Gobustan + the fire sites) are easiest as one
  organised day (~45–55 AZN/person plus entries) or a private car
  (~USD 80–90) — the sights are scattered and taxis-in-waiting
  add up to the same money with more hassle.
- **Baku ⇄ Sheki:** a private transfer with sightseeing stops
  (~150–180 AZN per car, 6–7 h with stops) is the move this guide
  is built on — the road *is* a highlight. Budget alternatives: the
  comfortable **overnight train** (dep ~23:30, arr ~6:20; berths
  ~8–20 AZN) or hourly buses (~8.40 AZN, 6.5 h) — both skip the
  best stops.

### Travelling with kids?

- An easy country with children: short city distances, the mud
  volcanoes are a genuine kid-thriller (bubbling, plopping,
  faintly ridiculous), and Azerbaijanis dote on little ones.
- The flame-and-fire sights (Yanar Dag, Ateshgah) need standard
  fire-adjacent supervision; the Gobustan day is long — pack
  snacks and the carrier.
- Sheki's palace has a strict no-touching rule that tests
  toddlers; the fortress lawns outside forgive them.

## Day 1 — Land in Baku, first walk in the Old City

Stay in or beside **Icherisheher** (the walled Old City) or along
the Boulevard — everything central is walkable from either.

- **Afternoon** — Land at Heydar Aliyev airport (GYD; ~25 min to
  the centre by Bolt or the H1 Aerobus). Direct budget flights
  reach Baku in ~3 h from Abu Dhabi, Sharjah and Dubai. Check in,
  first tea.
- **17:00** — Enter **Icherisheher** through the Double Gates:
  caravanserais, carpet shops, cats with tenure. Orbit the
  **Maiden Tower** from outside as the stone goes gold.
- **19:00** — Dinner in the Old City — a tendir-bread courtyard
  restaurant: warm bread from the clay oven, saj plates, pomegranate
  everything (~40–60 AZN for two).
- **20:30** — Walk out to see the **Flame Towers lit up** — the
  LED fire show washes over the whole skyline every evening.

**Where to stay in Baku** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Fairmont Baku, Flame Towers** — inside the icons
  themselves; Caspian views from the clouds.
- *Mid-range:* **The Merchant Baku** — superbly rated boutique at
  the Old City's edge, steps from Nizami Street.
- *Budget:* **Austin Hotel** — cheap, central, breakfast included
  (and no lift — pack accordingly).

## Day 2 — Baku: medieval morning, Zaha Hadid afternoon

- **9:30** — **Palace of the Shirvanshahs** (~9:00–18:00; ~15 AZN)
  — the 15th-century royal complex at the Old City's crown:
  courtyards, the shah's mosque, carved portals.
- **11:00** — **Maiden Tower** (~15 AZN) — climb the enigmatic
  12th-century tower for rooftops-and-Caspian views; nobody agrees
  what it was for, which is half the charm.
- **12:30** — Lunch off **Nizami Street** — the pedestrian
  shopping spine; qutab (stuffed flatbreads) make the perfect
  quick plate.
- **14:30** — Bolt to the **Heydar Aliyev Center** (~11:00–19:00,
  check Mondays; ~15–25 AZN) — Zaha Hadid's wave of white, the
  building that rebranded Baku. The exterior lawns alone justify
  it; inside are galleries and the classic-car collection.
- **17:30** — **Funicular up to Highland Park** (runs ~10:00–22:00,
  pennies) — the panorama: Flame Towers overhead, crescent bay
  below, at golden hour.
- **19:30** — Descend to the **Baku Boulevard** — the Caspian
  seafront promenade — and dinner nearby; end at the **Mini-Venice**
  canals or the Ferris wheel if the kids are still going.

## Day 3 — Fire country: Gobustan, mud volcanoes and eternal flames

The signature Azerbaijan day — four sites, one loop south and
north of the city. Organised day tour ~45–55 AZN/person + entries,
or private car ~USD 80–90.

- **9:00** — Depart south along the Caspian.
- **10:00** — **Gobustan National Park** (9:00–17:00; ~10 AZN
  including the reserve guide) — 6,000+ petroglyphs up to 40,000
  years old: dancing figures, reed boats, aurochs. The modern
  museum first makes the rocks come alive.
- **12:00** — The **mud volcanoes** — 4x4s shuttle you from the
  paved road up to a lunar field of gurgling, cold, grey cones
  (small per-jeep fee, cash). Absurd, wonderful, and the kids'
  favourite thing in Azerbaijan. Don't wear white.
- **13:30** — Late lunch back toward the city.
- **15:00** — **Ateshgah Fire Temple**, Surakhani (~9:00–18:00,
  ~9 AZN) — the pentagonal temple where Zoroastrian and Hindu fire
  worshippers tended natural gas flames for centuries; the central
  altar still burns.
- **16:30** — **Yanar Dag, the Burning Mountain** (~9 AZN) — a
  hillside that has been on fire for decades, fed by seeping gas.
  Most dramatic as the light fades — time it last, stand close,
  feel the heat.
- **19:00** — Back in Baku; easy dinner. You have now seen why
  they call it the Land of Fire.

## Day 4 — Baku your way: bazaars, carpets, hammam, jazz

- **9:30** — **Taza Bazar** — the green market: pomegranate
  pyramids, saffron, mountains of herbs, cheese sellers who insist
  you taste.
- **11:00** — **Carpet Museum** (closed Mon; ~10 AZN) — the
  rolled-carpet building on the Boulevard; Azerbaijan's weaving
  tradition is UNESCO-listed and the collection shows why.
- **13:00** — Lunch: dushbara (tiny dumpling soup) or plov —
  Azerbaijan's saffron-crowned version.
- **15:00** — Choose your afternoon: a proper **hammam** (the
  historic Old City baths take walk-ins; scrub ~20–40 AZN), the
  **Museum of Miniature Books** (free, charming, ten minutes), or
  Boulevard idling with a **Baku Eye** spin.
- **19:30** — Dinner then **live jazz** — Baku's jazz pedigree is
  real (mugham-jazz fusion was born here); the clubs around
  downtown run sets most nights. Pack for the mountains; cash out
  at an ATM tonight.

## Day 5 — The mountain road to Sheki

The best road day in Azerbaijan — private transfer with stops
(~150–180 AZN/car, ~6–7 h door to door).

- **8:30** — Leave Baku west across the semi-desert.
- **10:00** — **Diri Baba Mausoleum**, Maraza — a two-storey
  15th-century shrine grown out of a cliff face; ten minutes of
  stairs, one great photograph.
- **11:00** — **Shamakhi Juma Mosque** — one of the Caucasus's
  oldest mosque sites (founded 743), serenely rebuilt; free,
  modest dress.
- **12:30** — Detour up the Girdimanchay gorge to **Lahij** — a
  cobbled mountain village that has hammered copper for centuries:
  workshops ringing, tinned pots gleaming, lunch in a family
  courtyard (~15–25 AZN for two). The 40-minute canyon road up is
  its own attraction.
- **16:00** — On through hazelnut country to **Sheki**; check in.
- **18:30** — First taste of **Sheki halva** — the town's
  saffron-and-hazelnut baklava-like signature, sold everywhere and
  argued about locally; then dinner of **piti**, the clay-pot
  saffron lamb stew Sheki does best (~20–30 AZN for two).

**Where to stay in Sheki** (two nights)

- *Comfort:* **Sheki Saray Hotel** — the town's main full-service
  hotel, walkable to everything.
- *Modern mid-range:* **Macara Sheki City Hotel** — quiet and
  well-reviewed near the centre.
- *The experience:* **Yukari Karvansaray** — sleep in a genuine
  18th-century Silk Road caravanserai's vaulted brick cells:
  basic, atmospheric, impossible to book online (call ahead or ask
  on arrival) — choose it for romance, not luxury.

## Day 6 — Sheki: the palace, the crafts, the church on the hill

- **9:30** — Walk up through the **fortress walls** to the
  **Khan's Summer Palace** (10:00–18:00; ~5 AZN, interior visits
  guided, **no photos inside**) — two storeys built without a
  single nail, every surface painted, and the famous **shebeke**
  windows: thousands of hand-cut glass pieces set in wood, no glue.
  Small, perfect, worth the whole drive.
- **11:00** — The **shebeke workshop** by the palace — watch the
  craft done, buy a small panel; then the **Winter Palace**
  (~5 AZN, photos allowed) for the quieter twin.
- **13:00** — Lunch in the bazaar quarter; tea at the
  **Karvansaray courtyard** even if you're not sleeping there.
- **15:00** — **Kish village** (15 min by taxi/marshrutka) — the
  round-towered **Albanian church**, over eight centuries old, in
  a walnut-shaded hamlet — the relic of Caucasian Albania,
  Azerbaijan's pre-Islamic Christian past.
- **17:30** — Golden hour on the fortress lawns; halva shopping
  for the flight home.
- **19:30** — Farewell dinner — piti round two, or saj by the
  fireplace; Sheki evenings are mountain-cool all year.

## Day 7 — Back to Baku, fly home

- **9:00** — Direct return drive (~4.5–5 h without stops; or the
  overnight-train option the night before for budget romantics).
- **15:00** — Reach Baku with buffer for an evening flight — Gulf
  departures run late enough to make this comfortable; if yours
  is early, swap this for a sixth Baku night and do Sheki's
  return the previous evening.

## What to pack

- Layers: Baku is windy year-round ("city of winds" is official),
  and Sheki runs ~8°C cooler than the coast.
- Shoes you can ruin-adjacent: mud-volcano fields and Lahij
  cobbles.
- Modest cover for mosques and Ateshgah; a scarf serves.
- Sun kit for Gobustan's shadeless rock — even in October.
- Cash drawn in Baku before the Sheki leg.
- Room for halva, saffron, and the copper pot you didn't plan to
  buy in Lahij.

## FAQ

**Is Azerbaijan safe?**
Baku is one of the safest-feeling capitals anywhere — heavily
walked at night, low street crime — and the Sheki road is
ordinary tourist country. The closed Armenian border region is
far from every stop in this guide and irrelevant to it.

**Which Caucasus country first — Georgia, Armenia or Azerbaijan?**
Any order works except Armenia→Azerbaijan directly (that border
is closed; route via Georgia). The elegant grand tour: this guide,
then cross from Sheki's region into Georgia — **the Lagodekhi
border is ~1.5 h from Sheki, and Sighnaghi, where our Georgia loop
ends, is ~3 h away** — then Georgia → Armenia. Three of our guides,
one overland line.

**Is Baku expensive?**
The skyline lies. Outside the five-star lobbies, Baku costs
Tbilisi-plus-a-little: 5 AZN museum tickets, 4 AZN Bolt rides,
50 AZN dinners for two with wine.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Halal is the default in this Muslim-majority (and firmly secular —
alcohol flows freely) country. Vegetarians manage well: qutab with
greens or pumpkin, dovga (herb yogurt soup), aubergine everything,
and the bread-and-cheese-and-herb table that starts every meal.

**Do people speak English?**
In Baku's tourist core, increasingly; elsewhere Azerbaijani and
Russian dominate. Translation apps and goodwill cover the gaps —
and Turkish speakers will understand nearly everything.

**Is one night enough for Sheki?**
Two is right, as planned: the palace-and-crafts day needs the full
day, and arriving via the Lahij road eats the first. Day-tripping
Sheki from Baku (10+ hours of driving) is the region's most
common regret.
