The Okavango is a river that flows inland and dies in the sand. Each
year the flood arrives from the Angolan highlands months after the
local rains have stopped, spreading across the Kalahari into a
15,000 km² maze of channels and islands — and the animals come to it.

That is the trip. **Mokoro** — a shallow dugout poled from the stern
— takes you through papyrus channels at water level, close enough to
hear frogs. **Moremi** is the reserve on the delta's eastern edge,
with floodplains and some of the best leopard density in Africa.
**Chobe** in the north has elephant herds in the thousands, and a
river cruise that puts you level with them at dusk.

You should also know the thing Botswana guides tend to skirt: **this
is a deliberately expensive country to visit.** That is policy, not
accident, and it is worth understanding before you plan.

## Before you go: the cost model, the season and the camps

### Entry

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for up to 90 days — the EU,
  UK, US, Canada, Australia, South Africa and many others. Indian
  passports currently need a visa; check your own case.
- **Passport valid six months** with at least two blank pages,
  which is enforced.
- **A tourism development levy** is payable on arrival by most
  visitors — a small fixed sum, usually payable by card.

### Why it costs what it costs

- Botswana runs a **"high value, low volume" tourism policy**:
  limited concessions, low visitor numbers, high prices. The
  result is game viewing without a queue of vehicles, and a bill
  that reflects it.
- **Most delta camps are all-inclusive** — accommodation, all meals,
  drinks, and two game activities a day. Compare camps on that total,
  not on a room rate.
- **Light aircraft transfers** between camps are normal and are a
  meaningful part of the cost. They also have strict **soft-bag-only
  luggage limits**, typically around 20 kg including hand luggage.
- **Cheaper routes exist**: mobile camping safaris, self-drive to
  Moremi and Chobe with a rooftop tent, and day trips from Maun.
  They are genuinely good and a fraction of the price.

### The season decides the trip

- **The flood is out of step with the rain, and this confuses
  people.** Local rains fall December to March; the Angolan
  floodwater arrives in the delta around **May to August**.
- **June to September is the peak and the best**: high water for
  mokoro, dry land elsewhere so animals concentrate at water, and
  no rain. Cold nights.
- **October** is the hottest month and superb for game, brutal by
  midday.
- **December to March** is green season — lower prices, newborn
  animals, dramatic skies, but thick bush, scattered game and some
  camps closed.
- **Mokoro needs water.** In low-water years the channels near Maun
  can be unusable; confirm with your camp rather than assume.

### Health and practicalities

- **This is a malaria area.** Take advice on prophylaxis well before
  you travel, and use repellent at dawn and dusk regardless.
- **The pula (BWP).** Cards work in Maun and Kasane; camps bill
  in advance. Carry cash for tips and markets.
- **Tipping is expected** at camps, for guides and for general staff
  separately, and the camp will explain the convention.
- English is an official language and universally used in tourism.

## Day 1 — Land in Maun

- **Afternoon** — Maun is the delta's gateway: a small town with an
  airport that is busier with light aircraft than airliners.
- **Evening** — Sleep in a lodge by the Thamalakane River. Confirm
  your luggage limit for tomorrow's flight.

## Day 2 — Into the delta

- **Morning** — A **light aircraft transfer** into a delta
  concession, 20 to 40 minutes. The view of the channels from 500
  feet is the first extraordinary thing.
- **Afternoon** — Settle, and an afternoon **game drive** or first
  mokoro.
- **Evening** — Dinner under the stars, and the sound of the delta
  at night, which is not quiet.

## Day 3 — Mokoro, and walking

- **Morning** — **Mokoro** through the channels, poled by a guide
  standing behind you, at the water's eye level. Reed frogs,
  jacanas, lilies, and the occasional very large animal.
- **Afternoon** — A **guided walk** on an island, which is the only
  way to understand the tracks and the smaller things.
- **Evening** — Night drive if the concession permits it, which is
  where you find the cats.

## Day 4 — A full delta day

- **Dawn** — Game drive. The early one is always the better one.
- **Midday** — Rest. The camp will insist and be right.
- **Afternoon** — Mokoro or boat, depending on water.
- **Evening** — Sundowners somewhere with a view.

## Day 5 — East to Moremi

- **Morning** — Transfer to **Moremi Game Reserve**, the delta's
  protected eastern edge, by air or road.
- **Afternoon** — **Khwai** area game drive: elephant, buffalo,
  wild dog if you are lucky, and very good leopard odds.
- **Evening** — Camp.

## Day 6 — Moremi, properly

- **All day** — Two long drives with a break in the heat.
  Moremi's mix of floodplain, mopane and island makes it the most
  varied game viewing in the country.

## Day 7 — North to Chobe

- **Morning** — Transfer north to **Kasane** and **Chobe National
  Park**, by air or the long road.
- **Afternoon** — The **Chobe River cruise** at sunset: elephant
  herds crossing to the islands, hippo, crocodile, and a great deal
  of birdlife, all from water level.
- **Evening** — Kasane.

## Day 8 — Chobe, and the option next door

- **Morning** — A game drive along the Chobe riverfront, which has
  one of the highest elephant densities in Africa.
- **Afternoon** — **Victoria Falls is 80 km away** across the
  Zimbabwe border, and a day trip is entirely normal from Kasane.
  See our [Zimbabwe guide](/itineraries/zimbabwe-8-days-guide/) if
  you would rather give it proper time.
- **Evening** — Last night.

## Day 9 — Fly home

- **Kasane airport** has connections to Johannesburg and
  Johannesburg has everything else. Maun is the alternative if your
  route runs that way.

## What to pack

- **Soft bags only**, and under the airline's limit — usually about
  20 kg including hand luggage. Hard suitcases do not fit light
  aircraft holds.
- **Neutral colours.** Not for fashion: bright colours and white
  show at distance, and blue and black attract tsetse flies.
- **Warm layers for dawn drives**, June to August, when it is close
  to freezing in an open vehicle at 6am.
- **Binoculars**, one pair each.
- **Malaria prophylaxis and strong repellent.**
- **Type D/G/M plugs** — Botswana uses several; bring a universal
  adapter.

## FAQ

**Is Botswana worth the price?**
If uncrowded game viewing matters to you, yes — the low-volume
policy is why you will not share a leopard sighting with eleven
vehicles. If budget matters more, Tanzania and Kenya deliver
excellent safaris for less.

**Can I do it cheaply?**
Yes, and properly: mobile camping safaris and self-drive with a
rooftop tent into Moremi and Chobe cost a fraction of fly-in camps
and see the same animals.

**Mokoro or game drive?**
Both, and they are not alternatives. Mokoro is the delta at water
level and close to small things; drives cover ground and find the
big ones.

**When is best?**
**June to September** — high floodwater for mokoro, dry land
elsewhere concentrating game at water, no rain. Cold at dawn and
worth it.
