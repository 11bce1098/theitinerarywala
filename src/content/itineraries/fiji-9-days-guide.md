Fiji has 333 islands and a reputation built mostly on resort
brochures, which undersells it. The country underneath is a
Melanesian one with a strong village culture, where a visitor who
does the right things is treated with a warmth that is not part of
the room rate.

The trip splits in two. The **Yasawa Islands** — a chain running
north-west, reached by one daily catamaran — give you the beaches,
the reefs and the manta rays. **Viti Levu**, the main island, gives
you the villages, the highlands and a coast road along the reef.

The thing that makes this simple is the **Yasawa Flyer**, a catamaran
that runs up the chain each morning and back each afternoon, with a
hop-on pass that lets you island-hop without chartering anything.

## Before you go: boats, seasons and the village rules

### Entry, and the season

- **Most nationalities get a free four-month visitor permit on
  arrival** — the EU, UK, US, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, India
  and many more. Bring proof of onward travel and accommodation.
- **Passport valid six months**, and a departure ticket.
- **May to October is the dry season**: less rain, lower humidity,
  and the reliable choice. **November to April** is wet and warm and
  carries **cyclone risk**, which peaks January to March.
- **July and August** are busiest with Australian and New Zealand
  school holidays; prices rise accordingly.
- The water is warm year-round. **Manta rays in the Yasawas are
  most reliable May to October.**

### The boat pass is the itinerary

- **The Yasawa Flyer** leaves Denarau (near Nadi) each morning,
  runs up the chain dropping and collecting at each island, and
  returns in the afternoon. It is the only scheduled way through the
  islands.
- **A hop-on pass** priced by number of days is far better value
  than point-to-point tickets if you are visiting more than one
  island.
- **Island transfers are by small boat from the catamaran**, and in
  swell that is a wet transfer. Pack accordingly.
- **Book accommodation and the boat together** where you can; many
  island properties bundle them, and the boat sells out in season.
- **Islands are small and have one or two places to stay.** There is
  no walking to a different restaurant. Choose the property
  carefully, because for two days it is your whole world.

### The village rules, which matter

- **Villages are private communities, not attractions.** You visit
  by invitation or with a guide, never by wandering in.
- **Bring a gift of kava root** (*sevusevu*) if you are visiting a
  village — your host or guide will arrange it — and present it to
  the chief. This is the correct, expected form.
- **Cover shoulders and knees** in villages, and wear a *sulu*
  (sarong) over swimwear. Both men and women.
- **Remove hats and sunglasses** in a village, and do not touch
  anyone's head.
- **Take your shoes off** before entering a house or *bure*.
- **Kava** is mildly sedative, tastes of muddy pepper, and refusing
  it politely is fine — clap once, accept, drink in one, clap three
  times.

### Money and getting around

- **The Fijian dollar (FJD).** Cards work at resorts and in Nadi;
  the islands are **largely cash**, and there are no ATMs on most of
  them.
- **Withdraw enough cash in Nadi for the entire island leg.** This
  is the most common practical mistake visitors make.
- **Viti Levu's coast road** is served by cheap local buses and
  inexpensive taxis; agree the fare first.
- Tipping is not traditional; a "Christmas fund" box at resorts is
  the usual form.
- English is an official language and universally spoken.

## Day 1 — Land in Nadi

- **Afternoon** — Land, and stay near **Nadi** or **Denarau**, since
  the catamaran leaves early.
- **Evening** — Draw cash for the islands. The Garden of the
  Sleeping Giant or the Sabeto mud pools fill an afternoon if you
  land early.

## Day 2 — North into the Yasawas

- **Morning** — The **Yasawa Flyer** north. The run takes most of
  the day depending how far you go; the view does the work.
- **Afternoon** — Arrive at your first island — the **Naviti** or
  **Waya** groups are the usual middle of the chain.
- **Evening** — Dinner at the property, which is where everyone
  eats, and a beach with no lights on it.

## Day 3 — Reef, and the first swim

- **Morning** — Snorkel straight off the beach. The coral here comes
  close in, and most properties will run a boat to a better site.
- **Afternoon** — A village visit with *sevusevu*, if your hosts
  arrange one.
- **Evening** — Kava, and singing, which is not staged.

## Day 4 — Further north

- **Morning** — The Flyer again, an hour or two up the chain toward
  the **Sawa-i-Lau** area.
- **Afternoon** — The **limestone caves**: you swim through a short
  underwater passage into a second chamber, which is the one people
  talk about afterwards.
- **Evening** — A different island, a similar rhythm.

## Day 5 — Manta rays, in season

- **Morning** — In the **Naviti** channel between May and October,
  boats take snorkellers to swim with **manta rays** as they feed in
  the current. It is the single best thing in the islands.
- **Afternoon** — Beach, hammock, book.
- **Evening** — Last island night.

## Day 6 — Back south, and across to the coast

- **Morning** — The Flyer back to Denarau, most of the day.
- **Afternoon** — Drive along the **Coral Coast**, about ninety
  minutes.
- **Evening** — A coast resort or guesthouse.

## Day 7 — The highlands and the river

- **Morning** — **Sigatoka River** by longboat or a highland village
  tour, which is the cultural half of the trip.
- **Afternoon** — The **Sigatoka Sand Dunes**, an archaeological
  site as much as a landscape.
- **Evening** — Back on the coast.

## Day 8 — Reef, or the garden island

- **Option A** — Snorkel or dive the **Coral Coast** reef, reachable
  from the beach at low tide.
- **Option B** — A day trip inland to **Navala**, the village of
  traditional thatched *bure*, with a guide.
- **Evening** — Drive back toward Nadi.

## Day 9 — Fly home

- Nadi airport is small and straightforward. Most long-haul
  departures are late evening or overnight, so you often get the
  last day.

## What to pack

- **Reef-safe sunscreen**, and more of it than you think.
- **Your own snorkel and mask** if you have them — rental sets on
  small islands are well used.
- **A sulu or sarong** each, for villages and for everything else.
- **Cash in Fijian dollars**, drawn in Nadi, for the island leg.
- **A dry bag.** Every island transfer is a wet boat.
- **Type I plugs** (Australian style), 240V.
- **Reef shoes** for coral-strewn shallows.

## FAQ

**Do I need a resort, or can I do this cheaply?**
Both exist on the same islands. Backpacker and family-run places
sit beside expensive resorts along the Yasawas, use the same boat
and snorkel the same reefs.

**Is the Yasawa Flyer really necessary?**
It is the practical way through the chain. Seaplanes and charters
exist and cost many times more. The pass is what makes island
hopping affordable here.

**When will I see manta rays?**
**May to October**, in the Naviti channel, and it is never
guaranteed — but in season the odds are good and the properties run
boats daily when they are around.

**Is it a good family trip?**
Very. Calm lagoons, short boat hops, and a culture that is
genuinely fond of children. The wet transfers are the only thing to
plan around with very young ones.
