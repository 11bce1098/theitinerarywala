Greece invented half the ideas this site runs on — journeys,
hospitality, writing things down — and the classic first trip
still earns its fame: marble gods above Athens, an island of
windmills and expensive sunbeds, and then Santorini, where a
drowned volcano's rim wears white villages like snow.

This guide does the famous triangle honestly: the **Acropolis
under its new ticket rules**, **Mykonos without the bill
shock**, **Santorini built around what's actually best** (the
caldera-rim hike, the Bronze-Age city, the wine) rather than
just the sunset queue — and, running through it, the **Naxos
swap box** for readers who'd trade the scene for space at half
the price.

**Go May–June or September–early October** — swimming-warm,
sane prices, soft light. July–August brings peak crowds, peak
caldera rates and the **meltemi** wind (see the ferry box).
April and late October are lovely and cheap with a
some-things-shut asterisk. All costs are in euros (EUR).

## Before you go: the admin

- **Visa:** Greece is Schengen — the UAE playbook (VFS
  Global, €90, EES biometrics live, 4–8 weeks of lead) is in
  our [France guide](/itineraries/france-9-days-guide/).
- **The Acropolis rules changed — current version:** entry
  is **€30 year-round** (the winter half-price is gone), on
  **mandatory hourly timed slots** booked at the official
  **hhticket.gr** — and the beloved **€30 multi-site combo
  ticket was discontinued in 2025**: the Agora, Olympieion
  and friends are now separate tickets. Free-entry days
  still exist (first Sundays Nov–Mar, a handful of national
  dates) and are gloriously crowded.
- **The ferry box (read before booking):** conventional
  ships (**Blue Star**, ~€43–53 Piraeus→Mykonos, 4h40) are
  steadier, cheaper, deck-access lovely; **high-speed cats**
  (SeaJets et al., ~€80–90, 2h40) are faster and bumpier —
  and in **July–August's meltemi wind**, it's the fast cats
  that cancel first. Book on Ferryhopper, arrive 45 min
  early, take the seasickness tablet *before* sailing, and
  never book a ferry-to-flight connection under 6 hours.
- Cards everywhere; carry €50 in small notes for kiosks,
  buses and chapel candles. Tipping: round up, ~5–10% for
  table service. Tap water: fine in Athens, **bottled on
  the islands** (Santorini's is desalinated — short
  showers are island courtesy).
- **Athens pickpocket honesty:** metro line 3
  (airport–Monastiraki) and Monastiraki square are the
  classic zones — zips forward, phones off café tables.

## Day 1 — Land in Athens, Plaka at dusk

- **Afternoon** — Land ATH, a hub with non-stops from most of
  Europe, the Gulf and beyond.
  Metro line 3 into town (€9), check in around
  Plaka/Syntagma/Monastiraki.
- **18:00** — First walk: **Plaka's** lanes into
  **Anafiotika** — a whitewashed Cycladic mini-village
  smuggled under the Acropolis cliff — then Monastiraki
  square's happy chaos.
- **20:00** — Rooftop dinner with the lit Parthenon
  floating above the city (Monastiraki's rooftops make
  this easy; ~€20–35 pp) — Greece begins properly.

**Where to stay in Athens** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Hotel Grande Bretagne** — 4.7/5 on
  TripAdvisor (~4,700 reviews), the 1874 Syntagma grande
  dame; the roof garden's Acropolis view is a landmark
  itself. ~€400+.
- *The value king:* **The Athens Gate Hotel** — 4.5/5
  (~5,300 reviews), TripAdvisor's #1 value: rooftop
  breakfast facing the Acropolis. ~€180–250.
- *Plaka charm:* **Plaka Hotel** — 4.4/5 (~4,400
  reviews), in the heart of the old quarter. ~€140–190.

## Day 2 — The Acropolis, done right

- **07:45** — At the gate for the **8:00 slot** (€30,
  timed, hhticket.gr — first two slots beat both heat
  and cruise groups): up past the Propylaia to the
  **Parthenon**, the Erechtheion's caryatids, and the
  city-to-sea view that explains 2,500 years of
  real-estate obsession. Two hours; marble is slippery —
  grippy soles.
- **10:30** — The **Acropolis Museum** (~€15–20): the
  originals — caryatids, frieze — over the glass-floor
  excavation; the top-floor Parthenon hall aligns with
  the temple through the window.
- **13:00** — Plaka taverna lunch (~€12–18 — gemista,
  moussaka, the works).
- **15:00** — **Ancient Agora** (€10 — now a separate
  ticket, see above): Socrates' actual workplace, the
  Temple of Hephaestus (the best-preserved of them all),
  the rebuilt Stoa's museum.
- **18:30** — Sunset option A: **Lycabettus Hill**
  (funicular or 20-min path) — all Athens pinking below.
  Option B (book ahead): the coast run to **Cape Sounion**
  — Poseidon's temple over the sea at sundown (~€40–60
  tours).
- **21:00** — Souvlaki counter dinner (~€4–6 a stick —
  the budget's best friend; chicken versions everywhere).

## Day 3 — Athens at street level

- **09:30** — **Central Market's** theatre (fish-hall
  hollering, olive pyramids), coffee in **Psyrri's**
  street-art lanes.
- **12:00** — The **National Archaeological Museum**
  (€12): the Mask of Agamemnon, the Antikythera
  mechanism — the ancient world's greatest hits under
  one roof.
- **15:00** — Choose: Athens Riviera tram to a swim
  (Edem/Vouliagmeni), or Kolonaki café-and-boutique
  hours, or the Panathenaic Stadium photo (€10 — the
  1896 Olympics' marble horseshoe).
- **19:30** — Last-mainland dinner in Psyrri (live
  rebetiko music if you're lucky, ~€18–30). Pack for
  the boats; big luggage rolls fine on ferries.

## Day 4 — Sail to Mykonos

- **07:30** — Metro to Piraeus; **Blue Star to Mykonos**
  (~€48, 4h40 of deck coffee and island-spotting — or
  the 2h40 SeaJets at ~€88 if time beats romance).
- **13:00** — Check in near **Mykonos Town (Chora)**;
  the maze of white lanes was literally designed to
  confuse pirates — getting lost is the attraction.
- **17:00** — The circuit: **Little Venice's**
  wave-splashed balconies → the **windmills** ridge →
  Paraportiani church's melted-sugar geometry.
- **19:45** — Sunset with the windmills, then dinner —
  **the Mykonos price honesty:** this is Greece's most
  expensive table (mains €20–40; the €5 gyros counters
  two lanes inland are the local counterweight).

**Where to stay in Mykonos** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Myconian Ambassador** — 4.7/5 (~2,100
  reviews), Relais & Châteaux polish over Platys
  Gialos. ~€350+.
- *The value star:* **Nissaki Boutique Hotel** — 4.9/5
  (~2,100 reviews), TripAdvisor's #1 value: infinity
  pool, beach-strip location. ~€200–300.
- *Sunset-boutique:* **Aeolos Resort** — 4.9/5 (~2,100
  reviews), three pools and an evening saxophonist,
  because Mykonos. ~€220–320.

## Day 5 — Delos in the morning, a beach for the afternoon

- **09:00** — Boat to **Delos** (~€22 return + €12
  entry, 30 min): the sacred island where Apollo was
  born — an entire ancient city open to the sky:
  the Terrace of the Lions, mosaic-floored villas, the
  amphitheatre. Three shadeless hours — hat, water,
  awe. One of Greece's great sites, criminally
  underexplained by its neighbour's nightlife.
- **13:30** — Beach afternoon, decoded: **Platys
  Gialos/Psarou** are the scene (**sunbeds €40–100+ a
  pair at the famous clubs — that's the fee for the
  postcard**); **Paraga/Paradise** are younger and
  looser; **Agios Sostis** (north) is the free,
  taverna-backed local's answer. Pick your Mykonos.
- **19:30** — Golden-hour lanes, dinner inland where
  the prices calm down; pack for the caldera.

## Day 6 — Ferry to Santorini, first gasp

- **10:00** — Fast ferry **Mykonos → Santorini**
  (~€70–90, 2–3 h; meltemi rules apply).
- **13:00** — **Athinios port honesty:** the arrival is
  a cliff-switchback scrum — pre-book the hotel
  transfer or grab the €2.50 bus; taxis (~€35) vanish
  fast.
- **14:30** — Check in, walk to the caldera rail —
  and there it is: a 300-metre cliff of villages
  hanging over a drowned volcano. The photo does not
  prepare you; nothing does.
- **17:30** — **Fira** by golden hour: the cathedral
  terrace, cliff-lane cafés — and the **cable car
  (€6)** down-up to the old port if you want the
  view from the water. (**The donkey rides: skip
  them** — animal-welfare groups have documented the
  problem for years; the cable car exists.)
- **20:00** — First caldera dinner (~€25–45 pp with
  the view; €12–18 two lanes back).

**Where to stay in Santorini** (four nights — the
honest maths)

- *The caldera splurge:* **Astra Suites, Imerovigli**
  — long the connoisseurs' benchmark: Skaros Rock
  views without Oia's crush. €400–700 in season.
- *Caldera view on a budget:* **Keti Hotel, Fira** —
  the famous little cliff-ledge cheat code.
  ~€180–280.
- *The value base:* **Kamari or Perissa** black-sand
  beach hotels (€90–150) — sleep by the sea, visit
  the caldera daily by €1.80 bus. 
- *The hack:* two caldera nights + two beach nights
  = the photos *and* the budget.

## Day 7 — The caldera hike, Oia, and the sunset truth

- **08:00** — **The Fira → Oia caldera-rim hike**:
  10 km, 3–4 hours, the entire volcano at your left
  shoulder through Firostefani, Imerovigli and the
  empty middle miles. Start early (no shade), carry
  water — this is the single best thing on the
  island, and it's free.
- **12:30** — **Oia**: blue domes, marble lanes,
  bookshop-and-gallery browsing before the coaches
  peak.
- **14:00** — Descend the **300 steps to Ammoudi
  Bay**: fried tomato balls and grilled octopus at
  the water's edge (~€20–30); the brave swim off the
  rocks to the islet.
- **17:30** — **The sunset truth:** Oia's castle
  viewpoint at sunset is a stadium crush (claim a
  spot 90+ minutes early, then defend it). The
  connoisseur alternatives: **watch from Ammoudi
  below, from Imerovigli's Skaros Rock, or from your
  own hotel rail** — same sun, no scrum. Choose
  once, without FOMO.
- **20:30** — Bus/taxi back; late gyros counts as
  self-care.

## Day 8 — The Bronze Age and the vineyards

- **09:00** — **Akrotiri** (€12): the "Pompeii of the
  Aegean" — a 3,600-year-old city preserved by the
  very eruption that made the caldera, with
  three-storey houses and frescoed rooms under a
  cool roof. Add the **Red Beach viewpoint** next
  door (look, don't linger under the cliff —
  landslides are real).
- **13:00** — Lunch in **Pyrgos** village (the
  locals' hilltop favourite; castle-top view free).
- **15:00** — **Wine afternoon**: Santorini's vines
  grow in ground-hugging baskets against the wind —
  taste **assyrtiko** and sweet **vinsanto** at
  Santo Wines or Venetsanos (~€25–40 pp, caldera
  terrace included).
- **18:30** — **Skaros Rock, Imerovigli** for the
  free sunset the Oia crowd never finds.
- **20:30** — The dinner you dressed for (~€35–60
  pp on the rim) — or tomato fritters and fava at a
  Pyrgos taverna for a third of it.

## Day 9 — Black sand, blue water (or the catamaran)

- **Option A — the beach day:** **Perissa/Perivolos**
  black sand (loungers ~€10–20 — island-normal
  prices return), lunch barefoot, Kamari promenade
  stroll.
- **Option B — the splurge that earns it:** a
  **caldera catamaran half-day** (~€120–150 pp,
  small-group: hot springs, Red/White beach swims,
  BBQ and drinks aboard, sunset from the water) —
  consistently the trip's five-star memory for
  couples.
- **19:30** — Farewell dinner on the rim; the lights
  of Fira spill down the cliff like the lava never
  quite did.

## Day 10 — Home from JTR

- **Morning** — Santorini's little airport flies to
  Athens (45 min, €60–120) and, in season, direct to
  Europe's hubs — connect home from either. Last
  look from the plane window: the caldera draws its
  own map applause.

## What to pack

- Grippy sandals/trainers — marble (Acropolis) and
  caldera cobbles both polish slick.
- Sun kit at Aegean strength: hat, SPF50, refillable
  bottle; there is no shade on Delos or the rim hike.
- A light layer for ferry decks and meltemi
  evenings — the wind is real even in August.
- Swimwear ×2, snorkel mask for Ammoudi's rocks,
  reef-safe sunscreen.
- Modest cover for monasteries and churches
  (shoulders/knees; a scarf solves it).
- The seasickness tablets — before boarding, not
  after; motion-sensitive travellers pick Blue Star
  over the cats.

## FAQ

**Two islands in 10 days — not three?**
Two, firmly. Every island change costs half a day of
port logistics, and Santorini rewards the four
nights this plan gives it. Three islands in ten days
is a ferry timetable with occasional Greece
attached.

**Mykonos or the Naxos swap — really?**
Mykonos is a scene: gorgeous town, glamour, prices
to match — and Delos next door is a genuine wonder.
Naxos is an island: mountains, Venetian lanes,
kilometre beaches, half the bill. First-timers
chasing the classic go Mykonos (this guide);
returners and families take the swap and rarely
look back. There's no wrong answer, only an honest
menu.

**Is a caldera-view room worth €400?**
For one or two nights, almost everyone says yes —
coffee on your own rail over the volcano is the
island's core memory. For four nights, the
two-plus-two hack (rim + beach base) buys the same
photos and a €500 saving. Imerovigli gives Oia's
view without Oia's crush; that's the quiet pro
move.

**When exactly — and how bad is August?**
Late May–June and September are the answer:
28–30°C, swimmable seas, bookable tables. August
works if you've booked by March and enjoy company:
peak prices, the meltemi's ferry roulette, and
sunset crowds at football-match density.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Vegetarians eat like philosophers: gemista, fava,
briam, greek salad, spanakopita, tomato fritters —
half the taverna menu is meat-free by tradition.
Halal is thinner: Athens has a proper halal scene
(around Omonia/Metaxourgeio), the islands very
little — the working strategy is seafood
(everywhere, excellent) plus the veg canon, and
chicken gyros where the counter confirms sourcing.

**What's Greece guide #2?**
**Crete** (Knossos, Chania, the beach trinity) and
the **mainland icons** (Delphi + Meteora's sky
monasteries) are neck and neck — with the
**Naxos–Paros slow loop** as the wildcard the
comments keep requesting.
