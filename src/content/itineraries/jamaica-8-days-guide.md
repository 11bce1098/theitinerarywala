Most visitors to Jamaica see a strip of Montego Bay or Negril
from inside a wristband, and leave having met nobody. That is a
perfectly good holiday and it is not this one.

This route runs the other way round the island: **Kingston**
first, because the music that Jamaica exported to the world came
out of a specific few streets there and you can stand in them;
then up into the **Blue Mountains**, where the coffee grows at
1,200 m and the air is suddenly cold; then **Port Antonio** on the
north-east coast, which is the lushest and least developed part of
the island; and finally **Treasure Beach** on the arid south,
which looks and feels like a different country.

It is harder work than a resort. It is also the only version where
you come home with a sense of the place.

## Before you go: driving, Kingston and the seasons

### Entry and money

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for tourism. You must
  complete the **online immigration/customs form** before arrival —
  do it the day before, not at the airport.
- **Jamaican dollar (JMD)**, and **US dollars are widely accepted**
  at a rate that is rarely in your favour. Pay in JMD where you
  can.
- Cards work in hotels and larger restaurants. **Jerk stands,
  route taxis and markets are cash.**

### Getting around, honestly

- **Hiring a car gives you this itinerary.** Driving is **on the
  left**, the main roads are decent, and the **north coast highway
  is fast and tolled**. Minor roads are potholed and unlit, and
  **nobody should drive them after dark.**
- **Jamaican driving is assertive.** Overtaking happens where you
  would not expect it. Drive defensively and let people past.
- **Route taxis** — shared, marked with red plates — are how most
  Jamaicans travel and cost very little.
- **A driver for the week** is the alternative and is common. It
  costs more than a hire car and removes every navigational
  problem.
- **Avoid unlit rural roads at night**, and do not take shortcuts
  suggested by a map app in Kingston.

### Kingston, straight

- Kingston is a working capital of a million people with
  **genuinely dangerous neighbourhoods and entirely ordinary
  ones**. **New Kingston**, where you will stay, is the latter.
- **Trench Town and the downtown music sites are visited with a
  guide**, from an organised tour or a driver who knows them. That
  is not nervousness — it is how these visits are arranged, and
  the guides are often from those communities.
- Do not wander downtown after dark, do not display valuables, and
  the rest is a normal city.

### When to go

- **Mid-December to April is the dry season** and the peak — best
  weather, highest prices.
- **Late April, May and early June** are the sweet spot: dry
  enough, hot, and much cheaper.
- **June to November is hurricane season**, with **August to
  October the peak**. Rain comes in heavy bursts rather than all
  day.
- **Blue Mountain mornings are cold** — single digits at the peak
  before dawn — in any season.

## Day 1 — Land in Kingston

- **14:00** — Land at **Norman Manley (KIN)** on the Palisadoes
  spit, 20 minutes from New Kingston. **Fly into Kingston, not
  Montego Bay** — this itinerary runs the opposite way to the
  resort traffic.
- **15:30** — Drop bags in **New Kingston**.
- **17:00** — **Devon House**, a 19th-century mansion built by
  **Jamaica's first Black millionaire**, George Stiebel, who made
  it in Venezuelan gold. The grounds are a public park and the
  **ice cream** is a national institution — the queue is the
  review.
- **19:00** — Dinner. ***Jerk*** properly: pork or chicken rubbed
  with scotch bonnet and pimento, smoked slowly over pimento wood,
  chopped with a cleaver and served on paper with festival.
- **21:00** — Confirm tomorrow's guide for downtown.

## Day 2 — Trench Town, and the music

- **09:00** — The **Bob Marley Museum** at 56 Hope Road, his home
  and studio from 1975 until his death. The bullet holes from the
  1976 assassination attempt are still in the wall, deliberately
  left. The tour is guided, no photographs inside, and the last
  room is a cinema.
- **11:00** — **Trench Town Culture Yard**, downtown, with a
  guide. This is the government housing scheme where Marley lived
  as a teenager, where the Wailers formed, and where the term
  *trench town rock* comes from. It is a working community and the
  guides live there.
- **13:00** — Lunch downtown.
- **14:30** — **Tuff Gong** studio tour if it is running — still a
  working pressing plant and studio — or the **National Gallery**,
  which is small and excellent on Jamaican art.
- **16:30** — **Emancipation Park** and the Redemption Song
  statue.
- **19:30** — Dinner, and live music if it is a weekend. Kingston
  is where this all comes from and it is still happening.

## Day 3 — Up into the Blue Mountains

- **08:30** — Drive up. It is **only 25 km but takes two hours**:
  single-track switchbacks, blind bends, and a drop on one side
  with no barrier. Sound the horn on corners, which everyone does.
- **10:30** — **Craighton Estate** or **Clifton Mount** for a
  coffee tour. **Blue Mountain coffee** grows between 900 and
  1,700 m, is picked by hand, and about 80% of it goes to Japan.
  The tour walks the slopes and ends with a cupping.
- **13:00** — Lunch at **Strawberry Hill** or a guesthouse, with
  the whole of Kingston and the sea laid out two thousand metres
  below.
- **15:00** — Walk a forest trail — the mountains hold endemic
  birds, giant swallowtail butterflies and tree ferns, and it is
  **ten degrees cooler** than the coast.
- **17:00** — Check in at a mountain guesthouse.
- **18:30** — Dinner, early.
- **Optional, 01:00** — The **Blue Mountain Peak** hike: 7 miles
  up in the dark to 2,256 m for sunrise, and on a clear morning
  you can see Cuba. It is cold, steep and long, and it takes the
  night out of tomorrow.

## Day 4 — Over to Port Antonio

- **08:30** — Down the other side and east to **Port Antonio**,
  about three hours through the forest road or round the coast.
- **12:00** — Arrive. This was the island's first tourist town —
  Errol Flynn ran aground here in 1946 and stayed — and it has
  been quietly forgotten by the industry since, which is exactly
  its appeal.
- **13:00** — Lunch at the Boston Bay end.
- **14:00** — **Boston Bay** is **where jerk comes from.** The
  stands along the roadside here are the original and the pimento
  wood is cut locally. Eat again if you have room.
- **16:00** — **Frenchman's Cove**, where a freshwater river meets
  the sea on a small beach, so you swim between cold and warm
  water in the same place.
- **18:00** — The **Blue Lagoon** at dusk — 60 m deep, fed by
  underground springs, and the colour genuinely does shift through
  the day from turquoise to a deep indigo.
- **20:00** — Dinner in town.

## Day 5 — Rafting, and the falls

- **08:30** — **Rafting the Rio Grande** on a **bamboo punt** —
  thirty feet of lashed bamboo, poled by a captain standing at the
  front, drifting three hours down the river between forested
  hills. It was started by Errol Flynn, who raced them for bets,
  and the raft captains have been a licensed guild ever since.
- **12:00** — Lunch at the landing.
- **14:00** — **Reach Falls**, inland and far quieter than Dunn's
  River on the north coast: a series of pools under forest, with a
  guide who will take you up the riverbed through the falls
  themselves and into the underwater cave if you want it.
- **17:00** — Back to Port Antonio.
- **19:00** — Dinner. **Winnifred Beach** if you want one more
  swim — it is community-run, free, and the one the locals use.

## Day 6 — Across to the south coast

- **08:00** — The long leg: Port Antonio to **Treasure Beach**,
  four and a half to five hours, right across the island.
- **11:00** — Break it at **Bath Fountain**, a hot mineral spring
  in the south-east, or push on.
- **13:30** — Lunch on the way.
- **15:00** — Arrive **Treasure Beach**, and it is a different
  country: the south coast is **in the rain shadow and semi-arid**,
  with cactus, fishing boats, black sand and almost no hotels. The
  area runs a **community tourism model** — guesthouses rather than
  resorts, and a trust that puts a share back into the villages.
- **17:30** — Sunset on **Frenchman's Bay** or **Calabash Bay**.
- **19:30** — Dinner at a guesthouse, cooked to order because
  everything is.

## Day 7 — Pelican Bar, and the Black River

- **09:00** — The **Black River Safari** — a boat up Jamaica's
  longest river through mangrove, with **American crocodiles**
  basking on the banks, which the boatmen know individually and
  by name.
- **12:00** — **YS Falls** inland, a seven-tier cascade on a
  working estate with rope swings and a natural pool, and far
  fewer people than the north-coast equivalent.
- **15:00** — ***Floyd's Pelican Bar***, and leave time for it: a
  **shack built on stilts on a sandbar a kilometre out to sea**,
  reached only by boat, serving beer and fried fish to whoever
  turns up. You stand in waist-deep Caribbean around it. There is
  nothing else like it anywhere.
- **18:00** — Back ashore for sunset.
- **19:30** — Last dinner.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Treasure Beach to **Montego Bay (MBJ)** is about
  two hours and to **Kingston (KIN)** about three and a half.
  **Book your outbound from Montego Bay** if you follow this route
  — it saves a long drive back across the island.
- **Last stop** — Blue Mountain coffee, bought at the estate
  rather than the airport, and a bottle of overproof white rum
  that you will regret opening.
- **Note** — There is a **departure tax**, usually in the ticket.
  Check yours.

## What to pack

- **Reef-safe sunscreen** and more than you expect.
- **A warm layer and long trousers** for the Blue Mountains, which
  genuinely get cold at night and freezing at the peak.
- **Water shoes** for the falls and the river rocks.
- **A dry bag** for the raft and the Pelican Bar boat.
- **Repellent** — mosquitoes on the north-east coast at dusk are
  serious.
- **UK-style Type A/B plugs**, 110V — American style, not British,
  despite the driving.
- **Small JMD notes.** Nobody at a jerk stand has change for a
  five-thousand.

## FAQ

**Is Kingston safe to visit?**
New Kingston, Devon House, the museums and the uptown restaurants
are ordinary. **Downtown and Trench Town are visited with a
guide**, which is how it is done rather than a precaution being
suggested here — the tours are run by people from those
communities. Do not drive yourself around downtown at night. With
that, Kingston is the most interesting part of this trip.

**Can I do this without a car?**
Partly. Route taxis and buses link the towns cheaply, but
Treasure Beach, Reach Falls and the Blue Mountains all need
private transport at the last stage. A driver for the week is the
alternative and costs roughly double the car.

**Is it worth skipping Negril and Montego Bay?**
This itinerary thinks so. They have the famous beaches and almost
all of the resorts; **Port Antonio and Treasure Beach have the
island**. If you want two days of Seven Mile Beach, add them to
the end and fly out of Montego Bay.

**When is the weather best?**
**Mid-December to April** is driest and most expensive. **May and
early June** are nearly as good and much cheaper. Hurricane risk
peaks August to October.

**Is the Blue Mountain Peak hike worth losing a night over?**
If you like walking in the dark for five hours to be cold at
sunrise, yes, genuinely — and on a clear morning Cuba is visible.
If not, the coffee estates at 1,200 m give you the mountains
without the 1 a.m. start.
