Thailand is the easiest great trip in Asia — that's not faint
praise, it's the engineering. Well connected from almost
anywhere, visa-free for most tourist passports, US$1 street food that
outclasses most restaurants, and an internal flight network that
turns a three-Thailand itinerary — capital, mountains, islands —
into three easy hops.

Bangkok is sensory theatre: golden spires, boat-crossed rivers,
Michelin-starred woks on plastic stools. Chiang Mai is the calm
north: 300 temples inside a moated old town, and the best place
on earth to meet elephants without harming them. Phuket is the
Andaman playground — and the launchpad for the Phi Phi islands'
absurd turquoise.

**Go November–February** (the cool, dry, glorious season —
book ahead, everyone knows). March–May is hot; **February–April
is smoky-season in Chiang Mai** (crop burning — AQI apps don't
lie; more below); May–October is the Andaman monsoon: green,
cheap, with rough seas some days and boat cancellations
possible. All costs are in Thai baht (THB).

## Before you go: the visa just changed — read this first

- **The allowances changed on 15 September 2026, so check
  yours.** Thailand admits most tourist nationalities without a
  visa; **Indian passports now get 30 days**, down from the
  60-day exemption of 2024–2026, and several others were
  shortened at the same time. Still free, still no paperwork
  beyond the arrival card, and 30 days swallows this trip whole.
  Need longer? A **30-day extension costs ~฿1,900** at any
  immigration office.
- **The TDAC (Thailand Digital Arrival Card) is mandatory for
  everyone** — a free online form (passport, flight,
  first-night address) completed **within 3 days before
  departure**; the QR code is scanned at immigration and
  airlines can ask for it at check-in. Use only the official
  tdac.immigration.go.th — paid "agents" for this free form
  are the trip's first scam.
- Passport valid 6+ months; an onward/return ticket isn't
  strictly mandatory but immigration may ask — carry the
  booking anyway. The exemption follows your passport, not where
  you live.
- Frequent Thailand-hoppers note: back-to-back visa-exempt
  entries now attract scrutiny — a tourist pattern is fine, a
  living-there pattern isn't.

## Money, scams and the respect rules

- **Cash still rules street Thailand**: cards work in malls
  and hotels, but stalls, songthaews, boats and massage shops
  are cash-first — carry **฿2,000–3,000** in small notes.
  Yellow-and-green **7-Elevens** (every 200 metres, a
  national institution) top up everything.
- Tipping: not expected; rounding up or ฿20–50 for good
  service is warm. Massage therapists: ฿50–100 is kind.
- **The scam playlist, pre-heard:** anyone at the Grand
  Palace saying "**it's closed today**" is steering you to a
  gem shop — walk on, it's open. Tuk-tuk "฿20 city tours"
  end at tailor shops. Meter-refusing taxis are solved by
  **Grab/Bolt** (Thailand's Ubers — use them everywhere).
  Phuket's jet-ski damage scam is dodged by not renting jet
  skis. That's 95% of Thai crime against tourists: theatre,
  not violence.
- **The respect rules are real law:** Thailand reveres its
  monarchy — never joke about or disrespect the royal family
  (lèse-majesté carries prison); stand for the royal anthem
  (cinemas, 08:00/18:00 in parks). Temples: shoulders and
  knees covered, shoes off, never point feet at Buddhas,
  women don't touch monks. Buddha-image souvenirs are
  technically restricted — buy amulets, not statues.
- eSIM ~US$8–15 or airport SIMs; Grab, Google Maps and
  Translate cover the rest. Tap water no; ice in
  establishments is factory-made and fine.

## Day 1 — Land in Bangkok, eat in Chinatown

Stay on the **riverside** (ferries, sunsets, old Bangkok) or
along the **BTS Skytrain** (Sukhumvit/Siam — fastest city
logistics).

- **Afternoon** — Land BKK, a hub with non-stops from most of
  the world. Rail
  Link or Grab in (~฿350–500 by car, 45–60 min).
- **18:30** — Straight into the deep end: **Yaowarat
  (Chinatown)** after dark — neon Cantonese signs, wok fire,
  the ฿60–150 stalls with Michelin Bib stickers. Eat in
  sequence: guay jub pepper soup, mango sticky rice, kanom
  buang crêpes. This is the best first evening in Asia.

**Where to stay in Bangkok** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Okura Prestige** — 4.8/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~5,800 reviews), Japanese-precision tower with a
  cantilevered infinity pool, BTS-linked. ~฿6,500–9,000.
- *Mid-range:* **Eastin Grand Hotel Phayathai** — 4.9/5
  (~1,500 reviews), TripAdvisor's #1 value in the city,
  direct BTS access, skyline pool. ~฿3,000–4,500.
- *Budget/old-town:* **Rambuttri Village Inn** — the Khao
  San district's long-serving courtyard cheapie with a
  rooftop pool; simple, social, ~฿800–1,200.

## Day 2 — The golden day: palace, reclining Buddha, dawn temple

Dress for temples all day (covered shoulders/knees — light
cotton, not shorts-plus-scarf improvisation).

- **08:00** — **The Grand Palace & Wat Phra Kaew** at opening
  (**฿500**, kids under 120 cm free): the Emerald Buddha in
  its gilded cosmos, the mosaic guardians, the murals. Two
  hours before the heat and coaches. (Rehearse the scam
  response: it is not closed.)
- **10:30** — Walk 10 minutes to **Wat Pho** (**฿300**,
  includes a water bottle): the 46-metre gold **Reclining
  Buddha**, mother-of-pearl feet, and the courtyard stupas.
  This is also Thai massage's birthplace — the on-site
  school charges ~฿320/30 min for the real curriculum.
- **12:30** — Riverside lunch at Tha Tien pier stalls
  (~฿80–150).
- **13:30** — Cross-river ferry (฿5!) to **Wat Arun**
  (**฿200**) — the porcelain-encrusted Temple of Dawn;
  climb the steep central prang terraces.
- **15:30** — Long-tail or Chao Phraya Express back; ice
  coffee; optional **Jim Thompson House** (฿250 — the silk
  king's teak mansion and Thailand's best small museum).
- **18:30** — Golden hour: **Wat Arun viewed from across the
  river** at a Tha Tien rooftop (the photo), dinner around
  Khao San/Rambuttri's calmer lanes or a proper riverside
  restaurant (~฿200–450).

## Day 3 — Markets and choices

Pick by day-of-week and appetite:

- **Weekend?** — **Chatuchak Market** (free): 15,000 stalls,
  planet-scale — go at 09:00, exit by heat-stroke o'clock
  with coconut ice cream inside you.
- **The rails-and-boats day:** the **Maeklong Railway
  Market** (stalls fold back for the train, eight times
  daily) + **Damnoen Saduak floating market** combo tours
  (~฿1,300–1,800 pp, morning) — touristy, undeniably fun
  with kids.
- **The history day:** DIY train to **Ayutthaya** (from
  ~฿20–50!): the ruined 14th-century capital by bicycle —
  Buddha head in the banyan roots at Wat Mahathat, ฿50 per
  major temple.
- **19:00** — Back for the modern-Bangkok evening:
  **ICONSIAM's** food floor and light fountain, or **muay
  thai** at Rajadamnern (from ~฿1,500–2,500 — the real
  thing, electric even for non-fans).

## Day 4 — Fly north, temple dusk in Chiang Mai

- **09:30** — BKK → **Chiang Mai** (1h15; Thai/AirAsia/Nok
  from ~฿1,200–1,800 with a bag — book ahead, fares crawl
  upward).
- **12:30** — Check into the **old city** (the moated square
  mile) or riverside; lunch = your first **khao soi**
  (curried noodle soup with crispy nest — ฿60–90 —
  the north's dish and possibly Thailand's best).
- **15:00** — Old-city temple walk: **Wat Phra Singh's**
  gilded Lanna hall → **Wat Chedi Luang** — the massive
  ruined chedi that once held the Emerald Buddha; stay for
  the 17:00-ish **monk chat/evening chanting** (free;
  genuinely moving).
- **19:00** — **North Gate (Chang Phueak) night market**:
  the cowboy-hat khao kha moo (braised pork leg) queue is
  famous; halal and veg carts share the block (~฿50–120
  dinner).

**Where to stay in Chiang Mai** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Na Nirand Romantic Boutique Resort** — 4.9/5
  (~1,800 reviews), Lanna teak colonial under a riverside
  rain tree. ~฿4,500–6,500.
- *Mid-range:* **99 The Heritage Hotel** — 4.8/5, colonial
  courtyard calm inside the old city. ~฿1,800–2,800.
- *Budget:* the old city's guesthouse economy is Thailand's
  best — spotless family-run rooms at ฿600–1,000; pick by
  current reviews and stay inside the moat.

## Day 5 — The elephants, done right

- **07:30** — Pickup for **Elephant Nature Park** — the
  sanctuary that rewrote Asia's elephant tourism: **฿2,500
  per person** for the day visit (transport from the city
  and a huge vegetarian buffet included; **book weeks ahead
  — it's non-refundable and sells out**). You'll walk with
  rescued herds, feed the old ladies watermelon, and hear
  each elephant's logging-or-trekking-camp backstory.
- **The ethics box, plainly:** if a camp offers **riding,
  shows, or tricks — it's exploitation**, full stop
  (breaking an elephant to carry humans is exactly what it
  sounds like). Even bathing-with-elephants programmes have
  been phased out by the best sanctuaries as stressful.
  Watching giants be giants is the premium experience —
  this is the one booking on the site we'll be preachy
  about.
- **17:30** — Back in town, spectacularly happy. Massage
  (฿300–450/hour — the Women's Correctional Institution
  Vocational Centre's programme is famous and superb),
  dinner in a riverside garden (~฿150–350).

## Day 6 — The mountain temple, then knives out (cooking class)

- **07:30** — Red **songthaew** (shared truck-taxi, ~฿50–80
  each way from the zoo stop) winding up to **Wat Phra That
  Doi Suthep** (entry **~฿50**): 306 naga-flanked steps to
  the gold chedi with the whole valley below — mornings
  beat both haze and crowds. Optional add-on: Bhubing
  Palace gardens or a Hmong village further up.
- **12:30** — Back down for Nimman's café district — the
  north grows Thailand's coffee, and it shows (~฿70–120 a
  cup, worth it).
- **15:00** — **Thai cooking class** (~฿1,200–1,500,
  4 hours with a market tour): you will make khao soi and
  pad thai better than your hotel's, and the recipe booklet
  is the trip's most-used souvenir. (Sunday instead? The
  **Sunday Walking Street** market swallows the old city
  whole from 16:00 — do that.)
- **20:00** — Night Bazaar wander or rooftop farewell to
  the north.

## Day 7 — Fly to the island

- **10:00** — CNX → **Phuket** direct (~2 h; AirAsia/Thai
  from ~฿1,900–2,600 with a bag).
- **13:00** — Check in at **Kata or Karon** (this guide's
  base: real beaches, family-calm, 20 minutes from the
  circus). Patong is the nightlife capital — visit by
  choice, sleep elsewhere.
- **15:00** — Beach afternoon: Kata's surf-school waves
  (seasonal), Karon's three white kilometres. **Flag rule
  is law:** red flag = no swimming, monsoon rips are
  Phuket's real danger, not sharks.
- **18:30** — **Karon Viewpoint** for the three-bays sunset
  shot, then grilled seafood dinner — Muslim-owned
  beachfront grills around Kata/Rawai are both excellent
  and halal (~฿300–600 for two, pick your snapper by
  weight).

**Where to stay in Phuket** (three nights)

- *Beachfront luxury:* **Phuket Marriott Resort & Spa,
  Merlin Beach** — 4.7/5 (~8,000 reviews), private-bay
  seclusion with three pools. ~฿6,000–9,000.
- *Mid-range:* **Chanalai Flora Resort** — 4.9/5 (~4,000
  reviews), pools-and-views hillside steps from Kata
  Beach. ~฿2,500–4,000.
- *Value:* **Hotel Clover Patong** — 4.9/5 (~3,200
  reviews), TripAdvisor's #1 value on the island, rooftop
  pool (choose it if Patong's buzz is a feature for you).
  ~฿1,500–2,500.

## Day 8 — Phi Phi: the turquoise day

- **07:30** — Speedboat day tour to the **Phi Phi
  islands** (**~฿2,000–2,600 pp** including hotel pickup,
  buffet lunch and national-park fees — verify the park
  fee's inclusion when booking): **Maya Bay** (The Beach's
  bay — viewing platforms and shallows only, **no
  swimming**, and it typically **closes August–September**
  for reef recovery), the cliff-walled **Pileh Lagoon**
  swim, Viking Cave, snorkelling over parrotfish at Khai
  or Bamboo Island.
- **The honesty:** it's a conveyor-belt of longtails and
  speedboats because it's *that* beautiful — book the
  earliest departure, and choose operators capping ~30
  guests. Monkey Beach's macaques bite; feed nothing.
- **17:00** — Back; recovery massage (฿400ish); night
  market dinner (Chillva or Karon Temple market on its
  nights).

## Day 9 — Big Buddha, Old Town, last beach

- **09:00** — **The Big Buddha** (free; 45 m of white
  marble on the ridge, island-wide views; dress modestly)
  and **Wat Chalong**, Phuket's grandest temple (free,
  donations).
- **11:30** — **Phuket Old Town**: Sino-Portuguese
  shophouses in crayon colours along Thalang and Soi
  Romanee, third-generation Hokkien coffee shops, the ฿80
  local lunch (try Hokkien mee or roti at a Muslim
  canteen — the Old Town's halal heritage runs deep).
- **15:00** — Final beach hours; parents alternate massage
  shifts; kids build the last sandcastle.
- **19:00** — Farewell dinner: Rawai seafood grills or a
  Kata cliffside table (~฿400–900 for two). (Bangla Road,
  for the curious: one loud neon street in Patong —
  adults-only energy; families lose nothing by skipping.)

## Day 10 — Home, directly

- **Morning** — HKT flies **non-stop to several long-haul hubs**
  (Emirates, Etihad, flydubai) — the open-jaw (into
  Bangkok, out of Phuket) that makes this triangle
  effortless. Last mango sticky rice at the gate.

## What to pack

- Temple kit: covered-shoulder layers, slip-on footwear
  (you'll de-shoe ten times a day), a sarong solves
  everything.
- Reef-safe sunscreen (the Andaman's reefs need it),
  after-sun, and a rash vest for snorkel day.
- A dry bag for boats + waterproof phone pouch.
- Mosquito repellent (dusk, everywhere), rehydration
  salts, and the stomach kit you'll probably never open —
  street food from busy stalls has better turnover than
  hotel buffets.
- Light rain shell (any month can shower); one warmer
  layer for over-air-conditioned flights and malls.
- Room in the bag for the return: elephant-pants gravity
  affects everyone eventually.

## FAQ

**The visa change — does 30 days affect this trip?**
Not this one: 10 days sits comfortably inside the new
30-day exemption (in force **15 September 2026**; it was
60 days before). Just don't skip the **TDAC** — filled in
the 72 hours before departure, free, official site only —
airlines increasingly check it at the counter.

**Is Thailand safe? What actually goes wrong?**
Violent crime against tourists is rare; what goes wrong is
scripted: gem-shop tuk-tuks, "closed today" touts,
meterless taxis (Grab kills this), jet-ski deposits,
red-flag swimming, and scooter rentals — the true #1
tourist injury. No licence + no helmet + first-ever
scooter + Phuket hills is the accident recipe; this
itinerary simply never needs one.

**Elephant riding — really that bad?**
Yes. The training that makes an elephant rideable is
brutal, and reputable sanctuaries (Elephant Nature Park
first among them) exist precisely as the alternative.
Watch, feed, walk alongside — the experience is better
*and* kinder. If a place offers rides or shows, spend
your baht elsewhere.

**Which months for which coast?**
**November–February: everything works** (book early).
Andaman side (Phuket/Phi Phi/Krabi): best Nov–Apr, monsoon
swells May–Oct. Gulf side (Samui/Tao) runs roughly
opposite (driest Jan–Aug). **Chiang Mai's smoky season
(late Feb–mid Apr)** is real — asthmatics and families
should aim north-time at Nov–Jan. Songkran (13–15 April)
is the national water fight: chaos, joy, everything wet.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Easier than almost anywhere in this series: Thailand's
south is heavily Muslim — Phuket's Old Town and beach
grills have deep halal roots — Bangkok clusters halal
kitchens around Sukhumvit/Nana and Chinatown's edges, and
Chiang Mai's famous **Khao Soi Islam** serves the
signature dish halal by default. Vegetarians ride the
"jay" (เจ) vegan tradition, tofu pad thai and mango sticky
rice — but fish sauce hides everywhere: "mai sai nam
pla" (no fish sauce) is the phrase to screenshot.

**Phuket or Krabi for the island leg?**
Phuket wins this itinerary on pure logistics (direct
CNX flights in, long-haul out of HKT) and family
infrastructure. Krabi (Railay's cliffs, Ao Nang's
laid-back strip, closer Phi Phi runs) is arguably more
beautiful and 20% cheaper — the perfect swap for
second-timers, and a pillar of our next Thailand guide.

**What's Thailand guide #2?**
Either **Krabi + the Gulf islands** (Railay, Koh Samui,
snorkel-diving Koh Tao) or the **northern deep-cut**
(Chiang Rai's White Temple, the Golden Triangle, Pai).
The beaches are currently winning the argument.
