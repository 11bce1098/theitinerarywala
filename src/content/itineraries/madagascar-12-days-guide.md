Madagascar separated from Africa long enough ago that almost
everything living here evolved separately. Roughly **90% of its
wildlife exists nowhere else on earth** — more than a hundred lemur
species, two-thirds of the world's chameleons, baobabs found on no
other continent.

It is also, bluntly, **hard to travel**. The roads are the defining
constraint: distances that look like a morning take two days,
surfaces vary from tarmac to deep rutted track, and the schedule is
set by what the vehicle can manage. That is not a warning against
going. It is the thing to understand before you book.

This route takes the eastern rainforest for lemurs, the western dry
forest for baobabs, and the **Tsingy de Bemaraha** — a plateau
eroded into a forest of limestone blades — which is one of the most
extraordinary landscapes anywhere.

## Before you go: the roads, the season and the guides

### Entry

- **Visa on arrival or eVisa for most nationalities**, issued at
  the airport for stays up to 30, 60 or 90 days, with a fee payable
  in **euros or US dollars cash**.
- **Bring that cash in clean notes.** Card facilities at the airport
  are unreliable.
- Passport valid six months.

### The roads are the itinerary

This is the honest brief, and it matters more here than in any
other guide on this site.

- **There is very little paved road network.** The RN7 south and
  the RN2 east are surfaced; most else is not.
- **Antananarivo to Morondava is 700 km and takes two full days** by
  road. Many people fly it instead, in an hour.
- **The Tsingy road is the hard part**: from Morondava it is roughly
  8–10 hours of rough track plus **two river ferry crossings on
  wooden barges**, and it is **only passable in the dry season**.
- **You travel with a driver and a 4x4**, arranged through an
  operator. Self-drive is legal and inadvisable.
- **Domestic flights exist and are frequently delayed or
  cancelled.** Build slack around them; never connect an internal
  flight to an international one on the same day.

### The season decides what is open

- **April to November is the dry season** and the only realistic
  window for this route.
- **The Tsingy and its road close in the wet season**, roughly
  December to March, and cyclones affect the east coast in the same
  months.
- **June to September** is the reliable core: dry, cooler, and good
  for wildlife.
- **September to November** is best for lemur watching — babies are
  born, and the forests are active.

### Guides are compulsory, and worth it

- **Every national park requires a local guide.** This is not
  upselling; it is the rule, it funds the parks and the communities,
  and the guides are extremely good at finding animals you would
  walk past.
- **Night walks** are where the mouse lemurs and chameleons are, and
  are separately arranged.
- **Tip properly.** Guiding is the main local income at these parks.

### Money and health

- **The ariary (MGA)**, and the numbers are large. Cards work in
  Antananarivo hotels and almost nowhere else.
- **Draw cash in Tana**, and carry enough for the whole trip outside
  it.
- **Malaria is present across the country.** Take advice on
  prophylaxis well in advance.
- **Do not drink tap water.** Bottled or filtered only.
- French is the working second language; English is limited outside
  tourism.

## Day 1 — Land in Antananarivo

- **13:00** — Land at **Ivato (TNR)**. Immigration and the visa desk
  are slow; budget an hour before you reach the car park.
- **14:30** — Transfer into the city, 15 km that takes 45 minutes on
  a good day and well over an hour in afternoon traffic.
- **16:00** — Check in around **Isoraka** or **Tsiazotafo**, the two
  quarters worth walking after dark, and change money at the hotel
  or a bank — not at the airport, where the rate is poor.
- **17:30** — Walk the **Haute-Ville** steps for the view over the
  tin roofs, no more than that; the altitude here is 1,300 m and you
  have just flown.
- **19:30** — Dinner at a *hotely* in Isoraka. Zebu steak, rice with
  everything, and Three Horses Beer.
- **21:00** — Confirm three things before bed: cash in hand for the
  week, the driver's arrival time, and whether the Morondava flight
  is still running. All three change.

## Day 2 — East to Andasibe

- **07:00** — Leave early. The **RN2** east is 140 km and takes
  three to four hours, and the last stretch is slow behind trucks.
- **09:30** — Stop at **Moramanga** for coffee and the roadside
  stalls. The terraced rice paddies either side of the road are the
  landscape people picture when they picture Madagascar.
- **12:00** — Arrive **Andasibe**. Lunch at the lodge, then meet
  your guide: park walks here are guided by law, and the good guides
  are booked out by mid-morning.
- **15:00** — An easy first walk in **Analamazaotra** to get your
  eye in — chameleons, orchids, and probably a distant indri call.
- **18:30** — Dinner early; you are going out again.
- **19:30** — The **night walk** along the road outside the reserve
  (the park itself closes at dusk, so this is roadside, torchlight,
  and better than it sounds): mouse lemurs, leaf-tailed geckos,
  tree frogs, sleeping chameleons at eye level.

## Day 3 — Indri, and the rainforest

- **06:00** — Into **Analamazaotra** at opening. The **indri** calls
  between roughly 07:00 and 11:00, and the call — a rising siren
  that carries two kilometres — is what you came for. Reckon on
  two to three hours of walking to reach a family.
- **10:30** — Back for breakfast, or push on if the indri are
  moving.
- **13:00** — **Mantadia**, an hour north on a rough track, for
  primary forest, diademed sifaka and far fewer people. The road is
  the reason most visitors skip it.
- **16:00** — Alternative if the track is out: **Vakona Private
  Reserve**, where rehabilitated lemurs will land on your shoulders.
  It is a zoo in all but name — enjoyable, not wild, and worth being
  clear-eyed about.
- **19:30** — A second night walk if the first one delivered. The
  guides know which stretch of road has been productive this week.

## Day 4 — Back to Tana, and west

- **06:30** — Leave Andasibe early to beat the truck traffic back
  down the RN2.
- **10:30** — Antananarivo. If you are flying, go straight to Ivato;
  **Tsaradia's** Morondava flights are frequently retimed and
  occasionally cancelled, and the queue is not fast.
- **13:00** — Fly to **Morondava**, about an hour. The alternative
  is two days on the RN34 and RN35, which is a genuine option only
  if you have the days spare.
- **14:30** — Land into heat and humidity you did not have this
  morning — the west coast is a different country from the highlands.
- **16:00** — Check in along the sand spit at **Nosy Kely**.
- **18:00** — Grilled fish and the Mozambique Channel sunset. Early
  night: tomorrow starts before dawn.

## Day 5 — The Avenue of the Baobabs

- **05:00** — Out to the **Avenue of the Baobabs**, 20 km north on a
  sand road, 45 minutes. Sunrise is quieter than sunset and the
  light is just as good.
- **07:30** — Back for breakfast, and the heat of the day off your
  feet.
- **14:00** — **Kirindy Forest**, two hours north, and worth the
  drive: this is the reliable place for **fossa**, the island's
  largest predator, which behaves like a cat that has been told it
  is a dog.
- **17:00** — Back to the **Avenue** for sunset, when the tour buses
  and every vendor in the region arrive. Walk 200 m up the road and
  the crowd thins to nothing.
- **19:30** — Dinner in Morondava. Confirm the Bekopaka vehicle,
  the driver, and that the ferries are running — in a dry year they
  always are, but ask.

## Day 6 — North to the Tsingy

- **05:00** — Leave in the dark. This is the day the trip is built
  around and it is not a comfortable one.
- **08:30** — The **Tsiribihina** river crossing at Belo: a flat
  wooden barge, your vehicle driven onto planks, and a wait that
  runs from twenty minutes to two hours depending on what is ahead
  of you.
- **12:00** — Lunch at **Belo sur Tsiribihina**, the only real stop.
- **16:00** — The **Manambolo** crossing, second barge, same drill.
- **17:30** — **Bekopaka**, 8 to 10 hours after you left, dusty and
  rattled. The lodges here are better than the road suggests.
- **19:00** — Dinner, and register for tomorrow at the park office
  if your lodge has not already done it.

## Day 7 — Grand Tsingy

- **06:30** — Park office at opening for the **Andamozavaky**
  circuit. Harnesses are issued and their use is compulsory.
- **07:30** — Into the **Grand Tsingy**: limestone eroded into
  blades and needles, crossed on fixed cables, steel ladders and a
  suspension bridge strung between two pinnacles.
- **11:00** — The high traverse and the viewpoint over the whole
  grey forest of stone. Four to five hours of scrambling in total,
  some of it through gaps you turn sideways for.
- **13:30** — Out, and lunch. You will be finished for the day.
- **16:00** — Nothing. Sit still. This is not a day you stack
  anything else onto.
- **Note** — Not for anyone uneasy with heights or enclosed spaces.
  Everyone else: one of the best days available outdoors anywhere.

## Day 8 — Petit Tsingy, and the gorge

- **07:00** — **Petit Tsingy**, lower, shorter and far easier than
  yesterday — two hours, with Decken's sifaka in the shaded canyon
  floors.
- **10:30** — A **pirogue** on the **Manambolo gorge**, poled
  upstream between 100 m limestone walls, past **Vazimba** burial
  sites tucked into ledges in the cliff. Two hours on the water.
- **13:00** — Lunch at the lodge.
- **15:00** — The cave system near the gorge mouth, or a second
  short Tsingy walk if legs allow.
- **18:30** — Last night at Bekopaka. Repack for the road — dust
  gets into everything tomorrow.

## Day 9 — Back to Morondava

- **05:00** — The same track south, both ferries again. It is no
  shorter the second time.
- **12:00** — Belo sur Tsiribihina for lunch.
- **16:30** — Into Morondava with, if the timing has gone your way,
  an hour in hand.
- **17:30** — The **Avenue** one last time in the other light. Most
  people's best photograph of the trip is this one, not the first.
- **19:30** — Dinner on the beach, and a shower that takes a while.

## Day 10 — The coast, or Kirindy properly

- **Option A — a slow day.** Morondava's beach is not the Indian
  Ocean picture-postcard, but after four days of track it is
  exactly right. Boat out to **Betania** fishing village across
  the estuary in the morning.
- **Option B — Kirindy, at night.** Drive up in the afternoon,
  stay at the forest camp, and do the **night drive**: fossa,
  giant jumping rats, and seven species of nocturnal lemur. The
  better wildlife day by some distance.
- **16:00** — Whichever you chose, be back for the evening flight
  to Tana, or start the drive if you are going overland.

## Day 11 — Antananarivo

- **09:00** — The **Rova** on the hill: the Queen's Palace, burned
  out in 1995 and rebuilt over two decades, with the whole city
  below it.
- **11:00** — **Analakely** market and the covered stairs below it —
  the loudest, most useful hour in the city.
- **13:00** — Lunch in Haute-Ville.
- **15:00** — **Lemurs' Park**, 22 km west, if you want one more
  easy sighting — nine species in restored forest by the Katsaoka
  river. Or **Andravoahangy** market for raffia, woodwork and
  embroidered linen, which is the better use of the afternoon if
  you have had your fill of lemurs.
- **19:30** — Last dinner. Zebu again, and you will not mind.

## Day 12 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Free. Most international departures leave late
  evening, so you have the day, and Tana is a fine place to spend
  it slowly.
- **Afternoon** — Transfer to Ivato at least three hours out.
- **Do not** connect an internal flight to an international one on
  the same day. Domestic schedules here move, and the connection is
  the one thing on this trip with no slack in it.

## What to pack

- **Broken-in walking shoes with grip** for the Tsingy, which is
  sharp limestone.
- **Long sleeves and trousers** for forest walks and mosquitoes.
- **A head torch** for night walks, which are the best walks.
- **Cash in ariary and euros**, drawn and changed in Tana.
- **Malaria prophylaxis and strong repellent.**
- **Binoculars** — lemurs are usually high in the canopy.
- **Type C/E plugs**, 220V, where there is power.

## FAQ

**How bad are the roads really?**
Bad enough to plan the trip around. The Tsingy road is 8–10 hours of
rough track with two river ferries and is impassable in the wet
season. Everywhere else on this route is manageable with a 4x4 and
patience.

**Is twelve days enough?**
For this route, yes, and it is already ambitious. Adding the RN7
south to Isalo and Ifaty needs another week; Madagascar does not
reward rushing.

**When should I go?**
**April to November**, and **June to September** for the most
reliable conditions. The Tsingy is closed in the wet season, which
removes the best day of the trip.

**Will I definitely see lemurs?**
Yes. Andasibe's indri are near-certain with a guide, and the
habituated groups at Vakona are a guarantee. Fossa are luck.
