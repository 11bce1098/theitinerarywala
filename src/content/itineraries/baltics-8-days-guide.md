The Baltic capitals are what Prague was before the crowds
found it: complete medieval and art-nouveau old towns,
walkable in an afternoon and lovable for a lifetime, at
prices the euro zone forgot were possible. **Tallinn** is the
fairy tale — turrets, merchant houses and city walls so
intact they look rendered. **Riga** is the grand one — the
world's densest art-nouveau district and a food market
housed in four WWI zeppelin hangars. **Vilnius** is the
charmer — baroque courtyards, a bohemian "republic" with its
own constitution, and a lake-island castle half an hour away.

Three countries; **one Schengen visa, one currency, one
language of survival (English works everywhere)** — and
comfortable coaches stitching it together in four-hour hops.
The shape: **Tallinn (3 nights) → Riga (2) → Vilnius (2)**,
flying open-jaw. Figure **{{EUR 1100}} per person
** including the visa — the
cheapest multi-country Europe trip on this site. **Go
May–September** for long light and café squares; December
for three of Europe's prettiest small Christmas markets.

## Before you go: the admin

### One visa, three countries

- **Schengen visa, Baltic flavour:** one visa covers
  all three. Apply for the **main-destination
  country — Estonia on this route** (most nights AND
  first entry) — via its UAE application route
  (Estonia and Lithuania route through partner
  centres/VFS in Dubai; check the current handler
  when booking): **€90** + service fee, the usual
  file, **6–8 weeks out** to be safe, since Baltic
  consular capacity is smaller than France-scale
  operations. An existing multi-entry Schengen
  sticker works, as ever.

### Flights, and the coaches

- **Flights:** no UAE non-stops — one-stop
  **into Tallinn, home from Vilnius** via Istanbul
  (Turkish), Helsinki (Finnair) or Warsaw (LOT),
  ~US$540–870 return combined; airBaltic has
  also run seasonal Dubai–Riga directs some winters —
  worth a check for your dates.
- **The coaches ARE the plan:** **Lux Express** runs
  Tallinn–Riga (~4 h 30) and Riga–Vilnius (~4 h)
  many times daily — **fares from ~€13–25** booked
  ahead, with WiFi, coffee machines and seatback
  screens; the **Lounge class (+~€10)** buys
  airline-style seats and is this guide's favourite
  cheap upgrade. Buses beat the (still-unfinished)
  rail links; book seats a few days out in summer.

### Money

- **Money:** all three use the **euro**, cards tap
  everywhere (Estonia is the country that votes
  online; cash is nearly ceremonial). The usual
  rule: when a terminal offers your home currency, choose EUR.

### One gentle brief

- **One gentle brief:** these are proud EU/NATO
  states with distinct languages and identities —
  locals wince at "ex-Soviet bloc" framing. The
  occupation museums in all three capitals tell
  that story movingly; arrive curious.

## Day 1 — Land in Tallinn

- **Afternoon** — Land via your hub; the airport
  is absurdly close (tram 4, 15 minutes, €2).
  Check in by the Old Town.
- **17:00** — First wander: **Raekoja plats** (the
  Town Hall square, 700 years of it), Viru Gate's
  twin towers, and lanes that end in cafés
  wearing flower boxes.
- **19:30** — Dinner honesty for night one:
  **Olde Hansa**, the candle-lit medieval theatre-
  restaurant, is touristy and grinningly worth it
  once (elk, honey beer, €25–35 pp); the local's
  answer is Rataskaevu 16 and its famously
  bookable-out sister spots (€18–28 mains) —
  reserve ahead.

**Where to stay in Tallinn** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Hotel Telegraaf, Autograph
  Collection** — 4.7/5 on TripAdvisor (~2,200
  reviews), spa-calm in a former telegraph
  exchange, Old Town core. ~€220+.
- *Mid-range:* **My City Hotel** — 4.5/5 (~1,800
  reviews), on the walls' southern edge.
  ~€110–150. (Nunne Boutique, 4.8/150, is the
  #1-Best-Value boutique play.)
- *Value:* **ibis Tallinn Center** — 4.6/612
  reviews — ten minutes' walk out, ~€70–95.

## Day 2 — Tallinn: the two towns on the hill

- **09:00** — Climb **Toompea**, the upper town:
  the onion domes of **Alexander Nevsky
  Cathedral**, parliament in a pink castle, and
  the two viewing platforms — **Kohtuotsa**
  ("The Times We Had" wall) and **Patkuli** — for
  the red-roofs-and-spires panorama that sells
  Estonia.
- **11:30** — Walk the **town walls** (a few
  euros for tower sections) and Katariina käik,
  the craft-guild passage; lunch on pelmeni or
  the €10 lunch specials every café runs.
- **14:30** — **Telliskivi Creative City**: the
  rail-yard district of studios, murals, and the
  Balti Jaam market's food hall — Tallinn's
  modern half, ten minutes from its medieval one.
  (Photography stop: the Fotografiska outpost,
  ~€16.)
- **18:00** — Sunset back at Patkuli; dinner in
  town — and note how Estonia quietly does some
  of Europe's best modern cooking at half its
  prices.

## Day 3 — Kadriorg or the seaplanes, then a slow evening

- **09:30** — Choose your Tallinn:
  - **Kadriorg**: Peter the Great's pastel palace
    gardens + **KUMU** (~€14), the striking
    Estonian art museum; or
  - **Seaplane Harbour (Lennusadam, ~€20)**: a
    WWI seaplane hangar full of ships, a real
    1930s submarine you climb through — the
    family vote, every time.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then the balloon-or-boat
  hour: the Baltic promenade at Pirita, or Old
  Town café-crawling (Maiasmokk, 1864, for
  marzipan history).
- **17:00** — Pack light logistics: buy tomorrow's
  **Lux Express Lounge seats** if you haven't;
  final Old Town night walk — the lanes at dusk,
  lamps on stone, are the memory that lasts.

## Day 4 — Coach south, then Riga's grand entrance

- **09:30** — **Tallinn → Riga (~4 h 30,
  €15–25)**: pine forests, coastal flats, a
  border you won't notice crossing.
- **14:30** — Check in, straight out: **Old
  Riga** — the **House of the Blackheads (€7)**,
  rebuilt Gothic swagger on the Town Hall
  square; the **Three Brothers** houses; the
  cathedral square's street musicians.
- **17:30** — **St Peter's Church tower**
  (~€15, lift): the red-roof panorama with the
  Daugava river and the market hangars laid
  out below — best in evening light.
- **20:00** — Dinner in Old Riga (Latvian
  classics — grey peas with speck, river fish —
  €15–25 mains) — or the food-hall route at
  Centrālais Tirgus if it's a late-opening
  night.

**Where to stay in Riga** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Grand Palace Hotel** — 4.6/5
  (~1,100 reviews), old-bank elegance inside
  Old Riga. ~€220+.
- *Mid-range:* **Neiburgs Hotel** — 4.8/5
  (~1,200 reviews), art-nouveau apartments IN
  the old town — the thematically perfect
  base. ~€140–180.
- *Value:* **Konventa Sēta** — 4.6/174
  reviews — rooms scattered through a
  13th-century convent courtyard, ~€85–110.
  (Grand Poet by Semarah, 4.7/890, city-park
  side.)

## Day 5 — Art nouveau and the zeppelin market

- **09:30** — The **Art Nouveau quarter**:
  **Alberta iela** and Elizabetes iela, where
  Mikhail Eisenstein stacked facades with
  sphinxes, screaming masks and peacocks —
  the world's densest concentration of the
  style, free from the pavement (the Art
  Nouveau Museum's staircase, ~€10, for
  completists).
- **12:30** — **Riga Central Market**: four
  German zeppelin hangars turned into one of
  Europe's great food markets — smoked fish,
  rye bread, berry mountains; lunch at the
  gastro-hall for €8–12.
- **15:00** — Pick the afternoon: the
  **Occupation Museum** (moving, ~€8) and
  KGB Corner House for the history; or the
  30-minute train (€3) to **Jūrmala's**
  white-sand pines for the Baltic-seaside
  hour.
- **19:30** — Farewell-Riga dinner; Old Town
  lanes or the Miera iela café strip for the
  local mood.

## Day 6 — South again: baroque Vilnius

- **09:30** — **Riga → Vilnius (~4 h,
  €13–25)** — the second and final coach leg.
- **14:00** — Check in, then **Cathedral
  Square**: the white classical cathedral,
  its lone bell tower, and the "stebuklas"
  (miracle) tile — spin on it, wish included.
  Ride the funicular up **Gediminas Tower**
  (~€8) for the spires-and-forest panorama
  that explains "baroque city in a park".
- **17:00** — Pilies Street and the university
  courtyards (Europe's oldest east of
  Germany), Literatų Street's wall of book
  tributes.
- **19:30** — Dinner Lithuanian-style:
  **cepelinai** (zeppelin-shaped potato
  dumplings — order one, share it, thank us)
  or the beetroot-pink **šaltibarščiai** soup
  that looks Instagram-invented and is
  centuries old. Mains €12–20.

**Where to stay in Vilnius** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Hotel Pacai** — 4.7/5 (~270
  reviews), a baroque palace with frescoes
  over the beds. ~€220+. (Grand Hotel
  Vilnius Curio, 4.8/1,100, is the polished
  big-hotel option.)
- *Mid-range:* **Artagonist Art Hotel** —
  4.8/5 (~560 reviews), old-town gallery
  cool. ~€110–150.
- *Value:* **Comfort Hotel LT Rock'n'Roll** —
  4.5/2,300 reviews — bright rooms by the
  Hales Market, ~€70–90.

## Day 7 — Trakai's island castle, Užupis' republic

- **09:00** — Train to **Trakai** (30 min,
  ~€5): walk the lakeside path to the
  **Island Castle** (~€12–15) — red-brick
  towers on its own islet, reached by
  footbridges, ringed by yacht-dotted water.
  Try **kibinai** (the local Karaite
  pasties, €3–5) by the shore. Back by
  mid-afternoon.
- **16:00** — **Užupis**: the artists'
  quarter that declared itself a republic
  (April Fools', 1997, and never quite
  stopped) — read its constitution on
  mirrored plaques in 40 languages ("Everyone
  has the right to be idle"; "A dog has the
  right to be a dog"), get your passport
  stamped at the info shop, coffee by the
  river.
- **18:30** — Climb the **Hill of Three
  Crosses** for the sunset over a skyline
  that's all steeples and treetops; farewell
  dinner in the old town — the trip's last
  €20 feast.

## Day 8 — Fly home from Vilnius

- Morning: the **Gate of Dawn** and its
  golden Madonna chapel (free, quietly
  lovely) if unvisited; airport is 15
  minutes (€1 train!). One-stop home via
  Warsaw/Istanbul/Helsinki — three
  countries stamped, one visa used.

## What to pack

- Layers + rain shell in every season;
  Baltic summer is 18–24°C with theatrical
  showers, and sea wind is a permanent
  resident.
- Cobblestone-rated shoes — three old towns
  of stone, and Toompea/Gediminas are
  hills.
- A warm layer even in July for evening
  squares; proper winter kit Nov–Mar
  (December markets are magical and
  -5°C).
- **European Type C/F adapter.**
- Day-bag for coach days (bags go under;
  keep valuables + snacks with you).
- Swimwear only if Jūrmala's on your Riga
  day.

## FAQ

**Is it safe — and what about the Russia
question?**
The capitals are small, calm and
low-crime — solo travellers and families
rate them among Europe's easiest. On
geopolitics: these are EU and NATO
members whose daily life, flights and
tourism run entirely normally; the
borders with Russia/Belarus are simply
not where this route goes. Standard
advice as ever — glance at headlines
when booking — then enjoy three of the
continent's most relaxed capitals.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Honest scale: modest but workable
halal — each capital has a kebab-grill
bench and a few certified spots (Riga
and Vilnius's are small; Tallinn's
slightly wider), so the pattern is
kebab-reliability plus seafood/veg
dining. Vegetarians do fine:
šaltibarščiai (the pink soup) is
vegetarian by birth, potato pancakes,
grey-peas-without-speck on request,
Georgian restaurants (a Baltic
obsession — khachapuri everywhere), and
modern-veg cafés in all three
Telliskivi-style quarters.

**Are the coach legs a slog?**
They're the easiest intercity hops on
this site: 4-hour smooth motorways,
WiFi, coffee machine, films — and the
Lounge-class upgrade (~€10) turns them
into business class. Book morning
departures and each travel day still
delivers a full afternoon-evening in the
new city. (Flying between the capitals
exists but saves nothing once airports
are counted.)

**Which capital wins — and how would you
re-slice the nights?**
Tallinn for the postcard, Riga for
grandeur and the market, Vilnius for
atmosphere — most travellers' hearts
pick Vilnius and their cameras pick
Tallinn. With a ninth day, give Riga
the third night (Jūrmala AND the
art-nouveau day stop competing).
Reverse the whole route (VNO in) if
flight prices say so; it works
identically.

**December Christmas markets — worth
building the trip around?**
Yes, if you pack for real cold: Tallinn's
Town Hall square market (with its claim
to Europe's first public Christmas tree,
1441) is the region's star, Riga counters
with its own first-tree claim (1510) —
the rivalry is half the fun — and Vilnius
adds a light-wrapped cathedral square.
Short days, glowing squares, mulled
everything.

(the 2-hour ferry pairs them perfectly —
and connects to our
[Finland guide](/itineraries/finland-6-days-guide/)) —
or **Estonia's islands and coast**
(Saaremaa, Lahemaa's bog trails) for
returners. Votes welcome; both on the
