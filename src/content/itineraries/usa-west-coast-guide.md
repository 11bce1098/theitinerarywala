If our [East Coast guide](/itineraries/usa-east-coast-guide/) is America's
history and skyline, this is its fantasy and geology. Los Angeles
is the world's dream factory sprawled along a beach; Las Vegas is
the desert's fever dream — and, more usefully, the gateway to the
Grand Canyon and Antelope Canyon; San Francisco is the beautiful,
foggy, hill-stacked city every film has promised you.

The standard advice says the West needs a road trip. It doesn't —
not for this triangle. Three short flights connect the cities,
day tours with hotel pickup handle the canyons, and the only
driving you'll do is in the back seat of an Uber. Ten days, no
rental forms, no US traffic baptism.

**Go April–May or September–October.** June–August means 42–47°C
in Vegas and fog-chilled 15°C summers in San Francisco (pack for
both, always); winter is mild in LA, cool in Vegas, rainy-ish in
SF — and everything is cheaper. All costs are in US dollars (USD).

## Before you go: the admin

### The visa, in one paragraph

Everything about the US visa — the **~$435 real cost** per Indian
passport ($185 MRV + $250 Visa Integrity Fee), the **6–12 month
appointment waits from the UAE** since the July 2026 service
suspension, the 10-year B1/B2 consolation, ESTA for Visa Waiver
passports — is covered in depth in our
[East Coast guide](/itineraries/usa-east-coast-guide/). The one-line version:
**start the visa 8–12 months before you fly**, via
ustraveldocs.com, and let nothing else be booked non-refundable
until it's stamped.

### Money — this route's three traps

- **Resort fees.** Vegas hotels advertise $79 rooms and then add a
  mandatory **~$40–55/night "resort fee" plus tax at the desk**.
  It's legal, it's universal on the Strip, and it roughly doubles
  cheap-room maths — our budget below includes it. Compare
  *total* prices only.
- **Tipping and tax** work exactly as in the East guide: sales tax
  added at the register, 18–20% at sit-down restaurants, $2–5 per
  bag/valet/housekeeping day. Budget it like a tax.
- **LA moves by ride-hailing.** Public transit exists (the Metro
  B line does Hollywood, the E line reaches Santa Monica) and is
  fine by day, but this city was built for cars — expect
  **$25–45 per Uber hop** and ~$300 of the budget to go to them.
  Vegas and SF, by contrast, are walk-and-transit cities.

### SIM, plugs, kids

- Same as the East: eSIM ~$15–25, Type A/B plugs, 120V.
- **Kids:** Universal Studios is the anchor day (check ride height
  cut-offs — 102–122 cm gates the big ones); under-2s fly free on
  laps, canyon tours usually charge from age 2–3. The **Antelope
  Canyon day is a 14–15 hour bus marathon** — glorious for
  photographers, brutal for under-8s. The Grand Canyon West day is
  the gentler pick with small children.
- **Vegas with kids is legal and common** — they just can't stop
  on the casino floor (walking through is fine, 21+ to gamble or
  drink). The Strip at night is PG spectacle: fountains,
  volcanoes, gondolas.

## Day 1 — Land in Los Angeles, Hollywood at sunset

Stay in **Hollywood** (walkable core, Universal next door) — it
beats Santa Monica for a first, car-free visit.

- **Afternoon** — Land at LAX (direct ~16-hour flights from the
  Gulf). Uber/flat-rate cab to Hollywood ~$40–60, 30–50 min by
  traffic mood.
- **17:00** — First walk: the **Hollywood Walk of Fame** along
  Hollywood Blvd — the stars, the **TCL Chinese Theatre**
  hand-prints, the Dolby Theatre (Oscars' home). Tourist-tacky
  and mandatory, in that order.
- **18:30** — Uber 10 minutes up to **Griffith Observatory**
  (free, open late Tue–Sun): the Hollywood Sign on your right,
  the entire glittering LA basin below, telescopes on the lawn.
  The best first-night-in-America view on this coast.
- **20:30** — Dinner back on the boulevard, or the In-N-Out on
  Sunset — the $12 burger pilgrimage (order it "animal style").

**Where to stay in Los Angeles** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Hollywood Roosevelt** — 4.4/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~4,200 reviews), the 1927 hotel that hosted the first Oscars,
  David-Hockney-painted pool, directly on the Walk of Fame.
  ~$300–420.
- *Mid-range:* **The Garland** — 4.4/5 (~4,600 reviews), retro
  family favourite with a **free Universal Studios shuttle**.
  ~$200–270.
- *Budget-boutique:* **Magic Castle Hotel** — 4.7/5 (~3,900
  reviews), plain outside, legendary service inside (free snacks,
  poolside popsicle hotline — really). ~$180–240.

## Day 2 — Universal Studios Hollywood

- **08:45** — Be at the gates before the 9:00–10:00 opening
  (Tue–Thu are the quiet days). **Dated online tickets $109–154**
  — buy ahead, gate prices start ~$169. Express pass ($209+)
  is worth it only in school holidays.
- **The must-dos:** Super Nintendo World's **Mario Kart**, the
  **Studio Tour** (60 min through real working backlots — Jaws,
  Psycho house, King Kong; included with every ticket), Harry
  Potter's Forbidden Journey and a butterbeer, Jurassic World's
  splash finale.
- **Strategy:** big rides before 11:00, Studio Tour and shows in
  the crowded middle, re-rides after 17:00.
- **19:00** — Exit into **CityWalk** for a neon dinner (~$20–30),
  five minutes' walk. If you stayed at The Garland, the shuttle
  takes you home.

## Day 3 — The Pacific day: Getty, Santa Monica, Venice

- **09:30** — Uber west to the **Getty Center** (museum **free**;
  Van Gogh's Irises, Monet, and the architecture-garden-view trio
  worth the trip alone). Two hours.
- **12:30** — Optional 20-minute detour through **Beverly Hills**:
  Rodeo Drive's three absurd blocks, the sign photo.
- **14:00** — **Santa Monica Pier**: the Ferris wheel over the
  Pacific, the official **end-of-Route-66 sign**, fish tacos on
  the boardwalk (~$15).
- **16:00** — Rent beach cruisers and ride the oceanfront path
  25 minutes south to **Venice Beach**: skate park, Muscle Beach,
  the canals two streets back — LA's weirdness reserve.
- **18:30** — Sunset from the sand or the pier's wheel, dinner on
  Abbot Kinney Blvd (~$25–40), Uber home.

## Day 4 — Fly to Las Vegas, first Strip night

- **10:00** — LAX → LAS: ~1h10, from ~$50–90 booked a few weeks
  out. You'll see the desert switch on beneath you.
- **13:00** — Check in mid-Strip. Two Vegas rules: drink water
  constantly, and treat posted room rates as fiction until the
  resort fee is added.
- **15:00** — The **south Strip loop on foot** (it's further than
  it looks — 5 km feels like 10 in July): the **Bellagio
  Conservatory** (free), Paris's half-scale Eiffel Tower, the
  Venetian's canals and painted sky.
- **19:00** — Dinner at a buffet (Wicked Spoon, ~$40–50) or food
  court (~$15), then the **Bellagio Fountains** — shows every
  15–30 min through midnight, and the greatest free spectacle in
  America. Watch three.

**Where to stay in Las Vegas** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Venetian Resort** — 4.3/5 (~36,700 reviews),
  all-suite rooms twice normal size, canals indoors. ~$180–320
  plus fees.
- *Mid-range:* **Paris Las Vegas** — 3.9/5 (~39,900 reviews),
  centre-Strip under the Eiffel replica, fountain views across
  the street. ~$90–170 plus fees.
- *Budget:* **The LINQ Hotel** — 3.7/5 (~19,800 reviews), modern
  rooms at motel prices, dead-centre location by the High Roller
  wheel. ~$50–120 plus fees.
- All three add the **~$40–55/night resort fee** — no Strip hotel
  spares you.

## Day 5 — Grand Canyon day

- **06:30** — Hotel pickup for the **Grand Canyon West Rim bus
  tour**: **$109** including entry (Hualapai tribal lands), a
  Hoover Dam photo stop, and ~3.5 hours at the canyon — Eagle
  Point and Guano Point's 360° drop. Add the **Skywalk**, the
  glass horseshoe 1,200 m over the floor, for **$35** (book with
  the tour; phones go in lockers — buy the photo or trust your
  memory).
- **The honest choice:** the West Rim is *not* the national park —
  it's the closest rim (2.5 h vs 4.5 h each way) and the right
  call for a day trip. Purists wanting the classic **South Rim**
  panoramas can take the longer coach day (~$120–150, 14–15 h) —
  more canyon, much more bus.
- **18:00** — Back on the Strip. Shower, then a show night: **the
  Sphere** (Postcard from Earth or 2026's Wizard of Oz, from
  ~$99 — the 160,000-sq-ft interior screen must be seen to be
  believed), or Cirque du Soleil's **"O"** (~$120+).

## Day 6 — Antelope Canyon, or the mercy day

Two very different Day 6s — pick by stamina:

- **The marathon:** the **Lower Antelope Canyon + Horseshoe Bend
  tour** — from **~$165** including the Navajo-guided slot-canyon
  walk and the Colorado's great bend. It is every desert photo
  you've ever saved, and it costs a **4–5 a.m. pickup and a
  14–15-hour day**. Photographers won't regret it; small kids
  might.
- **The mercy version:** sleep in, pool morning, then a
  half-day **Hoover Dam** tour (~$60–90) or the **High Roller**
  wheel (~$25–35) and Venetian gondolas.
- **19:30** — Either way, end downtown at the **Fremont Street
  Experience** (old Vegas): the LED canopy show hourly, live
  bands, vintage neon — free, and more fun than half the Strip.

## Day 7 — Fly to San Francisco, cable cars by evening

- **10:00** — LAS → SFO: ~1h35, from ~$60–110. BART train from
  the airport into the city, $11, 30 min — welcome to a US city
  where transit just works.
- **13:00** — Check in at **Fisherman's Wharf** (flat, walkable,
  Alcatraz on the doorstep — the right first-timer base; Union
  Square is more central but edges the Tenderloin's rough blocks).
- **15:00** — Ride the **Powell–Hyde cable car** ($8 a tap, day
  passes ~$13 on the MuniMobile app) from the Wharf turnaround
  over Russian Hill — stand on the running board, obviously —
  hop off for **Lombard Street's** zigzag block.
- **17:30** — **Chinatown**: the Dragon Gate, Waverly Place
  lanterns, the fortune-cookie factory in Ross Alley (the oldest
  Chinatown outside Asia).
- **19:30** — Dinner in **North Beach**, the Italian quarter next
  door — Tony's pizza if the queue allows (~$25–35).

**Where to stay in San Francisco** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Argonaut Hotel** — 4.5/5 (~8,100 reviews),
  timber-and-brick cannery conversion on the Wharf, bay views.
  ~$280–400.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Caza Fisherman's Wharf** — 4.3/5 (~1,300
  reviews), fresh family-styled rooms, two blocks from Pier 39.
  ~$180–260.
- *Budget:* **Chancellor Hotel on Union Square** — 4.5/5 (~6,100
  reviews), 1914 charmer on the cable-car line, the city's
  best-rated honest bargain. ~$140–200.

## Day 8 — Alcatraz and the waterfront

- **08:45** — First ferry from Pier 33 to **Alcatraz** (day tour
  **~$48**, kids 5–11 discounted, the superb cellhouse audio tour
  included — narrated by former guards and inmates). **Book the
  moment your dates fix: tickets release 90 days out and sell out
  weeks ahead.** Only buy from cityexperiences.com — street
  resellers sell bay cruises, not the island.
- **12:30** — Back ashore: **Pier 39's sea lions** (free comedy),
  clam chowder in a sourdough bowl at Boudin (~$14), the WWII
  submarine USS Pampanito if the kids vote.
- **15:00** — Walk west along the water to the **Ghirardelli
  Square** hot-fudge sundae and **Hyde Street Pier's** tall
  ships.
- **17:00** — **Golden Gate viewpoint hour:** bus/Uber to Fort
  Point or Crissy Field as the fog lifts (or rolls in — both are
  the postcard).
- **19:30** — Dinner at the Wharf or a Mission-style burrito
  the size of your forearm (~$15) — the city's great food
  argument settles nothing.

## Day 9 — Bike the bridge, then the painted city

- **09:00** — Rent bikes at the Wharf (~$40/day) and ride the
  flat waterfront to the **Golden Gate Bridge**, then across it —
  2.7 km of orange steel and Pacific wind — downhill into
  **Sausalito**, the Mediterranean-pretty ferry town. Lunch on
  the harbour (~$20–30), **ferry back** with skyline views
  (bikes ride free). Half a day, zero regret; e-bikes flatten
  the one real hill.
- **15:00** — The photogenic afternoon: the **Painted Ladies** at
  Alamo Square, then the **Mission District's** mural alleys
  (Balmy and Clarion) and a proper espresso; with kids swap in
  **Golden Gate Park's** California Academy of Sciences
  (~$40, penguins-to-planetarium).
- **19:00** — Farewell dinner: North Beach Italian, Mission
  Mexican, or crab at the Wharf — book the one you'll dream
  about two weeks ahead.

## Day 10 — Home from SFO

- **Morning** — Last fog-and-sourdough breakfast; BART or Uber to
  SFO, which flies non-stop to most long-haul hubs. Triangle closed — and
  you never touched a steering wheel.

## What to pack

- Layers beat logic here: one suitcase must serve 45°C Vegas
  pavement and 15°C San Francisco fog — light clothes plus a real
  jacket, always.
- Serious sunscreen, cap and a refillable bottle for the desert
  days; the canyon tours are dehydration machines.
- Comfortable shoes (Universal is 15,000 steps; SF's hills are
  free gym), plus sandals for pools.
- Type A/B adapter, contactless card for BART/Muni taps,
  power bank for the 15-hour tour days.
- A dressier outfit if a Vegas show or nice dinner is planned —
  and ID/passport copies: Vegas cards everyone who looks under 35.

## FAQ

**Can this really be done without a car?**
This exact route, yes — that's why it's built as 3+3+3 with
flights and pickup-included tours. What a car *adds* is the
Pacific Coast Highway drive (LA→SF along Big Sur, 2 extra days)
— the greatest road on the continent and first in line for a
future guide. If you'd do that, you need an IDP alongside your
licence and nerves for US freeways.

**West Rim or South Rim for the Grand Canyon?**
Day-trippers: **West Rim** — 2.5 h away, 3.5 h at the canyon,
back for dinner. The **South Rim** is the deeper, grander
national-park experience but costs 9+ hours of bus for 3 hours
of rim. The no-compromise answer is an overnight South Rim
add-on — that's a different (wonderful) trip.

**Is the Skywalk worth $35?**
Split verdict, honestly: the glass floor is a genuine stomach
drop, but cameras are banned (lockers provided) and the queue can
eat 45 minutes. Guano Point, included free, has the better raw
view. Thrill-seekers yes; photographers save the money.

**Is San Francisco safe? We've read things.**
The tourist city — Wharf, North Beach, Chinatown, the parks, the
bridge — is walkable and fine with ordinary city sense. The
rough sights are concentrated: the Tenderloin and parts of SoMa
look hard and are best simply not wandered at night. Carless
visitors dodge the city's actual crime headline (car break-ins)
entirely.

**Vegas with a Muslim family — realistic?**
Yes, on your own terms: the spectacle (fountains, conservatory,
Fremont's canopy, the Sphere) is alcohol-and-gambling-optional,
halal restaurants cluster on and off Strip, and no one blinks at
families. It's loud and billboard-racy — some families love the
circus, some cap it at two nights, which is exactly what this
route does.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
All three cities are easy. LA is a halal heavyweight (halal
In-N-Out it isn't, but Westwood's Persian row and endless
zabiha spots are); Vegas buffets always run vegetarian lines and
halal steak/shawarma houses sit just off-Strip; SF's Tenderloin
edge hides superb Pakistani-Indian kitchens and the Mission does
vegetarian burritos. Apps (Zabihah) beat guesswork.

**Add Yosemite from San Francisco?**
Resist the day-trip temptation: it's 4+ hours each way for a
drive-through glimpse. Yosemite deserves 2 nights and a car —
we'd rather you did this trip properly and came back. (See the
PCH answer above — the same future guide.)
