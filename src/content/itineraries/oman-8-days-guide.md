Oman is what happens when a country has the Gulf's landscapes
and none of its hurry. An hour from Dubai (or one border
post, for the road-trippers this guide especially serves),
you're in a sultanate of real mountains and real history:
forts that never fell, canyon rims a kilometre deep, wadis the
colour of gemstones, and a coastline where sea turtles have
nested since before the pyramids.

The whole classic circuit — Muscat, Nizwa, the jebels, the
Wahiba dunes, the turtle coast — is one relaxed self-drive
loop on immaculate roads, with the friendliest souq culture in
the region: in Oman, nobody chases you, the tea is a gift, and
the price is nearly the price.

**Go October–March** — 25–30°C perfection. April and September
are warm shoulders; **June–August is 45°C+ inland** and for
another guide entirely (Salalah's monsoon-green *khareef* —
see the FAQ). All costs are in Omani rials (OMR; **1 OMR ≈
US$2.60** — the decimal briefing below matters).

## Before you go: visas, borders and the decimal trap

### Getting in

- **Many nationalities enter visa-free for up to 14 days** —
  the EU and UK, the US, Canada, Australia, Japan and others.
  Check yours before assuming you need anything.
- **GCC residents of any nationality can get a visa on
  arrival**, ~OMR 5, ~14 days, at airports and land borders:
  carry a passport with 6+ months, your residence card and a
  residence visa with 3–6 months left. Eligibility checks
  have loosened but still surface.
- **Everyone else, and the certainty play for anyone:** the
  **e-visa at evisa.rop.gov.om** (24–72 h, ~OMR 5–20 by
  duration) before you travel.
- **Indians without GCC residence:** Oman's e-visa via the
  same ROP portal (hotel booking + return ticket; ~OMR 20
  for 30 days) or visa-included packages. GCC nationals:
  no visa at all.
- **Overstays bite:** ~OMR 10/day — respect the stamp.

### The UAE-drive box (your readers' favourite route)

- **Crossings:** Al Wajajah/Hatta (E44 — the Muscat
  workhorse), Khatmat Malaha (E99, via Fujairah), Mezyad
  (Al Ain), plus the two Musandam posts.
- **The kit:** UAE **exit fee AED 35**; **Orange Card**
  cross-border insurance (~**AED 106+ for 5 days**, from
  your insurer or at-border brokers); vehicle **mulkiya**;
  and — the one that catches people — **rental or borrowed
  cars need a notarised, Arabic NOC** from the owner with
  chassis details (most UAE rental firms sell an
  Oman-package; confirm before booking).
- Muscat is ~4.5 h from Dubai via Hatta. Fly-in (1 h,
  multiple daily) + rent in Muscat is this guide's default;
  the drive is the weekender's classic.

### The decimal trap (read twice)

The rial divides into **1,000 baisa**, not 100 — so menus
and taxi quotes mix units: "**500**" means 500 baisa
(**half a rial, ~US$1.30**), while "5" means five rials
(~US$13). Until it's instinct, confirm: "rial or baisa?"
It's the only financial hazard in a country where haggling
is gentle and hassle is near zero.

### Rules of the road (and wadi)

- Roads are superb and **speed cameras are everywhere and
  sincere** — Omani fines arrive with Swiss punctuality.
  Petrol: ~**OMR 0.23/litre** (yes, really).
- **The wadi rule that keeps you alive:** never enter a
  wadi when it's raining anywhere upstream — flash floods
  are Oman's one real danger. Blue sky above you isn't
  enough; check the mountains.
- Modesty basics: shoulders/knees in towns; at wadis, swim
  in shorts + t-shirt/rash guard (both sexes — it's
  respectful and sun-smart). Alcohol: licensed hotel
  venues only. Friday mornings run on prayer time.

## Day 1 — Land in Muscat, Mutrah by evening

- **Afternoon** — Land MCT (1 h from Dubai, ~2.5 h from
  most Gulf points), collect the car (compact 4WD/SUV
  recommended — see FAQ; 2WD works with two tweaks).
- **17:00** — **Mutrah Corniche**: gold-stone merchant
  houses, the harbour fort, the Sultan's improbably
  gigantic yacht.
- **18:00** — **Mutrah Souq** — the Gulf's loveliest:
  frankincense smoke, silver khanjars, sandalwood halls.
  Haggling here is a polite waltz, not combat — 20–30%
  movements, tea offered meaning tea.
- **20:00** — Dinner on the corniche or grilled mishkak
  skewers + fresh juice (~OMR 3–6 pp). Early night —
  the mosque keeps morning hours.

**Where to stay in Muscat** (three nights + one at the
end)

- *The icon:* **The Chedi Muscat** — 4.6/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~4,700 reviews), Zen-Omani serenity and the famous
  103-m pool. ~OMR 180+.
- *Family luxury:* **Kempinski Hotel Muscat** — 4.8/5
  (~3,600 reviews), beachfront at Al Mouj. ~OMR 110–160.
- *Value:* **ibis/Golden Tulip tier, Ruwi–Qurum** — clean,
  cheap, central. ~OMR 25–40.

## Day 2 — The Grand Mosque, then Old Muscat

- **08:00** — **Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque** (free;
  **non-Muslim visiting hours Sat–Thu 08:00–11:00 only**,
  closed Fridays; women cover hair/arms/ankles — abayas
  lent at the gate): the 4,200 m² single-piece carpet,
  the eight-tonne Swarovski chandelier, marble courtyards
  for 20,000 — the Gulf's most beautiful building, and
  it's not close.
- **11:30** — **Royal Opera House** (exterior always;
  morning tours ~OMR 3) — the Sultanate's marble music
  box.
- **13:00** — Lunch: Omani majboos or the excellent
  Turkish/Lebanese row in Qurum (~OMR 3–6).
- **15:00** — **Old Muscat**: the ceremonial **Al Alam
  Palace** gates flanked by Portuguese forts Jalali and
  Mirani, then the superb **National Museum** (OMR 5).
- **17:30** — Qurum Beach sunset walk; karak from a
  kiosk (200 baisa — deploy the decimal knowledge).
- **20:00** — Seafood grill dinner (~OMR 6–10 pp).

## Day 3 — Forts and oases: Nizwa

Aim to sleep in Nizwa **Thursday night** if you can — the
Friday goat market is the reason.

- **09:00** — Drive inland (1.5 h on a motorway through
  the Hajar wall) via **Birkat Al Mouz**: abandoned
  mud-brick terraces and a UNESCO **falaj** channel
  running since antiquity.
- **11:30** — **Nizwa Fort** (**OMR 5**): the great round
  cannon tower — climb past murder holes and date-honey
  chutes to the palm-oasis panorama that explains
  everything about interior Oman.
- **13:00** — Date-and-kahwa tasting in the souq arcades;
  lunch nearby (~OMR 3–5).
- **15:00** — Side-trip: **Bahla Fort** (UNESCO,
  exterior grandeur) and **Jabreen Castle** (OMR 3 —
  painted ceilings, the connoisseurs' favourite), 40 min
  west.
- **18:00** — Nizwa souq at dusk: pottery, silver,
  Omani halwa (saffron-rose, sold warm).
- **Friday bonus, 06:30:** the **goat market** — a
  centuries-old auction circle of bleating commerce,
  utterly ungentrified. Best free show in Arabia.

**Where to stay in Nizwa** (one night): the **Antique
Inn** tier of restored old-town guesthouses (~OMR 35–50)
or Falaj Daris' garden-pool classic (~OMR 45–60).

## Day 4 — A kilometre of canyon, then the dunes

Two mountains, one choice (both need the early start):

- **Jebel Shams** — "the Grand Canyon of Arabia": drive
  to the 2,000-m rim (last stretch gravel — 4WD
  advised, careful 2WDs make it in dry weather) for
  **Wadi Ghul's** sheer kilometre drop, and walk a
  stretch of the **Balcony Walk (W6)** to the abandoned
  cliff village. Free, staggering, uncrowded.
- **Jebel Akhdar** — the Green Mountain's rose terraces
  and rim viewpoints (Diana's Point): **strictly
  4WD-only — a police checkpoint at the base turns 2WDs
  around**, no exceptions. April = rose harvest.
- **14:30** — Descend and cross to the **Wahiba Sands**
  edge (~2.5 h): meet your camp's 4WD at the tarmac
  (standard pickup — the 2WD driver's friend) or
  deflate-and-drive if experienced.
- **16:30** — **Dune evening**: sunset from the ridge
  (camps run dune-bashing and camels; sandboards free),
  then dinner under a sky with no light pollution
  within 80 km — the Milky Way performs.

**The camps** (one night, half-board): the **1000
Nights / Desert Nights Camp tier** runs ~OMR 35–60 pp
HB in standard tents, ~OMR 70–100+ for ensuite
"luxury" — book direct, confirm pickup point.

## Day 5 — Emerald pools, dhows, then turtles by torchlight

- **06:30** — Dune sunrise, Omani breakfast, out of the
  sands.
- **09:30** — **Wadi Bani Khalid** (free): palm-lined
  emerald pools between ochre cliffs — the classic
  swim (shorts-and-shirt rule; little fish provide a
  free pedicure whether requested or not).
- **13:00** — Coast road to **Sur**: lunch, then the
  **dhow yards** (free) — the last place on earth
  hand-building ocean-going wooden dhows — and the
  Ayjah lighthouse across the lagoon.
- **17:00** — On to **Ras Al Jinz**; check in, early
  dinner.
- **21:00** — **The turtle tour** (**~OMR 8–9,
  reserve-guided**): green turtles hauling ashore to
  nest — laying, covering, and hatchlings scrambling
  seaward in season. **The rules are the magic:** no
  phones, no white light, guide's red torch only,
  whisper distance. Peak nesting June–September, but
  this beach hosts arrivals essentially year-round;
  the 04:00 dawn tour is the photographers'
  (soft-light) alternative.

**Stay:** the **Ras Al Jinz Turtle Reserve** lodge
itself (~OMR 60–90 — you're steps from the briefing
room) or Sur's hotels 40 min back.

## Day 6 — Wadi Shab: the swim of the year

- **08:30** — North up the coast to **Wadi Shab**
  (arrive by 9:30 to beat tour buses): the **1-OMR
  ferry** across the lagoon, then a 45-minute canyon
  walk past falaj channels and plantations to the
  pools.
- **10:30** — The main event: swim three linked
  turquoise pools, then **through the rock crack**
  (a metre wide, swim-only) into the hidden
  **waterfall cave** — the single best adventure hour
  in the Gulf. Water shoes and a dry-bag; confident
  swimmers only for the cave (the outer pools suit
  everyone).
- **13:30** — Fishing-village lunch in Tiwi (~OMR 3–5).
- **15:00** — **Bimmah Sinkhole** (free): the
  turquoise collapse crater with its swimming
  staircase — 30 refreshing minutes.
- **17:30** — Motorway home to Muscat (90 min);
  farewell-eve shawarma or hotel-beach dinner.

## Day 7 — Muscat finale, sea optional

- **08:00** — **Dolphin watching** from Marina Bandar
  Al Rowdha (~OMR 12–15, 2 h — spinner superpods most
  mornings; Oct–Mar sea-state kindest) — or a lazy
  Qantab beach morning.
- **11:30** — **Amouage** perfume factory tour (the
  Gulf's great fragrance house; visitor centre) or
  the Bait Al Zubair museum.
- **14:00** — Last souq run: frankincense (Hojari
  grade, with a burner), Omani halwa, a khanjar for
  the wall — checked luggage, as ever.
- **19:00** — The farewell feast: **shuwa** if you
  find it (festival lamb, banana-leaf-and-sand-oven
  slow-cooked) or lobster majboos on the water
  (~OMR 8–15 pp), kahwa and dates to close — the
  ritual that started every Omani welcome all week.

## Day 8 — Home

- **Morning** — Return the car (fuel bill: a comedy),
  fly out of MCT — or point the bonnet at Hatta with
  the Orange Card in the glovebox and the halwa tub
  sweating gently in the boot.

## What to pack

- Wadi kit: water shoes, quick-dry shorts +
  rash-guard/t-shirt swimwear, dry-bag for the cave
  swim, microfibre towel.
- Mosque/fort modesty: light long layers; women a
  headscarf for the Grand Mosque (lent, but yours
  fits better).
- A warm layer — desert nights drop to 10–14°C in
  winter, and Jebel Shams' rim runs 15°C below
  Muscat.
- Sun kit at full Gulf strength; a cheche/scarf for
  the dunes.
- Torch with a **red mode** if you own one (turtle
  beach), power bank, car phone-mount.
- The decimal mantra: *rial or baisa?* — practised
  aloud once, saved daily.

## FAQ

**Do I really need a 4WD?**
Honest answer: **2WD completes 90% of this loop** —
motorways reach Nizwa, Sur and the wadis, desert
camps collect you from the tarmac, and careful 2WDs
manage Jebel Shams' gravel in dry weather. The two
hard exceptions: **Jebel Akhdar's police checkpoint
(4WD or turned around)** and driving *into* the
sands yourself. A compact 4WD (~OMR 10–15/day extra)
simply deletes every asterisk.

**Which visa route am I, exactly?**
GCC nationals: none. **UAE residents (any
nationality): visa-on-arrival ~OMR 5** with Emirates
ID + valid residency — with the honest caveat that
eligibility screening still occasionally surfaces,
so the **ROP e-visa 3–4 days ahead is the
zero-drama option**. Indians without GCC residence:
the ROP e-visa (~OMR 20/30 days) with hotel + return
proof. All roads run through evisa.rop.gov.om — no
agent needed.

**Will we actually see turtles?**
At Ras Al Jinz, near-certainly in **June–September**
(dozens nightly) and very probably in any month —
the reserve tracks arrivals and won't march you out
to an empty beach. The dawn tour adds hatchling odds
and photographable light. The contract: red light,
silence, distance — the guides enforce it because
it works.

**Is Oman safe? What's the hassle level?**
Among the safest countries on earth, full stop —
solo women road-trippers routinely rate it the
easiest Middle East trip they've made. The souq
hustle famous elsewhere simply doesn't exist here;
your risks are sunburn, speed cameras and
over-purchasing frankincense.

**Oman with kids?**
Superb: short drives, forts with cannons,
sinkhole swims, dune-sledding, turtle hatchlings —
and Omani restaurants treat children as honoured
guests. The two watch-items: wadi currents after
rain (the rule above) and the canyon rim's unfenced
edges — hold hands at Shams.

**What about Salalah and Musandam?**
Both are their own trips and both are coming:
**Salalah's khareef** (June–September, when the
desert turns Kerala-green under monsoon mist) is
the seasonal special candidate, and **Musandam's
fjords** the long-weekend dhow trip. This loop is
Oman's spine; those are its wings.
