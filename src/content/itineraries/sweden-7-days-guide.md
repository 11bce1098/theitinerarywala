Stockholm is built on fourteen islands and looks out at roughly
thirty thousand more. That geography is the trip: a compact,
walkable capital where you are never far from water, and a ferry
network that turns an afternoon into an island day.

The city itself holds two things worth crossing a continent for. The
**Vasa** — a warship that sank on her maiden voyage in 1628, sat in
the mud for 333 years, and was raised almost intact — and **Gamla
Stan**, the old town, which is a medieval street plan painted in
ochre and rust.

Seven days is enough for the city properly, two island days, and a
short run to **Uppsala**. Go in **June or July** if you can: the
evenings stretch past ten and the whole country moves outdoors.

## Before you go: cashless, and the seasons

### Entry, and the season that changes everything

- **Sweden is in Schengen**, so one visa covers it along with the
  rest of the area. The process, the lead times and the documents
  that cause refusals are in our
  [Schengen visa guide](/guides/schengen-visa-how-it-works/).
- Visa-free nationalities need nothing beyond a passport, with the
  **EES biometric border** now fully operational — allow extra time
  on first arrival.
- **Sweden is not in the euro.** It uses the **krona (SEK)**.
- **The season is the trip.** **June to August** gives long light,
  warm water and an archipelago in full use. **December** gives
  Christmas markets and dark by three. **November and February** give
  neither, and are best avoided unless the flights are free.

### Genuinely cashless

- **Sweden is the most cashless country you will visit.** Many
  cafés, museums and buses **do not accept cash at all** — this is
  not a preference, it is a sign on the door.
- Bring a card that works contactlessly and a backup on a different
  network. Cash is close to useless; do not change money.
- **Decline the terminal's offer to bill you in your home currency**
  — the markup is 5–10%.
- Sweden is expensive, but less so than its reputation if you eat
  where locals eat: the **dagens lunch** set menu at midday costs a
  fraction of the same food at dinner.

### Getting around

- **Stockholm's transport is one system** — metro, bus, tram and
  commuter ferry on a single travel card, bought in the app.
- **The metro is an attraction.** Many stations are carved into
  bedrock and painted; the blue line is the famous one, and riding
  it counts as sightseeing.
- **Archipelago ferries** run from the city centre. Summer service
  is frequent; off-season it thins sharply, so check the timetable
  before planning an island day.
- **Uppsala is 40 minutes by train**, frequent, and an easy half-day.

### The habits

- **Fika is not a coffee break, it is an institution** — coffee and
  something sweet, sitting down, usually twice a day. Take part.
- **Shoes off indoors**, always, including in many small guesthouses.
- **Allemansrätten**, the right to roam, means you may walk and swim
  almost anywhere in nature, provided you disturb nothing and leave
  nothing.
- English is close to universal and spoken very well.

## Day 1 — Land, and the old town at dusk

- **Afternoon** — Land, drop bags, walk into **Gamla Stan** — the
  narrow lanes, **Stortorget**'s painted facades and the palace.
- **Evening** — Dinner in the old town's back streets rather than on
  the main drag, then the waterfront as the light goes — which in
  June it barely does.

## Day 2 — The Vasa, and Djurgården

- **Morning** — The **Vasa Museum**, early, before the tour groups.
  Allow two hours; you will want them.
- **Afternoon** — **Djurgården** island: **Skansen**'s open-air
  museum, or the **ABBA** museum, or simply the park.
- **Evening** — **Södermalm** for dinner — the hilly, scruffier,
  better-eating side of the city.

## Day 3 — Out to the archipelago

- **Morning** — Ferry to **Vaxholm** or **Grinda**, an hour or two
  out, past red wooden cottages and bare rock.
- **Afternoon** — Swim if it is summer. Walk if it is not.
- **Evening** — Back on the late ferry, which in midsummer runs in
  full daylight.

## Day 4 — Museums, and the metro

- **Morning** — **Fotografiska** for photography, or the
  **Nationalmuseum** for the older collection.
- **Afternoon** — Ride the **blue line** for the cave stations, then
  **Kungsholmen** for the City Hall where the Nobel banquet is held.
- **Evening** — Fika properly, in a place with a queue.

## Day 5 — Uppsala

- **Morning** — Train north, 40 minutes. The **cathedral** is the
  tallest in Scandinavia; the **university** is the oldest in the
  Nordics.
- **Afternoon** — **Gamla Uppsala**'s royal burial mounds, which
  predate everything else on this trip by a very long way.
- **Evening** — Back to Stockholm.

## Day 6 — Stockholm your way

- Options: a second island, **Drottningholm** palace by boat, the
  **Moderna Museet**, or a long slow day of markets and fika.
- **Evening** — Last dinner. Try the herring, honestly prepared.

## Day 7 — Fly home

- Arlanda is 20 minutes by express train from the centre, and
  considerably cheaper by commuter train if you have the time.

## What to pack

- **Layers, even in July.** The archipelago is cooler than the city
  and the wind comes off the Baltic.
- **A swimsuit in summer.** Swimming from rocks is the point of an
  island day.
- **European Type C/F plugs.**
- **Comfortable shoes** — Stockholm is hillier than it looks,
  especially Södermalm.
- **An eye mask in June** if you are a light sleeper; it does not
  get properly dark.

## FAQ

**Is Sweden as expensive as people say?**
Beds and restaurant dinners, yes. Transport, museums and supermarket
food, no. Eating the set lunch and cooking one or two nights changes
the number substantially.

**Do I need any cash at all?**
Almost certainly not. Many places refuse it outright. Bring two
cards instead.

**Is seven days enough?**
For Stockholm and the archipelago, comfortably. Adding Lapland or
Gothenburg means adding four days — they are a long way north and
west respectively.

**When should I go?**
**June and July** for the light and the islands. **December** for
the Christmas markets and the dark cosiness. Avoid November and
February, which offer neither.
