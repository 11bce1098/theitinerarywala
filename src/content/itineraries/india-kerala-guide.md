If the Golden Triangle is India at full volume, Kerala is the same
country with the dial turned to evening: spice-trade lanes and
antique synagogues in Kochi, tea estates stacked to the clouds in
Munnar, a night drifting through palm-canal villages on your own
houseboat, and a beach where the loudest thing is a fishing boat
coming in. It's India's easiest introduction and its best
recovery — many travellers do our Golden Triangle guide first and
this one as the exhale.

**Go October–March** — dry, green, 28–32°C on the coast and a cool
20°C in Munnar. The monsoon (June–September) is its own product:
everything intensely green, hotel prices halved, and the
traditional **Ayurveda season** (the humidity is considered ideal
for treatment) — a fair trade if you accept daily rain. One
calendar trap: **Eravikulam National Park closes for roughly
February–March** (Nilgiri tahr calving season) — if the wildlife
stop matters to you, travel outside those weeks.

All costs are in Indian rupees (INR), with conversions calculated
at today's exchange rate. **Indian passport and OCI holders pay
domestic rates at the ticketed sights.**

## Before you go: the short version of the admin

Visas, UPI, SIMs and the scam list are identical to our Golden
Triangle guide, where they're covered in depth (short form: e-visa
for 171 nationalities at the official site only; the **UPI One
World wallet** lets G20-nationality visitors pay by QR like
locals; Airtel/Jio SIM at the airport). Kerala-specific notes:

- **Fly into Kochi (COK)** — direct flights from across the Gulf
  (this is the UAE's most-connected Indian state) and every Indian
  hub. The airport is ~40 km from Fort Kochi.
- **Hire a car with driver for the whole arc** (~₹3,000–4,000/day
  including fuel; arranged by any hotel or Kerala operator) — it's
  the standard way Kerala is done, costs less than two Gulf
  taxi days, and turns every transfer into sightseeing. Self-drive
  is possible but the hill hairpins and Indian traffic argue
  otherwise.
- **Book the houseboat before anything else** — the good boats and
  the Dec 20–Jan 5 peak (when rates double) go first. Book direct
  with operators rather than aggregator markups.
- Kerala is calmer than north India — the tout pressure of Delhi
  and Agra largely doesn't exist here — but the same app-taxi and
  official-counter habits still serve.

### Travelling with kids?

- Kerala may be India's best family destination: short drives,
  pools everywhere, a houseboat that doubles as the world's
  slowest theme-park ride, and beaches with gentle shelves
  (respect rip warnings and swim where locals do).
- Munnar's altitude means mosquito relief; the coast needs
  repellent at dusk.
- Kathakali's demon faces delight or terrify by temperament —
  the pre-show makeup session usually wins them over.

## Day 1 — Land in Kochi, evening among the fishing nets

- **13:00** — Land at Kochi, meet your driver, ~1 h to **Fort
  Kochi**. Check in, late lunch at a heritage café (**Kashi Art
  Café** is the institution).
- **16:30** — Walk the seafront to the **Chinese fishing nets** —
  the giant cantilevered structures the city has raised and
  lowered for six centuries; sunset behind them is Kochi's
  signature image. A small tip gets you onto a platform to help
  work one.
- **18:00** — **St Francis Church** (free) — India's oldest
  European church, where Vasco da Gama was first buried.
- **19:30** — Dinner: fresh seafood grilled at the net-side
  stalls (pick, pay, they cook) or a proper Keralan fish molee in
  a heritage restaurant.

**Where to stay in Kochi** (two nights, Fort Kochi side)

- *Heritage splurge:* **Brunton Boatyard (CGH Earth)** — a
  shipyard reborn as the city's grandest waterfront address.
- *Mid-range:* **Forte Kochi** — superbly rated colonial charmer
  on Princess Street, pool included.
- *Budget:* **Tissa's Inn** — the homestay-style boutique that
  outscores hotels twice its price.

## Day 2 — Spice lanes, a synagogue and painted demons

- **9:00** — **Mattancherry / Jew Town** by tuk-tuk or cycle: the
  **Dutch Palace** (small fee; closed Fridays) with its Ramayana
  murals, the antique warehouses, and the ginger-and-cardamom
  scent of the working spice godowns.
- **11:00** — **Paradesi Synagogue** (Sun–Thu ~10:00–12:00 and
  15:00–17:00; small fee; modest dress) — 1568, Belgian
  chandeliers, hand-painted Chinese floor tiles; the queue moves
  fast, the closing hours don't bend.
- **13:00** — Lunch in Jew Town or back in Fort Kochi; try a
  banana-leaf sadya thali somewhere busy.
- **15:00** — Slow afternoon: the Indo-Portuguese Museum, gallery
  hopping, or the ferry across to Ernakulam's markets (pennies,
  and the skyline view back).
- **17:00** — **Kerala Kathakali Centre**: arrive for the
  **make-up session** (~17:00) — watching the dancers paint the
  green hero-faces and demon reds is half the show — then the
  performance itself (~18:00; ~₹400–500). Loud, strange,
  unforgettable.
- **20:00** — Dinner and pack: the hills tomorrow.

## Day 3 — Climb to Munnar, tea to the horizon

- **8:30** — Drive to **Munnar** (~4 h, climbing 1,500 m) — the
  road itself is the attraction: rubber plantations, waterfalls
  (Cheeyappara mid-route), then suddenly the first tea slopes.
- **13:00** — Lunch in Munnar town, check in.
- **15:00** — **Tea Museum** (closed Mondays typically; small fee)
  — the colonial-era machinery still rolling leaf, with a tasting —
  then walk into the estate paths behind town: the manicured
  green corduroy in every direction is why you came.
- **17:30** — Sunset viewpoint chai; early dinner — Munnar sleeps
  early and so should you, if you take tomorrow's sunrise option.

**Where to stay in Munnar** (two nights)

- *Upscale:* **Eden Woods Resorts & Spa** — superbly rated,
  stream-side calm outside town.
- *Mid-range:* **Sterling Munnar** — big, comfortable, valley
  views, family-easy.
- *Budget:* **KTDC Tea County** — the state-run classic in town;
  reliable and well kept.

## Day 4 — Above the clouds: tahr, peaks and the highest tea

- **Option A, the big one — 4:30** — Jeep to **Kolukkumalai**
  (~₹3,000–4,000 per jeep, ~3 h round trip on a spectacularly bad
  road): the **world's highest organic tea estate** at 2,400 m for
  a sunrise above a sea of clouds, then a tour of the 1930s
  factory. The road is the price of the view; both are extreme.
- **Option B, the gentle one — 7:30** — **Eravikulam National
  Park** (7:30–16:00; foreigners ₹500, Indians ₹200; forest-bus
  shuttle only; **closed ~Feb–Mar for calving**; book online in
  peak season) — near-guaranteed sightings of the endangered
  **Nilgiri tahr** on the high grasslands under Anamudi, south
  India's highest peak.
- **13:00** — Lunch back toward town.
- **15:00** — The eastern loop drive: **Mattupetty Dam** and its
  lake, **Echo Point**, and up to **Top Station** on the Tamil
  Nadu border — the grandest viewpoint in the Western Ghats when
  the clouds allow.
- **19:00** — Dinner; try Kerala's beef fry with parotta — the
  hill-country classic.

## Day 5 — Down to the backwaters, aboard your houseboat

- **8:00** — Drive down the ghats to **Alleppey** (~5 h through
  spice and rubber country).
- **12:00** — **Board your houseboat** (noon check-in; deluxe
  1-bedroom from ~₹10,500/night all-inclusive — meals, crew,
  fuel; premium and luxury tiers above; **rates roughly double
  Dec 20–Jan 5**). Your crew of two-three cooks and pilots; you
  do nothing, which is the entire point.
- **12:30–18:00** — Cruise: out across **Vembanad Lake**, then
  into the narrow canals — paddy fields below water level, kids
  waving from school boats, kingfishers on every wire. Lunch on
  board is Keralan home cooking; the karimeen (pearl spot fish)
  is the order.
- **17:30** — Boats moor by village banks before dusk (rule, not
  choice): walk the towpath, watch the light die over the paddies,
  dinner on deck under the stars.

## Day 6 — Backwater morning, then the beach

- **6:30** — The best hour of the trip: mist on the water,
  fishermen setting nets, chai on deck. Optional village
  **shikara or kayak** detour into the smallest canals
  (~₹750–1,000/person) — the houseboat can't fit where the real
  life is.
- **9:30** — Disembark at Alleppey; 30 minutes north to
  **Marari Beach** — a fishing-village coast of palms and sand
  with none of Goa's noise.
- **11:00** — Check in, and downshift: this is the trip's
  do-nothing chapter, by design.
- **16:00** — Walk the beach as the fishing fleet comes in;
  fresh-catch dinner at a beach shack or your resort.

**Where to stay at Marari** (three nights)

- Marari runs from barefoot-luxury eco resorts (the famous
  beachfront ones with Ayurveda centres, ~₹12,000–25,000) down to
  family homestays a lane back from the sand (~₹3,000–6,000).
  Book by current reviews and beach proximity; December–January
  needs weeks of notice.

## Day 7 — Beach and Ayurveda

- **Morning** — Nothing, professionally. Swim early (calmest
  water), breakfast late.
- **14:00** — First **Ayurveda session** — Kerala is the home of
  the practice, and even sceptics surrender to an abhyanga
  massage (~₹2,000–3,500 at reputable centres; multi-day
  treatment courses need a doctor consult first — real Ayurveda
  is medical, not spa fluff, and good centres behave accordingly).
- **17:00** — Village walk or cycle: the church, the fish market,
  the toddy shop if you're curious (palm wine, an acquired
  taste, acquired quickly).

## Day 8 — The spare day — three ways to spend it

- **Option A — More nothing.** The correct answer for most.
- **Option B — Alleppey by land:** the beach and lighthouse, a
  second shikara ride at sunset, chai by the canals.
- **Option C — Culture finale:** a **village cooking class**
  (fish curry, appam — the souvenir skill) or a temple-festival
  hunt if the season obliges (November–April is festival
  country; ask your hosts what's on — elephants and drums may be
  an evening away).
- **Evening** — Last seafood feast; pack for the morning drive.

## Day 9 — Fly home from Kochi

- **Morning** — Drive to Kochi airport (~1.5 h; leave buffer for
  Kerala traffic), fly out — Gulf travellers are home by
  afternoon on the direct flights.

## What to pack

- Light cottons + one warm layer: Munnar evenings hit 12–15°C
  while the coast stays at 30°C.
- Mosquito repellent for the coast and backwaters at dusk;
  Munnar's altitude is the break from it.
- Modest cover for churches, the synagogue and temples; a scarf
  as ever.
- Reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, and shoes you can wet.
- Motion-sickness tablets for the ghat hairpins if you're prone.
- Old clothes for the Ayurveda oil sessions — the oil wins.
- Space for tea from Munnar, spices from Mattancherry, and
  banana chips you'll ration badly.

## FAQ

**Golden Triangle or Kerala first?**
Either works; they're opposite Indias. Triangle first if you want
icons and intensity, Kerala first if you want a soft landing. The
classic combination is Triangle then Kerala — chaos, then cure —
and 16 days covers both of our guides back to back.

**Is one night on the houseboat enough?**
For most, yes — it's a magical 22 hours and the novelty arc is
complete by the second morning. Two nights suit the truly
unhurried (the Alleppey–Kumarakom route earns it); day-cruises
miss the moonlit mooring, which is the best part.

**Is Kerala safe / good for families and older travellers?**
Among India's most relaxed states for all three: high literacy,
low hassle, strong healthcare, short drives. The usual sun,
traffic and sea-swimming cautions are the honest list.

**What about vegetarians and halal food?**
Both easy. Kerala's vegetarian repertoire (sadya feasts, thoran,
avial, appam with stew) is a cuisine in itself, and the state's
large Muslim community means halal kitchens are common —
Malabar biryani is reason alone.

**Monsoon — really an option?**
Genuinely, with eyes open: June–September means daily rain
(usually in bursts, not all day), the greenest landscapes of the
year, half-price hotels and prime Ayurveda. Houseboats still run;
beaches are for walking not swimming. Wrong for a first-time
photo trip; right for a slow, cheap, restorative one.

**What's the natural extension?**
South to **Varkala's** cliff beaches (2.5 h), east to **Thekkady/
Periyar** for spice gardens and wildlife between Munnar and
Alleppey (+1–2 days), or the overnight train north to Goa. Our
Golden Triangle guide covers the other India entirely.
