Nepal compresses more wonder per kilometre than anywhere on this
site. In one small country: seven UNESCO monuments in a single
valley, a lake town where the Annapurna range hangs over
breakfast like a painted backdrop, jungle lowlands where
one-horned rhinos outnumber tourists — and, on a clear morning,
a scheduled airliner that flies you past **Mount Everest** and
home in time for a second breakfast.

It is the best-value Himalaya there is, and for Indian
passports the friendliest border on earth — **no visa, no fee,
no form**. Everyone else gets a visa on arrival that takes
about fifteen minutes. This first Nepal guide is the
**no-trekking classic**: temples, mountains and rhinos with a
hotel bed every night. (The trekking specials — Poon Hill, and
the Everest Base Camp epic — are coming; see the FAQ.)

**Go October–November** — post-monsoon crystal air, the
mountains at their sharpest, and festival season (Dashain/
Tihar) as a bonus — **or March–April** (rhododendrons, warm).
December–February is cold but clear; **June–September is
monsoon**: green, cheap, and the peaks mostly hide. All costs
are in Nepali rupees (NPR).

## Before you go: the open border and the rupee-note rule

- **Indian passports: no visa exists.** Fly in with your
  **passport** (recommended; an original Election Commission
  **Voter ID** also works, especially at land borders —
  Aadhaar/PAN/licences do **not**). Children under 15: birth
  certificate + parent's ID. No fee, no stay-limit stamp for
  tourism. It is genuinely that simple.
- **Everyone else:** visa on arrival at Kathmandu airport —
  ~US$30/15 days, US$50/30 days, kiosk + cash counter;
  UAE and most passports qualify.
- **The rule that catches Indian travellers: ₹500 and ₹2,000
  Indian notes are not legal in Nepal.** Carry ₹100s (widely
  accepted at ~1.6 NPR) or just use ATMs/cards. Getting
  caught changing big INR notes is a genuine hassle.
- **SAARC pricing is your friend again:** Indian/SAARC rates
  run a fraction of foreigner tickets at the heritage sites —
  and **Pashupatinath is free for Indians** (foreigners
  NPR 1,000, and non-Hindus can't enter the main sanctum
  at all).
- **The aviation honesty, plainly:** Nepal's domestic flying
  has a patchier safety record than its neighbours. The
  sensible protocol this guide follows: **Buddha Air or Yeti
  ATRs, morning flights, never connect a Pokhara flight to
  your international departure same-day** (weather delays
  are routine — build the buffer night in, as this
  itinerary does). Roads are the alternative: slow, bumpy,
  scenic, fine.

## Money, altitude and small print

- Cards work at hotels and nicer restaurants; **Thamel
  stalls, taxis and temples run on cash** — NPR 500s and
  100s. ATMs everywhere (fee ~NPR 500).
- Tipping: not formalised — NPR 100–200 for drivers/porters,
  ~10% at tourist restaurants, guide tips by day.
- **No altitude worries on this route** (Kathmandu 1,400 m,
  Sarangkot 1,600 m) — this is the Himalaya at
  walking-around height.
- Kathmandu's dust is real: a light mask for traffic hours
  helps sensitive lungs. Bottled/filtered water only.
- Bargain in Thamel with a smile (start ~half), never at
  fixed-price shops; pashmina, singing bowls and khukuri
  knives are the classic hauls (knife goes in checked
  luggage!).

## Day 1 — Land in Kathmandu, Thamel evening

- **Afternoon** — Land KTM (4 h from the Gulf; 1.5–2 h from
  Delhi). Prepaid taxi into town (~NPR 800–1,000).
- **17:00** — First wander in **Thamel** — the joyful
  trekking-shop bazaar-maze — with a decompression stop in
  the **Garden of Dreams** (NPR 400, neo-classical calm).
- **19:30** — First **momos** (Nepal's dumpling religion,
  NPR 200–400 a plate) and thukpa; a Nepali band covering
  classic rock somewhere nearby is contractually
  guaranteed.

**Where to stay in Kathmandu** (three nights + one at the
end)

- *The legend:* **The Dwarika's** — 4.8/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~1,500 reviews), a museum-grade heritage hotel of
  carved-wood salvage; its Krishnarpan restaurant serves
  6–22 course Nepali feasts. ~NPR 40,000+.
- *Thamel calm:* **Oasis Kathmandu Hotel** — 4.9/5 (~2,200
  reviews), a quiet courtyard two lanes from the noise.
  ~NPR 8,000–12,000.
- *Value:* **Aryatara Kathmandu** — 4.9/5 (~1,700 reviews),
  breakfast included, walk-everywhere. ~NPR 5,000–7,500.

## Day 2 — The holy circuit: monkeys, kings and the great aarti

- **07:00** — **Swayambhunath** (the "Monkey Temple";
  NPR 200 foreigners/SAARC less): 365 steps to the
  all-seeing eyes, the whole valley waking below, monkeys
  running the place with confidence. Hold your snacks.
- **10:00** — **Kathmandu Durbar Square** (foreigners
  NPR 1,000 / **SAARC ~150**): the medieval royal plaza —
  Kasthamandap, the towering Taleju temple, and the
  **Kumari Ghar**, home of the living goddess (she appears
  at her window some mid-days; no photos of her).
- **13:00** — Newari lunch: bara, choila, the works
  (~NPR 400–700 pp).
- **16:00** — **Pashupatinath** — Nepal's holiest Shiva
  temple on the Bagmati (**free for Indians, who may enter
  the sanctum; foreigners NPR 1,000, outer complex only**).
  Walk the ghats respectfully — **cremations burn here
  around the clock; no photographs of the pyres** — and
  the sadhu-lined terraces above.
- **18:00** — Stay for the **evening Bagmati aarti**
  (~18:00, nightly): fire, bells and bhajans on the river
  steps — Varanasi's intimacy at a tenth of the crowd. The
  single most moving hour in Kathmandu.
- **20:00** — Dinner back in Thamel; sleep well — you've
  earned all three religions' blessings.

## Day 3 — Boudhanath breakfast, Bhaktapur afternoon

- **08:00** — **Boudhanath** (NPR 400): the white
  whale-eye stupa, one of the largest on earth — join the
  clockwise **kora** with the morning pilgrims, prayer
  wheels spinning, then breakfast on a rooftop café
  staring at the eyes.
- **11:00** — Drive 40 minutes east to **Bhaktapur**
  (foreigners NPR 1,800 / **SAARC ~500**): the best-
  preserved medieval city in the valley — the 55-Window
  Palace, five-tiered **Nyatapola** temple, pottery square
  where the wheels still spin. Eat **juju dhau** — the
  "king of curds" — from a clay bowl (NPR 100; a top-five
  Nepal memory for most visitors).
- **16:30** — Back to Kathmandu; **Patan's** golden Durbar
  Square (SAARC ~250) if energy allows — its museum is
  Nepal's best.
- **19:30** — Farewell-for-now dinner; pack a small bag —
  mountains tomorrow.

## Day 4 — Fly to Pokhara, float on Phewa Lake

- **09:00** — KTM → **Pokhara, 25 minutes** (Buddha/Yeti,
  from **~US$80–110**; **sit LEFT side** — the entire
  Himalaya files past the window). The tourist bus
  (~NPR 1,500–2,500, 7–8 winding hours) is the budget
  alternative.
- **11:00** — Check in **Lakeside**: Pokhara runs at half
  Kathmandu's pulse and twice its oxygen.
- **14:00** — Row (or be rowed) across **Phewa Lake**
  (~NPR 700–1,000/boat/hour) to the **Tal Barahi** island
  temple; Machhapuchhare — the "Fishtail" — floats on the
  water when clouds allow.
- **17:30** — Lakeside strip sunset: juice bars, bookshops,
  the mountains going pink.
- **19:30** — Lakeside dinner (~NPR 500–900 pp) — the
  town's Israeli-Italian-Indian-everything menus are a
  trekking-town inheritance.

**Where to stay in Pokhara** (three nights)

- *The icon:* **Fish Tail Lodge** — 4.7/5 (~1,800
  reviews), on its own peninsula, reached by rope-drawn
  pontoon; Annapurna views from the lawn. ~NPR 18,000–
  25,000.
- *Boutique:* **Temple Tree Resort & Spa** — 4.7/5 (~2,100
  reviews), pool and spa off the strip. ~NPR 14,000–
  20,000.
- *Value:* **Hotel Middle Path & Spa** — 4.9/5 (~1,700
  reviews), lakeside location at guesthouse money.
  ~NPR 6,000–9,000.

## Day 5 — Sarangkot sunrise, then (optionally) jump off it

- **04:45** — Drive up **Sarangkot** (1,600 m; ~NPR 200
  entry) for the sunrise that sells Nepal: first light
  igniting **Annapurna South, Machhapuchhare and
  Dhaulagiri** across a 140-km white wall. Cold, crowded
  at the rail, unforgettable. Tea at the top.
- **08:30** — **The paragliding option** (~NPR 10,000–
  12,000 for a 20–30 min tandem, video extra): Sarangkot
  is one of the world's great commercial flying sites —
  launch over the lake with hawks thermalling beside you.
  Operators are well-drilled; weather cancels honestly.
- **11:30** — Cross the lake and climb (or drive) to the
  **World Peace Pagoda** — the white stupa with the
  full lake-mountains-city panorama.
- **14:30** — The quick hits: **Davis Falls** (NPR 100),
  the **Gupteshwor Mahadev cave** opposite (a Shiva
  shrine deep under the waterfall — pilgrimage bonus for
  the devout), optional Seti gorge peek.
- **18:00** — Massage (~NPR 2,000–3,000/hour) or an early
  rooftop dinner as the fishtail catches the last light.

## Day 6 — The slow Pokhara day

- **Menu, pick freely:** the excellent **International
  Mountain Museum** (NPR 600 — Everest history, Sherpa
  culture, mountaineering kit through the ages); cycle
  the lake's quiet north shore; boat-and-swim at cleaner
  **Begnas Lake** (40 min away, blissfully local); a
  momo-making class; or absolutely nothing on a lakeside
  lawn — Pokhara's true specialty.
- **19:00** — Dal bhat done properly (the "dal bhat power,
  24 hour" plate — unlimited refills are the custom,
  ~NPR 400–800): tomorrow needs jungle energy.

## Day 7 — Down to the jungle: Chitwan

- **08:30** — Drive south (4–5 h, tourist bus ~NPR 1,500
  or private car ~NPR 12,000/vehicle) off the hills onto
  the steamy Terai plain — Nepal changes country entirely
  in an afternoon.
- **13:30** — Check into your **Chitwan lodge** (Sauraha
  side; 2-night full-board packages with all activities,
  naturalist, and the **NPR 2,000/day + VAT park fee**
  bundled run **~US$130–200 pp** mid-range; **Barahi
  Jungle Lodge** is the riverfront luxury benchmark,
  Sauraha's family lodges the value tier).
- **16:00** — **Sunset canoe** on the Rapti in a dugout:
  gharial and mugger crocodiles on the banks, kingfishers
  strafing, the jungle turning gold.
- **19:30** — **Tharu cultural evening** — the indigenous
  stick dance around the fire — then lodge dinner and the
  loudest cicadas of your life.

## Day 8 — Rhino day

- **06:30** — Bird walk with the naturalist (Chitwan logs
  500+ species before most guests wake).
- **09:00** — **Full jeep safari** into the park: the
  star is the **greater one-horned rhinoceros** — Chitwan
  holds ~700 and sightings are near-routine — plus
  spotted deer, wild boar, gaur, langurs, sloth-bear luck
  and the **Bengal tiger lottery** (rare, real, and the
  reason everyone whispers).
- **The ethics line, consistent with this site:** ride
  the jeep, skip the elephant — elephant-back safaris
  and bathing shows persist in Sauraha, and we don't
  fund them. The jeep sees more anyway.
- **16:00** — Village cycle or riverside sundowner;
  elephants (wild ones) sometimes cross at dusk — that's
  the version worth seeing.
- **20:00** — Last jungle night: Tharu dinner, firefly
  audit.

## Day 9 — Back to Kathmandu, farewell feast

- **08:30** — Drive back to Kathmandu (5–6 h; or the
  20-min Bharatpur flight, ~US$90, for the road-weary).
- **15:30** — Last Thamel sweep: the pashmina you
  negotiated on Day 1 is still there; so is the singing
  bowl.
- **19:00** — The farewell feast: **Krishnarpan at
  Dwarika's** (the ceremonial 6+ course Nepali dinner,
  ~NPR 5,000–8,000 pp — the splurge that doubles as a
  cultural finale) or a beloved Thamel thakali kitchen
  at a tenth of that.
- Pack. Set the alarm. Tomorrow you meet Everest.

## Day 10 — The Everest flight, then home

- **05:30** — Airport for the **Everest mountain
  flight** (~1 hour; **Indians ~₹12,150 / ~NPR 19,500 —
  the SAARC fare; other passports US$250–275**; Buddha
  Air/Yeti, every seat a window, cockpit visits in
  turn): the Langtang and Rolwaling ranges parade past
  until the black pyramid of **Sagarmatha — Everest —**
  fills the glass at eye level. Weather cancellations
  rebook or refund in full — the buffer this itinerary
  gives you.
- **10:00** — Back for breakfast, checkout, and the
  afternoon international flight home — mountains in the
  camera, no blisters on the feet.

## FAQ

**Truly no visa for Indians — what's the catch?**
No catch — the 1950 treaty keeps the border open both
ways. The two real rules: carry the right document
(passport or original Voter ID; kids under 15 need birth
certificate + parent's ID) and **leave ₹500/₹2,000
notes at home**. Every other nationality: US$30–50
visa-on-arrival, ten quiet minutes at the kiosk.

**Is the Everest flight worth it — and is it safe?**
It's the best US$140 (SAARC) an Indian traveller spends
in Nepal: a guaranteed-window hour with the highest
mountains on earth at eye level. On safety: book the
established carriers (Buddha/Yeti), fly the early-morning
calm, and accept weather cancellations gracefully — the
system errs cautious, which is exactly what you want.

**When exactly for the mountain views?**
**October–November is the answer** — post-monsoon
clarity, Sarangkot delivering daily. December–February:
cold mornings, still clear. March–April: warm,
rhododendron-red hills, slight haze creeping in by May.
**June–September: assume no mountains** and enjoy the
green (and the discounts).

**Chitwan — ethical wildlife or not?**
The park itself is a conservation triumph (rhinos back
from ~100 to ~700). Our line: **jeep and canoe safaris
yes; elephant-back rides and bathing shows no** — same
stance as our Thailand guide. Book lodges that don't
push elephant activities; the naturalist-led jeep sees
more animals anyway.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Vegetarians are home: dal bhat, veg momos, paneer
everything — half the country eats meat-free half the
time. Halal takes light planning: Kathmandu's Muslim
quarter around Jame Masjid (Durbar Marg) and several
Thamel restaurants serve halal, Pokhara has a handful;
lodges happily cook fish/veg on request. When in doubt,
the vegetarian lane in Nepal is genuinely excellent.

**What about the treks — Poon Hill, Everest Base Camp?**
Coming as their own guides: **Poon Hill** (4–5 teahouse
days, first-trek friendly) slots straight into this
route at Pokhara, and the **EBC epic** joins our
special-expedition shelf beside Siberia and Oymyakon.
If you needed a sign to start walking uphill after
dinner — this is it.
