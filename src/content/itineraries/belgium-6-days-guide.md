Belgium's advantage is compression. Four cities worth a day or more
each, all within an hour of one another by train, on a network so
dense that a car would be an active nuisance. You can base yourself
in one place and see the lot, or move twice and never pack in a
hurry.

**Bruges** is the famous one, and deserves it — a medieval merchant
town of canals and step-gabled houses, wrapped in coach tours
between ten and five. **Ghent** is what Bruges would be if people
actually lived there, with a river-front of guild houses and a
castle in the middle. **Brussels** has the finest market square in
Europe and a reputation for dullness it no longer merits. **Antwerp**
has Rubens, diamonds and the best-dressed people in the country.

Six days is enough for all four without rushing, and the trains mean
you barely notice the moving.

## Before you go: trains, timing and beer

### Entry

- **Belgium is in Schengen**, so one visa covers it. The process,
  the lead times and the documents that cause refusals are in our
  [Schengen visa guide](/guides/schengen-visa-how-it-works/).
- Visa-free nationalities need only a passport, with the **EES
  biometric border** now fully operational — allow extra time on
  first arrival.
- **The euro**, and cards work everywhere including on trains.

### The trains make the trip

- **Brussels to Bruges is about an hour**, Ghent about 30 minutes,
  Antwerp about 45. Trains run several times an hour and need no
  reservation.
- **Do not hire a car.** Parking in these old centres is restricted,
  expensive and slower than walking, and the trains are quicker
  door to door.
- **Brussels has multiple stations** — Midi/Zuid for international
  services, Central for the old town. Check which one your ticket
  names.
- Buy tickets at the machine or in the app; both are simple and
  English-language.

### The day-trip trap

- **Bruges between 10am and 5pm belongs to coach tours.** The
  difference between a magical Bruges and a disappointing one is
  entirely about being there outside those hours.
- **Sleep in Bruges for at least one night.** Early morning and
  after six, the town empties and becomes the place people describe.
- The same logic applies at smaller scale in Ghent, which is
  busiest at midday and lovely at night when the buildings are lit.

### Beer, and how to take it seriously

- **Belgian beer is a genuine food culture**, not a marketing line —
  hundreds of distinct styles, many brewed by or for abbeys, each
  with its own glass.
- **Order by style, not brand.** A *tripel*, a *dubbel*, a
  *witbier*, a *geuze* if you want the sour one that surprises
  people.
- **Strengths are high.** Many are 8–10%, served in 330ml glasses
  for good reason.
- Chips are a national dish, served from a *frituur* with mayonnaise
  and eaten standing up. The waffles come in two kinds — Brussels
  (light, rectangular, dusted) and Liège (dense, sweet, caramelised)
  — and the Liège one is better.

### When to go

- **April to June and September to October** are the best: mild,
  bright and less crowded than midsummer.
- **December** is Christmas-market season and genuinely lovely in
  Bruges and Ghent.
- **July and August** are warmest and busiest. It rains in every
  month; bring a jacket regardless.

## Day 1 — Land in Brussels

- **Afternoon** — Land, drop bags. **Grand-Place** first: the guild
  houses are the most concentrated piece of architecture in the
  country.
- **Evening** — Dinner near Sainte-Catherine rather than on the
  tourist strip off Grand-Place, which is all barkers and mediocrity.

## Day 2 — Brussels properly

- **Morning** — **Royal Museums of Fine Arts** for the Bruegels and
  the Magritte collection next door.
- **Afternoon** — The **comic-strip route** of painted walls, and
  Manneken Pis, which is small and which everyone photographs anyway.
- **Evening** — A proper beer bar with a list the length of a menu.

## Day 3 — Train to Bruges

- **Morning** — An hour by train. Drop bags.
- **Afternoon** — The **Markt** and the **belfry**, 366 steps for
  the view.
- **Evening** — **Bruges after six**, when the coaches have gone and
  the canals reflect the lamps. This is why you are sleeping here.

## Day 4 — Bruges at dawn, then Ghent

- **Early** — Walk the canals before eight. The town is silent and
  you will have it.
- **Late morning** — The **Groeningemuseum** for the Flemish
  Primitives, or a canal boat.
- **Afternoon** — Train to **Ghent**, 30 minutes.
- **Evening** — The **Graslei** waterfront, lit, with dinner
  alongside it.

## Day 5 — Ghent and Antwerp

- **Morning** — **Gravensteen** castle, and **Saint Bavo's** for the
  **Ghent Altarpiece**, which is worth the timed ticket.
- **Afternoon** — Train to **Antwerp**, 45 minutes: the cathedral's
  Rubens panels, the station itself (one of Europe's finest), and
  the fashion district.
- **Evening** — Back to Ghent or on to Brussels, depending where you
  fly from.

## Day 6 — Fly home

- Brussels airport is 20 minutes by train from the centre. Charleroi,
  used by low-cost carriers, is an hour by coach — check which one
  your flight leaves from.

## What to pack

- **A rain jacket.** It rains in every month, briefly and often.
- **Shoes for cobbles** — all four old centres are paved in them.
- **European Type C/E plugs.**
- **Layers**, even in summer; the North Sea is close and the
  evenings cool quickly.

## FAQ

**Is Bruges worth it, or is it a tourist trap?**
Both, depending entirely on when you are there. Between ten and
five it is a coach park. Before eight and after six it is one of the
best-preserved medieval towns in Europe. Sleep there and you get the
second one.

**Should I base in one city or move?**
Move once. Two nights Brussels, two Bruges, one Ghent works well, and
Antwerp is an easy day trip from any of them.

**Do I need a car?**
No, and it would slow you down. The trains are frequent, cheap and
faster once you count parking.

**Which city should I cut if I only have four days?**
Antwerp, reluctantly. Brussels, Bruges and Ghent are the core, and
Ghent is the one most people under-rate.
