Bhutan is the last of its kind: a Himalayan Buddhist kingdom
that never rushed, never colonised its own culture for
tourism, and famously measures **Gross National Happiness**.
There are no traffic lights in the entire country (Thimphu's
white-gloved policeman dances instead), dzong-fortresses still
run both church and state, and the air at Dochula Pass smells
of pine and prayer-flag cotton.

What you pay to be here depends entirely on your passport.
Indian, Bangladeshi and Maldivian nationals travel on the
**regional rate — a ₹1,200/night Sustainable Development Fee,
no visa, Voter ID accepted**; everyone else pays **US$100 a
night** and applies for a visa through a licensed operator.
Either way the catch is
choreography, not cost — permits, route papers and one sacred
cliff hike — and this guide runs the steps in order.

**Go October–November** (post-monsoon crystal: Himalaya views
daily) **or March–May** (rhododendrons, jacaranda-lilac
Punakha). December–February is cold, clear and quiet;
June–August is monsoon-green with hidden mountains. All costs
are in Bhutanese ngultrum (**BTN — pegged 1:1 with INR**, and
Indian rupees are accepted almost everywhere).

## Before you go: the SDF maths and the permit choreography

### The Indian-passport advantage (headline first)

- **No visa.** Entry permit instead — free — against an
  **Indian passport (6+ months) or original Voter ID**
  (Aadhaar/PAN/licences rejected; kids under 18 without
  either use birth certificate + parent's ID).
- **SDF: ₹1,200 per adult per night** (children 6–12
  ₹600, **under-6 free**) — the regional rate, currently
  **locked through August 2027**. A couple's 6-night trip
  pays ₹14,400 total; internationals pay US$1,200 for the
  same nights (plus a US$40 visa) — the widest gap between
  the two tiers Bhutan has ever run.
- **Apply two ways:** online at **immi.gov.bt** 4–5 working
  days ahead (pay SDF there, land smooth), or **on arrival
  at Paro airport (15–30 min)**. Land-crossers at
  Phuentsholing report 3–8-hour queues in season — fly, or
  arrive at opening.

### The choreography (the part that trips people)

- The entry permit covers **Thimphu and Paro only**. For
  **Punakha (and anywhere deeper) you need a Route Permit**,
  issued at **Thimphu's immigration office on weekdays**
  (1.5–2 hours) — which is why this itinerary goes
  **Thimphu first** and keeps Day 2 a weekday. Your
  guide/agent handles it with your originals.
- **The guide question, honestly:** in Thimphu and Paro,
  Indians can currently wander independently; **beyond
  (the Punakha leg), a licensed guide is expected with the
  route permit** and enforcement at checkposts is real.
  The standard, and frankly the best, solution: a **car +
  driver + guide package (~Nu 5,000–7,500/day all-in)** —
  on these mountain roads a local driver is a safety
  feature and a Bhutanese guide turns dzongs from pretty
  walls into stories.
- Money: **INR notes up to ₹500 are accepted** (₹2,000
  notes are not; small notes smoothest), ATMs are sparse
  and moody — carry cash for the week. Cards work at
  better hotels only.
- Small print worth loving: **Tuesdays are national dry
  days** (no alcohol sold), tobacco is restricted (declare
  and pay duty; smoke only in designated areas), **drones
  are effectively banned**, and dzongs require covered
  shoulders/knees, no hats, and no photos inside temples.

## Day 1 — The landing, then to the capital

- **Morning** — Fly into **Paro** (Druk Air/Bhutan
  Airlines only, via Delhi/Kolkata/Guwahati/Bagdogra —
  **book early, sit LEFT from Delhi**: on clear days
  Everest and Kanchenjunga file past the window). The
  final approach — banking between pine ridges onto one
  of the world's most exacting runways — is flown by only
  a few dozen certified pilots; applause is customary and
  earned.
- **12:00** — Permits stamped (pre-applied or on-arrival),
  meet the driver, and roll up the valley to **Thimphu**
  (1 h) — pausing at **Tamchhog Lhakhang's** 600-year-old
  iron-chain bridge.
- **15:00** — Capital orientation: Clock Tower Square,
  the handicraft lanes, the world's most charming traffic
  cop mid-ballet at the main junction.
- **19:00** — First Bhutanese table: **ema datshi**
  (chilli-cheese, the national dish — order it mild the
  first night, trust us), red rice, momos (~Nu 300–600
  pp).

**Where to stay in Thimphu** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Pemako Thimphu** — 4.6/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~1,200 reviews), Bhutanese craft at five-star polish.
  ~Nu 18,000+.
- *Boutique:* **The Postcard Dewa / The Pema by Realm
  tier** — view rooms and rooftop calm. ~Nu 8,000–12,000.
- *Value:* **Druk Hotel / Thimphu Towers** — Clock Tower
  Square classics. ~Nu 3,500–5,500.

## Day 2 — Thimphu: the Buddha, the dzong, the paperwork

- **09:00** — **Route-permit run** at immigration (your
  guide queues; you sign — 1.5–2 h, weekdays only; this
  is why today is a weekday).
- **10:30** — **Buddha Dordenma** (Nu 300): the 52-metre
  golden Buddha on the ridge, 125,000 smaller Buddhas
  inside, the whole valley below.
- **12:30** — Lunch downtown (~Nu 300–500), then the
  **National Memorial Chorten** (Nu 500) — join the
  slow tide of elders circumambulating with prayer
  wheels; Bhutan's daily devotion at its most moving.
- **15:00** — Culture hour by taste: the **Royal Textile
  Academy**, the giant-paper factory, or the **archery
  ground** (the national sport — 145-metre targets,
  taunting songs, free to watch; weekend matches are
  best).
- **17:00** — **Tashichho Dzong** (Nu 500; visitor entry
  after offices close ~17:00 weekdays): the seat of King
  and Je Khenpo — whitewashed walls, golden roofs, monks
  crossing courtyards at dusk.
- **19:30** — Dinner; weekend travellers add the
  **Centenary Farmers' Market**. Early night — passes
  tomorrow.

## Day 3 — Over Dochula to the warm valley

- **08:00** — Climb to **Dochula Pass (3,100 m)**: the
  **108 Druk Wangyal chortens** stacked on the saddle
  and — on clear mornings, near-daily Oct–Feb — a
  200-km wall of eastern Himalaya. Coffee at the pass
  café with the view doing the talking.
- **10:30** — Descend 1,800 vertical metres through
  cloud-forest into **Punakha's** subtropical valley —
  bananas and cactus by lunchtime (motion tablets for
  the wind-y bits; the road is good and the driver
  better).
- **13:00** — Riverside lunch (~Nu 400–600).
- **15:00** — The **Chimi Lhakhang** walk: 25 minutes
  through rice paddies to the 15th-century fertility
  temple of the "Divine Madman" — whose, let's say,
  *emblem* decorates village houses en route with total
  unselfconsciousness. Pilgrims seeking children receive
  blessings here; everyone receives a story (Nu 500).
- **18:30** — Farm-stay or riverside dinner; the valley
  hums with crickets a season warmer than breakfast.

**Where to stay in Punakha** (two nights): **Dhensa
Boutique** (forest-terrace serenity, ~Nu 15,000+),
**RKPO Green Resort** (~Nu 6,000–9,000), or **Meri
Puensum's** valley-view balconies (~Nu 4,000–6,000) —
book by current reviews; the valley's inventory is
small.

## Day 4 — The most beautiful building in the Himalaya

- **09:00** — **Punakha Dzong** (Nu 500): moored like a
  white ship at the meeting of the Mother and Father
  rivers — Bhutan's masterpiece, where kings are crowned.
  In **April–May the jacarandas turn the walls lilac**
  and photographers weep quietly. Allow two hours; the
  murals repay the guide's stories.
- **11:30** — Walk the **Punakha Suspension Bridge**
  (160 m of prayer-flagged sway over the Po Chhu —
  free, giggly, gorgeous).
- **13:00** — Lunch, then choose the afternoon:
  - **Khamsum Yulley Namgyal Chorten** — 45 minutes up
    through paddies to the queen's four-storey chorten
    and the valley's best panorama; or
  - **White-water rafting** on the Mo Chhu (~Nu
    2,500–3,500 pp, gentle grades — the scenic float
    past the dzong is the point).
- **19:00** — Last warm-valley evening; try **suja**
  (butter tea) with zow shungo — acquired tastes,
  acquired here.

## Day 5 — Back west to Paro

- **08:30** — The road back over Dochula (second chance
  at the big view — mornings are kindest) and down the
  Paro valley (~4 h with stops).
- **14:00** — **Rinpung Dzong** ("fortress on a heap of
  jewels") and the covered **Nyamai Zam** bridge below —
  the classic Paro composition — then the **National
  Museum** in the round watchtower above (Nu 500).
- **16:30** — **Kyichu Lhakhang** (Nu 500): one of
  Bhutan's two oldest temples (7th century), orange
  trees fruiting improbably in the courtyard, prayer
  wheels worn mirror-smooth.
- **18:00** — Paro town's two charming streets; early
  dinner, early night, water bottles filled — **the
  cliff is tomorrow**.

**Where to stay in Paro** (two nights): **Zhiwa Ling
Heritage** (the grand Bhutanese-owned flagship — carved
timber, its own temple; ~Nu 20,000+), **Naksel
Boutique** (forest-edge, ~Nu 8,000–12,000), or the
valley's farmstays (~Nu 3,000–5,000 — dinner with the
family included in spirit and often in fact).

## Day 6 — Tiger's Nest, then the hot stones

- **07:00** — **Taktsang — the Tiger's Nest** (Nu
  1,000): the monastery glued to a cliff-face 900 m
  above the valley, where Guru Rinpoche arrived on a
  flying tigress. The honest brief: **2–3 hours up** on
  a good trail (ponies to the midpoint, ~Nu 700–1,000,
  uphill only), tea-house viewpoint at halfway, then
  the famous **700 steps down-and-up** past the
  waterfall to the gate. Bags, cameras and phones stay
  in lockers — the golden rooms and the meditation cave
  are for eyes and memory. Altitude tops out ~3,120 m:
  slow is the whole technique.
- **13:00** — Trail-foot lunch, legs vibrating
  agreeably.
- **15:30** — The correct reward: a **farmhouse
  hot-stone bath (dotsho)** — river stones fired for
  hours, dropped hissing into a wooden tub of
  artemisia-strewn water (~Nu 1,200–2,000 pp; book
  morning-of). Follow with a farm dinner and a taste
  of **ara** (the local spirit) if it's not Tuesday.
- **19:30** — Last supper: ema datshi ordered
  *properly* spicy now. You've earned rank.

## Day 7 — Fly out

- **Morning** — The Paro runway show in reverse
  (LEFT side again for the giants, Delhi-bound).
  Permits surrendered, prayer flags in the luggage,
  blood pressure measurably improved. **Extra-day
  owners:** the Haa Valley over the Chele La pass
  (Bhutan's highest road) is the classic add.

## What to pack

- Layers for three climates in one day: Thimphu
  mornings 5–12°C in season, Punakha afternoons 25°C+,
  Dochula wind always.
- Broken-in walking shoes with grip (Taktsang's trail
  + 700 stone steps), a walking pole if knees ask.
- Dzong-and-temple dress: long sleeves/trousers,
  no hats inside, socks you're proud of (shoes come
  off).
- **Cash: INR in ₹100/200/500 notes** for the week
  (₹2,000 notes refused; ATMs are rumours outside
  cities).
- Motion tablets for the passes, sunscreen +
  lip balm (altitude sun), a headlamp for farmstays.
- A soft heart for dogs: Bhutan's strays are
  numerous, calm and adored — the nightly barking
  chorus is included in every room rate.

## FAQ

**Is the guide mandatory or not — final answer?**
Current practice: **Thimphu and Paro, no — beyond
(Punakha included), yes in effect**: route permits and
checkposts expect a licensed guide on the paperwork,
and enforcement is real. Since a driver is
near-essential on these roads anyway, the
car-driver-guide bundle is less a tax than the trip's
best upgrade — Bhutan's guides are storytellers,
fixers and hot-stone-bath negotiators in one.

**How hard is Tiger's Nest, really?**
A determined beginner's hike: 900 m of altitude gain,
2–3 hours up at chat pace, ponies covering the first
two-thirds for the unsure (uphill only — the descent
is everyone's own knees). The 700 final steps are the
honest sting. Grandparents summit daily; so do
five-year-olds, insufferably. Start at 7, carry
water, go slow — the cliff does the rest.

**When is the SDF window — and will it last?**
The ₹1,200 regional rate (and the internationals'
US$100 halved rate) is officially extended **through
31 August 2027**. Beyond that, policy history says
rates revisit. Translation: for anyone on the
regional rate this is the cheapest legal Bhutan in
decades — a reason to go sooner, not later.

**Festivals — should we time for a tshechu?**
If you can: **Paro Tshechu (March–April)** and
**Thimphu Tshechu (September–October)** fill dzong
courtyards with masked cham dances and the whole
valley in its finest gho and kira. Book flights and
rooms months ahead and expect the SDF-era's biggest
crowds — a dedicated festival guide is on our list.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Vegetarians have accidentally found their national
cuisine: ema datshi, kewa datshi (potato-cheese),
shamu datshi (mushroom), veg momos and red rice make
half of Bhutanese food meat-free by default. **Formal
halal certification is essentially absent** (meat is
imported and mixed-sourced) — Muslim travellers
overwhelmingly eat vegetarian here, which in Bhutan
is no sacrifice at all.

**What's Bhutan guide #2?**
**Phobjikha Valley** (the glacial bowl where
black-necked cranes winter, Nov–Feb) extends this
exact route by two days — or the **festival special**
above. The cranes are currently winning.
