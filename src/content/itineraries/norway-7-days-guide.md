Norway solved sightseeing by accident: its public transport
IS the attraction. The **Bergen Railway** climbs over a
treeless mountain plateau past frozen lakes; at Myrdal a
branch line — the **Flåm Railway** — drops 866 vertical
metres down a cliff-face to a village on a fjord; and from
that village an electric boat hums through the
**Nærøyfjord**, a UNESCO waterway so narrow the cliffs seem
to close behind you. Stitch them together and you've done
"Norway in a Nutshell" — this guide does it independently,
cheaper, with nights where the tour buses don't stay.

The practical wins: Oslo is a short hop from anywhere in
Europe and a ~7 h non-stop from the Gulf, one **Schengen
visa** covers it (Norway runs its own VFS desks), and
Norway's trains sell
**minipris tickets from NOK 249–399** if you know the
90-day trick below.

The shape: **Oslo (2 nights) → the rail-and-fjord day →
Flåm (1 night) → cruise + connections → Bergen (3
nights)**, open-jaw home from Bergen. **Go May–September**
(waterfalls biggest in May–June; light endless to July).
Figure **{{NOK 15300}} per person
lakh)** including the visa, excluding flights — pricey,
softened by the packed-lunch system every Norwegian uses.

## Before you go: the admin

- **Schengen visa, Norway flavour:** Norway takes its
  own applications at **your nearest VFS Global centre** —
  **€90** + service fee, biometrics, proof of residence,
  bank statements, insurance (€30,000), itinerary; the
  same drill as our other Schengen guides, ~15
  calendar days, apply 4–6 weeks out. An existing
  multi-entry Schengen sticker works.
- **The minipris trick (this guide's biggest saver):**
  Vy releases Bergen-line tickets ~**90 days out**,
  cheapest first — **NOK 249–399** vs NOK 1,000+
  walk-up. Set a calendar reminder for day-90 and book
  Oslo→Myrdal the hour they drop. Seats: **left side
  (A) out of Oslo** for the lakes; the guide's couple
  trick is one seat each side.
- **Money:** Norway is functionally cashless — you can
  land, travel a week and leave without seeing a
  krone. No tipping culture (round up at most). When
  terminals offer to bill in your home currency: decline.
- **The eating system, stated early because it IS the
  budget:** restaurant mains run NOK 250–450, but
  (1) **Norwegian hotel breakfasts are vast and
  included** — eat like a Viking at 8am; (2) the
  national habit is the **matpakke** — packed lunch
  from supermarkets (Kiwi, Rema 1000: bread, brunost,
  smoked salmon — lunch for two ~NOK 200 vs 700);
  (3) tap water is glacier-grade. Dinner out becomes
  the day's one bill.
- **Weather:** "There's no bad weather, only bad
  clothing" is the national motto, and Bergen — 240
  rain days a year — enforces it. Waterproof shell,
  always, even in July.

## Day 1 — Land in Oslo, walk on the opera house

- **Midday** — Emirates' morning non-stop lands after
  lunch. Airport train in (Flytoget ~NOK 250 or the
  regular Vy train ~NOK 130 — same tracks, 10 min
  slower, half price: the first matpakke-spirit
  decision).
- **15:00** — **Oslo Opera House**: walk UP its
  marble roof from the fjord's edge — the building is
  a public hill, free, with harbour views; then the
  new waterfront promenade past the sauna rafts to
  **Aker Brygge**.
- **19:00** — Dinner note for this site's readers:
  **Grønland**, ten minutes east of the station, is
  Oslo's halal district — Pakistani, Somali, Turkish
  kitchens at Oslo's kindest prices (NOK 150–250
  mains). Norway's best biryani argument starts
  here.

**Where to stay in Oslo** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **The Thief** — 4.6/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~3,000 reviews), art-crammed design hotel on the
  Tjuvholmen waterfront. NOK 3,500+.
- *Mid-range:* **Thon Hotel Rosenkrantz** — 4.7/5
  (~2,600 reviews), central, with the breakfast
  spread Norwegian hotel legends are made of
  (dinner buffet often included too). ~NOK
  1,700–2,200.
- *Value:* **Citybox Oslo** — 4.0/5 (~2,500
  reviews), self-check-in smart rooms by the
  station. ~NOK 1,100–1,400.

## Day 2 — Oslo: sculptures, polar ships, Munch

- **09:00** — **Vigeland Sculpture Park** (free,
  always open): 200+ bronze and granite figures of
  humanity in every mood — the Angry Baby's fist is
  Oslo's most-rubbed landmark. Norway's most visited
  site costs nothing.
-- **11:30** — Ferry or bus to the **Bygdøy museum
  peninsula**: pick two of the polar trio — the
  **Fram** (walk aboard the actual polar ship,
  ~NOK 180) and **Kon-Tiki** (Heyerdahl's balsa
  raft, ~NOK 160) pair perfectly.
- **15:00** — Back to the centre: **MUNCH** (~NOK
  180) for The Scream in its tower by the opera —
  or the Nobel Peace Center by the harbour.
- **18:00** — Karl Johans gate stroll, palace
  gardens, early dinner and pack: tomorrow is the
  headline day, and the train leaves at dawn-ish.
- **Logistics tonight:** buy tomorrow's matpakke
  supplies; luggage tip — you'll carry bags through
  three vehicles tomorrow, so pack a duffel-light
  layer system.

## Day 3 — The great crossing: Bergensbanen + Flåm Railway

- **08:25** — **Oslo S → Myrdal** on the Bergen
  Railway (~5 h; minipris booked at day-90; window
  A-side): birch forests, Geilo's ski slopes, then
  the **Hardangervidda** — Europe's highest mountain
  plateau, treeless and lunar, reindeer if you're
  lucky, snow patches even in July. Finse station
  (1,222 m) starred as Hoth in Star Wars.
- **13:30** — Change at tiny **Myrdal** (rear
  carriages disembark closest; the connection is
  organised around tourists, don't panic) onto the
  **Flåm Railway** (**NOK 510 one-way high season**,
  45 min): one of the steepest standard-gauge lines
  on Earth, 20 tunnels, the train PAUSES at
  **Kjosfossen falls** where a red-dressed huldra
  dances in the spray (summer theatre, gloriously
  odd).
- **14:30** — Roll into **Flåm**, a village of 350
  people at the head of the Aurlandsfjord. Check in,
  then walk ten minutes out along the fjord path and
  let the scale recalibrate you.
- **17:00** — Optional: the **Stegastein viewpoint**
  shuttle (~NOK 400 return) — a wooden diving board
  650 m above the fjord — best in evening light.
- **19:30** — Dinner at the **Ægir brewery hall**
  (Viking-timber brewpub, ~NOK 300–400 mains) —
  after the day you've had, earned.

**Where to stay in Flåm (one night):** the historic
**Fretheim Hotel** (the 1870s fjord grande dame,
~NOK 2,400–3,200) or **Flåmsbrygga** beside the
brewery — tiny village, book with the trains, go by
current reviews.

## Day 4 — The Nærøyfjord, then into Bergen

- **09:00** — Slow fjord morning: rent a kayak or
  e-bike up the Flåm valley, or simply drink coffee
  facing the water like a sensible person.
- **12:00** — The **Nærøyfjord cruise, Flåm →
  Gudvangen** (~2 h, **~NOK 550–720** by season —
  book ahead in summer; the silent electric
  vessels make it feel like the fjord is holding
  its breath): into the UNESCO arm where cliffs
  rise a kilometre on both sides, waterfalls
  thread down them, and hamlets appear that are
  reachable only by boat.
- **14:30** — Connection ballet, all timed for
  this route: **bus Gudvangen → Voss** (~NOK 150,
  down the hairpins of the Nærøydalen) then
  **train Voss → Bergen** (~NOK 150–250, 1 h 15).
- **17:30** — **Bergen**: check in, straight out
  to **Bryggen** — the leaning wooden Hanseatic
  wharf, 900 years of fish-trade history in
  golden-hour colours.
- **19:30** — Fish market dinner (the honest note:
  it's touristy and priced like it — NOK 300–500;
  the seafood is still superb) or Skostredet's
  indie kitchens for less.

**Where to stay in Bergen** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Opus XVI** — 4.7/5 (~1,000 reviews),
  run by Edvard Grieg's descendants in his
  family's bank building. NOK 2,800+.
- *Mid-range:* **Thon Hotel Orion** — 4.4/5
  (~1,100 reviews), harbourside steps from
  Bryggen, that breakfast again. ~NOK 1,500–1,900.
- *Value:* **Citybox Bergen** — 4.1/5 (~390
  reviews), ~NOK 1,000–1,300. (Boutique wildcard:
  Charmante Skostredet, 4.7/331.)

## Day 5 — Bergen: the mountain and the fjord

- **08:30** — **Fløibanen funicular** up Fløyen
  (**~NOK 190 return** — or ride up, walk the
  lovely 45-min path down): the city, its seven
  mountains and the island-studded sea in one
  sweep. Trolls in the forest playground for
  kids; cinnamon buns at the top café for
  everyone.
- **11:00** — **Bryggen properly**: the wonky
  timber alleys behind the facades, the Hanseatic
  Museum's merchant rooms (~NOK 200), St Mary's
  Church — then KODE's art palaces (Munch room,
  ~NOK 200) if the sky argues.
- **14:00** — **Mostraumen fjord cruise from the
  harbour** (~3.5 h, **~NOK 900–990**): Bergen's
  own mini-fjord run — mountainsides closing in,
  a waterfall the boat noses under, goats on the
  shore. The right-sized second fjord after
  yesterday's giant.
- **19:00** — Dinner in the Skostredet quarter;
  rain-proof plan B is a bergensk fiskesuppe
  (fish soup) anywhere warm.

## Day 6 — Bergen your way

- Pick the day by weather (**yr.no** is Norway's
  morning ritual):
  - **Blue sky:** the **Ulriken cable car** +
    ridge walk — Bergen's highest mountain,
    bigger views than Fløyen;
  - **Grey sky:** **Old Bergen open-air museum**,
    the aquarium with the kids, or KODE round
    two;
  - **Either:** the light-rail pilgrimage to
    **Troldhaugen** — Grieg's lakeside villa and
    summer lunchtime concerts (~NOK 220).
- **16:00** — Souvenir hour: brunost (brown
  cheese) and a hand-knitted something — the
  troll can stay in the shop.
- **19:30** — Farewell dinner up in the Bryggen
  lofts (~NOK 400–600 pp for the good stuff) —
  toast the week with glacier water, on the
  house, always.

## Day 7 — Fly home from Bergen

- Morning light-rail to the airport (~NOK 45,
  30 min) and home via Oslo or a European hub —
  open-jaw complete, no backtracking, all fjord.

## What to pack

- The waterproof shell — worn on more days than
  not in Bergen — plus fleece and thermals even
  in July (Hardangervidda's platform stops are
  4°C in summer).
- Comfortable one-bag packing: day 3–4 is a
  train-train-boat-bus-train relay and YOU carry
  everything.
- Walking shoes with grip (Fløyen path, wet
  cobbles), sunglasses (fjord glare), swimsuit
  (harbour saunas, hotel pools).
- Refillable bottle — Norway's tap water
  embarrasses the bottled industry.
- **European Type C/F adapter**, power bank for
  the long train day.
- Appetite discipline for 8am: the hotel
  breakfast is a strategic resource.

## FAQ

**Is Norway really that expensive?**
Yes — and manageable. The sticker shock is
restaurants and alcohol; the relief is that the
scenery is free, transport is fair (that
NOK 349 minipris crosses the country), tap
water is superb and the breakfast-matpakke
system cuts food costs by half. Budget
travellers do Norway on hostel + supermarket
terms for 60% of this guide's number; nobody
does it on Thailand money.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Better than the fjords suggest. **Oslo's
Grønland district is a genuine halal quarter**
(and the budget-food answer for everyone);
Bergen has a smaller bench of kebab, Turkish
and Indian spots. On the fjord legs, assume
seafood-halal or self-catered — supermarkets
label well. Vegetarians: every menu has real
options now, brunost and porridge culture
helps at breakfast, and Oslo's veg scene is
quietly excellent.

**Norway in a Nutshell — buy the package or
DIY like this guide?**
The famous package bundles exactly these legs
with zero flexibility, no overnight in Flåm,
and a mark-up. DIY (this guide) books the
same four tickets in 20 minutes, adds the
fjord night — the single best upgrade — and
saves money that becomes the Mostraumen
cruise. Buy the package only if booking five
tickets frightens you more than it should.

**When should we go — and what about the
northern lights?**
**May–June** for thundering waterfalls and
20-hour light; **July–August** for warmth and
crowds; **September** for value, gold larches
and the first auroras on dark clear nights
(Bergen–Oslo latitude sees them only weakly —
proper lights live up north, in the seeded
guide below). Winter here is its own
magical, dark, different trip.

**Can we do this with kids / seniors?**
It's the gentlest spectacular trip in Europe:
zero compulsory hikes, trains with big
windows, boats with bathrooms, villages with
ice cream. The one care point is the Myrdal
platform change (5–10 minutes, luggage in
hand) — pack rollable-light and it's a
non-event.

**What's Norway guide #2?**
**Tromsø in winter** — aurora camps, whale
fjords, dog sleds — or the **Lofoten Islands**
fly-drive for the Arctic postcard. Votes
welcome; both on the list.
