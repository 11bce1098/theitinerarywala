Our first two USA guides ([East Coast](/itineraries/usa-east-coast-guide/),
[West classic](/itineraries/usa-west-coast-guide/)) were built to need no car.
This one is the opposite: the car *is* the trip. The Pacific Coast
Highway between Los Angeles and Monterey is the drive every rental
ad has been imitating for fifty years — surf on one side, cliffs
on the other, a bridge photo you've already seen a hundred times —
and Yosemite is what happens when granite decides to show off.

**And 2026 is the year it came back.** After three years of
landslide closures, **Highway 1 through Big Sur fully reopened on
14 January 2026** — the first complete peak season since 2023. A
February storm shut it briefly again (this road breathes); it has
run clear since, but the lesson stands and is baked into this
guide: **check Caltrans QuickMap the morning you drive it.**

**Go May–June** (waterfalls thundering, hills green, everything
open) **or September–October** (warm, clear, crowd-light — but
Yosemite's falls run near-dry; more below). July–August brings
coastal fog and peak crowds; winter brings storm-closure roulette
on Highway 1 — forecasters give **63% odds of a strong El Niño
winter 2026–27**, so late-season travellers should book flexible.
All costs are in US dollars (USD).

## Before you go: the admin

### Visa, in one line

The **~$435-per-person** US visa reality ($185 + $250 Integrity
Fee), the **6–12 month UAE appointment waits**, ESTA for Visa
Waiver passports — all covered in depth in our
[East Coast guide](/itineraries/usa-east-coast-guide/). Start it 8–12 months
out; nothing below matters without it.

### Driving in America — the honest briefing

- **Licence:** a valid licence in English (UAE licences qualify)
  is accepted by California law and the major agencies; an
  **International Driving Permit** is cheap insurance for
  Indian-issued licences — get one, carry both.
- **Traffic drives on the right**, same as the UAE — Gulf
  residents adapt in minutes. Speed limits are enforced (65–70
  mph freeways, less on Highway 1's curves), phones are
  hands-free only, and you may turn right on red after a full
  stop unless signed otherwise.
- **The rental maths, honestly:** a compact runs ~$45–70/day, but
  **full insurance (LDW + liability) adds ~$30–45/day** — book a
  package with cover included (rentalcars.com-style aggregators)
  rather than fighting the counter upsell. Drivers under 25 pay
  a surcharge. LA-pickup → SF-drop **one-way fees within
  California are usually $0–100** — confirm before booking.
- **Fuel:** California petrol is America's priciest (~$4.50–5.50
  /gallon; **$7+ in Big Sur** — fill up before). Foreign cards
  often fail at the pump's ZIP-code prompt: pay inside.
- **Car seats are law:** under-2s rear-facing, under-8s in
  boosters — rentals charge ~$14/day each, so frequent-flyer
  families bring their own.
- **The one rule that saves your trip:** never leave *anything*
  visible in a parked car — not a jacket, not a charger. Smash
  -and-grab theft at viewpoints and city streets is California's
  actual tourist crime. Boot everything, always.

### Route logic (why this direction)

LA → coast → Monterey → Yosemite → San Francisco, dropping the
car **the hour you arrive in SF** — you skip the city's $50–70
/night hotel parking and finish car-free where a car is a
liability. (Purists note: southbound gets the ocean-side
pullouts. See the FAQ.)

## Day 1 — Land in LA, sleep by the pier

No car today — nobody should meet a US freeway on 3 hours of
aeroplane sleep. Uber to Santa Monica (~$40–55 from LAX, 25 min).

- **17:00** — **Santa Monica Pier**: the Route 66 "End of the
  Trail" sign, the solar Ferris wheel, street performers.
- **18:30** — Barefoot hour on the beach; sunset at the pier's
  end.
- **20:00** — Dinner on Third Street Promenade (~$20–35), early
  night — tomorrow the key turns.

**Where to stay in Santa Monica** (one night)

- *Luxury:* **Shore Hotel** — 4.6/5 on TripAdvisor (~7,200
  reviews), solar-cool glass box directly opposite the pier.
  ~$350–450.
- *Mid-range:* **Hilton Santa Monica** — the area's best-value
  full-service pick per TripAdvisor's rankings, ~$220–290.
- *Budget:* honest answer — beach-adjacent Santa Monica has no
  cheap beds; inland motels along Lincoln Blvd from ~$140, or
  start the trip one Uber further at an LAX airport hotel.

## Day 2 — Malibu's coves to Santa Barbara

- **09:00** — Collect the car (Santa Monica branches beat LAX
  queues), point it north on **Highway 1**: the ocean arrives
  immediately.
- **10:00** — **Malibu**: walk Point Dume's bluff (whales in
  season) or **El Matador Beach's** sea-stack coves — the
  postcard 45 minutes.
- **12:30** — Lunch at **Malibu Seafood** or biker-famous
  **Neptune's Net** (~$15–20) — fried fish, ocean across the
  road.
- **14:00** — Cruise to **Santa Barbara** (90 min): check in,
  then the **Old Mission Santa Barbara** (the "Queen of the
  Missions", ~$15) and the white-stucco, red-roof old town.
- **17:30** — **Stearns Wharf** for sunset; dinner on lower
  State Street (~$25–40) — this town eats well.

**Where to stay in Santa Barbara** (one night)

- *Luxury:* **Santa Barbara Inn** — 4.7/5 (~850 reviews),
  beachfront with direct ocean views. ~$320–450.
- *Mid-range:* **The Eagle Inn** — 4.7/5 (~3,000 reviews),
  Spanish-revival charmer with fireplace rooms, two blocks from
  the sand. ~$200–280.
- *Budget:* **Beachside Inn** — TripAdvisor's #1 value here,
  breakfast included, from ~$110–150.

## Day 3 — Danish detour, then Cambria's moonstone shore

- **09:30** — Twenty minutes inland to **Solvang**, California's
  full-costume Danish town: windmills, æbleskiver pancake balls,
  gloriously unnecessary. One hour is exactly right.
- **11:30** — Rejoin the coast at **Pismo Beach** for a
  clam-chowder-in-sourdough lunch on the pier (~$15).
- **14:00** — Coffee stop in **San Luis Obispo** (Bubblegum
  Alley: exactly what it sounds like), then the quiet golden
  stretch to **Cambria**.
- **16:30** — **Piedras Blancas elephant seal rookery** (free,
  boardwalk viewing): hundreds of 2-tonne seals bellowing,
  moulting and body-slamming 10 metres away. Weirdly
  unmissable.
- **18:00** — **Moonstone Beach boardwalk** at sunset, then
  dinner in Cambria's pine-village centre (~$25–35).

**Where to stay in Cambria** (one night)

- *Splurge:* **Blue Dolphin Inn** — 4.7/5, oceanfront on
  Moonstone Beach, fireplaces and breakfast hampers. ~$250–350.
- *Mid-range:* **Cambria Pines Lodge** — 4.4/5 (~10,000
  reviews), lodge-and-gardens quirk with live music at the bar.
  ~$160–220.
- *Budget:* **FogCatcher Inn** — 4.3/5, across from the beach,
  ~$130–180.

## Day 4 — Hearst Castle, then the Big Sur run

The day the trip is named for. Fill the tank in Cambria (Big Sur
pumps charge $7+), download offline maps (cell service vanishes
for an hour), and **check Caltrans QuickMap once before rolling**.

- **08:40** — First tour at **Hearst Castle** (**$35 adult / $18
  child**, book on ReserveCalifornia up to 60 days out): the
  hilltop folly where Hollywood partied in the 1930s — Roman
  pool, private zoo remnants, Citizen Kane's real-world source.
  Two hours with the bus ride up.
- **11:30** — North past Ragged Point and the highway narrows
  into **Big Sur proper**: 90 miles of cliff-hung road. Pull
  out often (pullouts only — never stop in the lane); expect a
  couple of one-lane signal waits, the price of the 2026
  reopening.
- **13:30** — **Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park** (~$10/car):
  the 10-minute walk to **McWay Falls**, the 25-metre waterfall
  that drops straight onto a turquoise-cove beach.
- **15:00** — Late lunch at **Nepenthe** (~$25–40) — the
  clifftop terrace 250 m above the Pacific; overpriced,
  over-beautiful, obligatory. (Big Sur Bakery is the cheaper
  local's answer.)
- **16:30** — **Pfeiffer Beach's** purple-tinged sand and
  keyhole arch (unsigned narrow road, ~$15/car) *or* push on to
  reach **Bixby Creek Bridge** for golden hour — the 1932 arch
  every car advert rents.
- **18:30** — Roll into **Carmel-by-the-Sea** for a storybook
  -village stroll and dinner on Ocean Avenue (~$30–45), then
  ten minutes to Monterey for the night.

**Where to stay in Monterey** (one night)

- *Luxury:* **Monterey Plaza Hotel & Spa** — 4.6/5 (~8,100
  reviews), built over the water on Cannery Row. ~$320–450.
- *Mid-range:* **Portola Hotel & Spa** — 4.2/5 (~5,300
  reviews), by Fisherman's Wharf. ~$180–260.
- *Budget:* **Lone Oak Lodge** — 4.3/5 (~1,500 reviews), clean
  motor-lodge value, ~$110–160.

## Day 5 — Aquarium morning, Sierra evening

- **08:00** — The **17-Mile Drive** through Pebble Beach
  (~$12/car, credited back if you buy breakfast at the resort):
  the Lone Cypress, Spanish Bay's crashing surf, golf's most
  photographed coastline. 75 unhurried minutes.
- **10:00** — **Monterey Bay Aquarium** at opening (**$65
  adult / $50 child-youth** — steep, and worth it once): the
  kelp-forest tank, sea otters at feeding time, the open-sea
  wall of tuna and turtles. 2.5 hours.
- **13:00** — Quick Cannery Row lunch (~$15–20), then inland:
  Salinas Valley farm country, Pacheco Pass, the Central Valley
  — **four hours** to the Yosemite gateway.
- **18:00** — Check in (El Portal/valley), early dinner, early
  night. Mountains keep farmer's hours.

**Where to stay for Yosemite** (two nights)

- *Historic splurge:* **The Ahwahnee** — 3.9/5 (~3,500
  reviews), the 1927 granite-and-timber icon on the valley
  floor; presidents and queens slept here. ~$500+.
- *The location pick:* **Yosemite Valley Lodge** — 3.7/5
  (~4,900 reviews), motel-simple but a stroll from Yosemite
  Falls. ~$260–320. **In-park rooms open ~a year ahead on
  travelyosemite.com and sell out — book the day your dates
  fix.**
- *Budget:* **Curry Village** heated tent cabins — 3.8/5
  (~4,300 reviews), shared bathhouses, unbeatable
  granite-at-dawn atmosphere. ~$140–180. (Missed all three?
  Gateway towns — El Portal, Mariposa, Oakhurst — sit 30–75 min
  out and cost half.)

## Day 6 — Yosemite Valley, floor to rim

**No entry reservation is needed in 2026** — the timed-entry
system was dropped this year — but the park still recommends
**arriving before 8 a.m.** in season; buy the **$35/vehicle
digital pass (7 days)** on Recreation.gov to skip the gate queue.

- **07:30** — **Tunnel View** first: El Capitan, Half Dome and
  Bridalveil Fall in one windscreen — the Ansel Adams shot,
  still true.
- **08:30** — Valley floor loop by car and foot: **Bridalveil
  Fall** boardwalk, **El Capitan meadow** (binoculars find the
  climbers — dots on a 900-metre wall), **Yosemite Falls** base
  trail. *Honesty: the falls are snowmelt-fed — thunder in
  May–June, reduced by August, near-dry by late September.
  Autumn trades waterfalls for golden light and empty trails.*
- **12:30** — Picnic or grill lunch at the Lodge (~$12–18).
- **14:00** — The trip's hike: **Mist Trail to Vernal Fall
  footbridge** (2.5 km return, doable with game kids) or
  further to the top (steep granite steps, spray-soaked in
  spring).
- **17:30** — Drive **Glacier Point Road** (open ~late May–
  Oct/Nov) an hour up to **Glacier Point**: Half Dome at eye
  level, the valley 1,000 m below, sunset turning the granite
  pink. The single best view in California.
- **Bear rules are real:** food and anything scented goes in
  bear boxes or with you — never overnight in the car. Rangers
  fine; bears break windows.

## Day 7 — Sequoias, then the city by the bay

- **08:30** — South to the **Mariposa Grove**: 500 giant
  sequoias including the 3,000-year-old Grizzly Giant — trees
  older than most countries. Two hours on shuttle-and-boardwalk.
- **12:00** — Point the car west: ~4 hours to San Francisco.
- **16:00** — **Drop the rental at a downtown SF branch** — the
  smartest hour of the whole plan (SF hotel parking runs
  $50–70/night, and the city break-in problem isn't worth it).
  From here you're car-free.
- **17:30** — Check in, then the **Powell–Hyde cable car** over
  Russian Hill as the fog performs; dinner in Chinatown or
  North Beach (~$25–35).

**Where to stay in San Francisco:** our
[West Coast guide](/itineraries/usa-west-coast-guide/) has the verified
three tiers — **Argonaut** (4.5/5, Wharf luxury), **Hotel Caza**
(4.3/5, family mid-range), **Chancellor on Union Square**
(4.5/5, the value classic).

## Days 8–9 — San Francisco, the short version

Two days, fully detailed as Days 7–9 in the
[West Coast guide](/itineraries/usa-west-coast-guide/); the compressed plan:

- **Day 8** — **Alcatraz first ferry** (~$48 — **book 90 days
  out**, it sells out weeks ahead), Pier 39 sea lions, chowder
  in sourdough, Ghirardelli sundae, Golden Gate viewpoint at
  Crissy Field for fog-lift hour.
- **Day 9** — Rent bikes, **ride across the Golden Gate Bridge
  to Sausalito**, ferry back with skyline views; afternoon
  Painted Ladies + Mission murals and burritos; farewell crab
  dinner at the Wharf.
- *Already did our West Coast trip?* Trade these for day trips
  it couldn't reach without a car — Muir Woods' redwoods
  (shuttle from Sausalito) or Napa/Sonoma tasting rooms — or
  end at Day 8 and bank a day.

## Day 10 — Home from SFO

- **Morning** — BART to SFO (30 min, $11), then long-haul
  home. One thousand miles of California in the rear-view;
  the car went back days ago.

## What to pack

- Layers over logic: one day can span 15°C coastal fog and 30°C
  Central Valley. A real jacket for Big Sur mornings and
  Glacier Point sunset, always.
- Offline maps downloaded (Big Sur and parts of Yosemite have
  no signal) and a car phone mount.
- Walking shoes with grip for Yosemite granite; sandals for
  beaches.
- Binoculars — El Capitan's climbers, whales off Point Dume,
  condors over Big Sur.
- Picnic kit (cooler bag, board, knife): the best lunches on
  this route are pullouts, not restaurants.
- IDP + home licence + booking printouts in one folder;
  Type A/B adapter, power bank.

## FAQ

**Is Highway 1 actually open?**
As of publication, yes — fully reopened 14 January 2026 after
three years of landslide closures, and clear since a brief
February storm shutdown. But this road closes and reopens with
the weather (a strong El Niño is forecast for winter 2026–27):
**check Caltrans QuickMap (quickmap.dot.ca.gov) the morning you
drive**, and know the inland US-101 bail-out exists if a
segment shuts. If Big Sur is closed, the trip still works:
101 to Monterey, day-trip the open end of the coast.

**Can I drive on an Indian or UAE licence?**
Yes — California accepts valid foreign licences for visitors,
and rental majors take English-language licences (UAE cards
qualify directly). Carry an **IDP with an Indian licence** —
agencies and police both relax at the sight of one. Driving is
right-hand-side, same as the UAE; Indian drivers find the
discipline (lane-keeping, stop signs, yielding to pedestrians)
the bigger adjustment than the side.

**Waterfalls or warm weather — when exactly?**
**May–June is the answer** if Yosemite's falls matter: full
snowmelt thunder, green hills, everything open (Glacier Point
Road opens ~late May). September–October gives the coast its
clearest skies and the valley its emptiest trails — but
Yosemite Falls runs near-dry. There is no month that maxes
both; pick your priority.

**Doable with young kids?**
Yes — this route's drives are short (the longest hop is 4
hours) and every day ends somewhere with a beach, seals,
otters or sequoias. California's car-seat law is strict
(under-8s in boosters); reserve seats with the car or bring
your own. Skip the Vernal Fall upper steps; the elephant
seals, aquarium and Mariposa Grove are the kid-day anchors.

**Why not drive it southbound? I read the ocean side is better.**
It is — southbound (SF→LA) puts the pullouts on the ocean side
of the road. We run it northbound anyway for trip logic:
Yosemite slots naturally before a car-free SF finish, and you
never pay SF parking. Crossing the highway at viewpoints just
takes patience. If pullout-side perfection matters, mirror the
whole route: fly into SFO, out of LAX.

**Halal and vegetarian on the road?**
The cities bookending the trip are easy (see the West guide);
the coast between is the thin stretch — Santa Barbara and
Monterey have halal options, Big Sur and Cambria effectively
don't, and this is where the picnic kit and seafood (grilled,
unambiguous) carry the day. Vegetarians do fine everywhere —
California cuisine half-worships vegetables.

**What about the Southwest — Zion, Antelope, Monument Valley?**
A different, redder road trip out of Las Vegas — and the
natural next guide in this series. This one is coast and
granite; that one is desert and sandstone. Do this first;
you'll be back.
