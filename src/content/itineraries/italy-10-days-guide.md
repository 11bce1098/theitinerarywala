Italy is the trip people measure other trips against. Rome is
three thousand years stacked in layers you walk through on the
way to breakfast; Florence invented the Renaissance and still
fits inside an afternoon stroll; Venice should not exist, and
does, and floats. The high-speed line sews all three together in
under-two-hour hops — this is Europe's easiest great itinerary
and its most heavily booked.

That last part is the 2026 catch: **Italy now runs on timed
tickets.** The Colosseum releases 30 days out and evaporates in
hours; the Uffizi charges extra for certainty; Venice charges
day-trippers just to walk in on peak dates. This guide's job is
mostly to put the right booking dates in your calendar — the
beauty handles itself.

**Go April–June or September–October.** July–August is 35°C+,
queue-crushed, and half of Italy closes for its own holidays in
mid-August; winter is short-queued, moody and cheap (Venice fog
is a feature). All costs are in euros (EUR).

## Before you go: the admin

### Visa and borders

Schengen rules are covered in depth in our
[France guide](/itineraries/france-9-days-guide/); the essentials: ~60
nationalities enter visa-free (the **EES biometric border** is
live — allow extra time at first entry — and the **€20 ETIAS**
authorisation is expected from late 2026); Schengen-visa
passports (India included) apply from the UAE with the usual
file. **Italy's UAE applications run through VFS Global** —
fee €90 + service charges, typical processing ~15 days, but
appointment slots in season need **4–8 weeks of lead**, so start
early. Proof of funds, confirmed refundable bookings and
insurance (€30,000 cover) remain the make-or-break documents.

### Money and the small print

- Cards work everywhere; keep €50 in small notes for gelato,
  markets and church-crypt donations.
- **The coperto is not a scam:** €1.50–3 per person appears on
  most restaurant bills as a cover charge. It's legal and
  universal. Service is included beyond that — **no 20%
  tipping**; rounding up is generous.
- **Tourist taxes are real and rising:** Rome charges ~€4–7.50
  per person per night, Florence ~€5–8, Venice ~€1–5 — paid at
  the hotel. Budgeted below.
- **Venice's access fee (2026):** on ~60 peak dates
  (April–July, mornings-to-16:00), **day visitors pay €5**
  (€10 if booked under 4 days ahead). **Overnight guests are
  exempt — but must still register online for an exemption QR
  code**; fines run to €300. Your hotel will remind you; do it
  anyway.
- Free water: Rome's **nasoni** street fountains run cold and
  drinkable — refill, don't buy.

### The Italian clock (and the tourist-trap radar)

- Lunch 12:30–14:30, dinner from 19:30 — earlier than Spain,
  stricter than you think. Many kitchens close 15:00–19:00.
- **Never eat where a host waves a laminated photo menu** —
  especially within sight of the Trevi, San Marco or the
  Rialto. Walk two streets; prices halve and food doubles.
- Cappuccino is breakfast; after lunch it's espresso. Nobody
  will stop you — but now you know why the barista smiled.
- **Pickpocket honesty:** Termini station, the Rome metro,
  vaporetto crushes and every queue you'll stand in are prime
  territory. Zipped crossbody in front, phone off the café
  table — the whole defence.

### Trains, SIM, kids

- **Two operators compete** on the triangle: **Trenitalia's
  Frecciarossa** and private **Italo**. Rome→Florence ~1h35,
  Florence→Venice ~2h05; fares **from €20–40 booked 2–4 months
  out**, double walk-up. Compare both (Trainline/Omio show
  them together); seats are reserved, luggage free.
- eSIM ~€10–20 for the trip; Italy's 4G is fine, Venice's
  alleys defeat GPS — screenshot your hotel's walking route.
- **Kids:** Italy adores them. Under-18s enter the Colosseum
  and Accademia free, under-7s the Vatican; gelato is the
  universal negotiation currency; Venice is one giant
  hide-and-seek map. The trains are the nap slots.

## Day 1 — Land in Rome, the centro at dusk

Stay in the **centro storico** (Pantheon/Navona — walk
everywhere, pay for it) or **Monti** (village-in-the-city near
the Colosseum, our pick for value).

- **Afternoon** — Land at FCO, which takes non-stops from most
  of the world.
  **Leonardo Express** to Termini (€14, 32 min), check in.
- **17:30** — First passeggiata: **Piazza Navona** → the
  **Pantheon's** porch (interior tomorrow — it's ticketed now)
  → **Trevi Fountain** as the lights come on. Coin over the
  left shoulder; the €0.50 is the best value in Rome.
- **20:00** — First dinner near Campo de' Fiori: cacio e pepe,
  carciofi if it's season, house wine by the litre (~€25–35
  a head).

**Where to stay in Rome** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Albergo del Senato** — 4.8/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~5,100 reviews), your window opens onto the Pantheon's
  portico. ~€280–400.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Artemide** — 4.8/5 (~9,600 reviews),
  rooftop bar and spa on Via Nazionale, 10 minutes' walk from
  everything. ~€180–250.
- *Budget:* **iQ Hotel Roma** — 4.7/5 (~5,600 reviews), fresh
  rooms by Termini, family-run efficiency. ~€130–180.

## Day 2 — Ancient Rome, timed to the minute

- **08:30** — **The Colosseum**, first slot. The ticket
  (**€18**, includes **Forum + Palatine Hill** within 24 h)
  releases **exactly 30 days ahead at 08:45 Rome time on
  ticketing.colosseo.it** and morning slots go within hours —
  set an alarm, this is the trip's hardest ticket. The €24
  Full Experience adds arena floor or underground. Under-18s
  free.
- **11:00** — Walk the **Roman Forum** (Caesar's burial site,
  the Senate house) up the **Palatine Hill** for the overview
  of it all.
- **13:30** — Lunch in **Monti**: pizza al taglio standing up
  or a trattoria pasta (~€12–20).
- **15:30** — **Capitoline Hill** — Michelangelo's piazza, the
  she-wolf — then the **Vittoriano terrace lift** (~€15) for
  the 360° skyline, or free views from behind the monument.
- **18:00** — Aperitivo hour in Monti (Aperol, crisps, people
  in very good trousers, ~€8–10).
- **20:30** — Dinner: Roman classics — amatriciana, saltimbocca
  (~€25–35 a head).

## Day 3 — The Vatican and Trastevere

Dress code is real: shoulders and knees covered for the
basilica and museums.

- **08:00** — **Vatican Museums** at opening (**€20 + €5
  online reservation — the €5 is mandatory in practice**; only
  tickets.museivaticani.va is official, everything else
  resells). Head straight down the one-way river to the
  **Sistine Chapel** before the crowds compress, then walk
  back for the Raphael Rooms and the maps gallery. Three
  hours. Closed Sundays (free but chaotic on the month's last
  Sunday).
- **12:30** — Lunch in Borgo Pio (~€15–20), two quiet streets
  of it.
- **14:00** — **St Peter's Basilica** (free; security queue
  30–60 min): Michelangelo's Pietà, Bernini's baldachin. The
  **dome climb** (~€10–17, 551 steps or lift+320) earns the
  best view in Rome.
- **17:30** — Cross Ponte Sant'Angelo (Bernini's angels,
  castle photo) and walk the river down to **Trastevere**.
- **20:00** — Trastevere dinner and a wander: fried
  artichokes, supplì, nightcap in Piazza Santa Maria (~€25–40
  a head).

## Day 4 — Rome's second layer

- **09:00** — The **Pantheon interior** (€5, book online or
  queue early): the 2,000-year-old concrete dome with its open
  eye. Still the best building in Europe; fifteen minutes is
  enough, an hour is better.
- **10:30** — The **tridente**: Spanish Steps, Via dei
  Condotti window-pain, then **Villa Borghese** gardens — and,
  if you booked it, the **Borghese Gallery** (~€17,
  reservation mandatory, releases well ahead): Bernini's
  Apollo and Daphne mid-transformation.
- **13:30** — Market lunch at **Campo de' Fiori** or Testaccio
  Market (~€10–15).
- **15:30** — The quiet hits: the **Aventine Hill** — the
  keyhole view of St Peter's, the Orange Garden terrace —
  and Santa Maria in Cosmedin's **Mouth of Truth** (hand in,
  honesty optional).
- **19:00** — Last Roman dinner; gelato benediction at a
  proper gelateria (look for covered tubs and pistachio the
  colour of actual pistachios). Pack for the 09:00 train.

## Day 5 — The Frecciarossa to Florence

- **09:00** — Roma Termini → **Firenze S.M.N., 1h35** (from
  €20–30 booked ahead). Drop bags.
- **11:30** — Walk into the **Piazza del Duomo** cold — no
  photo prepares you for the marble. Circle the **Baptistery's
  Gates of Paradise**, then climb: **Brunelleschi's dome**
  (Brunelleschi Pass ~€30, **timed slots that sell out weeks
  ahead**) or Giotto's bell tower for the view *of* the dome
  (the photographer's choice).
- **13:30** — Lunch: **All'Antico Vinaio's** €8 schiacciata
  (the queue moves fast) or Mercato Centrale's upstairs food
  hall.
- **15:00** — **Piazza della Signoria** (the open-air
  sculpture gallery, the Palazzo Vecchio) → **Ponte Vecchio**
  and its goldsmiths.
- **18:00** — Cross to the Oltrarno and walk (or bus 12/13) up
  to **Piazzale Michelangelo** for the sunset over the whole
  postcard. Every Florentine cliché is up here being true.
- **20:30** — Oltrarno dinner — ribollita, pappardelle al
  cinghiale (~€25–35 a head).

**Where to stay in Florence** (three nights)

- *Luxury:* **Portrait Firenze** — 4.9/5 (~1,200 reviews),
  Ferragamo-family suites over the Arno by the Ponte Vecchio.
  ~€600+.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel David** — 4.9/5 (~4,900 reviews),
  family-run riverside warmth, free evening drinks. ~€160–220.
- *Budget:* **Hotel Pendini** — 4.7/5 (~4,700 reviews),
  vintage rooms on Piazza della Repubblica, unbeatable
  position. ~€120–170.

## Day 6 — The Renaissance, both galleries

Both close **Mondays** — this is why it's Day 6, not Day 7.

- **08:15** — **The Uffizi** at opening (**€25 gate / €29 with
  the advance reservation — pay the €4, certainty is the
  point**; from 2026 there's also a €16 after-16:00 discount
  slot). Botticelli's Venus and Primavera, Leonardo, Titian,
  Caravaggio; the corridor windows over the Arno are their own
  gallery. Three hours minimum. Closed Monday.
- **12:30** — Lunch at **Mercato Centrale** (~€10–15), then
  the leather-market gauntlet of San Lorenzo.
- **15:00** — **The Accademia** (€16 + €4 booking): one long
  hall of Michelangelo's unfinished Prisoners fighting out of
  their marble, and then **David**, absurdly better in person.
  Ninety minutes. Book the slot when you book the Uffizi.
- **17:30** — Santa Croce (Michelangelo's and Galileo's tombs,
  ~€8) or gelato-and-wander.
- **20:00** — The **bistecca alla fiorentina** night: sold by
  the kilo (~€50–60/kg, feeds two), rare or not at all, at a
  proper Tuscan grill.

## Day 7 — A Tuscan day: Pisa and Lucca, or Siena

Two honest options by train/bus — no tour bus required:

- **The classic combo:** regional train to **Pisa** (1 h,
  ~€9 each way): the Leaning Tower delivers exactly the photo
  and climb (~€20, timed) it promises, the Duomo beside it is
  the underrated masterpiece — two hours covers the Field of
  Miracles. Then 30 minutes to **Lucca**: rent bikes and ride
  the intact Renaissance **city walls** (4 km of tree-lined
  ramparts), climb the tower with oaks on top, dinner back in
  Florence.
- **The hill-town day:** bus (~1h15) to **Siena** — the
  shell-shaped Campo, the zebra-striped Duomo, medieval Italy
  with the volume up. Deeper than Pisa; photo less famous.
- Either way: **20:30** back in Florence for a last aperitivo
  on a rooftop (view of the dome, ~€12).

## Day 8 — To Venice, the reveal

- **09:00** — Firenze S.M.N. → **Venezia Santa Lucia, ~2h05**
  (from €25–40). Sit left for lagoon glimpses.
- **11:30** — Walk out of the station doors: the Grand Canal
  is simply *there*. Ride **vaporetto line 1** the slow way
  down the whole canal to San Marco (single €9.50 — buy the
  **48-hour pass, ~€35**, it pays for itself by ride four).
- **13:00** — Check in, lunch standing at a **bacaro**:
  cicchetti (€1.50–3 each) and an ombra of wine — Venice's
  tapas, Venice's prices' one mercy.
- **15:00** — **St Mark's Square**: the Basilica's golden
  mosaics (**€3 timed entry — book online**, skip the €6
  no-booking queue; modest dress), the Campanile lift (~€12)
  or save the tower for Day 9's empty morning.
- **18:30** — Get lost on purpose — Venice rewards no map
  between 6 and 8 p.m. — surfacing at the **Rialto Bridge**
  at dusk.
- **20:30** — Seafood dinner away from San Marco's laminated
  menus (Cannaregio or Castello back streets, ~€30–45 a
  head).

**Where to stay in Venice** (two nights)

- *Luxury:* **Hotel Palazzo Stern** — 4.8/5 (~2,600 reviews),
  Grand Canal terrace breakfasts by the Accademia bridge.
  ~€300–450.
- *Mid-range:* **Hotel Antiche Figure** — 4.8/5 (~5,800
  reviews), canal-front opposite the station — arrival could
  not be easier. ~€200–280.
- *Value-boutique:* **Rosa Salva Hotel** — 4.9/5 (~1,700
  reviews), steps from San Marco above the city's old pastry
  house. ~€180–250. (True budget in Venice means **Mestre**
  on the mainland — 40% cheaper, 10-minute trains, still
  inside the access-fee-exempt comune — a fair trade for
  families.)

## Day 9 — Empty Venice, then the painted islands

- **07:00** — The trip's secret hour: **San Marco before the
  day-trippers** — the square shared with pigeons and
  photographers only. Coffee standing at a bar (€1.30; the
  same cup seated on the square costs €7 and rents you the
  orchestra).
- **09:00** — **Doge's Palace** (~€30, book online): the
  Tintoretto-ceilinged council halls, then across the **Bridge
  of Sighs** into the prisons. Two hours.
- **11:30** — Vaporetto 12 to the islands: **Murano** for a
  glass-furnace demonstration, then **Burano** — the
  fisherman's houses in forty shades of defiance, the leaning
  campanile, lunch of risotto de gò (~€25). Half a day; the
  boat rides are the lagoon's best tour.
- **17:30** — Back for golden hour. **The gondola question,
  honestly:** the official rate is **€90 for 30 minutes**
  (€110 after 19:00), per boat not per person — split six
  ways it's a bargain, as a couple it's a splurge that many
  call the trip's peak anyway. The €2 **traghetto** ferry
  crossing is the sixty-second budget version.
- **20:00** — Farewell dinner in Dorsoduro, spritz on the
  Zattere as the lagoon goes pink.

## Day 10 — Home from Venice

- **Morning** — One last campo coffee, then **Alilaguna boat**
  (~€15) or bus to VCE — home via a Gulf or European hub.
  Open-jaw (into Rome, out of Venice) means the triangle never
  backtracks.

## What to pack

- The 20,000-step shoes — cobbles, dome stairs, bridges;
  Venice alone is 400 bridges of them.
- **Shoulder/knee cover** for the Vatican, St Mark's and every
  church you'll duck into (a light scarf solves it).
- A zipped crossbody worn in front (see pickpockets, above).
- Refillable bottle (Rome's nasoni), sunglasses, and a light
  layer for train air-conditioning.
- Comfortable smart-casual for evenings — Italians notice, and
  so will your photos.
- Kids: a gelato budget line and a Venice "find the lion"
  scavenger list (winged lions are everywhere — it works).

## FAQ

**Is 10 days enough for three cities?**
Yes — the 4+3+2 weighting is deliberate: Rome needs the four,
Venice concentrates. With 2 extra days, add a Tuscany night
(Siena) or the Dolomites from Venice. With fewer than 8, cut
the Tuscan day trip before you cut Venice to one night.

**Which tickets actually sell out?**
In order of panic: **Colosseum** (30-day window, gone in
hours), **Brunelleschi's dome** (weeks ahead), **Borghese
Gallery** (small timed batches), **Uffizi/Accademia** summer
slots, **Vatican** morning slots. And remember the Monday
trap: **Uffizi and Accademia both close Mondays**.

**The Venice access fee — do we pay it?**
Staying overnight in Venice (or Mestre): **no — but you must
register the exemption online** and carry the QR; fines reach
€300. On the ~60 peak dates (April–July 2026), day-trippers
pay €5 (€10 last-minute). If your Venice leg is a day trip
from Florence, budget it and book it.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Vegetarians live magnificently — pasta pomodoro, cacio e pepe,
margherita, ribollita, risottos and gelato are all meat-free
by birth. Halal takes more work than Spain's big cities:
Rome's Esquilino/Termini quarter and Florence's station area
have solid halal kitchens; Venice thins out — seafood
(everywhere, unambiguous) is the workhorse. Say "senza
guanciale" where pork lurks in sauces.

**Gondola or skip?**
Officially €90/30 min per boat (six seats) — split with
another family it's €30 a couple and unambiguous yes; alone,
ride vaporetto line 1 at dusk first (€9.50, same canal,
longer) and see if the itch survives. The €2 traghetto
crossing is the honest micro-dose.

**Rome in a wheelchair / with a stroller?**
Harder than most capitals — cobbles, steps, packed metros —
but improving: the Colosseum, Vatican and Uffizi all run
step-free routes and free companion entry; Venice is the
tough one (bridges), though line-1 vaporetti and the main
routes are manageable. Plan museum-heavy, hill-light days.

**Should we add the Amalfi Coast?**
Not to this trip — it deserves its own (Naples, Pompeii,
Positano, Capri: the south is a different country in the best
way). It's first in line for our second Italy guide.
