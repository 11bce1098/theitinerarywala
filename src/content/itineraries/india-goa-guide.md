There are two Goas, and most trips only meet one. North Goa is the
famous one — beach shacks, cliff forts, the best casual food scene
in India. South Goa is the better beach — wide, quiet,
palm-backed. Between them sit a Portuguese Latin Quarter and the
largest churches Asia's colonial era ever built. This guide does
all of it in one week: three nights north, three nights south,
culture in the middle — and tells you the transport and season
truths that Goa's crowded guidebook shelf tends to soften.

**Go November–February** for the classic season (28–32°C, calm
sea, everything open). October and March are warm shoulders at
better prices. **Know the December reality:** Christmas–New Year
week doubles or triples prices, and festival weeks pack the north.
Monsoon Goa (June–September) is lush, moody and 60% cheaper — but
the sea is unswimmable and shacks are shut; it's a different,
quieter product.

All costs are in Indian rupees (INR), with conversions calculated
at today's exchange rate.

## Before you go: the short version of the admin

Visas, UPI One World, SIMs and general India notes live in our
Golden Triangle guide. Goa specifics — and these matter more here
than anywhere in India:

- **Two airports, 60 km apart.** **Mopa (GOX)** in the far north —
  newer, growing international schedule — and **Dabolim (GOI)**
  mid-state, closer to the south. Check which one your flight
  actually uses before booking hotels; a wrong assumption costs a
  ₹2,500 taxi.
- **There is no Uber or Ola in Goa.** The taxi unions keep them
  out. Your real options: **prepaid airport counters** (Mopa →
  Calangute ₹2,000–2,500; Dabolim → Calangute ₹1,400–1,800),
  the state's **GoaMiles app** (~20% under street rates; drivers
  sometimes cancel long runs), hotel-arranged cars (~₹3,500 for
  8 h/80 km), and motorcycle-taxi "pilots" for solo hops.
  Budget transport honestly — it's Goa's one genuinely expensive
  item.
- **The scooter rule that gets tourists fined:** rent only
  vehicles with **black number plates** (yellow/white lettering) —
  white private plates are illegal to rent and police impound them.
  ₹500–800/day, helmet always (checks are real, so is
  drink-driving enforcement — Goa is not lawless, whatever its
  reputation).
- **Sea safety, plainly:** swim between the flags at lifeguarded
  beaches, never at night, never after drinking; pre-monsoon and
  monsoon currents are killers every year. The calm-looking sea is
  the one that isn't.

### Travelling with kids?

- Goa is India's easiest beach holiday with children: shallow
  gentle water at **Palolem**, shacks that welcome kids for hours,
  and short distances.
- The north's traffic and party strips are the drawback — the
  south half of this guide is the family half; families can
  simply invert the ratio (2 north, 4 south).
- Dudhsagar's jeep track is bumpy but school-age kids love it;
  the spice-farm lunch day is the easiest crowd-pleaser.

## Day 1 — Land, north base, first sunset

Base yourself around **Candolim–Sinquerim** — central-north
position, calmer than Baga, everything reachable.

- **Afternoon** — Land, prepaid taxi or GoaMiles to the north
  coast; check in, drop everything.
- **17:00** — Walk **Candolim/Sinquerim beach** as the sun drops —
  first feet in the Arabian Sea, Aguada fort on the headland.
- **19:30** — First shack dinner: grilled kingfish, butter garlic
  prawns, a cold drink with sand underfoot. This is 60% of why
  people love Goa; no correction needed.

**Where to stay, north** (three nights)

- *Upscale:* **Seashell Suites and Villas** (Candolim) — superbly
  rated family-run villas and gardens.
- *Mid-range:* **Ginger Goa Candolim** — clean, well-run, walkable
  to the strip.
- *Budget:* **Silver Sands Sunshine** — on Beach Road, minutes
  from the sand.
- *The scene alternative:* the **Assagao–Anjuna** belt inland has
  Goa's boutique-village vibe — pick by current reviews if cafés
  beat beachfront for you.

## Day 2 — Forts, cliffs and the Assagao table

Scooter or hired car day along the north coast.

- **9:30** — **Fort Aguada** (~9:30–18:00, small fee) — the big
  17th-century Portuguese fort and lighthouse over the sea.
- **11:30** — North to **Anjuna**: the rocky cliff-line café
  stretch; Wednesdays add the famous **flea market** (in season).
- **13:00** — Lunch on the Anjuna–Vagator cliffs, sea below.
- **15:30** — **Vagator beach** and the red cliffs; then up to
  **Chapora Fort** for sunset — minimal walls, maximum view, and
  every Indian visitor re-enacting *Dil Chahta Hai* (small climb;
  go 45 min before sunset).
- **19:30** — Dinner in **Assagao**, Goa's food capital — the
  garden-villa restaurants here are the best casual dining in
  India and they book out in season: reserve a day or two ahead.

## Day 3 — Old Goa and the Latin Quarter

The culture day — and the one most beach trips wrongly skip.

- **9:00** — **Old Goa** (~30–40 min inland): the **Basilica of
  Bom Jesus** (~9:00–18:30, Sunday from later; free) — home of
  St Francis Xavier's relics and Asia's great Baroque interior —
  and the vast white **Sé Cathedral** across the lawn. Two hours
  covers the UNESCO core.
- **12:00** — Into **Panjim**: lunch of the definitive Goan fish
  thali (the old-school Panjim institutions do it best,
  ~₹250–400).
- **14:00** — **Fontainhas**, the Latin Quarter — ochre and
  indigo Portuguese houses, azulejo tiles, tiny chapels; wander
  solo or take the excellent 2-hour heritage walk (~₹500–900).
  Gelato and gallery stops built in.
- **17:00** — Mandovi riverfront stroll; the floating casinos are
  a Goa fact — go if it amuses you, budget it as entertainment
  not investment.
- **19:00** — Back north for a final Assagao/Anjuna dinner, or
  eat in Panjim and ride home under the lights.

## Day 4 — Spice farm south, arrive Palolem

- **9:30** — Check out; car south (~2 h to Palolem, ₹1,500–2,000 —
  or a hired car for the day to include the stop).
- **11:00** — **Spice plantation, Ponda** (~₹600–800 including a
  guided walk and Goan buffet lunch) — cardamom, pepper vines,
  vanilla and cashew-feni talk; touristy, fragrant, worth it.
- **15:00** — Arrive **Palolem**, check in. The crescent bay —
  calm, palm-backed, boat-dotted — is South Goa's postcard.
- **17:00** — Swim the gentle shelf, then walk the full crescent
  as it golds.
- **19:30** — Beachfront dinner; the south's shacks are quieter
  and close earlier — the soundtrack is waves, not bass.

**Where to stay, south** (three nights)

- *Resort:* **The LaLiT Golf & Spa** — the sprawling private-beach
  five-star, for the full flop.
- *Mid-range:* **Gateway Goa, Palolem** — superbly rated rooftop
  sea views above the bay.
- *Budget:* **Hilia's Retreat** — spotless and two minutes from
  the sand; Palolem's classic beach-hut rows (₹1,500–4,000) are
  the alternative in season.

## Day 5 — The slow south

- **7:30** — Optional **dolphin boat** from Palolem (~₹300–500
  shared) — humpback dolphins most mornings, plus **Butterfly
  Beach** drop-offs.
- **10:30** — **Agonda** (15 min north) — the quietest major
  beach in Goa: one long walk, one long lunch.
- **15:00** — Choose: **Cabo de Rama fort** on its headland
  (free, dramatic, empty), kayak rental back at Palolem
  (~₹300–500/h), or professional nothing.
- **18:00** — Sunset from **Palolem's south rocks** or Agonda;
  seafood dinner where the day leaves you.

## Day 6 — Jungle day: Dudhsagar — or the deeper flop

- **Option A — Dudhsagar Falls** (season roughly **October–May**):
  India's fourth-highest waterfall, 310 m of milk-white cascade in
  the Western Ghats. Organised day trips run ~₹2,200/person
  including the Mollem jeep safari, lifejacket, entry and a spice
  plantation + lunch (pickups mainly from north hotels — from the
  south, have your driver run you to the **Kulem jeep stand** and
  book directly, closer and cheaper). Falls close ~16:30; the
  plunge-pool swim is the payoff. Bumpy, splashy, great.
- **Option B** — A second full south day: yoga shala morning,
  Patnem's mellow sands, an Ayurveda massage, the hammock you've
  been auditioning all week.
- **Evening** — Farewell feast: order the Goan trinity properly —
  fish curry rice, pork vindaloo (the real, vinegary original) or
  chicken cafreal, and bebinca for dessert.

## Day 7 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Last swim at first light — the sea is calmest and
  the beach is yours — then out via **Dabolim** (~1.5 h from
  Palolem) or Mopa (~2.5 h; allow buffer either way).

## What to pack

- Reef-safe sunscreen in quantity, hat, sunglasses — the beach
  math is relentless.
- Swimwear ×2 (one always drying) and a cover-up: swimwear is for
  the beach, not the market or temple.
- Sandals plus one closed pair for scooters, forts and the
  Dudhsagar rocks.
- A dry bag for the jeep-and-waterfall day; quick-dry towel.
- Mosquito repellent for dusk; light long sleeves double for AC
  and evening bugs.
- International driving permit if you'll rent wheels — police
  checks ask.
- Room in the bag for cashew feni, kokum, and spice-farm loot.

## FAQ

**North or south — if I had to choose one?**
Nightlife, food scene and things-to-do: north. Beach itself,
calm and families: south. That's the entire debate, and it's why
this guide simply does both — the 90-minute transfer is the
cheapest upgrade in Goa.

**Is Baga–Calangute worth visiting?**
One curious evening if you like crowds-as-spectacle; as a base,
we'd steer you to Candolim, the Assagao belt, or the far-north
Morjim–Ashwem stretch. India's most famous beach strip is its
least Goan corner.

**Is Goa just parties?**
That reputation is 20 years stale. The modern draws are the food
scene, Portuguese heritage, quiet southern sands and the Ghats'
green interior — parties exist for those who seek them and are
entirely avoidable for those who don't.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Both straightforward: Goa's kitchens run seafood-first (halal
travellers should confirm meat specifics per restaurant — seafood
is the easy default), and vegetarian thali houses and the Assagao
café scene cover plant-based eating generously.

**Monsoon Goa — yes or no?**
Yes *if* you want green hills, empty roads, spa prices and don't
need the sea. No for a first classic beach trip. Dudhsagar in
monsoon is at maximum thunder but access switches to the train
window view — the jeep track closes.

**What pairs with Goa?**
Fly-in pairings: Mumbai (1 h) for the city contrast, or Hampi's
ruins (overnight bus/drive) for the adventurous. Within our
guides, Kerala is the like-minded southern sibling — sea, spice
and slow — for a two-week coastal India run.
