Germany's two great cities disagree about almost everything, and
that's the trip. Munich is the south: church towers and beer
gardens, lederhosen worn without irony, the Alps filling the end
of the street on clear days — with King Ludwig's Neuschwanstein,
the castle Disney copied, two hours away. Berlin is the
capital-of-the-20th-century: the Wall's scar line, museums
holding Nefertiti, techno in old power stations, and a
parliament you can walk on top of, free.

An ICE train sews them together in four hours. Ten days does
both properly, plus the Alps, plus a day in a second country if
you feel like it — your Schengen visa doesn't notice borders.

**Go May–June or September** (September in Munich means
**Oktoberfest** — mid-September to early October: the greatest
party on earth *and* doubled hotel prices; decide deliberately,
book a year out or dodge the dates). December is the other
Germany: Christmas-market season, glühwein and lights — a
future guide of its own. All costs are in euros (EUR).

## Before you go: the admin

### Visa and borders

Schengen mechanics — the EES biometric border now live, the €20
ETIAS coming for visa-free passports, the standard file — are
covered in depth in our [France guide](/itineraries/france-9-days-guide/).
Germany-specific: UAE applications run through **VFS Global**
(Dubai/Abu Dhabi), fee **€90** + service charges, and summer
appointment lead runs **4–8 weeks** — apply where Germany is
your main destination (most nights), which this itinerary
satisfies. Bank statements, employment proof and refundable
bookings remain the file's spine.

### Money — Germany's cash surprise

- The country that invented the €2,000 espresso machine still
  loves **cash**: plenty of bakeries, beer gardens, market
  stalls and even restaurants are cash-only or card-above-€10.
  **Carry €50–100** at all times — this catches every
  first-timer.
- Tipping: round up or ~5–10% ("machen Sie 28" as you hand over
  €30). Service is included; nobody expects US percentages.
- **Pfand:** bottles carry a €0.08–0.25 deposit — return them
  at any supermarket machine, or leave them *beside* bins for
  collectors (a quiet local courtesy).
- Tap water isn't standard in restaurants — order
  "Leitungswasser" and expect the occasional frown; bottled
  ("still" or "sprudel") is the default.

### The Sunday rule, trains, kids

- **Sundays, nearly everything retail is CLOSED** — shops,
  supermarkets, malls. Museums, bakeries (mornings),
  restaurants and beer gardens stay open. Plan museum days and
  park days on Sundays; do shopping Saturday.
- **Trains price like airlines:** the ICE Munich–Berlin runs
  ~4 hours with **Super Sparpreis fares from ~€20–40** released
  months out (walk-up can be €120+). Book when dates fix.
  Honesty: **Deutsche Bahn's punctuality is a running national
  joke** — pad any tight connection by one train.
- **The regional-ticket cheat codes:** the **Bayern-Ticket**
  (~€40–45 for two, per day) covers all Bavarian regional
  trains and local transport — it *is* the Neuschwanstein and
  Salzburg day-trip ticket. The **€58/month Deutschland-Ticket**
  covers regional transit nationwide and can beat everything if
  your trip sits inside one calendar month — do the maths in
  the DB Navigator app (your one essential download).
- **Kids:** under-18s free at Neuschwanstein and most state
  museums, under-6s free almost universally, beer gardens are
  family playgrounds (really — playgrounds *in* the gardens),
  and the trains are entertainment.

## Day 1 — Land in Munich, first Maß

Stay in the **Altstadt** (walk everywhere) or by the
**Hauptbahnhof** (cheaper, connected, 12 minutes' walk in).

- **Afternoon** — Land MUC (non-stop from most of Europe, the
  Gulf and North America);
  S-Bahn or Lufthansa Express bus in (~€13–15, 40 min).
- **17:30** — First walk: **Marienplatz** — the neo-Gothic
  Rathaus with its glockenspiel silent until morning — through
  to the **Viktualienmarkt's** closing hour.
- **19:30** — The initiation: a beer hall. **Hofbräuhaus** for
  the world-famous circus (oompah band, litre Maß, pretzels
  the size of steering wheels) or **Augustiner Bräustuben**
  for what locals actually drink (~€20–30 with dinner;
  alcohol-free wheat beer is a fully respectable order here).

**Where to stay in Munich** (five nights)

- *Old-town classic:* **Platzl Hotel** — 4.5/5 on TripAdvisor
  (~3,300 reviews), Bavarian-smart rooms 100 m from
  Marienplatz. ~€250–350.
- *Historic mid-range:* **Hotel Torbräu** — 4.6/5 (~1,800
  reviews), in business since 1490, by the Isartor gate.
  ~€200–280.
- *Value:* **Hotel Mirabell** — 4.4/5 (~1,700 reviews), fresh
  and family-run near the station. ~€130–180.

## Day 2 — Munich old town, properly

- **09:30** — Climb **St Peter's tower** (~€5, 306 steps) for
  the red-roof-and-Alps panorama, positioned for the **11:00
  Glockenspiel** performance on Marienplatz below (also
  12:00; it's charmingly clunky — knights joust, coopers
  dance, tourists film).
- **11:30** — **Frauenkirche's** twin onion domes, the
  **Residenz** (~€10 — the Wittelsbachs' 130-room palace;
  the Antiquarium hall alone justifies it) or the tiny rococo
  explosion of the **Asamkirche**.
- **13:30** — **Viktualienmarkt lunch**: leberkäse semmel or
  cheese stalls, a shady beer-garden table in the middle
  (~€10–18).
- **15:30** — **The English Garden**: bigger than Central
  Park — watch the **Eisbach river surfers** at the south
  entrance (surfing, in a city park, in Bavaria), then walk
  north to the **Chinesischer Turm beer garden** for the
  full self-service ritual (you may even bring your own
  picnic food; buying the beer is the rent).
- **19:30** — Dinner in Glockenbach or Haidhausen — modern
  Munich beyond the halls (~€20–35).

## Day 3 — Neuschwanstein: the fairy-tale day

- **07:00** — Regional train toward **Füssen** (~2 h;
  a **Bayern-Ticket** covers both of you + the bus), bus
  73/78 to Hohenschwangau village.
- **The ticket truth:** castle interiors are **timed guided
  tours only** — **€21 adult + €2.50 booking fee, under-18s
  free** — and slots **sell out days-to-weeks ahead**
  (same-day tickets exist in theory, disappear by
  mid-morning in practice). Book on the official
  Hohenschwangau shop the day your dates fix.
- **10:30** — Walk up (30–40 min, or shared horse-carriage/
  shuttle) to **Neuschwanstein** — Ludwig II's 1869 opera-set
  castle, all turrets and Wagner murals, unfinished when the
  king died mysteriously in a lake. The 30-minute interior
  tour is honestly the *lesser* half —
- **12:00** — — because the prize is the **Marienbrücke**
  bridge view: the castle floating over the gorge, the
  Alpsee behind. Queue for the bridge photo, then take the
  path 100 m *past* it for the same shot minus forty
  elbows.
- **14:00** — Lakeside hour at the **Alpsee** (swans,
  swimming in summer) or the yellow **Hohenschwangau**
  castle where Ludwig actually grew up.
- **18:30** — Back in Munich; easy dinner near the hotel.

## Day 4 — Top of Germany, or Mozart's Salzburg

Two brilliant days; pick by weather (webcams decide):

- **The Alps day:** train to Garmisch (Bayern-Ticket), then
  the **Zugspitze** — cogwheel train + record-breaking cable
  car to **Germany's summit at 2,962 m**: **€78 adult / €39
  child round trip (2026 summer rate)**, glacier terrace,
  400 peaks on a clear day. Descend via the **Eibsee** cable
  car and walk part of that impossibly turquoise lake before
  the train home.
- **The Austria day:** regional train to **Salzburg**
  (~1h50 — and yes, your Schengen visa crosses this border
  without blinking): Getreidegasse and Mozart's birthplace,
  the fortress funicular, Mirabell's *Sound of Music*
  gardens, apfelstrudel. Cheaper than the mountain, heavier
  on charm.
- **19:30** — Either way: back for a Munich beer-garden
  evening if the weather holds.

## Day 5 — Dachau, then a gentler afternoon

- **09:00** — **Dachau Concentration Camp Memorial** (S2 +
  bus, ~45 min; **free**, audio guide ~€4.50): the first
  camp, 1933, and the template. Three hours of heavy,
  necessary history — the "Arbeit macht frei" gate, the
  barracks, the memorial chapels. *With kids: the site
  advises 12+; families with younger children should swap
  this for Nymphenburg Palace and read together instead.*
- **14:00** — Deliberately light afternoon: **Nymphenburg
  Palace** gardens, or **Olympiapark + BMW Welt** (the
  showroom is free and the building alone earns it).
- **19:00** — Farewell-Bavaria dinner: schweinshaxe or
  käsespätzle done properly (~€20–35). Pack for the north.

## Day 6 — The ICE to Berlin, the Gate at dusk

- **09:00** — Munich Hbf → **Berlin Hbf, ~4 h** (Sparpreis
  from ~€20–40 booked early). Germany scrolls by at 300
  km/h: Franconia, Thuringia's forests, the flat north.
- **14:00** — Check in, then walk the axis: **Brandenburg
  Gate** → the **Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe**
  (walk into the stelae field; it's designed to disorient)
  → **Pariser Platz**.
- **17:30** — **The Reichstag dome** — **free, but register
  online weeks ahead** (passport details required; same-day
  slots exist at the service centre with 2+ hours' notice,
  originals-only ID): Norman Foster's glass spiral over the
  Bundestag chamber, sunset over the city. The best €0 in
  Germany.
- **20:00** — Dinner initiation: **currywurst** at a proper
  Imbiss (~€5) as starter, Berlin's world-food scene as the
  main (~€15–30).

**Where to stay in Berlin** (four nights)

- *Luxury:* **Hotel Adlon Kempinski** — 4.6/5 (~5,200
  reviews), *the* address beside the Brandenburg Gate since
  1907. ~€400+.
- *Mid-range:* **Meliá Berlin** — 4.5/5 (~7,300 reviews),
  riverside between Friedrichstraße and Museum Island;
  TripAdvisor's #1 value here. ~€150–220.
- *Value:* **Holiday Inn Express Alexanderplatz** — 4.3/5,
  breakfast included, U-Bahn on the doorstep. ~€100–140.

## Day 7 — The Wall day

- **09:30** — **Berlin Wall Memorial, Bernauer Straße**
  (free): the last complete stretch — wall, death strip,
  watchtower — with the documentation centre's viewing
  platform. This, not Checkpoint Charlie, is where the Wall
  makes sense.
- **12:00** — **Tränenpalast** (free — the "Palace of Tears"
  border hall at Friedrichstraße) and lunch nearby.
- **14:00** — **Topography of Terror** (free, on the Gestapo
  site) and a walk past **Checkpoint Charlie** (photo, ignore
  the actors) — then U-Bahn east.
- **16:00** — **East Side Gallery**: 1.3 km of the Wall
  turned into murals — the Fraternal Kiss, the Trabant
  bursting through — golden light on the Spree side.
- **19:30** — **Kreuzberg** evening: Berlin invented the
  döner kebab and Kreuzberg perfected it — from €5
  masterpieces to Turkish grills, this is also the trip's
  halal home turf. Finish with a canal-side bar (~€15–25
  all-in).

## Day 8 — Museum Island and the East

- **09:30** — **Museum Island** (**€24 day ticket covers all
  four open museums + the Pergamon Panorama**; single
  museums €14): start at the **Neues Museum** before 11:30 —
  **Nefertiti** holds court alone in the North Dome — then
  the Alte Nationalgalerie's Romantics or the Bode's
  sculpture rotunda. **Honesty: the Pergamonmuseum itself is
  closed until June 2027**; the "Das Panorama" rotunda
  across the canal (included) shows 80 originals with a
  360° reconstruction — good, not the same, and this guide
  won't pretend otherwise.
- **13:30** — Lunch at Hackescher Markt's courtyards
  (Hackesche Höfe street art through the back gates).
- **15:30** — Pick your east: the **DDR Museum** (~€13 —
  hands-on Ostalgie: drive a Trabi simulator, open the
  listening devices) or the **TV Tower** at Alexanderplatz
  (~€25, book a slot) for the full-city sweep.
- **19:30** — Dinner in Prenzlauer Berg — leafy, stroller-
  thick, excellent kitchens (~€20–35).

## Day 9 — Potsdam: the palace day

- **09:00** — Regional train to **Potsdam** (~40 min, covered
  by a Berlin ABC day ticket): **Sanssouci** — Frederick the
  Great's "without worries" vineyard palace (**~€22 with the
  park's palaces; timed entry, book morning slots**) — the
  terraced gardens alone are worth the trip, and they're
  free.
- **13:00** — Lunch in the **Dutch Quarter's** red-brick
  lanes.
- **14:30** — **Cecilienhof** (where Truman, Stalin and
  Churchill carved up 1945) for the history-completists, or
  simply more park.
- **17:30** — Back for the last Berlin evening: rooftop hour
  (Klunkerkranich if queues allow), then the dinner you've
  been saving — Michelin-bistro Berlin runs cheaper than
  anywhere comparable in Europe (~€35–60).

## Day 10 — Home from BER

- **Morning** — Airport Express from Hbf (~30 min). Direct
  flights home. Two Germanys, one train line, zero
  rental cars.

## What to pack

- Layers and a rain shell — Munich's Alpine weather turns in
  an hour, Berlin adds wind; summer evenings still want a
  jacket.
- Proper walking shoes (15,000-step cities) with grip for
  castle paths and the Marienbrücke boards.
- **Cash wallet habit**: €50–100 on you, coins for Pfand
  machines and bakery breakfasts.
- Passport originals for the Reichstag (copies rejected) and
  train checks.
- A dressier layer for the nicer Berlin dinner; sunglasses
  for the Zugspitze glacier glare (genuinely needed).
- Kids: the wooden-toy budget for Christmas-shop windows
  exists whether you plan it or not.

## FAQ

**Is Neuschwanstein worth the day?**
Yes — with recalibrated expectations: the 30-minute interior
tour is a curiosity (Ludwig finished only a third of it);
the *day* — the Marienbrücke view, the Alpsee, the walk —
is the masterpiece. Book the tour for the story, come for
the postcard. Miss the ticket window and the exterior +
bridge still justify the trip.

**Oktoberfest — plan around it or into it?**
Into it, deliberately, or around it, strictly. Mid-September
to the first October Sunday: hotel prices double-to-triple
and tents are booked out months ahead (though walk-in
mornings work on weekdays). Any other date, Munich's beer
gardens deliver 80% of the joy at 40% of the cost, year
round.

**Halal and vegetarian food?**
Berlin is one of Europe's great halal cities — the
Kreuzberg/Neukölln döner-and-grill economy is vast, and the
city's Turkish and Arab kitchens are everywhere. Munich is
thinner but fine (halal spots cluster near the station;
Türkitch is the local favourite). Vegetarians do better
than Germany's sausage image suggests: käsespätzle, obazda,
pretzels (lye-baked, not lard), falafel Berlin, and every
menu now carries veggie mains. The trap: many traditional
soups and potato dishes hide speck — ask.

**Munich or Berlin with kids?**
Both, differently: Munich is the outdoor parent's city
(English Garden, Alpsee swims, the toboggan-adjacent joy of
the Zugspitze snowfield) — Berlin is the teenager's (DDR
Museum's Trabi, East Side murals, food they choose
themselves). Dachau is recommended 12+; Sachsenhausen
likewise — plan the split day accordingly.

**Can we add Prague or Vienna?**
Both are 4–5 ICE/railjet hours from this route's ends and
both deserve their own trips (and, being Czechia/Austria,
the same Schengen visa). As a +2-day extension from Berlin,
Prague is the natural add. Otherwise: future guides.

**What's Germany guide #2?**
December's **Christmas markets** (Nuremberg–Munich–
Rothenburg) or the **Rhine + Romantic Road** castles-and-
villages loop. The markets version joins our winter
specials the moment it's written.
