Uzbekistan is what happens when the greatest architecture of the
medieval world survives into an era of cheap flights and almost no
crowds. Samarkand's Registan is the single most spectacular square
in Asia; Bukhara is a living old town where the minaret that awed
Genghis Khan still stands; and Khiva — just named to National
Geographic's Best of the World 2026 list — is an entire walled city
you sleep inside. High-speed trains link most of it, dinner costs
pocket change, and the route runs one direction with no
backtracking: land in Tashkent, fly home from the far end.

Timing matters here: **April–May and September–October** are the
season. Summer hits 40°C+ on the desert legs; winter is doable and
empty but cold. This guide assumes shoulder season.

All costs are in Uzbek som (UZS), with conversions calculated at
today's exchange rate — roughly 12,800 som to the US dollar, so the
numbers look enormous and the prices are tiny.

## Before you go: the 15-minute admin

### Visas — newly easy

- **Visa-free (30 days):** most European passports, the UK, Japan,
  Korea, GCC states including the UAE, and ~90 others. No forms,
  no fees.
- **Indian passports — new for 2026:** Uzbekistan announced
  **30-day visa-free entry for Indian citizens on 30 August 2026**.
  The announcement is recent, so confirm the implementation status
  before booking; the fallback is the painless **e-visa** at
  [e-visa.gov.uz](https://e-visa.gov.uz) — ~USD 20, fully online,
  issued in ~3 working days, 30-day stay.
- **US citizens** and several others use the same e-visa.
- **Hotel registration:** licensed hotels register foreign guests
  automatically — keep the slips (or e-registration confirmations)
  until you exit; border officers occasionally ask.

### Money

- Currency: the som (UZS). You will be a millionaire by lunch;
  ~12,800 som ≈ USD 1.
- **Cards work in cities now** — Visa best, Mastercard widely —
  at chain hotels, upmarket restaurants and supermarkets. But
  bazaars, taxis, guesthouses and most local restaurants are
  **cash country: expect ~80% of spending in cash** outside
  Tashkent.
- ATMs: **Kapitalbank and NBU** are the reliable ones for foreign
  cards; withdraw big (daily limits ~2–5 million som) to save
  fees, and choose "without conversion" when the machine asks.
- Bring **crisp US dollars** as backup — worn notes get refused at
  exchange, a Central Asian universal.
- The scale of things: plov 25,000–40,000 som, a taxi across town
  15,000–40,000, museum entries 50,000–100,000.

### SIM and data

- Tourist SIMs are cheap and easy: **Ucell, Beeline or Mobiuz**
  counters at Tashkent airport or any city branch, passport
  required, ~40,000–90,000 som for generous data.
- **Yandex Go** is the taxi app everywhere on this route — install
  it before you fly and pay drivers in cash. It removes every
  negotiation this trip would otherwise contain.

### Trains — the skill this trip runs on

- The **Afrosiyob** high-speed train links Tashkent → Samarkand
  (~2h10) → Bukhara (~1.5h more). Book at
  [railway.uz](https://railway.uz) — tickets open **60 days out**
  and popular departures **sell out days ahead** in season;
  foreign cards have worked reliably on the site since early 2026.
- **To Khiva:** the new **Jaloliddin Manguberdi** high-speed
  service (launched May 2026) runs the full Tashkent–Samarkand–
  Bukhara–**Khiva** line — but only ~3 days a week, so match your
  Bukhara→Khiva day to its timetable when you book everything
  else. On non-train days, a shared or private car crosses the
  Kyzylkum desert in ~6–7 hours (~USD 60–80 per car) — a
  legitimately atmospheric drive.
- Passports are checked at boarding; arrive 30 minutes early.

### Travelling with kids?

- Uzbeks adore children — expect your kid to be fed, photographed
  and adored at every bazaar.
- The cities are flat and stroller-workable (cobbles aside), train
  legs are short, and courtyard guesthouses give run-around space.
- The two watch-items: sun and heat even in shoulder season
  (hats, water, midday breaks), and minaret climbs — steep, dark
  and not toddler territory. Registan's vast open square, though,
  is a four-year-old's dream racetrack.

## Day 1 — Land in Tashkent

Direct flights reach Tashkent in ~3 hours from the Gulf (flydubai,
Air Arabia, Uzbekistan Airways) and nonstop from most regional
hubs. Assumes a morning arrival.

- **12:00** — Check in, SIM sorted, som withdrawn. Lunch near the
  hotel — start as you mean to continue: somsa (tandoor-baked meat
  pastries) and green tea.
- **15:00** — **Khast Imam complex** (~9:00–18:00; small fees) —
  Tashkent's spiritual heart, home to the **Uthman Quran**, one of
  the oldest Qurans on earth, written within decades of the
  Prophet's death.
- **17:30** — **Amir Timur Square** and a first ride on the
  **Tashkent metro** (~3,000 som a token) — the stations are
  Soviet palaces: chandeliers at Kosmonavtlar, cotton-blossom
  ceilings at Pakhtakor. Photography is allowed now; it wasn't for
  decades.
- **19:30** — Dinner: shashlik and salads at a chaikhana
  (teahouse) — 60,000–100,000 som for two.

**Where to stay in Tashkent** (two nights)

- *Upscale:* **Hyatt Regency Tashkent** — the city-centre
  standard-setter.
- *Mid-range:* **InterContinental Tashkent** — superbly rated,
  central; mid-range money here buys five-star space.
- *Budget:* **Wyndham Tashkent** — near the metro, clean and
  spacious with breakfast.

## Day 2 — Tashkent: bazaar, metro art and the plov pilgrimage

- **9:00** — **Chorsu Bazaar** — the turquoise-domed market:
  mountains of spices, dried fruit, warm non bread from the
  tandoor. Breakfast is whatever you point at.
- **11:30** — Metro-art hop back across town (Alisher Navoi,
  Kosmonavtlar stations).
- **12:15** — **Central Asian Plov Centre (Besh Qozon)** — the
  pilgrimage lunch: plov cooked in cauldrons the size of hot tubs,
  serving thousands daily. **Arrive by 12:30 — when the plov runs
  out, lunch is over** (~40,000–60,000 som with all the sides).
- **14:30** — Pick one: the **Museum of Applied Arts** (ceramics
  and suzani embroidery in a decorated mansion, ~50,000 som), or
  with kids, **Magic City** park's fairy-tale kitsch.
- **17:00** — Free evening — Broadway Street's artists, Tashkent
  City park's fountains.
- **21:00** — Pack light for the trains; tomorrow the Silk Road
  starts. Confirm your Afrosiyob seats.

## Day 3 — Afrosiyob to Samarkand, Registan at dusk

- **8:00** — **Afrosiyob to Samarkand** (~2h10; economy ~USD 20,
  business ~USD 33). Steppe out the window, tea trolley inside.
- **10:30** — Arrive, drop bags. Lunch near the Registan — try
  Samarkand's own plov (it's a rivalry; you're the judge).
- **13:00** — **Gur-Emir Mausoleum** (~9:00–19:00, 75,000 som) —
  Tamerlane's tomb under a ribbed turquoise dome, the blueprint
  for the Taj Mahal's ancestry.
- **15:30** — **The Registan** (7:00–24:00 — yes, midnight;
  100,000 som) — three madrasahs of tilework facing a single
  square, the reason Uzbekistan is on your list. Go now for
  afternoon light, stay through dusk: the buildings ignite at
  golden hour, and evenings often add a **light-and-sound show**
  visible free from the square.
- **20:00** — Dinner near the square — mid-range Samarkand runs
  ~120,000–200,000 som for two with wine (Uzbek wine is a
  cheerful surprise).

**Where to stay in Samarkand** (two nights)

- *Upscale:* **Hilton Samarkand Regency** — the modern Silk Road
  complex: views, pools, shuttle to the sights.
- *Mid-range:* **Kosh Havuz Boutique Hotel** — courtyard charm and
  a rooftop restaurant, walkable to the Registan.
- *Budget:* **ANTICA Family Guest House** — a 150-year-old family
  house with garden breakfasts, minutes from Gur-Emir.

## Day 4 — Samarkand: the avenue of the dead and the bread bazaar

- **8:00** — **Shah-i-Zinda** (~9:00–19:00 official, gates often
  earlier; 80,000 som) — a rising lane of mausoleums in the
  bluest tile on earth. First hour = pilgrims and photographers
  only; by 10:30 it's tour groups shoulder to shoulder.
- **10:30** — **Bibi-Khanym Mosque** (75,000 som) — Tamerlane's
  giant, half-ruin and all swagger — then next door into **Siyob
  Bazaar**: buy the famous Samarkand non bread (locals carry
  stacks home on flights) and halva by the slab.
- **13:00** — Lunch at a bazaar-side chaikhana.
- **15:00** — **Ulugbek Observatory** (75,000 som) — the
  astronomer-king's giant sextant trench, from when Samarkand
  measured the stars better than anywhere in Europe.
- **17:30** — Optional: paper-making at Konigil village
  (mulberry-bark paper, the old way), or rest.
- **19:30** — Second Registan evening — it's a different building
  by night, and your ticket day is done, so the square itself is
  free to linger in.

## Day 5 — Down the line to Bukhara

- **9:30** — Afrosiyob **Samarkand → Bukhara** (~1.5 h; arrives
  Bukhara's Kagan station, 15 min by taxi from the old town).
- **12:00** — Check in inside the old town; lunch at **Lyab-i-Hauz**
  — the 17th-century pool plaza shaded by mulberry trees, the
  city's living room.
- **14:00** — The **trading domes** — centuries-old covered
  bazaars still selling carpets, ikat silk, scissors from the
  blacksmith dynasty — and **Magoki-Attori**, the mosque built on
  a Zoroastrian temple.
- **16:30** — **Poi Kalyan complex** (mosque 20,000 som) — the
  **Kalyan Minaret**, 47 m of brick patterning so beautiful that
  Genghis Khan, who flattened everything else, ordered it spared.
- **18:30** — Rooftop teahouse facing the minaret for sunset —
  the classic Bukhara hour.
- **20:00** — Dinner off Lyab-i-Hauz — Bukhara's plov, kebabs, and
  the local specialty of apricot-stuffed everything.

**Where to stay in Bukhara** (two nights)

- *Boutique:* **Alexia Suite** — superbly rated suites in the
  heritage quarter.
- *Mid-range:* **Kavsar Boutique Hotel** — characterful rooms on a
  quiet courtyard by Lyab-i-Hauz.
- *Budget:* **Lyabi-House Hotel** — a restored 19th-century
  merchant house in the middle of everything.

## Day 6 — Bukhara: the Ark, the hammam and the last emir

- **9:00** — The **Ark Fortress** (~9:00–18:00, 60,000 som) —
  Bukhara's royal citadel, 1,500 years of emirs, with the
  photogenic **Bolo Hauz Mosque** and its twenty carved pillars
  across the road.
- **11:30** — Wander north to **Chor Minor** — the four-towered
  gatehouse that looks airlifted from a fairytale — through
  neighbourhoods where old Bukhara still just lives.
- **13:00** — Long chaikhana lunch; it's siesta-hot even in
  shoulder season.
- **15:00** — Choose: **Sitorai Mokhi-Khosa**, the last emir's
  summer palace of chandeliers and peacocks (~50,000 som), or a
  scrub-and-steam in the 16th-century **Bozori Kord hammam**
  (book a slot; ~USD 15–25).
- **18:00** — Golden hour on the old town's rooftops, then a
  slow farewell dinner — book a courtyard restaurant with live
  music tonight.

## Day 7 — Across the desert to Khiva

Match this day to the **Khiva train's ~3-weekly timetable** when
you book; otherwise it's the desert road.

- **Option A — Train:** the Jaloliddin Manguberdi high-speed
  service, Bukhara → Khiva in ~3.5–4 h — the Kyzylkum desert at
  250 km/h.
- **Option B — Car:** private or shared car, ~6–7 h across the
  desert past camels and cotton, with a chaikhana stop on the
  Amu Darya. More time, more story.
- **16:00** — Arrive **Khiva**, check in beside (or inside) the
  walls. First walk into **Itchan Kala** as the day cools — the
  squat turquoise **Kalta Minor** minaret glowing in low sun is
  the arrival image.
- **18:30** — Watch sunset from the **Kuhna Ark watchtower**
  (small separate fee) — mud-brick city, turquoise domes, desert
  beyond. NatGeo put Khiva on its 2026 list for exactly this.
- **20:00** — Rooftop dinner in the old town — order **shivit
  oshi**, Khiva's startling green dill noodles; you can't get them
  anywhere else.

**Where to stay in Khiva** (two nights)

- *The experience:* **Orient Star Khiva** — rooms in the cells of
  an 1855 madrasah inside the walls; character first, luxury
  second, and worth it for one stay.
- *Comfort:* **Hotel Bankir Khiva** — superbly rated, rooftop
  views, beside the walls.
- *Budget:* **Islam Khodja guesthouse** — family-run inside the
  old city, breakfast under the vines.

## Day 8 — Khiva: a whole day inside the walls

- **8:30** — Buy the **Itchan Kala combined ticket** (9:00–21:00
  officially, gates open earlier; 250,000 som — covers most
  monuments for two days) and start before the day-trippers:
  the **Juma Mosque**'s forest of 200+ carved wooden pillars,
  some a thousand years old.
- **10:30** — Climb the **Islam Khodja minaret** — Khiva's
  tallest; steep, dark, and the view over the mud-walled city is
  the best in Central Asia.
- **12:30** — Lunch, then the shade hours: **Tosh Hovli palace**'s
  harem courtyards and the **Pakhlavan Makhmud mausoleum**
  (30,000 som) — Khiva's most sacred, most beautiful interior.
- **16:00** — The craft lanes: Khiva's wood-carvers and
  fur-hat-makers work in open shops; prices are the best on the
  whole route.
- **18:00** — Walk the **city walls** at sunset — the full
  circuit of ramparts is the trip's closing scene.
- **20:00** — Final dinner on a roof; count domes.

## Day 9 — Fly home via Tashkent

- **Morning** — Taxi to **Urgench airport** (~35 min), fly to
  Tashkent (~1h40, ~USD 40–70, several daily) and connect home the
  same day. Buy your bread and halva at Siyob-stocked airport
  shops if you failed earlier — but you won't have.

## What to pack

- Sun kit even in shoulder season: hat, sunglasses, SPF — the
  desert sun is sincere.
- Modest cover for mosques and mausoleums; a scarf for women at
  active shrines (Shah-i-Zinda, Pakhlavan Makhmud).
- Real walking shoes — old-town lanes are cobbles and dust.
- A water bottle and rehydration salts for the desert legs.
- Crisp US dollars as backup cash, and a coin-and-small-notes
  wallet: som comes in fat stacks.
- Stomach basics — the food is fresh but rich; oily plov humbles
  the unprepared.
- An empty bag corner for ikat fabric, ceramics and Samarkand
  bread. Non-negotiable.

## FAQ

**Is Uzbekistan safe?**
One of the safest countries you can visit — violent crime against
tourists is close to unheard of, streets feel calm at night, and
police are helpful (the checkpoint-heavy old days are gone).
Standard bazaar-pickpocket care applies.

**When exactly should I go?**
April–May: blossoms, green valleys, perfect 20s°C. September–
October: golden light, grape harvest, warm days. June–August is
40°C+ on the Bukhara–Khiva leg — only for the heat-proof. Winter
is empty and photogenic but cold and some trains thin out.

**Do people speak English?**
In hotels and tourist restaurants, increasingly yes; elsewhere
Uzbek and Russian rule. Yandex Translate plus goodwill covers the
rest — and goodwill here is abundant.

**Is the food friendly to vegetarians and halal travellers?**
Halal is the default — this is a Muslim-majority country, so meat
is broadly halal and alcohol optional. Vegetarians need mild
effort: plov is meat-based (some places do a version without),
but lagman noodles, fresh salads, somsa with pumpkin, bread and
bazaar fruit go far.

**Should I be worried about the trains selling out?**
It's the one genuine logistics risk. Afrosiyob economy sells out
days ahead in season and the Khiva train runs ~3 days a week —
book the rails before the hotels, at the 60-day mark if you can.

**Can I run this route in reverse?**
Yes — fly Tashkent→Urgench first and train back east, ending with
the Registan as the finale. It works equally well; we start in
Tashkent because arrival admin (SIM, cash, jet lag) is easiest in
the capital.
