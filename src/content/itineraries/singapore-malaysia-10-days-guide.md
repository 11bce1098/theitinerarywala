Most people pick one and wonder about the other. They sit two hours
apart, they share a border you can cross overland, and together they
make the easiest first trip to Asia you can plan: English everywhere,
drinkable tap water at one end, and food that is the reason to come.

**Singapore** is the soft landing — a city that works, where the best
meal of your trip costs about the same as a coffee. **Malaysia** is
the one with the texture: a Dutch-red colonial port at **Melaka**,
**Kuala Lumpur**'s towers and cave temples, and **Penang**, which is
arguably the finest eating city in Southeast Asia.

Ten days splits cleanly: three nights in Singapore, one in Melaka,
two in KL, three in Penang. Fly **into Singapore, home from Penang**
and you never double back.

## Before you go: two countries, two arrival cards

### Neither needs much, and both need a form

- **Most passports need no visa for either country.** Singapore
  admits most visitors visa-free, and Malaysia's exemption covers
  most nationalities for 30–90 days. Check yours; the exemption
  follows your passport, not where you live.
- **Two free arrival cards, both mandatory, both online.** Singapore
  wants the **SG Arrival Card**, filed within three days of arriving.
  Malaysia wants the **MDAC**, filed within three days of arriving.
  Neither costs anything, and the copycat sites that charge for them
  are the only expensive part.
- File the Malaysian one **before you leave Singapore**, not at the
  border. It takes two minutes on hotel wifi and saves an argument.
- If you do need a Singapore visa, the base fee is small but agent
  totals run higher — and you must apply through an authorised agent
  rather than directly.

### Two currencies, and they are not close

- **Singapore dollars (SGD)** and **Malaysian ringgit (MYR)** — and
  the ringgit is worth roughly a third of the Singapore dollar, which
  catches people out on day four. A meal that felt cheap in Singapore
  is startling in Penang.
- Cards work everywhere in both. Singapore is effectively cashless;
  Malaysia less so — carry ringgit for hawker stalls, markets and
  small-town taxis.
- **Tap your contactless card on every MRT gate and bus in
  Singapore** — no ticket needed, no card to buy.
- **Grab runs Malaysia** — cars, food, everything. It also works in
  Singapore, where taxis are honest anyway.
- Alcohol is heavily taxed in both, and rare in conservative parts of
  Malaysia. Budget for it or plan around it.

### Crossing the border

- **The land crossing at Johor Bahru** is the classic route and the
  cheapest, but the causeway queues are legendary at weekends and on
  public holidays. Cross **midweek and mid-morning**, never on a
  Friday evening.
- **The simpler alternative is to fly.** Singapore to Kuala Lumpur is
  among the busiest air routes in the world — hourly departures, an
  hour in the air, and fares that often beat the bus once you value
  the day you get back.
- This itinerary goes **overland to Melaka** (about four hours by
  coach, and Melaka is worth the stop), then **KL to Penang by
  train** on the electrified line, which is comfortable and fast.
- **You clear immigration twice at a land crossing** — out of one
  country, into the other. Keep passports accessible and expect the
  coach to wait for everyone.

### The things each country expects

- **Singapore's rules are real**, not folklore: no eating on the MRT,
  no durian in hotels, and littering fines that are enforced.
- **The chope**: a packet of tissues on a hawker table means the seat
  is taken. It is not litter, and moving it is rude.
- **Halal is the default in Malaysia** rather than a request — look
  for the JAKIM logo if it matters to you, and note that
  non-halal food is clearly separated rather than absent.
- **Dress modestly for mosques and temples** in Malaysia; most lend
  robes at the door.

## Day 1 — Land in Singapore, Marina Bay switches on

- **Afternoon** — Land, drop bags. Singapore's airport is a sight in
  its own right, but save it for the end — you fly out of Penang, so
  see **Jewel** now, on the way in.
- **Evening** — **Marina Bay** after dark: the Supertrees light up
  twice a night, free, and the waterfront walk is the city's
  introduction to itself.

## Day 2 — Gardens, and three cultures in three stops

- **Morning** — **Gardens by the Bay**: the Cloud Forest before the
  heat, the Flower Dome after.
- **Afternoon** — **Chinatown, Little India and Kampong Glam**, three
  MRT stops apart and completely different from one another. Eat in
  all three.
- **Evening** — A hawker centre. **Michelin-listed stalls cost a few
  dollars**, and the queue tells you which one.

## Day 3 — Singapore your way

- Options: **Sentosa** for the island day, the **Botanic Gardens**
  for the quiet one, or the **Night Safari**, which is better than it
  sounds.
- **Evening** — Book the morning coach to Melaka and file your MDAC.

## Day 4 — Overland to Melaka

- **Morning** — Coach to **Melaka**, roughly four hours including the
  border. Two passport queues, one lunch stop.
- **Afternoon** — **Dutch Square** and the hill above it: a Portuguese
  gate, a Dutch church and a British lighthouse in ten minutes of
  walking, which is the whole history of the place.
- **Evening** — **Jonker Street**, best on a weekend night when the
  night market runs.

## Day 5 — Melaka slow, then north to KL

- **Morning** — A river walk, then the Baba-Nyonya heritage houses —
  the Peranakan culture that grew where the trade routes met.
- **Afternoon** — Coach to **Kuala Lumpur**, about two hours.
- **Evening** — The **Petronas Towers** from the park below, where
  the fountains run after dark and the view costs nothing.

## Day 6 — Caves, towers and the old quarter

- **Morning** — **Batu Caves** early: 272 coloured steps, a limestone
  cavern at the top, and monkeys with no respect for anybody.
- **Afternoon** — **Merdeka Square** and the old railway station, then
  **Central Market** for lunch.
- **Evening** — A rooftop somewhere, or **Jalan Alor**'s street-food
  strip, which is the better meal.

## Day 7 — The train north to Penang

- **Morning** — **KL → Butterworth** on the electric train, about
  four hours and genuinely comfortable, then the ferry or bridge
  across to **George Town**.
- **Afternoon** — Drop bags and walk. George Town's old quarter is a
  UNESCO site and best discovered on foot without a plan.
- **Evening** — Your first Penang meal. Ask anyone where they eat;
  follow that answer.

## Day 8 — George Town properly

- **Morning** — The **clan jetties**, the Blue Mansion, and the
  street art that made the city famous.
- **Afternoon** — **Penang Hill** by funicular for the view and five
  degrees of relief.
- **Evening** — **Gurney Drive** or a neighbourhood hawker centre.
  This is the eating capital of the trip; plan two dinners if you can.

## Day 9 — Penang at your pace

- Options: **Kek Lok Si** temple, the **spice garden**, **Batu
  Ferringhi**'s beach, or simply another day of George Town's food.
- **Evening** — Last meal. Make it char kway teow.

## Day 10 — Fly home from Penang

- Penang's airport is small and quick. Direct flights reach the Gulf
  and much of Asia; most other routings connect through KL.

## What to pack

- **UK-style Type G plugs in both** — one adapter, the whole trip.
- **Light, modest clothing.** It is hot and humid year-round in both
  countries, and shoulders and knees matter at temples and mosques.
- **A real umbrella.** Tropical rain arrives fast, hard and briefly.
- **Two eSIMs or a regional plan** — a Singapore-only eSIM stops
  working the moment you cross.

## FAQ

**Is the land border worth it, or should I just fly?**
Take the coach if you are stopping in Melaka, which this itinerary
does — it is on the way and the flight is not. If you are going
straight to Kuala Lumpur, fly: it is an hour, it is cheap, and the
causeway can eat an afternoon.

**Which country should get more days?**
Malaysia. Singapore is compact and expensive; three nights covers it
properly. Malaysia has more ground and costs less to cover it.

**Is this a good first trip to Asia?**
It is the one I would pick. English is widely spoken in both, the
transport works, the tap water is drinkable in Singapore, and nothing
about it requires improvisation.

**Do I need two SIM cards?**
One regional eSIM covering both is simplest. A country-specific plan
will stop at the border, usually on the coach, usually when you want
a map.
