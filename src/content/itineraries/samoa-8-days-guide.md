Most Pacific destinations sell you a resort. **Samoa sells you a
*fale*** — an open-sided thatched hut on the sand, with a mat, a
mosquito net and roll-down blinds, usually including breakfast and
dinner, for a fraction of what a hotel costs. You sleep with the
sea three metres away and no wall between you and it.

That is possible because of ***fa'a Samoa*** — the Samoan way —
which is not a tourist phrase. Around **80% of land is still
customary**, held by extended families under a *matai* chief
system that has governed here for three thousand years and was
never dismantled. Almost everything you will visit is owned by a
village, charges a small fee to that village, and is the better
for it.

The island itself delivers: **To Sua Ocean Trench**, a collapsed
lava tube in a garden that you climb down a ladder into;
the **Saleaula lava fields** on Savai'i, where an eruption buried
a village and left a church standing in the flow; and **blowholes
that throw spray higher than the palms.**

## Before you go: Sundays, fales and the two islands

### Entry and money

- **Most nationalities enter visa-free** for 60 or 90 days.
- **Samoan tālā (WST).** Cash is essential outside Apia — **fales,
  village fees and buses take nothing else.** Draw everything in
  Apia or at the airport.
- Cards work at Apia hotels and supermarkets and very few other
  places.

### Sunday is genuinely different

- **Samoa is deeply Christian, and Sunday is observed.** Most
  shops, many restaurants and some attractions close; villages
  hold services morning and evening and expect quiet.
- ***Sa*** — evening prayer curfew — happens in villages around
  dusk, for perhaps ten or fifteen minutes. **If you are walking
  or driving through a village when the bell or conch sounds,
  stop** and wait it out. People will not be rude about it, and
  stopping is the courteous thing.
- **Go to a village service if invited.** The singing is
  extraordinary and the invitation is genuine.
- **Sunday *to'ona'i***, the family feast after church, is the
  meal of the week.

### Fales, and village fees

- **Beach fales** are the accommodation that defines Samoa:
  open-sided, on the sand, usually **half-board included**, often
  with shared bathrooms. They cost a fraction of a hotel.
- **Almost every beach, waterfall and swimming hole is village
  land** and charges a **small fee** collected at a hut on the
  way in. This is correct, it goes to the village, and it is
  usually a few tālā.
- **Dress:** shoulders and knees covered away from the beach, and
  **do not wear swimwear through a village.** A *lavalava* —
  wrapped cloth — solves it and everybody wears one.

### Getting around, and the ferry

- **Hire a car** for Upolu. Driving is **on the left**, which
  Samoa switched to in 2009 — one of the only countries ever to
  change sides.
- **Buses** are brightly painted wooden-bodied trucks, cheap,
  infrequent, and run to no timetable. Worth doing once.
- **Savai'i is reached by ferry** from Mulifanua to Salelologa —
  about **90 minutes**, several times daily, and it takes cars.
  **Schedules change and the last sailing is earlier than you
  expect**; check locally rather than online.

### When to go

- **May to October is the dry season**, cooler and less humid, and
  the time to come.
- **November to April is the wet season and the cyclone window**,
  with heavier rain and higher humidity. It is also cheaper and
  greener.
- It is warm year-round — high twenties to low thirties, always.

## Day 1 — Land in Apia

- **Most flights land in the small hours.** Faleolo (APW) is 35 km
  west of Apia, about 45 minutes.
- **10:00** — Having slept, walk **Apia**: the seafront, the
  **clock tower**, and the **Flea Market** for *lavalava*, shell
  jewellery and *siapo* bark cloth.
- **12:00** — The **Fish Market**, which is at its best very early
  but is good at any hour.
- **13:30** — Lunch, then **draw all the cash you need for the
  week**.
- **15:00** — **Robert Louis Stevenson Museum** at Vailima, the
  house he built and died in at 44 — Samoans called him *Tusitala*,
  the teller of tales, and carried his coffin up Mount Vaea.
- **16:30** — The **climb to his tomb** on Mount Vaea: 45 minutes
  up a forest track, steep and humid, with the epitaph he wrote
  for himself on the stone at the top and the whole harbour below.
- **19:00** — Dinner in Apia. **Oka** — raw fish in coconut cream
  and lime — and *palusami*, taro leaves baked in coconut.

## Day 2 — The north coast, and east

- **08:30** — Collect the car and head east along the north coast.
- **09:30** — **Piula Cave Pool**, in the grounds of a Methodist
  theological college: a freshwater spring in a cave beneath the
  church, running straight out into the sea. You can swim through
  a short underwater passage between two chambers if you want to.
- **11:00** — **Falefa Falls** and **Fuipisia Falls**, the latter
  a 55 m drop you look over the lip of.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then on to **Lalomanu** on the south-east
  corner.
- **15:00** — **Lalomanu beach**, consistently called one of the
  best in the Pacific — white sand, a reef a hundred metres out,
  and **a line of beach fales along it**.
- **16:00** — Check into a fale. Snorkel straight off the beach.
- **18:30** — Dinner at the fale, cooked by the family who run it,
  eaten at a long table with whoever else is staying.
- **20:30** — The blinds roll down and that is the whole building.
  You will hear the reef all night.

## Day 3 — To Sua Ocean Trench

- **08:30** — **To Sua**, half an hour west along the south coast,
  and get there early — it fills by eleven and the ladder takes
  one person at a time.
- **09:00** — It is a **30 m collapsed lava tube** in the middle
  of a manicured garden, connected to the sea by an underwater
  tunnel so it rises and falls with the tide. You go down a
  **steep wooden ladder** to a platform and then into water that
  is startlingly clear and deep.
- **10:30** — Swim toward the tunnel mouth and feel the surge.
  Time it with the tide — mid-tide is best, and the staff will
  tell you.
- **12:00** — The gardens, the blowholes on the shore below, and
  the cliff-edge swings.
- **13:30** — Lunch, then **Lotofaga** and the south coast road.
- **15:30** — **Sopo'aga Falls** viewpoint, and the **Togitogiga
  Falls** in O Le Pupu-Pue national park, where you can swim
  under a low cascade.
- **18:00** — Back to Lalomanu.

## Day 4 — The south coast, and the lava

- **08:30** — West along the south coast, which is the quieter and
  prettier side.
- **09:30** — **Giant Clam Sanctuary** at Savaia: a village-run
  reserve where you snorkel over dozens of *Tridacna* clams up to
  a metre across, blue and green mantles, in shallow water.
- **11:30** — **Return to Apia**, or stay south.
- **13:00** — Lunch at **Manono-tai** if you take the boat —
  **Manono island** has no cars, no dogs and a footpath right
  round it in 90 minutes, and it is the easiest way to see a
  village that functions entirely traditionally.
- **16:00** — Back to the mainland.
- **18:00** — Sleep near **Mulifanua** for the morning ferry, or
  in Apia.
- **19:30** — Dinner, and **confirm the ferry time**.

## Day 5 — Ferry to Savai'i

- **08:00** — The **ferry from Mulifanua to Salelologa**, about 90
  minutes across the Apolima Strait with the car.
- **10:00** — **Savai'i** is larger than Upolu, far less
  developed, and feels like Samoa fifty years ago. It is also the
  **sixth largest island in Polynesia** and almost entirely
  volcanic.
- **11:00** — **Saleaula lava fields** on the north coast. The
  **Mt Matavanu eruption of 1905–11** poured across this coast for
  six years and buried five villages. What is left is a black
  expanse you walk across, with the **shell of the LMS church
  standing in the middle of it** — the lava flowed in the door and
  around the walls and set, and there is a nun's grave the flow
  parted around and left untouched.
- **13:30** — Lunch.
- **15:00** — **Lava tube caves** at Letui or the **Dwarf's Cave**
  at Paia, which is village-guided, pitch black and genuinely
  large.
- **17:00** — Check into a beach fale at **Manase** on the north
  coast.
- **19:00** — Dinner, and the north coast is very quiet indeed.

## Day 6 — Blowholes and waterfalls

- **08:30** — **Alofaaga Blowholes** at Taga on the south-west
  coast, and this is Savai'i's set piece: the sea forces through
  lava tubes and fires spray **thirty metres and more into the
  air**. The local men will **throw a coconut into the hole** and
  it is launched out of sight. Pay them for it.
- **11:00** — The **Afu Aau (Olemoe) Falls**, inland: a wide fall
  into a deep cold pool in forest, with a village fee at the gate,
  and almost nobody there.
- **13:30** — Lunch at **Satupa'itea**.
- **15:00** — The **Canopy Walkway** at Falealupo, a rope bridge
  into a 40 m banyan on the island's western tip, if it is open —
  check, because it has been closed for repairs at times.
- **17:00** — **Cape Mulinu'u**, the westernmost point, and a
  sunset that for a century was billed as the last of the day
  anywhere on earth — until Samoa **jumped the dateline in 2011**
  and became one of the first places to see tomorrow instead.
- **19:00** — Back to Manase.

## Day 7 — Savai'i, slow, and back

- **08:00** — Snorkel off Manase, or walk the coast.
- **10:00** — **Mt Matavanu crater** if the track is passable — a
  guided walk to the rim of the cone that caused the lava field,
  with a village guide and a jeep most of the way.
- **13:00** — Lunch, then **the ferry back** to Upolu — allow
  plenty of time and check the sailing.
- **16:30** — Land at Mulifanua, and drive to Apia.
- **18:00** — **A fiafia night** if your hotel runs one — Samoan
  dance including the *siva* and the *fire knife*, which
  originated here, performed by staff and often by their children.
  It is unapologetically for visitors and it is also very good.
- **20:30** — Last dinner.

## Day 8 — Fly home

- **Morning** — Faleolo is 45 minutes from Apia. **Many
  departures are in the small hours**, so check whether your
  "morning" flight is actually tonight.
- **Last stop** — the **Flea Market** for *siapo* bark cloth and
  coconut oil.
- **Note** — Spend the tālā; it is not changeable elsewhere.

## What to pack

- **A *lavalava*** — buy one on day one at the market. It solves
  every modesty question and is what everyone wears.
- **Reef shoes.** Much of the swimming is over coral and lava
  rock, and the lava is sharp.
- **A torch.** Fales have no electric light after the generator
  goes off, and the caves need one.
- **A mosquito net is usually provided** — check when booking, and
  bring repellent regardless; **dengue occurs**.
- **A dry bag** for the ferry and the boat to Manono.
- **Australian-style Type I plugs**, 230V.
- **Cash in small notes**, drawn in Apia.

## FAQ

**Are beach fales really that basic?**
Open sides with roll-down blinds, a mattress on the floor or a
simple bed, a mosquito net, and a shared bathroom block. **That is
the whole building.** Half board is normally included and the food
is cooked by the family. People who want a door and air
conditioning will not enjoy it; people who want to hear the reef
all night will not stay anywhere else again.

**How strictly is Sunday observed?**
Strictly. Shops shut, villages hold services, and the whole
country slows down. **Plan a beach day or a travel day around
it**, and if you are in a village at dusk when *sa* is called,
stop walking for ten minutes. It is a small courtesy and it is
noticed.

**Upolu or Savai'i?**
Both, and this itinerary splits them. **Upolu** has Apia, To Sua
and Lalomanu; **Savai'i** has the lava fields, the blowholes and
far fewer people. Savai'i is where most visitors say the trip
actually happened.

**Samoa or Fiji?**
**Fiji** has more resorts, more flights and the Yasawa island-
hopping. **Samoa has the stronger living culture** — fa'a Samoa
is intact in a way that is rare — and the beach fale system,
which has no Fijian equivalent at that price.

**When should I go?**
**May to October**, the dry season. November to April is cyclone
season, wetter and cheaper.
