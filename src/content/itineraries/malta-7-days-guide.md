Malta is smaller than the Isle of Wight and holds more history per
square kilometre than anywhere in Europe. The **Ġgantija** temples
on Gozo were built around **3600 BC** — older than Stonehenge,
older than the pyramids at Giza, and the oldest free-standing
structures on earth.

Three and a half thousand years later the **Knights of St John**
survived the Great Siege of 1565 and built **Valletta** as a
fortress city on the headland above the harbour, in grid-planned
baroque, which is why it looks like nothing else in the
Mediterranean.

Everything is within an hour of everything else. **English is an
official language.** And the practical catch is one booking you
need to make now rather than later.

## Before you go: the Hypogeum, the buses and the season

### Getting in, and getting around

- **Malta International (MLA)** is at Luqa, 8 km from Valletta —
  20 minutes by bus or taxi.
- **You do not need a car**, and in Valletta and Mdina you cannot
  use one. Buses reach everywhere, cost a few euro with a
  **Tallinja** card, and are slow but frequent. Hire a car for one
  or two days if you want the south and the north coast quickly.
- **Driving is on the left**, a British inheritance along with the
  red post boxes and the plug sockets.
- **Ferries** matter more than roads: Valletta–Sliema and
  Valletta–Three Cities are 5–10 minutes and save a long loop;
  **Ċirkewwa–Mġarr** to Gozo is 25 minutes and runs every 45
  minutes or so, more in summer.

### Book the Hypogeum now, before anything else

- The **Hypogeum of Ħal Saflieni** is a 5,000-year-old underground
  burial complex cut into living rock across three levels. It is
  the only one of its kind anywhere.
- **Only about 80 visitors are admitted per day**, in groups of
  ten, to limit humidity damage. Tickets go on sale **weeks to
  months ahead and sell out**.
- Book it the moment your dates are fixed, then build the rest of
  the week around the slot you get. A small number of
  last-minute tickets are released at the National Museum of
  Archaeology, and queuing for them is a gamble.

### When to go

- **April to June and September to early November** are the
  answer: warm sea, bearable sun, and the Blue Lagoon is not a
  car park.
- **July and August** are hot, crowded and expensive, and Comino
  becomes genuinely unpleasant.
- **December to March** is mild, green, very quiet, and the sea is
  too cold for most people. Valletta in winter is lovely and half
  the price.
- **Note:** many smaller sites close early — 16:30 or 17:00 — and
  some shut on Sundays. Check before you build an afternoon around
  one.

### Money and practicalities

- **Euro.** Cards work everywhere; small kiosks and the fish
  market want cash.
- **Maltese and English are both official.** Everything is signed
  in both and everyone speaks English.
- **Modest dress in churches** — shoulders and knees — and St
  John's Co-Cathedral bans stiletto heels, which would go through
  the marble tomb slabs you walk on.

## Day 1 — Land, and Valletta at dusk

- **14:00** — Land at **MLA**. The X4 bus or a 20-minute taxi puts
  you in Valletta.
- **15:30** — Drop bags. **Stay in Valletta itself** rather than
  Sliema or St Julian's: the capital empties in the evening and
  having it to yourself after six is the best thing about being
  there.
- **16:30** — Walk in through **City Gate** and down **Republic
  Street**, the spine of the grid. Valletta is 1 km by 600 m and
  you can cross it in fifteen minutes.
- **17:30** — **Upper Barrakka Gardens** for the view over the
  **Grand Harbour** — the Three Cities opposite, Fort St Angelo on
  the point, and the dockyards below. The **Saluting Battery**
  fires at **12:00 and 16:00** daily.
- **19:00** — **Strait Street**, once the sailors' bar strip and now
  the restaurant quarter, for dinner. *Fenek* — rabbit, stewed in
  wine and garlic — is the national dish and not a gimmick.
- **21:00** — Walk the empty streets. The day-trippers have gone
  back to Sliema and the limestone glows under the lamps.

## Day 2 — Valletta properly

- **09:00** — **St John's Co-Cathedral** at opening. The exterior
  is a plain fortress box; **the interior is the most densely
  decorated baroque room in Europe** — every surface carved and
  gilded, and the floor is **400 tomb slabs of inlaid marble**
  marking the knights buried beneath.
- **09:45** — The Oratory holds **Caravaggio's *The Beheading of
  Saint John the Baptist*** — his largest canvas, **the only
  painting he ever signed**, and signed in the blood running from
  the saint's throat. He painted it as a knight of the Order and
  was expelled months later for brawling.
- **11:00** — The **Grandmaster's Palace** and its armoury.
- **12:30** — Lunch, and a *pastizz* from a hole in the wall —
  ricotta or mushy peas in flaky pastry, about 50 cents, and the
  national snack.
- **14:00** — The **ferry to the Three Cities**, five minutes
  across the harbour. **Birgu (Vittoriosa)** is older than
  Valletta, was the Knights' first base, and is quieter and more
  lived-in — narrow lanes, the Inquisitor's Palace, and **Fort St
  Angelo** on the point where the Great Siege was decided.
- **17:00** — A *dgħajsa* — the traditional harbour boat — back,
  or the ferry.
- **19:30** — Dinner in Valletta.

## Day 3 — The south: temples, and the underground

- **Your Hypogeum slot governs this day.** Build the rest around
  whatever time you were given.
- **09:00** — **Ħaġar Qim** and **Mnajdra** on the south cliffs,
  20 minutes apart on one ticket. Megalithic temples from around
  **3600–3200 BC**, now under protective canopies. **Mnajdra's**
  lower temple is aligned so that the equinox sun strikes the main
  axis and the solstices light the edges of the doorway — built by
  people without metal tools.
- **11:00** — The **Hypogeum** if that is your slot: three levels
  cut down into rock with hand tools, the **Oracle Room** with its
  deliberate acoustic resonance, and an estimated 7,000 burials.
  You go in with nine other people and no cameras. **An hour, and
  the strangest hour in Malta.**
- **13:00** — **Marsaxlokk** for lunch: a working fishing village
  of *luzzu* boats painted with the **Eye of Osiris** on the bow,
  and the fish restaurants along the front. **Sunday morning is
  the big market.**
- **15:30** — The **Blue Grotto** at Wied iż-Żurrieq — small boats
  into sea caves where the water goes an improbable electric blue.
  **Morning light is better**; the boats stop running when the
  swell is up, so this is the flexible item.
- **18:00** — Back to Valletta.

## Day 4 — Mdina, Rabat and the centre

- **09:00** — **Mdina**, the old capital, 20 minutes inland.
  Walled, hilltop, Norman and baroque, and **about 250 people
  live inside** — which is why it is called **the Silent City**.
  Cars are banned apart from residents'.
- **09:30** — Go in through the main gate (which you will
  recognise from *Game of Thrones*), then get lost. The lanes are
  narrow enough that two people pass sideways, and the limestone
  takes the light beautifully in the morning.
- **10:30** — **St Paul's Cathedral** and the bastion walk, with
  the whole island laid out below and Valletta visible on the
  coast.
- **12:00** — **Rabat** immediately outside the walls, and lunch —
  this is where the restaurants are cheaper and the locals eat.
- **13:30** — **St Paul's Catacombs**: four hectares of Roman and
  early Christian rock-cut tombs under the town, with carved
  dining platforms where families ate meals beside their dead.
- **15:30** — The **Dingli Cliffs**, Malta's highest point at
  253 m, for the drop straight into the sea and no development on
  it.
- **17:00** — **Mosta** and its **rotunda**, with one of the
  largest unsupported domes in Europe — and a replica of the bomb
  that fell through it during Mass in April 1942 and failed to
  explode.
- **19:30** — Back for dinner.

## Day 5 — To Gozo

- **09:00** — Bus or drive to **Ċirkewwa** at the northern tip, 45
  minutes, then the **ferry to Mġarr** — 25 minutes, and you pay
  only on the way back.
- **10:30** — Gozo is greener, slower and a third the population
  density. Collect a hire car or arrange a driver; the buses here
  are infrequent.
- **11:00** — **Ġgantija** at Xagħra: two temples inside one wall,
  built **around 3600 BC**, with blocks over five metres and
  fifty tonnes. These are **the oldest free-standing structures
  in the world** — a thousand years before the pyramids. The
  museum alongside makes the chronology land.
- **13:00** — Lunch at **Victoria (Rabat)**, the island's small
  capital.
- **14:30** — The **Cittadella** above it: a fortified hilltop you
  walk the ramparts of, with the whole of Gozo visible in every
  direction and the sea on three sides.
- **16:30** — **Dwejra**: the **Azure Window collapsed into the
  sea in a storm in March 2017** and is simply gone — what remains
  is the Inland Sea, a lagoon joined to the open water by a cave
  tunnel that boats run through, and the Blue Hole, which is one
  of the best shore dives in the Mediterranean.
- **18:30** — Sunset there, then dinner in **Xlendi** or
  **Marsalforn**.

## Day 6 — Gozo, and Comino

- **08:00** — **Comino early, or not at all.** The **Blue
  Lagoon** is a sandbar channel of genuinely turquoise water
  between two islands — and in July and August it holds several
  thousand people and a wall of deckchairs. The first boat of the
  day, or the shoulder season, is the whole difference.
- **10:30** — Walk away from the lagoon. Comino has **about three
  permanent residents**, no cars, and the path round to **Santa
  Marija Bay** and the **Crystal Lagoon** loses the crowd in
  fifteen minutes.
- **13:00** — Back to Gozo for lunch.
- **14:30** — **Ramla Bay**, the red-sand beach below Xagħra, with
  **Calypso's Cave** in the cliff above it — where Homer's nymph
  is supposed to have kept Odysseus for seven years.
- **16:30** — **Ta' Pinu** basilica, isolated in open country, and
  its wall of crutches and letters left by people who believe they
  were healed there.
- **19:00** — Last Gozo dinner. Gozitan cheeselets — *ġbejniet* —
  and the island's own wine.

## Day 7 — Back, and fly home

- **09:00** — Ferry back to Malta, 25 minutes.
- **10:30** — **Sliema** or **St Julian's** for the last couple of
  hours if your flight is late — the ferry across to Valletta from
  Sliema gives the classic photograph of the city rising out of
  the water, and it costs under two euro.
- **12:30** — Lunch, then the airport, 20 minutes from anywhere.
- **Last buy** — **Kinnie**, the bitter-orange soft drink nobody
  else makes, and a bag of *ġbejniet* if you can carry them.

## What to pack

- **Shoulders and knees covered** for churches, and **no stiletto
  heels** in St John's — they damage the marble tomb floor.
- **Proper shoes** for the temple sites and the Cittadella walls,
  which are uneven limestone.
- **Sun protection.** There is very little shade anywhere on these
  islands, and the limestone reflects.
- **Swimming things** from May to October, and **reef shoes** —
  most of the best swimming is off rock, not sand.
- **UK-style Type G plugs**, 230V.
- **A light layer for the ferry**, which is windy in any season.

## FAQ

**Is seven days too long for somewhere this small?**
No, and the temples are why. Malta has **three UNESCO listings**
and six megalithic temple complexes, plus Valletta and the
Hypogeum. A long weekend covers the capital; a week lets you take
Gozo slowly, which is the half most people wish they had given
more time to.

**Do I need to book anything else in advance?**
The **Hypogeum**, weeks ahead, without exception. Everything else
can be bought on the day outside high summer.

**Is the Blue Lagoon worth it?**
Early in the day or outside July and August, yes. In peak season
at midday it is one of the most crowded beaches in the
Mediterranean, and Gozo's own bays are better.

**Which island should I sleep on?**
Both. Four nights in Valletta and two on Gozo is the split this
itinerary uses, and Gozo after the last ferry is a different
country from Malta at midday.

**Can I do it without a car?**
Yes. Buses reach every site in this guide. A car for the south
coast day and the Gozo day saves perhaps three hours each time,
and nothing else.
